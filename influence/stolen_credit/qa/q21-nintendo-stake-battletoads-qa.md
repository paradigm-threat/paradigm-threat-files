# Q21 — Why does Nintendo’s stake in Rare matter, and what does Battletoads two-player prove?

**Question:** If Rare made the games, why bring Nintendo’s ownership percentage and Battletoads co-op into the credit argument?

## Answer

**Industry position:** Nintendo owned about half of Rare as a second-party investment. Blank credits were Chris Stamper’s anti-headhunter policy. Battletoads is famous for being hard. Two-player is just old-school punishment. None of that proves Kyoto finished the carts.

**This site:** Three public facts lock together.

Nintendo’s roughly **49%** stake is the primary citation that Nintendo had skin in the finish. A near-half owner does not treat the cart as a UK hobby. That stake is why a finished, publishable, high-enough product could still ship even when the British face looked loose. It is the motive for Nintendo-side craft and publish standards to stay in the pipeline. It is not, by itself, a recovered memo that R&D typed every byte.

The blank credits are the citation that Rare was barely attending to the products as authored work. About **6 of 40+** Rare NES games print any staff credit. *Battletoads* prints none. A shop that owns the craft prints names. A shop that leaves a blank page is not minding authorship — and is the same class of shop that does not mind whether the second controller works.

*Battletoads* two-player is the QA pinnacle of that gap. Friendly fire with no off switch, shared continues so one death restarts both, and on the US NES cart a **Clinger-Wingers** bug that leaves Player 2 unable to finish the stage (Japan and Europe fixed that stage bug). That mode is not “hard.” It is unfinished co-op bolted onto a hit. Something in the room could still ship a soundtrack and a brawler that sold. The named UK shop could not even QA the mode that required the most local attention. A team that manufactured the whole game end-to-end does not ship that.

**Working conclusion:** the stake explains why excellence still appears; the blanks explain Rare’s indifference to the credit object; two-player *Battletoads* is playable proof Rare was not the careful manufacturer of the whole product.
