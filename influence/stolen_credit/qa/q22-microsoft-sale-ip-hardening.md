# Q22 — What does the Microsoft sale of Rare prove?

**Question:** Rare sold to Microsoft on 24 September 2002. Isn’t that just a normal studio buyout — and aren’t the later Wise and Stamper interviews just nostalgia?

## Answer

**Industry position:** Microsoft paid about $375 million for a hit studio and Xbox exclusives. The Stampers took the money and moved on. Later interviews are oral history. The Xbox years were a new platform learning curve, not proof the Nintendo-era shop never had craft.

**This site:** The sale is not the calendar of a shop that still believed its Nintendo heyday would keep shipping.

Rare’s peak product was the Nintendo second-party years. When that lane ended, the Xbox-era titles did not match *Battletoads*, *Donkey Kong Country*, or *GoldenEye*. A studio that still had that finish in-house would not need to exit into a rival platform’s catalog. The failed IBM/MIDI spend and the blank NES credits already say Britain could not finish the carts without Nintendo R&D. The **24 September 2002** sale is the business proof of the same gap: when the Kyoto partnership was gone, the name remained and the level did not.

Do not call $375M an “extremely low fire sale” as if that number alone were a courtroom exhibit. Contemporary coverage treated the Stampers as winners. Read the exit as the price of a **brand and a catalog** whose craft story was about to need hardening — not as proof Microsoft secretly valued Rare at zero.

**Working theory on the interviews:** After the transfer, the new IP holders had motive to investigate the old products and harden a public authorship story. The Wise “no MIDI / I typed hex” lines and the Stamper lockdown gloss sit **years and decades after** the carts, on the buyer side of the calendar. That is the wrong shape for people who sold the IP and walked into unrelated lives. It is the right shape for **post-sale IP hardening**: an offer too good to refuse, or deal terms that required the old faces to stand behind the catalog. The Stampers’ “we sold and moved on” line does not explain why the credited faces keep repeating the independence legend. Microsoft’s private diligence memo is unrecovered. The motive and the interview dates are what the public record leaves.
