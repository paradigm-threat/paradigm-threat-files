# Q1 — Why Barney, Teletubbies, and Pokémon?

**Question:** Why talk about a Barney generation, a Teletubbies generation, and a Pokémon generation instead of just saying “later fans”?

## Answer

Those names are a date, not a taste insult. *Barney & Friends* reached American public television in 1992. *Teletubbies* first aired on BBC Two on 31 March 1997. *Pocket Monsters Red* and *Green* went on sale in Japan in 1996. Cards and the TV series followed in Japan under that full name. The American games and cartoon arrived in September 1998 as *Pokémon*, in part because Pocket Monsters sat close to *Monster in My Pocket*. The later Morrison suit, and why a California toy shop losing on letters and syllables can still be heard as a Japan-trade story, is in the letter. Kids born in the 1990s, and many born late in the 1980s, got that Western launch as first major software. They missed the company that could still finish a world. They met the brand after the split.

In the mid-1990s, people who had already lived through the Nintendo years were already nervous. A kids collecting franchise that had sounded like a toy to most people, and like a problem only to people other people called paranoid, was about to be on American TV news as a political fight. The Japan run happened first. Then it was brought over.

A later FAQ that only says “nostalgia gap” is the ordinary answer. The missing years are why the later cohort cannot see the remakes as a loss. The maze is just the world to them. The older cohort still has a before.
