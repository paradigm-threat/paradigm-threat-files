# Q17 — If everyone signed an NDA, how can the credit story be tested?

**Question:** Nintendo and its contractors sit under NDAs. Doesn’t that mean outsiders cannot know who wrote the music, so the published credits and interviews have to stand?

## Answer

**Industry position:** Confidentiality is normal. Tools, driver source, and who sat in the room are trade secrets. The public record is the credit line plus later interviews. Silence is not evidence of theft.

**This site:** The NDA is why a tell-all will not arrive. It is also why the test has to be forensic. What remains in public is the cart, the printed credits, the file size, the loop lengths, and the words the credited face used years later. That pile is the available record. Reading it is how the drought gets deduced.

An NDA explains why Kyoto staff do not narrate the compile. It does not turn a 2011 interview into a 1991 receipt. *Battletoads* shipped in 1991 with no music credit. The hex legend and the Stamper anti-headhunter gloss harden in interviews and fan canon years and decades afterward. Those later texts are IP hardening written onto a blank page. They are not the room.

A forensic look asks what the available objects can still show. The MIDI path Wise describes did not become the soundtrack. The loops are game-craft lengths inside about a megabyte of NSF room. Only about 6 of 40-plus Rare NES games print any staff credit. A retail ROM cannot be decompiled back into the lost source, so “fingerprint” talk does not reopen the NDA or prove Kyoto was absent. The designed silence is the climate. The blanks, the failed tool, and the song form are the evidence that climate still leaves on the table.
