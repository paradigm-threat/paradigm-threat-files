# Q8 — Can a holder sit on a world forever?

**Question:** Is an IP holder allowed to sit on a property forever, never let anyone else try visions that were already in the original, and ignore critics who were inspired by the first version and uninspired by the official substitute — even at the volume of the Weather Channel music campaigns?

## Answer

Under current term and trademark practice, yes. That is the problem. The ethical answer from this reading is no. A finished world that entered the culture as a cartridge is not a private cabinet the holder may occupy until the 2080s while shipping a lesser official copy and treating every other vision as theft.

The Weather Channel analog is the house sound of Local on the 8s. Until 2013 the forecasts ran on jazz people lived with. The channel replaced it with production music made for the brand. Viewers wrote. A We the People petition asked them to resume the jazz. The holder kept the new package. A game company gets the same courtesy at a higher stake: millions of letters would still not pry the original world into competing hands.

If someone loved the first version and is uninspired by the remake, sitting on the IP and ignoring that person is already the demonstrated behavior. It is ethically viable only if exclusive control of childhood icons is already accepted as the government of culture.
