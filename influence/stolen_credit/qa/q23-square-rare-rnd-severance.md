# Q23 — Is Square’s PlayStation move the same pattern as Rare?

**Question:** Rare was sold to Microsoft. Square left Nintendo for Sony. Aren’t those different stories?

## Answer

**Industry position:** Different companies, different deals. Square chose PlayStation because *Final Fantasy VII* needed CD capacity. Rare chose Xbox money. Neither story implies Nintendo R&D built their engines or music.

**This site:** Different transactions. Same **result class**.

Both moves **rip the product away from the Nintendo R&D lane**, then fail to reproduce the heyday without it. Rare’s severance is the **24 September 2002** Microsoft sale and the inferior Xbox catalog. Square’s severance is the **1996** platform divorce and the post-*VII* drop.

On Square, this site’s working read is that *Final Fantasy VII* still sits on a pipeline from the Nintendo-era climate — including an author institutional claim that the world/field stack traces to a Nintendo-built engine, labeled as such because Square never printed Nintendo as engine vendor. The battle/world sprite mismatch on *VII* is the schism: an upgraded PlayStation-era battle system on top of an earlier stack that was never unified. *FF8* and *FF9* ride that post-switch family instead of inventing a clean new one. A major new engine push waits for *FFX* (2001), years after *VII* (1997). The ambition bar that made *VII* land is read as a Nintendo-era product; after the defection, Square could not remake that peak on *VIII*/*IX*.

| | Rare | Square |
|---|---|---|
| Peak | Nintendo second-party years | SNES + the *VII* rupture window |
| Severance | Sold to Microsoft, 24 Sep 2002 | Left Nintendo for PlayStation, 1996 |
| After | Xbox catalog inferior to Nintendo-era | *VIII*/*IX* on the *VII* lineage; peak not repeated |
| Public story | Independent British shop / Wise hex | Independent Square PS1 house |

Do not collapse them into one buyout. Keep them as one pattern: when the Kyoto finish leaves the room, the noun stays and the level drops — until someone hardens a story that the leftover company did it alone.
