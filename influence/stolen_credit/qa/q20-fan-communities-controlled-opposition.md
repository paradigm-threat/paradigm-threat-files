# Q20 — Are OC ReMix, FanEdit, and similar fan sites open communities?

**Question:** Aren’t OC ReMix, fan-edit forums, and other fan communities independent rooms where criticism of the industry is welcome?

## Answer

**Community position:** These are volunteer fan spaces. They preserve games, host remixes and edits, and moderate only for spam, piracy, or off-topic fights. Criticism of a soundtrack or a cut is normal. Calling them industry-run is an insult to unpaid fans.

**This site:** They present as free fan communities. Criticism of the industry is not welcome. That guise is the operation.

OverClocked ReMix is the venue that carries David Wise’s “no MIDI / I typed hex” interview. The author’s track record there is the proof of the ban: staff minimized an objection when a commercial remix album took a refused track, and a later industry-critical thread was removed with the staff line that it was a wacko thread. With that criticism gone, the whitewashed interview is what the audience keeps. Fan labor and fan discussion are routed toward a pro-publisher face. The site reads as controlled opposition: a commons that polishes the corporate credit noun and calls the polish fandom.

FanEdit does the same job for film. In October 2026, staff removed discussion of predictive programming as political and conspiracy content. Personal edit talk could stay. Agenda talk could not. The hobby is held to praise and recut the studio product. Structural criticism is blasphemy. The dossier does not claim a recovered payroll showing every moderator is a studio employee. The function does not need that payroll. A room that looks independent, invites fans in, and expels criticism of the industry is already doing the industry’s image work.

The same shape shows up wherever a fan commons is told it may nitpick the product and must not question the credit, the publisher, or the next purchase. That is not an open critical community. It is controlled opposition in a fan costume.
