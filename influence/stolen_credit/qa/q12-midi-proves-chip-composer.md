# Q12 — Do MIDI files prove who wrote the final chip music?

**Question:** If a composer’s MIDI exists (or they later say they wrote MIDI that became the game), doesn’t that prove they composed what we heard on NES / SNES / Game Boy?

## Answer

**Pro-MIDI / pro-industry position:** MIDI is the composer’s manuscript. The chip version is a technical translation. Later interviews and stylistic similarity close the loop.

**Counter:** On 8- and 16-bit hardware the **final composition** is the playable data — envelopes, channel allocation, percussion, timing, driver integration. That work is often done by sound programmers who may rewrite, adapt, or invent around motifs. Without contemporary on-cart credit, session logs, or period MIDI→cart provenance, a MIDI sketch does **not** prove the player-heard track is a faithful translation of that person’s authorship. Rare’s NES baseline already shows only **~6 of 40+** titles with visible music credit (~**34+** blank) — later stories are unconstrained by period receipts. Retrospective credits and “sounds like” claims are secondary.
