# Q24 — Was Banjo-Kazooie a free pass for a new, lower industry?

**Question:** Was the move from SNES *Donkey Kong Country* to *Banjo-Kazooie* and *Donkey Kong 64* just 3D progress — or the start of a cheaper industry aimed at kids who did not know the craft years?

## Answer

**Industry position:** *Banjo-Kazooie* and *Donkey Kong 64* were critically acclaimed N64 hits. 3D open levels replaced 2D side-scrollers. Collecting is the genre. *Banjo* is a classic. Nostalgia for pre-rendered *Country* is not a design indictment.

**This site:** *Banjo* got a **free pass because it was not a sequel**. There was no visible violation of taking an amazing pre-rendered 2D *Donkey Kong Country* and shipping a shoddy 3D continuation under the same legacy noun. The name itself gives the product away. *Banjo-Kazooie* is not a serious title with tradition, history, or legacy. It is a **level-one product for a brand-new generation that does not know any better**.

**Author read of the two titles.** *Donkey Kong* had already branched into a small universe, like Mario — a thing Rare itself could no longer build or maintain at the old craft level. *Banjo*, by contrast, is extremely manageable: no high expectations, a fresh start, a soft brand. If Rare had never shipped *DK64*, one could still call *Banjo* “some project.” They did ship *DK64*. They reset a legacy IP for a new young audience that did not know *Country*, and they opened it with the notorious **DK Rap**. That proves the agenda. *Banjo* is the same idea without the franchise debt: not a high-quality challenge to the industry, but part of a **new industry** envisioned for the Microsoft / Sony era — one that would work very differently going forward.

**Primary argument for the 21st-century quality plummet.** What had been an inspirational, educational craft enterprise reduced itself to a **Barney & Friends–style gaming industry**: soft, collectible, low-expectation products for cohorts who never saw the NES/SNES peak. Internal Nintendo products kept being sabotaged on that same calendar (Cube look lock, Wii motion locks, remake droughts). *Banjo* is the soft launch of that weather. *DK64* is the same weather forced onto a name that still carried *Country*’s reputation.

**What critics actually said (evidence, not the whole thesis).** Launch scores for *Banjo* were strong — that is the free pass in the press. The brutal language lands on *DK64*: *Next Generation*’s **“interactive egg hunt”**; *GameSpot*’s chores split; later *EGM* that *Mario 64* breathed life into 3D platforming and ***DK64 sucked it all out***; Jonas Kaerlev blaming *DK64* for killing collection platformers; *GameSpot* 2023 calling *DK64* a **game-long exercise in busywork**; Stamper’s **“more things to collect”** brief. Those citations prove the product direction was noticed. This site’s claim is stronger: the direction was the **new industry**, and *Banjo*’s praise is how a soft brand escapes the sequel test *DK64* failed in public.
