# Before Barney, Teletubbies, and Pokémon

Shoddy video game remakes exist so nobody has to ask who still owns the leftover original and often unrealized vision

![Living-room NES years on one side of a torn Nintendo mark; Barney, Teletubbies, and Pokémon on the other](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/before-barney-and-pokemon.png)

Nintendo split in half in our lifetime. The half that knew how to make a game is gone.

If you were born in the 1990s, or late enough in the 1980s that purple dinosaurs and pocket monsters were already the water you swam in, you did not miss a few old cartridges. You missed the real Nintendo. You missed a few years when a living room machine could feel like the best object a factory knew how to make, and then you spent the rest of your life being sold the wreck of that company under the same name. People my age still call your cohort the **Barney generation**, the **Teletubbies generation**, and the **Pokémon generation**, because those were some of the first major things that reached you. That is not a comment on your taste. It is a way of naming when you arrived, and the trouble is that arrival date: you never saw the Nintendo years as the previous generation did.

I grew up in the years before those shows. The Nintendo that arrived in our houses was still a craft shop that could be terrible at business and still ship something whole. The Nintendo *you* met is a brand office that can be excellent at business and still ship something hollow. The split happened in public. It was painful to watch. It was not organic. The piece we loved is almost entirely out of reach. The horizon is locked into new employees, new visions, and new gamers. If this pattern continues, the brand will mint the next replacement the same way.

> **TL;DR:** Kids who arrived after Barney, Teletubbies, and Pokémon never saw the Nintendo years as the previous generation did. Remakes do not reset those clocks the way Disney’s mouse campaign moved a cliff. They exist because sitting silent would force a question the companies cannot afford: may a holder lock a world forever and ignore everyone still inspired by the original. That is already a political attack on ordinary people. The leftover fight between generations is only whether that outcome is objectively good and includes everyone, not just newer demographics.

## The years you did not get

Nintendo’s living-room machine in Japan was the Famicom. In America it was the NES. That was the revolution: cheap enough to be in the house, sharp enough that a kid treated it as the real world after school. The Super NES after it still belonged to that shop. People in the rooms that drew the sprites and wrote the tunes put love into the product. The love is not a metaphor. The care is audible in the tunes and visible in how a walk cycle lands. A player can still interact with it.

Then the compact disc arrived as the next place games were supposed to live, and the partnership around that disc blew up. Sony had been building a CD add-on with Nintendo. Nintendo flipped the announcement toward Philips. The PlayStation was born out of that rupture, and Square took *Final Fantasy VII* to Sony’s machine. The full calendar of that fight is already written in the [Nintendo–Sony breakup](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/nintendo-sony-breakup.md) article. Michael Crichton, in the same years, put Sony and Philips in a novel about a product captain being deleted by office politics. That warning is the subject of [Crichton's Prophecy](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/crichtons-prophecy). After that rupture, the company that had made the revolution started making decisions that looked like self-sabotage if anyone still thought the goal was the best quality game - for *everybody* including older fans.

![Crichton's Prophecy — DigiCom office politics, Sony and Philips in the same years as the Nintendo rupture](https://paradigmthreat.net/science/computing/crichtons-prophecy.png)

The Nintendo 64 was sold as the future. For the 2D craft that had just peaked on the Super NES, it was a downgrade. *Donkey Kong Country* (DKC) had been pre-rendered pictures on a cartridge. That look did not transfer. *DK 64* was the 3D substitute. The name continued. The TLC and traditions **did not**. Handhelds got the leftover 2D. Home consoles got the new religion. That is a shop being told to abandon the skill it had just perfected rather than “graphics improved.”

Credit followed the same cut. The people who did the work disappeared from the public story. Supervisors and familiar Western names absorbed the nouns. [Minako Hamano and the Whitewash](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/minako-hamano-and-the-whitewash.md) is one track a reader can open twice and watch the composer line hop. The sabotage sat at the highest level. The people it punished first were the lower-level employees who had put the hours in. The people it punished next were *all of us* (who paid the $$$ for all these products). We got the hatred between camps of fans, the prices, the shoddy reissues, and a product that no longer belonged to the hands that loved it.

## Barney, Teletubbies, Pokémon

*Barney & Friends* reached American public television in 1992. *Teletubbies* said Eh-Oh on BBC Two on 31 March 1997. The American cartoon and Game Boy games under the contracted name *Pokémon* arrived in September 1998, riding a Japan-first run told next. Those dates sit on top of the Nintendo rupture. A child born in 1992 was four when the Teletubbies arrived and six when Pikachu arrived on American TV. That child did not have a Super NES peak in memory. That child had a purple dinosaur, four brightly colored creatures with televisions in their stomachs, and a collecting loop. Those were not side shows. They were the first major software of a life.

In the mid-1990s, American audiences who had already lived through the Nintendo years became nervous. A kids franchise that most people still treated as a toy — the kind of worry that got someone called paranoid — was about to be on television news as a political problem. The nervousness did not start from a Western marketing meeting. It started from a full run that had already happened in Japan, then was brought to America.

Kids who had already lived through the Nintendo years met Barney and Pokémon with a kind of wisdom. That wisdom does not make anyone better at paying rent, but it did help those kids navigate the maze that grew out of the divisive decisions - Nintendo’s above the rest - that split communities and nose-dived game quality. They could tell when a new thing was a product built to occupy a child, and when a new thing was a world someone had actually finished. They could tell when a company was talking to them as customers it intended to keep, and when it was ignoring their desires and instead only talking to a replacement customer it intended to mint.

The maze is the later industry: sequels that hate their own history, remakes that insult the word 'pixel', online stores that rent a game the buyer already paid for, fan wars about which official version is “the one,” and a press that treats each of those as a launch-week event instead of a loss of gamer agency. If you arrived after Barney, it may appear as if this maze (that existed since they were born) was simply the world **that had always existed**. Churches like to claim the same ancient authority that can't be backed up by actual paperwork. Did the industry view video games as a new church? If not, what explains all the brand evangelism? 

But this is not only about video games. Movies walked the same slope. The late 1990s still had pictures that treated an audience as adults. The 2000s trained that audience to accept a reboot, a joke that undercuts the scene, and a political sermon bolted onto a franchise that used to carry a simpler charge. Two later essays on that Hollywood turn are [Not Like This](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/not-like-this) and [Everything Burns](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/everything-burns). The point here is the rhyme. The living room and the multiplex went hollow on the same calendar.

## Pocket Monsters, then Pokémon

*Pocket Monsters Red* and *Green* went on sale in Japan in 1996 for the Game Boy. Nintendo did not treat the pair as a guaranteed hit. Sales were slow at first. Word of mouth, and the hunt for the hidden creature Mew, turned the games into a sleeper. A Japanese trading-card game followed later in 1996. The animated series began in 1997. Through all of that, the public name in Japan stayed Pocket Monsters.

Once that run was already a phenomenon in Japan, Nintendo prepared a North American launch. In September 1998 the Western games and the cartoon arrived under the contracted name *Pokémon*. Nintendo did not bring over the full English words Pocket Monsters. Those words sat too close to an existing American toy line, *Monster in My Pocket*. In [*Morrison Entertainment Group Inc. v. Nintendo of America, Inc.*, 56 F. App’x 782 (9th Cir. 2003)](https://labusinessjournal.com/news/toys-toy-firm-will-appeal-pocket-monster-suit/), the court recorded that *Pokémon* is the shortened form of Japanese *Pocket Monster*, and that *Pocket Monster* is close in meaning to *Monster in My Pocket*, then still found the written marks different enough to grant Nintendo summary judgment on reverse confusion. Parties, the Po/Ma letter count, and the kids’ logo survey sit in the [intellectual-property enclosure notes](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/investigations/ip-public-domain-enclosure-nintendo-disney-investigation.md).

The suit was filed in March 2000. The Ninth Circuit landed in 2003. By then American *Pokémon* was already water — games, cards, cartoon, a billion-dollar year. This was mop-up for a senior US mark squeezed by a renamed hit, not a live resistance war that could still stop the import. Morrison’s counsel said everybody thought they were a rip-off of Pokémon. [Sheff’s 1993 Japan-trade living-room panic](https://www.baltimoresun.com/1993/05/18/how-super-mario-conquered-america/) was leftover atmosphere someone *could* still hear under a Kyoto parent and a California toy shop. It is not the calendar’s main story. The leftover language fight is what the panel ran.

American kids therefore met that renamed Western launch as news: a collecting loop the earlier Nintendo years had not prepared them for. The people who had been nervous in the mid-1990s were watching that import, not a surprise born in California.

## Disappointment became the industry standard

There is no need for a list of every release. The weather is enough. From the late 1990s forward, disappointment stopped being a bad launch and became the normal temperature. A few expensive exceptions were treated as proof the era was healthy. They were not. They were the last heat in a cooling engine. Older players complained in the same voice, year after year: this is not as good as what we had, this port added waiting that the cartridge never had, this remake is a store page wearing the old name, this sequel is a lecture. The complaints were consistent because the product was consistent. The good news ran out. The social form of that standard is peer enforcement: kids, and the room that grew up under it, attack each other for complaining about quality. Hobby policing replaces the remake.

Square’s *Final Fantasy* reissues are the long version of that weather. The PlayStation copies of the Super NES games [added load times](https://archive.rpgamer.com/games/ff/ffa/reviews/ffastrev2.html) the cartridge did not have. Later phone copies redrew the faces. The [Pixel Remaster](https://press.na.square-enix.com/FINAL-FANTASY-PIXEL-REMASTER-SERIES-CELEBRATES-TWO-MILLION-SHIPMENTS-A) sold the word pixel on sprites that are not even the *original pixels* — leftover blocks kept as nostalgic costume on an upgrade that is already less pixelized than the cartridge. People still bought them. The older generation had treated pixels as a hardware stage that would keep being upgraded; what they got was parked pixel games, incomplete HD, and a full price ($74.82 USD) for closed original code upgraded on a proprietary engine the public never receives unless it leaks. That is nostalgic virtue signaling. The TLC contrast is Team Ninja’s successive 3D *Ninja Gaiden* work on Xbox and PlayStation. Even that shop did not break the industry standard: in June 2008, as *Ninja Gaiden II* shipped, [Tomonobu Itagaki resigned and sued Tecmo](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2008/06/itagaki-quits-tecmo-sues/); without him, *Ninja Gaiden 3* sucked. Penny Arcade is one of the agents that got the room used to this weather.

*Duke Nukem Forever* is another specimen of the product the room was trained to absorb. It sat in development for so long that the title became a joke, then shipped in 2011. *Duke Nukem 3D* in 1996 already had suggestive, softcore-adjacent bits — interactive strip clubs, secret porn-shop areas — so pixelized they read as novelty, not adult entertainment. Forever took the other road: crude-realism writing and outdated mechanics when the novelty path was already clear. The urinal opening with twin cheerleaders is one specimen of that writing, not the whole charge. Reviews were overwhelmingly negative on both counts. The Redner Group, the US PR firm on the game, then [tweeted that outlets whose “venom” had gone too far would be reviewed for who got future review copies](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-13795782). The tweets were deleted. 2K dropped the firm. Saying the blacklist threat out loud made a public ethics scandal in games journalism.

## Penny Arcade

Penny Arcade started as two people drawing a gamer viewpoint. They made their own little products. They looked like they had no industry leash. That is why they could be trusted. Around **2000–2001** the affiliate network [eFront](https://www.penny-arcade.com/news/post/2005/05/10/this-is-long-and-boring) took what they thought was a sponsorship and advertising deal, took control of the page’s ads, stopped paying, and — by Gabe’s later account — had actually signed away the rights to the strip. Holkins and Krahulik had quit their day jobs on a few months of checks. When the money died they nearly closed the comic; reader donations kept it alive. Later the brand became PAX, merch, and a charity stack. The trust they sold is still the point. A voice that sounds like “one of us” can teach a room to endure bad news for years, as long as the room can laugh about it with that voice. That is controlled opposition as a function: make monopoly weather feel like a joke the room shares, not a fight with a lever in it.

On 3 June 2009 they posted [“All Is Forgiven.”](https://www.penny-arcade.com/comic/2009/06/03/all-is-forgiven) The implication in this strip was that it sat at the tail end of a huge run, maybe years long, of terrible news from the games industry. The Wii Vitality Sensor was seeming like the last straw. But then one last, almost minor mention: a Metroid game from Team Ninja, the people who had made 3D *Ninja Gaiden* playable. Dysphoria and rage convert into shiny euphoria. Metroid had been popular and under-treated. A new game from that shop, with that action team, sounded like Nintendo was finally going to try: 2D, 3D, 2.5D, everything at once. The strip’s job that week was to reset the drought. The room had suffered, but now **all is forgiven** because Nintendo is back! 

![Penny Arcade, All Is Forgiven, 3 June 2009 — Vitality Sensor rage, then a Metroid game from Team Ninja](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/penny-arcade-all-is-forgiven.jpg)

*Metroid: Other M* shipped in 2010 as third-person 3D and 2.5D locked to the Wii Remote, with first-person missile aim and a plot that made Samus wait for Adam’s authorization to use powers she already had. The reviews were extremely bad. [Game Informer scored it 6.25](https://gameinformer.com/games/metroid_other_m/b/wii/archive/2010/08/27/nintendo-s-team-ninja-team-up-robs-metroid-of-its-atmosphere-and-character), called Samus a “whiny, talkative child,” and named it Nintendo’s most disappointing release in quite some time. The release that was supposed to restore faith did the opposite, but it still accomplished the main agenda - cowing the audience and buying Nintendo another 10 years or so before they would have to... worry about making another metroid game for the original fans... 

The next 2D Metroid on a living-room machine after *Super Metroid* in 1994 was *Metroid Dread* in 2021. Fusion, Zero Mission, and *Samus Returns* stayed on handhelds. Dread finally tried a traditional side-view again, and then put in a new timing mode Nintendo itself described as nearly impossible: the [E.M.M.I. melee counter](https://www.nintendo.com/en-za/News/2021/June/Metroid-Dread-Report-Vol-2-Researching-the-E-M-M-I-1997672.html). That new gimmick pushed away people who came for a new *Super Metroid* the way shake-to-roll pushed away people who came for a new *DKC*. The story rewrite is the other half. In the old games Samus was a hunter in a suit, raised by an ancient bird people, fighting parasites called Metroids. Dread splits those bird people into a peaceful tribe and a warrior tribe. The warrior chief slaughters the peaceful ones, and his blood is revealed to be in Samus. She uncharacteristically rips enemies bodies apart with her hands, and finishes the game as one of the parasites she used to hunt as the big reveal. Dread was widely liked as a 2D Metroid, but more damage was done to the original lore and vision. Samus's personality, once undefined, silent and respectable, now twists and changes with every release into something different - Merciless, tainted, ultimately unsatisfying.

## The Cube, the Wii, the Switch

The vacuum after *DKC 3* in 1996 lasted until 2010. Fans got used to the silence. Stakeholders did not. When *Returns* finally appeared, it was a shaped answer, not an open door. The pattern has a name, [vacuum inverse](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/VACUUM_INVERSE.md): the people who feel the silence first are not the public. The public has already adapted. The return is timed so the question “why did they stop?” never becomes a mandate to continue forever.

The GameCube was the window where the old craft could have come home. Retro Studios, working with Nintendo people who still knew the earlier rooms, shipped *Metroid Prime*. That game belongs on any honest 21st-century list.

The Cube pad already had two analog sticks. DualShock and Xbox already used two equal sticks for look. Nintendo made the second stick a small yellow nub and did not map look to it. That is sabotage — programming, not a missing part. The official line (adventure, lock-on, C-stick for beams) is the cover. *TimeSplitters* had to limp on that nub; Prime used lock-on. Stores already put Prime in the same FPS class as Halo and *Half-Life 2*; fans put Samus, Master Chief, and Gordon Freeman in one armored set. I remember a shop ad with the three together. *Prime 2* even poked Halo 2’s [*I Love Bees*](https://spong.com/article/7855/Nintendo-opens-up-another-32-Halo-2-poking-promotional-websites) campaign. Halo-style look on Prime 1 was a few bits of code.

*DKC Returns* should have arrived in that same hardware generation on a GameCube controller. It did not. Nintendo held it for the Wii, forced motion (and the same lock on *Prime 2* / *Prime 3*), and withheld a classic-controller path. Shake-to-roll was sold as enhancement. In February 2023 the Switch sold *Metroid Prime Remastered*; in January 2025, *DKC Returns HD*. Both play with ordinary buttons; Prime finally uses two sticks for look. Same games. That is the admission that 2002 was a code choice. Wii *Returns* had shipped in November 2010; the HD copy arrived fourteen years later as a late store product, not a restoration of the GameCube window.

The Wii U was a good product. It finally had HDMI. The second screen allowed a kind of play that showed up in *Star Fox Zero*. I was impressed by *Star Fox Zero*. It belongs with the small list. A player has to own a Wii U to play it. It was never ported to the Switch. It will not be. Nintendo abandoned that direction and went somewhere more mundane, aimed at new users, not at the people who had been waiting since the SNES. [Platinum talked about wanting the port](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/platinum-wants-to-bring-star-fox-zero-to-switch/). Nintendo did not give it to them.

That is the split, in hardware in the hand. One half still knew how to make *Prime*, *Returns*, *Tropical Freeze*, and *Star Fox Zero*. The other half knew how to make a new audience and a new control scheme and a new silence after the launch window. We watched them be torn into those two pieces. The first piece is gone.

## Speech through toys

Nintendo’s 1980s games were not Mickey. In 1998 Disney was staring at a 1928 mouse that was about to become public speech. The company [led the push that bought twenty more years](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1998-oct-18-mn-33635-story.html) for works already made. Mario in 1985 was nowhere near that cliff. Square’s 1987 *Final Fantasy* was nowhere near it. Those clocks were not arbitrarily shoved backward as a named rescue of those titles. The dates, the 1998 hike, and the preservation rule that only opens when a game is no longer reasonably for sale are in the [intellectual-property enclosure notes](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/investigations/ip-public-domain-enclosure-nintendo-disney-investigation.md).

The situation we're stuck in is that the companies cannot stop shipping remakes, ports, and rental catalogs, even when the copies are substandard and even when the copies split the audience into people who defend the new official thing and people who still want the original. That quality is not an accident they would rather avoid. It is the proof they must keep publishing something that wears the name to maintain control over the IP. On the legal end they are 100% covered, but if they sat quiet, if the old game were simply locked in a vault and not for sale, the ethical question would be in the open: is a holder allowed to sit on a world forever and never let anyone else try a vision that was already in the first version?

That question is the real one. Someone was inspired by the cartridge and is uninspired by the Pixel Remaster. The company can ignore that person. It can ignore a stack of letters the size of the campaigns people ran when The Weather Channel dropped the jazz that had been the house sound of Local on the 8s. In 2013 the channel replaced that music with production tracks made for the brand. Viewers wrote. A [White House petition asked them to resume the jazz](https://petitions.obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/petition/urge-weather-channel-stop-giving-names-winter-storms-because-it-trivializes-them-resume/index.html). The holder of the house sound still got to keep the new package. A game company gets the same courtesy at a higher stake. The original world stays locked. The official substitute stays the only legal storefront. Critics are treated as people who will never be happy.

Childhood faces are a speech channel. Disney could put [Donald Duck in a Nazi factory](https://www.waltdisney.org/blog/disney-wwii-propaganda-pt1) and walk a German boy through [Hitler Youth schooling](https://d23.com/a-to-z/education-for-death-film/) in 1943, because Disney held the face. In 2026, [viral videos that looked like LEGO minifigs](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cjd8jrd1vnyo) carried a war message about Iran and the United States. Those clips were not a LEGO Group film. They were lookalikes. They still worked because children already know the bricks. The holder wants that power. The holder does not want the public to have it. Santa Claus and Abraham Lincoln are the other kind of face: anyone may use them, and taste is the only brake. Mario is not that kind of face.

Crossovers pile the locked faces into one product. *Kingdom Hearts* and *Smash Bros.* are demonstrations that the faces can be gathered. They also multiply the companies that must say yes before anyone else may use the picture people actually remember. Fan anger does not shorten a copyright. It does make the legal stack messier. The public never quite receives the icon.

## Take a stand or accept the cage

Looked at one piece at a time, this reading sounds like a person raining on a parade. One shoddy remake is a store page. One missing port is a business choice. One motion-control lock is a gimmick. One generation label is an old fan being rude. Put those pieces in a row and the charge looks small. Isolated complaints do not make the case. They make the complainant. A social-media thread can do the same job: name Pixel Remaster weather under someone’s personal craft post, and the room answers with hobby policing and insults instead of remake quality. The camps fight. The industry stays off the page. The anonymized specimen sits in the [intellectual-property enclosure notes](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/investigations/ip-public-domain-enclosure-nintendo-disney-investigation.md).

The complete case is this. A corporation may sit on a property that entered the culture as a finished world, ship a lesser official copy, and refuse every other vision that was already latent in the first one. That is ethical only if exclusive speech through childhood icons is already accepted as the normal government of culture. It is a political attack by corporations on ordinary people. The point of the attack is that those people never compete. Information stays inside the brand. Agency stays inside the license. That outcome does not have to be imagined. It is the present. Games, movies, and the leftover public domain all already run that way.

The leftover divider is not a puzzle about whether the pattern can lead there. It already did. The leftover divider is only whether that outcome is good. One side wants more of the corporation’s vision: official remakes, official rental catalogs, official crossovers, and silence from anyone still attached to the first version. The other side is a political stand against that enclosure: the old work should, after a limited time, become material other people may use, including people who loved the original and hated the substitute. There is no third position that still tells the truth about the remakes. Anyone who has followed this far is already on one of those two sides.

The generations have to come together. More division is the industry’s win. Stop falling for the tricks that mint replacement customers and set camps on each other. Talk across the camps. Accept the plurality of opinions that already exist. Restore some of that ancient pattern of respecting elders for their wisdom — in video games, books, movies, and politics. As long as we stay divided, none of us will ever be happy. And more to the point, it is only a matter of time before the industry abandons you.

## Framing and limits

This reading assumes [prisca sapientia](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md): older work can be better than later institutional product, and a later official copy is not automatically an upgrade. The generation labels name first influences and missing years, not that every child who watched Teletubbies is empty.

**Documented** calendars and products (hardware dates, Morrison mop-up, eFront deal, *All Is Forgiven*, *Other M* / GI 6.25, Dread E.M.M.I. language, Forever / Redner, Weather Channel jazz drop, wartime cartoons and 2026 brick-look videos as media facts) sit in the body and the [enclosure](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/investigations/ip-public-domain-enclosure-nintendo-disney-investigation.md) / [vassal](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/stolen_credit/investigations/nintendo-vassal-hardware-self-sabotage-investigation.md) notes. This page does not adopt the factual claims those war videos make about strikes or casualties.

**Author:** Switch-as-sabotage motive; Cube *Prime* look withheld as programming (remaster as admission); shop-ad memory of Samus / Chief / Freeman; Pixel Remaster as nostalgia costume; PA controlled opposition as **function** (eFront → messaging → charity arc); disappointment as industry standard plus peer policing; Forever as wrong path vs 1996 novelty; generations-together closer. Not a criminal or staffing indictment. Forever is not a census of industry sex content or a specimen for content involving children. Facebook camp-defense thread: names withheld; specimen only. Peer policing is not a counted “all kids” poll. Body **you** addresses the Barney / Teletubbies / Pokémon cohort; the final **you** is that same address on abandonment. Child’s Play and MSF are parallel charity-theater geometry, not the same money hops.

The stacked pattern is the case. Isolated launch-week complaints are not.
