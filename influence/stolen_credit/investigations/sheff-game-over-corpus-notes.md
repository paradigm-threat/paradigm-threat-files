## Factual references (by topic)

### Org / R&D

- Yamauchi put chiefs under him over **R&D 1, 2, and 3**; within groups, teams were **pitted against each other** (hardware and software).
- **R&D 1** (Yokoi): games including **Metroid**; later **Game Boy** (~30 engineers on that team, per Sheff).
- **Metroid:** Samus revealed as a woman after defeating Mother Brain; book uses Mother Brain as boss name and as nickname for Yamauchi’s office realm.
- **R&D 2** (Uemura): Famicom/NES hardware; peripherals including Communications Adapter / network.
- **R&D 3** (Takeda): games such as *Star Tropics*; **MMC / mapper chips** that extended Famicom capability beyond original NROM (enabling more complex titles including Zelda / Metroid / Kid Icarus class games in Sheff’s account).

### Craft names stated

- Miyamoto worked with in-house composer **Koji Kondo** on Super Mario games; Kondo recordings sold commercially; symphony / cover uses mentioned.
- No Metroid / R&D1 **composer** staff named.

### Business / license mechanics (stated)

- Early Famicom licensees paid Nintendo a royalty of **about 20 percent** per cartridge (example: Konami).
- NES used a built-in **lock-out chip**; Yamauchi stated it was for quality/censorship and anti-counterfeiting, and that it protected Nintendo’s profit from every game sold (Sheff cites ~700 yen / ~$5 per game estimate in that passage).
- License structure limited titles / controlled manufacturing and distribution (developer complaints and antitrust material appear in ch.11–12 as reported speech and proceedings — treat as *reported*, not as Sheff’s verdict).

### Scale figures Sheff states (1991–92 era)

- *Super Mario Bros. 3* US revenues “topped $500 million”; US+JP unit sales cited as 7M + 4M.
- By **1991**, Nintendo “supplanted Toyota as Japan’s most successful company” on indices Sheff lists (growth, profitability, market penetration, stock).
- **1991** profits: Nintendo ~**$1.5M per employee** (Japan employee base ~850 in that comparison); Sony (~50,000 employees) earned **$400M less** than Nintendo that year (Sheff’s comparison).
- **1992** licensee cartridge sales: **170 million** carts worldwide @ average **~$40** → ~**$7B** (Sheff’s aggregate).
- Super NES US launch push: **$25M** ad campaign; press claim of sales rate “twelve units every retail minute.”

### US legal / NOA (stated events)

- **Universal** lawsuit over *Donkey Kong*; Nintendo awarded **$1.8M** (Sheff).
- NOA principals repeatedly: **Minoru Arakawa**, **Howard Lincoln**.

### Multimedia / Sony / Philips (ch.15 — dateable claims)

| Claim in book | Detail |
|---------------|--------|
| Philips / Sony multimedia partnership | Fractures; Philips proceeds with **CD-I** alone |
| Philips **CD-I** consumer release | **October 1991**; price cited **$1,000** then later **$799**; Magnavox label mentioned elsewhere in book |
| Sony + Nintendo **Play Station** | Announced **summer / June 1991 CES** (Chicago) |
| Hardware shape (announcement) | Port compatible with Super Nintendo + **CD-ROM**; Super Discs up to **~680 MB** |
| Licensing term (Sony’s announcement, as quoted) | Sony as **“sole worldwide licenser of the Super Disc format”** |
| Deal substance (as Sheff states) | Nintendo had granted Sony rights to **control and profit from CD-based software** on that machine |
| Deal signing year (as Sheff states) | Contract roots **~1988** |
| Parallel | Sony also shipped computer **CD-ROM**; planned cheaper game box vs CD-I |

**Not in this book (factual gap):** post-CES Philips SNES-CD cancel detail (HANDS/SCCP), CD-i *Hotel Mario* / Zelda games, Square→PlayStation *Final Fantasy VII* (1996).
