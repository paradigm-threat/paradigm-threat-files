## 3. British loyalty and the passive revolution

**Documented:** Gandhi spent **1893–1914** in South Africa as a British subject. During the **Boer War** (Britain vs the Dutch-settler republics, land and gold) he organized an **Indian ambulance corps** to carry and treat wounded **British** soldiers — not to fight the empire ([Not Even Past](https://notevenpast.org/gandhi-the-imperialist/)). He used derogatory language toward Black Africans (“Kaffirs,” “savages”) and urged councils to separate Indians from them ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-india-34265882); [EPW](https://www.epw.in/journal/2021/32/perspectives/gandhi-and-re-enactment-racism.html)).

**Documented as public doctrine:** nonviolence (*ahimsa* / satyagraha) as the method of the independence movement.

**Author:** India was a subcontinent that might have broken from Britain by **force**. A doctrine of not fighting takes the fight out of an uprising. That is how a movement gets **routed**. Gandhi is the instrument. The same ploy shows up in other British regions. This is not a unique spiritual accident.

Partition and Godse’s accusation that Gandhi favored Muslim demands remain **contested** in the public record ([Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/articles/south-asia/2017-10-19/gandhis-role-partition-india)). They are **not** this section’s lock.

## 4. Brahmacharya — Documented legend vs Author cover

### 4.1 What is in print (Documented)

After Kasturba Gandhi died in **1944**, the sold story is that Gandhi began sharing his bed, naked, with young women as a test of **brahmacharya** (celibacy / sense-control). Quality-press and scholarly accounts name **Sushila Nayar** (physician) and grand-nieces **Abha** and **Manu**, then in their **late teens**, about sixty years younger ([Guardian — 2018](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2018/oct/01/gandhi-celibacy-test-naked-women); Lal, “Nakedness, non-violence…”). Manu Gandhi (born **1927**) joined him in **1943** at **fifteen**; the concentrated “experiment” window in the diary literature is **December 1946–February 1947** (Noakhali), when she was about **nineteen** ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/opinion/gandhis-other-great-trial-and-manu-gandhi-101732976460461.html); [Indian Express — Manu diary](https://indianexpress.com/article/lifestyle/books/beloved-child-of-mother-bapu-6037432/)). Gandhi’s own framing: the women were a **temptation**; if he was not aroused, the vow held. Associates objected; some resigned. Popular hagiography (including Attenborough’s film) omitted the episode for decades. Manu’s diaries later reached the National Archives of India and print (Suhrud ed., OUP).

**That is the public-sphere rumor rule:** Wikipedia / quality press / published diary **count**. The legend is **not** a forum invention.

**Documented ages in that literature are late teens / young women, not a named child-docket.** Author’s kill-shot line still uses **“little girls.”** Keep both labels. Do not collapse them. Do not write a graphic.
