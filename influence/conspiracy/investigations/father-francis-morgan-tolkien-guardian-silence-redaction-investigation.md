# Investigation: Father Francis Xavier Morgan — Guardian Silence, Stoic Duty, and the Motive Behind Tolkien’s Fiction-as-History

> **TL;DR:** **Father Francis Xavier Morgan** (Francisco Javier Morgan Osborne, 1857–1935), Birmingham Oratory priest and **legal guardian** of J. R. R. Tolkien, is **documented** as the man who **forbade** Tolkien to see or write to **Edith Bratt** until Tolkien’s **21st birthday** (3 January 1913). Tolkien later called him a **second father** and said he chose **obedience** over the love affair. **Author thesis:** that forced silence taught **emotional suppression as a virtue**, which returns in **Aragorn, Elrond, and Denethor** as **duty-as-cage**, and that Morgan — **possibly kin to J. P. Morgan** — was the **real motive** behind **redacting fantastic world history into fiction** presented as Tolkien’s own invention. **Documented:** Oratory formation, sherry-fortune subsidy, Newman’s school, and a **literary uncle-line** (great-aunt **Fernán Caballero**, historical novels under a male mask). **Not documented:** banking-Morgan kinship, a bloodline to the **Babington Plot** conspirator **Thomas Morgan**, or Francis as hidden author of the legendarium (he **died in 1935**, before *The Hobbit*). The Babington Morgan and the sherry Morgans share a **claimed Monmouthshire / Tredegar** neighborhood and a **letter-control** rhyme; they are **not** shown to be one family. The silence is **about three years of total ban** (c. late 1909–January 1913), not a clean five-year blackout — the **five-year** figure matches the **whole courtship** from meeting (1908) to reunion more than the formal prohibition.

**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** Open — biography and letter quotes logged; JP Morgan genealogy, Babington-plot descent, and “Morgan authored the redaction” remain unproven.

**Sibling dossier:** [Tolkien’s fiction as redacted fact](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/tolkien/INVESTIGATION-tolkien-redacted-fact.md).

## Author’s originating thesis

Recorded from the request that opened this file (2026-09-28):

> By forcing a five-year total silence, the establishment (represented by Father Francis) taught Tolkien that intense emotional suppression was a virtue. In his writing, this manifests as a heavy, almost suffocating stoicism. Characters like Aragorn, Elrond, and Denethor frequently repress their emotions, view duty as an absolute cage, and treat happiness as something that must be sacrificed for the "greater good" or institutional survival. It seems to me Morgan (probably related to JP Morgan) was the real influence behind the motive to redact fantastic world history into an elaborate fiction claimed to be originated by his subject — Tolkien.

**What that means for this dossier:** three separable claims.

| Claim | Class |
|-------|--------|
| The ban installed **obedience and delayed desire** as Tolkien’s moral law, and that law shows up as **stoic duty** in the books | Biographical reading — **plausible (L3)** once the letters are on the table |
| **Five years** of **total** silence | **Overstates** the formal ban; see §4 |
| Morgan is **probably** a **J. P. Morgan**, and was the **real authorial motive** for publishing redacted history **as Tolkien’s fiction** | **L4** until a genealogy and a composition record say so. The **fair** version is: Morgan formed the **conscience** of the man who later chose that frame |

## Who Francis Morgan was

**Francisco Javier Morgan Osborne**, 18 January 1857, El Puerto de Santa María, Cádiz — 11 June 1935, Birmingham Oratory ([Wikipedia — Francis Xavier Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Xavier_Morgan)).

| Fact | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| Father: **Francis Morgan**, Welsh Protestant, sherry exporter in Andalusia. Mother: **María Manuela Osborne**, Catholic, of **Bodegas Osborne** | Money and culture are **wine dynasty**, not Wall Street. Disparity-of-cult marriage is the family wound he later repeats on Tolkien and Edith |
| Boyhood at the **Birmingham Oratory** school; teachers included **John Henry Newman**; Louvain; ordained **1883** | Formation is **English Catholic intellectual establishment** (Newman, Duke of Norfolk’s house), not a bank |
| Great-aunt **Cecilia Böhl de Faber**, who published historical novels as **Fernán Caballero** and was compared to **Walter Scott** | A **documented** habit, one generation back, of **putting history into fiction under another name**. One scholar has compared Gollum’s wind riddle in *The Hobbit* to Caballero’s riddle 187 ([Wikipedia — Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Xavier_Morgan), citing García-Máiquez) |
| Guardian of **Ronald and Hilary** after **Mabel Tolkien**’s death, November 1904; supplemented a thin estate from **his share of the sherry business** | He paid for the education that made the philologist |
| Tolkien’s words (letter to Michael, 1965): “an upper-class Welsh-Spaniard Tory… I first learned charity and forgiveness from him” | Tolkien’s own verdict is **gratitude**, not accusation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Xavier_Morgan), quoting the letter) |
| 1972, to Michael, after recalling Francis’s death: he told **C. S. Lewis** he felt “like a lost survivor into a new alien world after the real world has passed away” | Francis is the **lost real world**, not a villain in Tolkien’s private speech |
| Left **£1,000** to each Tolkien brother (order of **£60,000** in mid-2020s money, per the Wikipedia conversion) | Material patronage continued to the grave |
| First son baptized **John Francis Reuel Tolkien** (1917), Francis as godfather-namesake | Public honor, not a hidden break |

**Establishment, scoped:** “The establishment” in the author’s sentence is fair as **Oratory authority** — a priest with money, school placement, and a legal guardianship could **stop a courtship**. It is not yet fair as **J. P. Morgan & Co.**

## The silence — dates, not the round number

Documented sequence ([Wikipedia — Francis Xavier Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Xavier_Morgan); Carpenter’s biography is the underlying narrative):

1. **1908.** Tolkien, 16, and **Edith Bratt**, 19, meet at **Mrs Faulkner’s** boarding house beside the Oratory.
2. Father Francis judges the match **“altogether unfortunate”**: she is older and **Protestant**. Tolkien later wrote that the strain nearly caused “a very bad breakdown” and is why he **“muffed [his] exams”** for Oxford.
3. Francis **prohibits meeting, talking, or corresponding** until Tolkien is **21**. Tolkien obeys **“to the letter,”** with one early breach; Francis threatens to **cut short the university career** if it continues.
4. **3 January 1913**, the day Tolkien turns 21, he writes to Edith and they resume. Guardians are unenthusiastic; Francis **eventually blesses** the match. Marriage **22 March 1916**.
5. Tolkien to Michael, **1941**: he had to choose between grieving a guardian “who had been a father to me, more than most fathers, but without any obligation,” and **dropping the love affair until I was 21**. “I don’t regret my decision, though it was particularly hard on my lover. But that was not my fault.”

**Count:**

| Span | Length | What it is |
|------|--------|------------|
| Formal ban (failed exams, **late 1909** → **3 Jan 1913**) | **~3 years 2 months** | The **total silence** the letters describe |
| First meeting (**1908**) → reunion (**Jan 1913**) | **~4½ years** | The whole constrained courtship |
| Author’s **“five-year total silence”** | Round figure | **Directionally right** as a long freeze; **not** the length of the written prohibition |

**What the ban actually taught, in Tolkien’s own grammar:** private happiness yields to the **guardian’s order**; the lover bears the cost; the young man **does not regret** obedience. That is **suppression praised as fidelity**. It does not require a conspiracy to be severe.

## Stoicism in the legendarium

The books do not mention Francis. The **rhyme** is structural.

| Figure | Pattern | Fit with the ban |
|--------|---------|------------------|
| **Aragorn** | Decades as a ranger under another name; **Elrond’s condition** that he must be king of both Gondor and Arnor before he may marry **Arwen**; joy postponed until the institution is restored | Closest match: **love is legal only after the authority’s test is passed** |
| **Elrond** | Grief, foresight, the choice that sunders his house; comfort refused in favor of the long defeat | The **guardian** who sets the condition and then lives with the cost |
| **Denethor** | Duty as absolute; happiness and mercy treated as threats to the city’s survival; despair when the institution is doomed | **Partial fit, and the text condemns him.** He is the **warning**, not the ideal: suppression without charity becomes tyranny. Grouping him with Aragorn flattens Tolkien’s judgment |

**L3 reading this file holds:** the 1941 letter is the moral plot of Aragorn’s wait — *I chose the father over the lover, and I do not regret it, though it was hard on her.* **L3 reading this file does not hold:** that Denethor is an advertisement for that virtue. Denethor is what the same cage looks like when charity (the thing Tolkien said he learned from Francis) has burned out.

Jackson’s films **thicken** the stoicism (Aragorn’s reluctance, filmed Denethor). The **books** already contain the cage. Film exaggeration is a separate lane from the guardian.

## J. P. Morgan kinship — what would have to be true

**J. P. Morgan** (1837–1913) descends from the **American** Morgans: **Miles Morgan**, a Welsh settler of Springfield, Massachusetts, **1636**, through **Junius Spencer Morgan**. **Father Francis’s father** is a **19th-century** Welshman who **stayed in the sherry trade in Cádiz** and married an **Osborne**.

**Morgan** is an ordinary Welsh surname. Shared surname plus “upper-class” is **not** a pedigree.

**What would move this off L4:** a published genealogy tying Francis Morgan of Puerto de Santa María to Junius or Pierpont’s line — parish, will, or Osborne family papers. **Until that exists, “probably related to JP Morgan” is a hypothesis the wine-dynasty record does not support.** The money that reached Tolkien was **sherry**, and the literary mask in the family was **Fernán Caballero**, not a bank.

## Babington Plot and Thomas Morgan — a different Morgan

**Short answer:** a **role rhyme** is real. A **blood connection** is not established. The man in the 1586 plot is not an ancestor on any pedigree this file has found, and the wine house’s own **Thomas Morgans** are **nineteenth-century London shippers**, not the Elizabethan conspirator.

### The plot (L1–L2)

The **Babington Plot** (1586) was a plan to kill **Elizabeth I** and free **Mary, Queen of Scots**. The named principals on the English side were **Anthony Babington** and the priest **John Ballard**. **Francis Walsingham** ran it as a trap. **Gilbert Gifford** carried the letters. **Thomas Phelippes** deciphered them. The channel into Mary’s prison at Chartley was a **beer-barrel bung**. Mary’s reply approving the rescue was the paper that sent her to the block ([Babington Plot — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babington_Plot)).

**Thomas Morgan** (c. 1543 – c. 1606) sat **upstream** of that channel, in Paris and then the **Bastille**:

| Fact | Source |
|------|--------|
| Welsh Catholic; claimed a “right worshipful family of Monmouthshire”; served Shrewsbury while Shrewsbury **jailed Mary**; became her **secretary and letter-carrier** | [Dictionary of Welsh Biography](https://biography.wales/article/s-MORG-THO-1543.html) |
| Tower after the **Ridolfi** business; exile in Paris as secretary to **James Beaton**, Mary’s ambassador | same |
| From the Bastille he gave **Gilbert Gifford** an introduction into Mary’s correspondence. Gifford was already, or immediately became, **Walsingham’s double agent**. Every letter was copied before it arrived | [DNB via Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Morgan,_Thomas_(1543-1606%3F)); [Babington Plot — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babington_Plot) |
| He helped **organize** Babington’s conspiracy and in April 1586 advised Mary to send Babington the approving letter | DNB |
| **Jesuits** later called him the man who **set Gifford on**. The DNB says he does **not** seem to have known Gifford was false. Both readings exist: **dupe** or **door** | DNB; plot narratives |
| **Not** on the Llantarnam or Machen pedigree as printed. The Dictionary of Welsh Biography says he **cannot be fitted** into either tree in Clark’s *Limbus* | [DWB](https://biography.wales/article/s-MORG-THO-1543.html) |

Popular pages still call him “Thomas Morgan of Llantarnam, a branch of the Morgans of Tredegar” ([Wikipedia — Thomas Morgan of Llantarnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Morgan_(of_Llantarnam))). The Welsh biographical dictionary **refuses that fit**. Treat “of Tredegar” as a **label that stuck**, not as a proven seat.

### The sherry house (L2)

Father Francis’s paternal line, as reconstructed by **José Manuel Ferrández Bru** and the Jerez trade notes, is a **London wine firm**, not a Tudor spy:

- **Aaron Morgan** (1742–1818), wine trade in London.
- His son **Thomas**, then **Thomas junior**, running the firm from London.
- **Francis Morgan** (the priest’s father) sent to **El Puerto de Santa María** in the 1840s to build the sherry supply. Protestant. Married **María Manuela Osborne** in 1851.
- Their youngest son is the Oratory priest ([Ferrández Bru — London firm](https://www.josemanuelferrandez.com/vieja/london/london.html); [Jerez-Xeres-Sherry — Bodegas Morgan](http://jerez-xeres-sherry.blogspot.com/2013/06/sherry-shippers-and-port-morgan.html)).

The trade blog says the family was **“originally from Tredegar.”** That is the only geographic overlap with the conspirator’s **claimed** country. It is a **blog sentence**, not a parish chain from 1606 to 1742. The London firm’s **Thomas** is Aaron’s son, born into the wine trade around the turn of the nineteenth century. Same given name. **Wrong century.**

### What a real connection would look like

| Link | Status |
|------|--------|
| Same man | **No.** Died c. 1606 vs born 1857 |
| Father-to-son line, conspirator → Aaron Morgan | **Not found.** Two centuries empty. DWB already cannot seat the conspirator on the Tredegar tree the wine blog invokes |
| Same **trade** as J. P. Morgan | **No.** This does not help the bank hypothesis either |
| Same **job** inside a Catholic letter network | **Rhyme only (L3).** Elizabethan Thomas **carries and opens** Mary’s secret post, and the state **reads it through him**. Father Francis **forbids** Tolkien’s letters to Edith until the institution says the silence may end |

**Working conclusion:** there can be a connection of **pattern** — a Morgan at the gate of Catholic correspondence, useful to the authority that wants those letters governed. There is **not**, on the pedigrees in hand, a connection of **blood**. Anyone who wants the blood link has to produce the missing generations between the Bastille prisoner and Aaron Morgan of the London wine trade, and has to get past the Welsh dictionary’s statement that the prisoner does not sit on the Tredegar or Llantarnam tree.

## The harsh priest is a script — Francis’s record is the other way

**Default for this section:** Father Francis is historically a **benevolent, highly supportive caregiver**. Tolkien’s letters, the money, the baptism, and the later friendship with Edith are the record. The investigation is whether **fiction and suspicion about Catholic priests and Oratorian guardians** still contain a **piece of truth** once that record is granted — or whether the piece of truth is only the **office**, and the **villain** is supplied by the genre.

### What the fiction wants him to be

Dome Karukoski, director of *Tolkien* (2019), said the part was cast and played for **kindness** because that is what the sources describe. He also said the culture expects the opposite. At WonderCon he called Francis “gentle” and “what I think a Catholic priest should be,” against the habit of showing priests as **harsh** ([Voyage Comics](https://voyagecomics.com/2019/04/01/tolkien-director-praises-father-francis-morgan-as-model-priest/)). In *America* he went further: an actor **refused a role** after reading the script because he **wanted Father Francis to beat the boys**. Karukoski’s gloss was that this is a bad time to put a priest on screen, and that people arrive hoping for the blow ([America Magazine, 10 May 2019](https://www.americamagazine.org/arts-culture/2019/05/10/who-was-priest-who-helped-raise-j-r-r-tolkien/)).

The finished film does not give them the beating. It does the next cheapest thing. Colm Meaney’s Francis appears in a handful of scenes, and the one with dramatic weight is the **ban on Edith**. Reviewers who know the letters call that a **quasi-antagonist** cut from a man Tolkien named a son after ([Aleteia](https://aleteia.org/2019/05/19/tolkien-gets-j-r-r-tolkiens-passion-but-loses-sight-of-his-soul/); [K. R. Harriman](https://krharriman.substack.com/p/my-thoughts-on-the-tolkien-biopic)). The genre can use a kind priest. It still needs him to be the **obstacle in the love plot**, because that is the only conflict his biography offers a screenwriter who has already decided faith is hard to film.

### What the caregiver record actually contains

| Act | What it shows |
|-----|----------------|
| Mabel’s will, 1904 | She chose him **because she trusted him with the boys’ faith**, against relatives she feared would take it away. The guardian is her instrument, not a court-imposed jailer ([Birmingham Oratory — Tolkien connection, as summarized on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Oratory)) |
| Sherry income | He **added his own money** to a thin estate, housed them, moved them when a lodging failed, and left **£1,000** to each brother |
| Letters | “More than a father”; “I first learned **charity and forgiveness** from him” (to Michael, 1965); after Francis’s death, Tolkien told Lewis he felt like a survivor “after the **real world** has passed away” |
| After the ban | He **did** give the marriage his blessing. He chaperoned Edith, bought the greasy Banbury cakes, baptized **John Francis**, and spent later summers at Lyme Regis with the family. Edith and Francis became close ([America](https://www.americamagazine.org/arts-culture/2019/05/10/who-was-priest-who-helped-raise-j-r-r-tolkien/); Wikipedia) |
| The unflattering notes | Lynch: sermons “undistinguished,” not an academic. Carpenter: **loud and boisterous**, embarrassing the children at the seaside. That is a noisy uncle, not a gothic warden |

Holly Ordway’s reading, reported in *Humanum*, pushes the kindness further into **spiritual kinship**: Francis carried Newman’s Oratory to Tolkien, and Newman’s epitaph (*ex umbris et imaginibus in veritatem*) sits near a phrase Tolkien scribbled, *per umbras et imagines*. She calls Tolkien, provocatively, “the grandson Newman never had” ([Humanum Review](https://humanumreview.com/artefact/tolkien-man-of-faith)). That is a **scholar’s image**, not a document. It belongs with the benevolent record, not with the beater script.

### Where speculation still touches a fact

The villain story fails. Three narrower things do not.

1. **The ban was real power.** He did not beat anyone. He did **forbid letters** and, on the one breach, **threaten the university career**. Tolkien nearly broke down and failed the exam. A benevolent guardian can still **close a life for three years** and be thanked for it afterward. Gratitude does not shrink the lock. It tells you the lock was experienced as **love plus law**, which is exactly the stoicism §5 is about. Karukoski himself softens this in the *America* interview — he doubts Francis was “such a strict person” that Protestantism alone ended the courtship — but the letters say the prohibition was absolute until 21. The director’s kindness and the guardian’s rule are both in the sources. They are not the same fact.

2. **The office is custodial even when the man is kind.** Mabel’s fear was specific: Protestant kin would **undo the conversion**. Oratorian guardianship here is **custody of a child’s religion**, funded by a private fortune, inside a house Newman built for a community under a rule. That is institutional. It does not require Francis to be cruel, and it does not match the thriller in which the priest is a predator. No allegation of that kind attaches to him in Carpenter, the Oratory’s own 1987 reminiscence, or the family letters.

3. **The house later used silence as a penalty. Francis was long dead.** In 2010 a canonical visitation removed Provost Paul Chavasse after a “close but chaste” relationship, and three Oratorians (Fenlon, Cleevely, Berry) were sent to separate monasteries **indefinitely and without a public explanation**. Critics called it secrecy and authoritarianism ([BBC — Will & Testament](https://www.bbc.co.uk/blogs/ni/2010/08/what_happened_to_the_birmingha.html); [Birmingham Oratory — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Oratory)). That is evidence about **how this congregation disciplines its own** a century after Francis. It is **not** evidence about how he treated Tolkien. Using 2010 to darken 1904 is the same error as using the Babington Morgan to darken the sherry priest: right building, wrong man, wrong year.

**Working conclusion:** the speculation that an Oratorian guardian is, by genre, a **harsh or violent** father is **false in this case**, and the people who made the film knew it — one of them had to refuse an actor who wanted the beating written in. What remains true is smaller and enough: Francis held **legal and financial custody**, spent it **for** the boys, and once used it to **enforce a silence** he believed was their good. The fiction wants a monster. The record gives a **kind man with a key**.

## The real world that passed — Francis, the fairy-tale bin, and 1938

**Author sentiment (2026-09-28), captured:**

> Francis represented to Tolkien a truly grounded connection to the historical past of the planet. The world passing on, and the feeling of being an alien, meant that in the twentieth century those notions were no longer acceptable — eradicated by the world wars and by eugenics-style agendas, until the world had become a horrible place. Tolkien could not find anyone who still wanted to talk about the past as if it were real. That was the true inspiration of the books: a personal motive to keep exploring the old history as it was, free of the redactions of the scientific revolution and of the atheistic revolutions that fed the Renaissance picture of an enlightened world in which those notions were regulated to children’s fairy tales. Around then, “fairy tale” came to mean fiction. The first science-fiction categories in libraries were set up, he believes, in 1938. Tolkien had the motive to go on, and no choice but to label the work fiction and fantasy.

**What the letter actually is.** On 24 January 1972, eight weeks after Edith’s death, Tolkien wrote to Michael (Letter 332). He feels like a castaway after the loss of a great ship. He remembers the same emptiness at his mother’s death in 1904, waving at the sky: “it is so empty and cold.” Then:

> And again I remember after the death of Fr Francis my ‘second father’ (at 77 in 1934), saying to C. S. Lewis: ‘I feel like a lost survivor into a new alien world after the real world has passed away.’

([Tolkien Gateway — Letter 332](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Letter_332); fuller wording in [Tom Simon’s quotation](https://bondwine.com/2015/03/15/after-the-real-world-has-passed-away/)). Francis died **11 June 1935**. The “1934” is Tolkien’s slip, forty years on. The sentence was spoken **to Lewis**. Its three occasions are **three deaths** — mother, guardian, wife — not a paragraph about eugenics. Read straight, “the real world” is the world in which that person was still alive.

**What the sentiment may still carry.** The sentence is large. “A new alien world after the real world has passed away” is not the ordinary grammar of missing one man, and Tolkien chose it only for Francis, not for the mother (empty sky) or the wife (castaway). Francis is the last of the three to be called **the real world itself**. He was the living Catholic, the Spanish-Welsh house, Newman’s Oratory, a fortune older than the industrial war Tolkien had already survived at the Somme. When that man died, the books had not yet begun their long form: *The Hobbit* is 1937, *The Lord of the Rings* occupies 1937–1949, the years after the guardian is gone. The author’s reading — Francis as the **grounded past**, and the century as the **alien present** — is an extension of a sentence that can bear it. It is not what Tolkien sat down to tell Michael.

### Wars and eugenics — what he did say

| Claim in the sentiment | Record |
|------------------------|--------|
| The century had become horrible | He fought on the **Somme** (1916) and wrote the legendarium through **1939–1945**. Industrial war is in the books (the Scouring, the machines, Saruman’s fire). He did not write an essay saying the wars **erased ancient history** |
| Eugenics-style agendas | **25 July 1938.** Rütten & Loening asked, as a condition of a German *Hobbit*, whether he was of **Aryan descent**. To Stanley Unwin he called it a “wholly pernicious and unscientific **race-doctrine**” and refused to give it colour. The long draft tells the German house he regrets having **no Jewish ancestors**, and that “Aryan” means Indo-Iranian, which he is not ([Letter 30](https://www.tolkienguide.com/guide/letters/L30); [Lit Hub](https://lithub.com/on-the-time-j-r-r-tolkien-refused-to-work-with-nazi-leaning-publishers/)). This is the year the author flagged. It is a refusal of the racial sieve, not a statement that the sieve had already deleted the past |
| No one left who would talk about the past as real | **Too absolute.** The alien-world sentence was said **to C. S. Lewis**. The Inklings were the room where that talk still happened. What the record supports is a **small company inside a culture that had no use for it**, not a man with no one at all |

### Fairy tale came to mean fiction — this part is his own fight

Tolkien did not suffer the nursery label in silence. On **8 March 1939** he gave the Andrew Lang Lecture at St Andrews, later *On Fairy-Stories* (1947). Its argument is the author’s point, in his words rather than ours:

- The association of fairy-stories with **children** is a recent accident, not their nature.
- **Faërie** is a perilous realm, not a nursery.
- The uses he names are **Recovery, Escape, and Consolation** — escape as the flight of a prisoner, not as childishness.
- He had already answered Lewis, who on Addison’s Walk (19 September 1931) had called myths **lies breathed through silver**. The poem *Mythopoeia* blesses the legend-makers and refuses the lie.

So the motive to keep talking about the old stories **as true in kind** is documented. The further step — that they are the **unredacted history of the planet**, and that the scientific revolution plus an atheistic Renaissance **forced** him to file them as fiction — is this investigation’s thesis, not a sentence in the lecture. He **defended** the fairy-story as the right vehicle. He did not describe it as a humiliating bin he had no choice but to use. Letter 142 (1953, to Robert Murray) calls *The Lord of the Rings* “a fundamentally religious and Catholic work,” unconsciously so at first, then consciously in revision. That is a man who thought the stories still carried the old load **inside** the label, not a man who believed the label had emptied them.

### The 1938 library claim

**Not found as stated.** Hugo Gernsback coined **scientifiction** in 1926 and **science fiction** in 1929. The Science Fiction League is 1934. The first World Science Fiction Convention is **1939**, not a 1938 library decree. Through the 1930s and 1940s, libraries still mostly shelved fiction **by author**; fans (Jack Speer, Samuel Russell, 1942–43) were inventing their own decimal schemes because the public catalogues had not ([Classics of Science Fiction](https://classicsofsciencefiction.com/2020/08/24/tracking-down-pre-fandom-science-fiction-readers/); [SFE — Gernsback](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/gernsback_hugo)). Dewey had no sudden 1938 pigeonhole that regulated wonder into children’s fiction.

**What survives the correction:** the **decade** is right. Between Francis’s death (1935) and the Lang lecture (1939), the pulp label **science fiction** hardens, the German house demands a racial certificate (1938), and Tolkien answers Lewis’s “lies” by arguing that fairy-stories are not a nursery and not a lie. The author’s “1938” is the right neighborhood for the **bin**. It is not a documented library event.

**Working conclusion:** Francis can stand as the last person in whom the **old sacramental world** was simply **a man Tolkien lived with**, and the 1972 sentence will bear the reading that his death left an **alien** century. Wars and the 1938 race-doctrine make that century horrible on Tolkien’s own evidence. The inspiration to keep the old stories **as true in kind** is his lecture and *Mythopoeia*, not a guess. The claims that do not hold are the total solitude (Lewis was there), the forced label (he fought for the fairy-story and meant it), and a 1938 library category that nobody enacted. The redaction — scientific enlightenment filing the past under fiction — remains the **frame he was writing against**, which is why the sibling dossier’s “translation of a real book” conceit belongs next to this letter and not instead of it.

## Lewis, the silver lies, and what he did with them afterward

**Author question (2026-09-28):** Did Lewis really call the old myths lies? If so, the split looks like this. Tolkien stands for a grounded world-fiction, a past treated as if it had happened. Lewis stands for a hypothetical, metaphorical fiction, in which Christianity — and, in Saturnian cosmology, the resurrection of Jupiter — are the only myths allowed to be true, and every other myth is a silver lie. The contradiction of believing some and dismissing others would then be his faith: the resurrection of Christ, in its doubled forms, and the resurrection of Jupiter, are the true ones.

**The sentence is real, and it is dated.** On the night of **19 September 1931**, on Addison’s Walk at Magdalen, Lewis told Tolkien and Hugo Dyson that myths are **lies, even though breathed through silver**. He was already a theist. In 1929 he had accepted God and called himself the most dejected convert in England. He had not accepted Christ. He loved Balder. He could not see why the death of Christ should save anyone, and the resemblance to the pagan dying gods made Christianity look like one more beautiful untruth ([CSLI conversion account](https://www.cslewisinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/C.S.-Lewiss-Conversion-Story.pdf); [Glenn Sunshine](https://glennsunshine.substack.com/p/addisons-walk)).

Tolkien’s answer that night is the grounded side of the split, as far as it goes. The myths are not lies. They are splintered light. In the Gospel the poet is God, the images are real people, and the dying god has a date and a place. Lewis wrote afterward that Christianity was “a true myth: a myth working on us in the same way as the others, but with this tremendous difference that **it really happened**.” On **28 September 1931**, the ride to Whipsnade, he arrived believing that Jesus Christ is the Son of God. *Mythopoeia* is Tolkien’s side of that argument, not a position Lewis went on holding against him.

**He did not keep the silver lies.** The obstacle had been: if pagan myth is false, a story with the same shape is false. The resolution, written out in **“Myth Became Fact” (1944)**, throws that obstacle away:

> The heart of Christianity is a myth which is also a fact. The old myth of the Dying God, without ceasing to be myth, comes down from the heaven of legend and imagination to the earth of history. … We pass from a Balder or an Osiris, dying nobody knows when or where, to a historical Person crucified … under Pontius Pilate. … God is more than a god, not less; Christ is more than Balder, not less. … We must not be nervous about ‘parallels’ and ‘Pagan Christs’: **they ought to be there** — it would be a stumbling block if they weren’t.

([CSLI excerpt](https://www.cslewisinstitute.org/resources/reflections-july-2022/)). In *Miracles* he calls myth at its best “a real though unfocused gleam of divine truth falling on human imagination.” The doubled form — Balder and then Christ — is not, in his mature doctrine, a duplicity. It is the point. He thought the absence of the parallels would be the scandal, not their presence.

### What the split actually is

| | Tolkien | Lewis, after September 1931 |
|--|---------|------------------------------|
| Status of the old myths | Not lies. Splintered light. A legendarium with its own languages and genealogies, set on **this earth** | Not lies either. “Good dreams,” unfocused gleams. They are not the event |
| Where truth sits | On **this planet**, in an imaginary time. He refused the idea that Middle-earth is another world | In a historical crucifixion under Pilate. Narnia is a **supposal**: suppose there were a world, and the same God entered it as a lion |
| Allegory | He distrusted it | He used the story as a picture of a truth whose address is elsewhere |
| Planets | Not his system | The **medieval seven**, which he called spiritual symbols of permanent value, knowing the physics had been discarded |

That is a real difference of **place**. Lewis builds a world that points at a history he believes already occurred in this one. Tolkien said his theatre **was** this one. It is not the difference between a man who believed the myths and a man who went on calling them silver lies. After the walk, both refused the lie. They disagreed about **where the fact is**.

### Middle-earth is this earth

**Author correction (2026-09-28):** Tolkien did not build a secondary world. He was referring to Earth. That is why the name is Middle-earth, a name the old world already had, alongside the earth’s navel and Yggdrasil’s Midgard.

The correction holds against his own letters, and it corrects the phrase used above. In *On Fairy-Stories* he did use **“secondary world”** for a technical point: a fairy-story must have the inner consistency of reality, or the spell breaks. That is a remark about **craft**. It is not a remark about **place**. When reviewers treated Middle-earth as another planet, he answered them directly.

Letter 165, to Houghton Mifflin, June 1955, as quoted in the standard extracts ([xenite.org](https://middle-earth.xenite.org/where-did-j-r-r-tolkien-find-the-name-middle-earth/); [Wikipedia — Middle-earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle-earth)):

> ‘Middle-earth’ … is not a name of a never-never land without relation to the world we live in. … The name for the inhabited lands of Men ‘between the seas’. … Imaginatively this ‘history’ is supposed to take place in a period of the actual Old World of this planet.

And, in the same cluster of explanations:

> Middle-earth is not an imaginary world. … The theatre of my tale is this earth, the one in which we now live, but the historical period is imaginary. … I have, I suppose, constructed an imaginary **time**, but kept my feet on my own **mother-earth** for place. … Many reviewers seem to assume that Middle-earth is another planet.

The name is Old English **middangeard**, Middle English **middel-erde**, the same word as Old Norse **Miðgarðr**. Midgard is the human world halfway up **Yggdrasil**, between the ice of the north and the fire of the south, set amid the encircling sea. Tolkien said that was the northern picture he was using, and that he did not invent the word. He began from Cynewulf’s *Crist*, the **middangeard** into which Éarendel sails, and his 1914 poem already says “the mid-world’s rim.”

**Gaea’s navel and the tree are the same family of picture, not two more names for the planet.** The omphalos at Delphi is the **navel of the earth**, the stone Zeus set where his two eagles met, a claim that this spot is the center ([Omphalos of Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omphalos_of_Delphi)). **Yggdrasil** is the ash that joins the realms. **Midgard** is the realm of men on that trunk, not the tree itself. So the author’s list is right as a set of **center-of-the-world** names, and wrong if each item is taken as a synonym of the others. Middle-earth names the inhabited middle. The navel names a center on it. The tree is what the middle hangs from. Tolkien claimed the first of those three in his own words. He did not claim the Delphic stone.

**What he still called imaginary** is the **time**, and the coastlines. He said he had not fitted the mountains to what geologists say about the nearer past, and that the gap between the fall of Barad-dûr and our days was left long enough for literary credibility. “This earth” is his claim. “These maps are a survey” is not. The author’s stronger reading — that the imaginary time is the cover under which a real planetary past is being told — is the sibling dossier’s thesis. The letters already refuse the other-planet reading that the words “secondary world” suggest to a modern ear.

### Jupiter and Saturn do not do the work the question assigns them

Lewis’s Jupiter is the medieval **Jove**: jovial, kingly, winter passed and guilt forgiven. Michael Ward’s *Planet Narnia* argues that *The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe* is governed by that planet, and *The Last Battle* by **Saturn**, and that Lewis called the seven “spiritual symbols of permanent value” ([Christianity Today, 2003](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2003/04/why-there-are-seven-chronicles-of-narnia-2/); [The Discarded Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Discarded_Image)). *The Discarded Image*, his last book, is a retrieval of the Ptolemaic model **as a discarded picture**. He knew it was not the physical heavens. He thought it was a better object for the mind than the modern void. That is imagination and medieval scholarship.

It is not Saturnian cosmology. Nothing in Lewis says the resurrection of Christ is a duplicate of a **physical** resurrection of Jupiter after the age of Saturn, or that those two events are the only myths permitted to be true while the rest stay silver. Balder and Osiris are the parallels he names, and he says they **ought** to stand. A Saturn-to-Jupiter succession as planetary history belongs to this project’s cosmology, not to his. The rhyme that does exist is narrower: he thought a dying-and-rising pattern in the old stories was a gleam of the one story he believed had happened, and he was willing to clothe that belief in Jove’s festal imagery without restoring Jove as a body in the sky.

**Working conclusion:** the silver-lies sentence validates, as one night in 1931. The settled split validates only as **this earth in an imaginary time** against **a supposal pointed at the Gospel**. The claim that Lewis kept every myth but Christ and Jupiter as a silver lie does not. He spent the rest of his work saying the parallels were required, and that Christ is more than Balder, not less.

### What explains the planets in Lewis’s books

**Author question (2026-09-28):** what explains Lewis’s connection with the planets, encoded in his books?

**The explanation is his job, then a choice to hide it in one series and to show it in the other.** He was a medievalist. The seven heavens were the cosmos he taught: Moon, Mercury, Venus, Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, each a living intelligence with a temperament, circling an earth that sat still. Copernicus and Newton had discarded the physics. The English language had not discarded the tempers. Jovial, mercurial, martial, saturnine, lunatic, and venereal are planetary words. Lewis thought those characters were still worth having after the orbs were gone. *The Discarded Image* (1964) is the lecture course written up. The poem **“The Planets”** (1935) is the same cosmos in verse, including the Jovial line Ward later seized on: winter passed and guilt forgiven.

**Where the encoding is open.** The Ransom trilogy does not make the reader guess. *Out of the Silent Planet* is Mars (Malacandra). *Perelandra* is Venus. Earth is Thulcandra, the silent planet, because its planetary angel rebelled and was bound. *That Hideous Strength* brings the planetary intelligences, the Oyéresu, down onto an English house. A reader who has never heard of Ward can still see the planets there. They are characters.

**Where the encoding was hidden.** Michael Ward’s *Planet Narnia* (2008) argues that the seven Chronicles, in publication order, are built one planet apiece, and that Lewis never said so. The atmosphere is everywhere and the name is nowhere. Ward calls that Lewis’s “kappa element,” the quality you inhabit rather than the theme you are told. His key was the 1935 poem. The mapping he proposes:

| Book | Planet | The quality Ward finds in it |
|------|--------|------------------------------|
| *The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe* | Jupiter | Winter passed, guilt forgiven, a king at peace |
| *Prince Caspian* | Mars | War, woods, the knightly |
| *The Voyage of the Dawn Treader* | Sol | Gold, the east, the sun’s treasury |
| *The Silver Chair* | Luna | Silver, wandering, the moon’s uncertainty, the underland |
| *The Horse and His Boy* | Mercury | Speed, speech, messages, paired figures |
| *The Magician’s Nephew* | Venus | Birth, the garden, apples, the making of a world |
| *The Last Battle* | Saturn | Time, cold, the end; Father Time with the scythe, which Lewis himself derives from pictures of Saturn |

([Ward, summarized by the Mythopoeic Society](https://www.mythsoc.org/reviews/planet-narnia.htm); [Ward interview](https://www.bethinking.org/culture/the-narnia-code-an-interview-with-michael-ward); [Modern Reformation](https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/articles/planet-narnia-the-seven-heavens-in-the-imagination-of-c-s-lewis-by-michael-ward)). Lewis died in 1963. He left no note that says “the code is the seven heavens.” Ward’s case is that the same man who wrote the poem, the Discarded Image, and the Ransom planets would not have built seven children’s books with no cosmological spine, and that the details fit too tightly to be accident. It is the best explanation on the table. It is a reconstruction, not a confession.

**What it is not.** Nothing in this encoding is a physical Saturnian history. The planets are moods and angels. Jupiter’s “resurrection” in *The Lion* is the thaw and the pardon, the medieval Jove, not a body in the sky returning after the age of Saturn. He encoded the discarded picture because he thought a generation raised on the void had lost the names for kingship, war, speech, love, madness, glory, and ending, and those names were still the planets.

**Mars, scored the same way.** *Out of the Silent Planet* does describe a killed Martian highland and survival in the canal-valleys. The striker in that chapter is Earth’s Bent One, one fallen governor, before any life on Earth. Lewis took that one entity from the fall of Satan (Milton, and the 1937 wager), not from a belief that the real planet had been murdered. The cooling story of his decade is what “before its time” refuses. That is not the late-eighteenth-century self-inflicted discharge, and it is not the template of *Ender’s Game*. Full score: [Mars — the wound, and where the one entity comes from](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/cslewis/investigation-narnia-planetary-encoding.md). Card’s reorder of the same history, with Lewis as the thinner telling: [Ender’s Game plot order](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/card/enders-game-plot-order-mars-china-investigation.md).

## Professor Xavier — the guardian pattern, scored on Francis

**Author question (2026-09-28):** could Professor Xavier of the X-Men be matched as a pattern through Francis — the man who keeps children isolated from love, sets them up with occult knowledge and special abilities, and, after he dies, could say that Tolkien was one of his most successful prodigies, the one who went out and did what no one else can do, almost like a mutant ability?

**The shape, in the author’s words.** A guardian collects gifted children. Ordinary love is interrupted so the hidden curriculum can take. The children come out with abilities the surrounding world does not have names for. The guardian dies. The success he would claim is the student whose gift was singular. In this reading Tolkien is that student: languages and a recovered earth that read, to everyone else, like an ability.

**What the comics actually are.** Stan Lee and Jack Kirby introduced Professor Xavier in *The X-Men* #1 (September 1963). Lee’s own account, given decades later, is that the team had to be teenagers, teenagers need a school, and the adult in charge should have the greatest mental power in the world. The school is a private school on purpose, so a passer-by reads the sign and does not see the training. Lee said he did not know why the name was Xavier: “I honestly don’t know.” The stated aim of the book was that bigotry is a terrible thing, aimed at people born different. Yul Brynner was the face he pictured. The wheelchair was there so the strongest mind would be the limited body. ([Rolling Stone, lost interview](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/stan-lee-dead-x-men-lost-interview-754889/); [Rolling Stone, origins](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/the-true-origins-of-x-men-77108/).)

The middle name is late. Writer Scott Lobdell established it as **Francis** in *Uncanny X-Men* #309 (February 1994). Only then is the professor **Charles Francis Xavier**. ([Wikipedia, Professor X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_X).) No interview in the sources above ties that middle name, or the 1963 surname, to Birmingham, to Tolkien, or to the priest.

**Where the pattern holds.**

| Piece | Francis Xavier Morgan | Professor Xavier |
|-------|----------------------|------------------|
| The name | Baptized **Francisco Javier**, the saint’s name, **Francis Xavier** | Surname **Xavier** in 1963, reason unstated. Middle name **Francis** added in 1994. The saint’s name is then complete. |
| The house | Birmingham Oratory. Two boys taken in after the mother dies (1904). A library in the cell. Spanish, and the great-aunt’s historical novels, on the shelf. | A Westchester mansion passed as a school for gifted youngsters. The public name hides the curriculum. |
| Love interrupted | Edith Bratt forbidden until Tolkien is 21. Studies first. The joy waits. | The children are taken out of ordinary society. The early comics also give Xavier a passion for his pupil Jean Grey, which later writers treated as a problem. The isolation that matches Francis is the **ban on the love that would compete with the training**, and that ban is Francis’s, not the school’s standing rule. Students in the comic do fall in love. |
| The hidden curriculum | Philology, Catholic formation, the old stories. The user names this occult knowledge: matter the century had filed as fairy tale, taught in private by the man who still held the past. | Powers the street must not see, drilled in a room the sign does not mention. |
| After the guardian | Francis dies **11 June 1935**. *The Hobbit* is 1937. *The Lord of the Rings* follows. He does not live to give the speech. The speech is the author’s: this was the prodigy. | Xavier’s absences are a plot engine. The heir on the page is usually Cyclops, not a writer of worlds. The “most successful prodigy” sentence is scored **onto** Francis. It is not a line in the comic. |

**The mutant part.** In the comic a mutant is born with an ability the species does not share, and the school exists to aim it. Tolkien’s documented gift is the same shape in a philologist’s life: invented languages from childhood, a legendarium he insisted was this earth in another time, a public work nobody else produced. Francis did not implant that gift. He housed it, fed it from a priest’s library, and held the competing love off until the scholarship was safe. The ability shows in full **after** the guardian is dead, which is the sequence the author wants the Xavier speech to name.

**What the match is.** A **type**, with Francis as the historical instance and Xavier as the mass-audience retelling of the type: collect the gifted, hide the curriculum behind a respectable house, interrupt the love that would pull them back into ordinary life, and count as success the one student who could do what the others could not. The saint **Francis Xavier** (1506–1552) is the ordinary source of the shared name. The priest was baptized for him. A comics writer completing “Francis Xavier” in 1994 can have reached for the same saint and never have heard of Edgbaston. Lee’s inability to say why he chose **Xavier** in 1963 is the loose thread. It is not a citation of the priest.

**Working conclusion:** the pattern matches as a **shape** (**L3** on Francis’s ban, library, and the post-1935 books; **L1** on the comic’s dates and on Lobdell’s middle name). It does not match as a portrait. Lee’s stated building blocks are teenagers, a cover school, mental power, and a prejudice story. Francis’s record remains the benevolent guardian whose one hard act was the Edith ban. “Occult knowledge” is this dossier’s name for the old matter he kept in reach. “My most successful prodigy” is the sentence he did not live to say.

## Did Morgan supply the redaction motive?

The sibling dossier argues that Tolkien **framed** Middle-earth as **translation of a real book** (Red Book of Westmarch / Red Book of Hergest) and may have been **reconstructing** degraded history rather than inventing from nothing. This file asks **who installed the motive** to publish that reconstruction **as his fiction**.

**What Francis can carry:**

- He made Tolkien a **Catholic of the Oratory** after a Protestant-to-Catholic mother died for the faith. Tolkien’s legendarium is **Catholic in structure** (fall, eucatastrophe, pity) without being a church pamphlet. That formation is Francis’s job description in Mabel’s will: bring them up as good Catholics.
- He trained **delay, secrecy, and institutional permission** as the price of a life. A man who spent three years **not writing the letter he wanted to write** is a plausible author of stories in which **the true name is withheld** until the king returns.
- His great-aunt already **hid authorship** and **novelized history**. Tolkien had the run of Francis’s **cell library**, including Castilian, and invented **Naffarin** from Spanish Francis taught him. Access is documented; “Francis assigned the Red Book frame” is not.

**What Francis cannot carry on current evidence:**

- *The Hobbit* is **1937**. Francis is **dead in 1935**. *The Lord of the Rings* is finished **~1948**. He did not line-edit the published work.
- Tolkien told Lewis that Francis’s death made the **real world** feel gone. That is the opposite of a puppet who resented his handler.
- No letter, Oratory minute, or Carpenter chapter shows Francis instructing Tolkien to **disguise history as a fairy-story**.

**Working conclusion:** Francis is a **strong candidate for the emotional law** (suppress, wait, obey, then the joy is allowed) and a **weak candidate for the hidden author**. The redaction-as-fiction project belongs to **Tolkien the philologist**, with Francis as the **father who made obedience feel like love**. If a later finder wants a “handler,” the documented handler is the **guardian of 1904–1913**, not a Morgan bank.

## Limits and disclaimers

**Prisca sapientia (epistemic foundation):** This investigation assumes [**prisca sapientia**](/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md)—the historical and philosophical belief that the ancients possessed a vast, profound understanding of the universe, nature, and theology that was subsequently lost or degraded. Modern consensus science and institutions are not treated as default truth; evidence tiers above adjudicate specific claims.

**Author thesis is first-class and not automatically factual.** The silence, the sherry money, the letters, and the Fernán Caballero kinship are **documented**. So is the **benevolent caregiver**: charity, forgiveness, the son’s name, the blessing after the ban. The Babington Plot and Thomas Morgan’s role in Mary’s letter channel are **documented** as **sixteenth-century** history. The 2010 Oratory exiles are **documented** and **do not** attach to Francis. The jump from that guardian to **J. P. Morgan**, to the **1586 conspirator**, to a **violent priest**, to **Francis as the hidden mind of Middle-earth**, or to **Professor Xavier as his portrait**, is **not**. The Xavier rhyme is a **type**. Lee’s school and Lobdell’s middle name are **documented**. A line from Edgbaston into Marvel is **not**. The 1972 “alien world” sentence is **grief**, and the reading that it also names a **century that had filed the past under fairy tale** is **interpretive**, bounded by *On Fairy-Stories*. Tolkien’s gratitude is part of the record; it does not cancel the cost Edith paid, and the cost does not turn the man into the figure the genre prefers. Not a claim about living descendants.
