Helen Mirren is one of the most demonstrably unwilling actresses in the history of the job. The demonstration is her own sentences. For thirty years she has said she cannot do the part, she does not like the work, she is happy not to work, and then she has done the work and said she loved it.

On Saturday, 24 October 2026, I was at the SCAD Savannah Film Festival. She was there for the opening film, [*A Talent for Murder*](https://filmfest.scad.edu/schedule/talent-murder), and for the [Legend of Entertainment Award](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/10/25/helen-mirren-receives-legend-entertainment-award-scad-savannah-film-festival/). She stood on the step-and-repeat in a pink top, mouth open, with cameras and a crowd between her and the wall. She looked glad to be there. That look is the public yes. The record underneath it is the unwillingness. It looked to me like the twentieth or the thirtieth murder role, and like a person celebrating a picture the picture had not earned.

## The film the night was selling

[*A Talent for Murder*](https://variety.com/2026/film/awards/helen-mirren-scad-film-festival-talent-for-murder-1236871208/) is directed by Anton Corbijn. Bleecker Street and LD Entertainment released it in the United States on 23 October 2026. Alden Ehrenreich plays a young man sent by a New York publisher to Switzerland. His job is to talk a famous writer, played by Mirren, into one more book in a thriller series. The writer is a fictional Patricia Highsmith. The story comes from Joanna Murray-Smith’s play *Switzerland*. [The Guardian called the film a hit-and-miss thriller based on a play](https://www.theguardian.com/film/2026/sep/15/talent-for-murder-movie-review), the kind of writer-in-a-thriller setup that *Sleuth* and *Deathtrap* already used, and said the swing almost lands.

The other reviews split the same way. The performance is the part they keep. The movie is the part they drop. [Screen Daily said Mirren brings a snippy panache, and that the film never rises above a nifty diversion](https://www.screendaily.com/reviews/a-talent-for-murder-review-helen-mirren-is-spiky-author-patricia-highsmith-in-anton-corbijns-underwhelming-thriller/5220450.article). The cat-and-mouse game is glib. The Ripley pastiche is elegant and empty. The payoff disappoints. [RogerEbert.com said it is a talky drama that cannot get out from under Tom Ripley](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/a-talent-for-murder-helen-mirren-alden-ehrenreich-movie-review-2026). It leaves the closed room that would have made the play work, sidelines Mirren for long stretches, and cannot decide what movie it is. [Variety praised her hawkish, caustically funny performance and still said the buildup is more satisfying than the payoff](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/a-talent-for-murder-review-helen-mirren-1236861698/), and that 105 minutes is cramped for a plot that never knots the way a Highsmith story should.

That is a failed passage from stage to screen, carried into a festival opening by the affection people already have for her. She was praised. Ehrenreich was praised. The film was described as underwhelming, talky, and still a play. On the Saturday I saw, the public event treated the package as a triumph. One day of release is too short a clock to call the gross a bomb. The success on offer in Savannah was her body in the room.

## The record of not wanting it

Each pair below is the demonstration. She says she does not want the work. She takes the work. She says, afterward, that it was among the best things she has done. The earlier sentence stays on the page beside the later one.

Her parents did not want the acting job. It was unreliable. She qualified as a teacher anyway, and then she left the teaching. [The 1994 *Independent* profile](https://www.helen-mirren.net/articles/199403theindependent/) is where that start is told.

In 1977, at the Silver Jubilee, she was a [“grumpy anti-monarchist”](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/celebritynews/9243541/Helen-Mirren-Royal-Family-won-me-over-but-Im-a-working-class-dame-at-heart.html), appalled by the celebrations. Decades later, at Venice, she said [“I must say I slowly fell in love with her,”](https://www.standard.co.uk/showbiz/mirrens-royal-rebirth-7217788.html) that her parents “were very very anti-monarchy and anti-class system,” and that after playing “an essence of the Queen I’ve lost that chip on my shoulder.” By the Diamond Jubilee, 4 May 2012, she hoped [“to see Charles become king one day”](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/celebritynews/9243541/Helen-Mirren-Royal-Family-won-me-over-but-Im-a-working-class-dame-at-heart.html) and said, “I’m not a republican any more — not so voraciously, anyway.” Charles is the man who had dubbed her Dame in 2003.

*Excalibur* came with its own refusal. She and Nicol Williamson had done *Macbeth* together, and by her account it was horrendous. When John Boorman wanted Williamson as Merlin, [she did not think she could do the film](https://www.top10films.co.uk/85321-helen-mirren-on-making-excalibur-from-uncertainty-to-triumph/). I’ll just put up with it.’” On the picture she [“finished up loving him.”](https://www.top10films.co.uk/85321-helen-mirren-on-making-excalibur-from-uncertainty-to-triumph/) The rehearsal lines had sounded embarrassing.
