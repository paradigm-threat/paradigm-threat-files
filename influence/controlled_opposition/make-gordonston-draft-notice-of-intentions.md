## Email to Art Fair committee (draft — not sent)

**Subject:** Heads-up on next year’s Art Fair website — my intentions

Hi everyone —

I’m writing with a heads-up, not a mid-season exit. I will finish this year’s fair the way I said I would: site, artist checkout, jurying, booth fees collected and transferred to the destination bank, late entries and the ordinary email traffic. I’m not yanking anything out from under artists while that work is still live.

Frankly, though, I need to be honest about next year. When I came on, I thought I was helping a charity — Juliette Gordon Low, kids, Girl Scouts, the whole story people still tell. What I found out later is that the park is private trust land run like a guest list, and the Art Fair sits on top of that. I have a real problem putting more discount labor into a private enterprise on private property when I’m not allowed to speak my mind around here anymore. I got banned from the online community and nobody spoke up when it counted. People said that’s just the way it is. Every poster I’ve put on the public board in the park gets ripped down; sometimes mean things get written back. It isn’t a welcoming place for me anymore, and it isn’t what I thought I was volunteering for.

I’m not blaming the Art Fair committee for that. Most of you are volunteering out of the goodness of your heart and a real desire to see Gordonston Park community-managed somehow. Same with a lot of GNA work. The people who actually own the hard choice are the park trustees. They’re the ones I’m holding responsible for how the land is run. Everyone else is basically free labor on their property.

I want to be honest about the charity goal. Even if the trustees were fully on board, getting Georgia property-tax “purely public charity” status back would not be easy. Chatham can still say no. You can’t lock that in before the park actually runs like a children’s charity again. Private trust ownership can stay forever — I’m not asking the trustees to give the land away. What I’m asking for is a real promise that we will try to reach that Low / Scout / Savannah’s-children goal: reopen in a way that includes Twickenham, rebuild or reorganize toward a real public charity use, and see it through in good faith. If we try hard and it turns out unfeasible — county says no, money isn’t there, we have to quit the attempt — that’s enough for me. At least I know we tried. Right now the history looks like we didn’t try anything and we’re not going to.

Frankly, I also don’t see another real path to rebuild the playground. We already worked that out in hard numbers. The wooden playground itself was originally an Eagle Scout Service Project — there’s still a BSA plaque on a post. A full commercial replacement runs on the order of three hundred to seven hundred fifty thousand dollars and up. That is not happening for this neighborhood. The only path we found that is financially feasible is Plan A: nearly all volunteer labor, nearly all donated materials, a build captain on the structure, and a new Eagle Scout–led effort for the sub-phases and fundraising — cash contingency on the order of zero to a few thousand dollars. Anything else is fantasy money.

That Plan A only works if the story Scouts and donors hear is the original children’s charity vision — Savannah’s children, Twickenham in, not a private guest-list park. Last time Eagle labor built under a charity story that, when Chatham finally looked, staff said never properly qualified. I don’t think troops or Coastal Georgia Council are going to pour that labor and in-kind fundraising into a Residents Only club once they dig into lost charity status and the rest of this history. They would sign on to undo the damage and push hard for the Low / Scout vision. As far as I’m concerned, that is the only solution available to us.

So the minimum to keep me on the Art Fair website next cycle is not a stamped exemption. It’s that promise to try, with Twickenham access as part of the try. I’m leaving the door open. If that promise doesn’t show up in some form I can take seriously, I won’t be the person setting up next year’s fair online.

I’m telling you now so you have time. If you plan to go forward without me, start looking for the next developer — rebuild hosting, checkout, and jurying, or sign onto an existing platform that handles that stack (which is what I wanted to do with a real nonprofit before I found out this wasn’t one). Or hand the fee system back to whoever ran it before. Someone will still have to rebuild the public site; the old one was already down a long time ago, and restoring it and building the fair around it was my service at an extreme discount.

I won’t put a countdown or kill date on the public website. You’ll have the operational details in writing if we get to a formal resignation. Until then, assume I’m finishing 2027 properly and that next year is the open question.

I still love the history here, and I still respect a great deal about this neighborhood. That’s just how it is for me now — faith in the charity story is gone, and I won’t pretend otherwise.

Thanks for reading this without treating it as a threat. It isn’t one. It’s a notice of intentions while there’s still time to talk.

Ari Asulin  
ari@asu.edu

## Note to GNA (shorter pass-along — draft, not sent)

**Subject:** FYI — Art Fair website / my intentions on the park

Hi —

I’m sending a fuller note to the Art Fair committee about the website and checkout. This is the short version for GNA.

I will finish the 2027 Art Fair work I already owe the artists. After that, I don’t plan to set up another year of the site unless there’s a serious promise to try for the old children’s / public-charity vision again — including reopening to Twickenham as part of that try. I’ve also had a rough run locally — banned online with silence around me, posters on the park board ripped down, sometimes nasty notes — and frankly that doesn’t feel like a place I can keep volunteering for.

Door’s open if that promise to try shows up. If not, the committee will need another developer or platform before next year’s ramp. Details are in the Art Fair email.

Ari Asulin
