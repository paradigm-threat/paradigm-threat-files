## 1. Working thesis (**R**)

From 2004 through 2006 an industry-aligned takeover of OC ReMix ran through the judges panel: a massive forum deletion (UnMod), removal of the old guard, and replacement by a pro-industry staff dominated behind the scenes by Larry (**Liontamer**). Dave (**djpretzel**) later resigned, and Larry has run the site de facto for over twenty years. The accomplished goal was to keep the community flush with the industry: criticism of the industry banned, and no project that would compete with the industry.

That paragraph is the search frame. It is author and insider speculation. It is not a finding.

## 2. Method — a missing confession is normal, and it is not a finding

Other communities in this corpus match the same pattern. None of them contain a smoking-gun admission of an industry sellout. Sellout is still a well-known and accepted description of what many people in these scenes did. Absence of a confession is the normal state of the pattern. It is not, by itself, a reason to drop the OCR case. It is also not a promotion of (**R**) to a finding.

The rhyme this repo already treats as a pattern, not as a confession, is [MAGFest — community governance](magfest-community-governance-investigation.md) **§10**: the same author calls the recurring shape a silver platter, and states that file has no single smoking gun for industry influence. The parent OC ReMix file **§6.2** is the same living search. This file does not add admissions those dossiers do not contain.

This file stays open on purpose. Each block below is either an observed fact, the trend that would count, or evidence still required.

The incidents below are the public pattern around the same man. They are not a showing that OCR’s judges caused them.

**Uncredited reuse on *Sonic and the Black Knight* (S).** The 2009 soundtrack lists Tommy Tallarico as the music credit on “Molten Mine,” “Great Megalith,” and “The Cauldron.” [Sonic Stadium, 5 January 2024](https://www.sonicstadium.org/articles/sonic/sonic-and-the-black-knight-composers-contributions-questioned-r1810/), reporting comparisons from Sonic Music Facts: “Molten Mine” and “Great Megalith” were set against *Black Dawn* (1996), whose music credits name Todd Dennis; “The Cauldron” was matched to a track on a Todd Dennis demo reel for *Adrenix*, which that article dates as a 1998 PC game. Jun Senoue and the SEGA sound team are named on the arrangements of two of the three. The writeup’s conclusion is that he did not compose the three songs. This file has not heard the stems. The resemblance and the credit split are the sourced part. A grant of rights from Todd Dennis is not here.

**Zelda sheet music (S).** Laura Intravia arranged the *Legend of Zelda* 25th Anniversary Medley for Video Games Live and performed its flute solo in 2011. In April 2024 she wrote that someone was selling her sheet music illegally, that she never signed a contract giving anyone exclusive rights to it, and that orchestras performing it had transcribed the flute solo by ear ([Time Extension, 7 April 2024](https://www.timeextension.com/news/2024/04/musician-behind-vgls-zelda-medley-hints-that-tommy-tallarico-is-selling-arrangements-he-doesnt-own); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Tallarico)). The posts quoted there do not name him. She said the seller is “exactly who you think.” Wikipedia records the accusation as against Tallarico. Pay and a missing printed credit stay the user’s account past those quotes (**R**). A contract or a receipt is not in this file.

**The “oof” (S).** The sound later used as Roblox’s death effect is the *Messiah* (2000) effect, created in September 1999. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Tallarico) credits sound designer Joey Kuras, then a Tommy Tallarico Studios employee, and leaves open that Tallarico may have been involved. Tallarico has claimed that Kuras created it, that the two collaborated, and that he created it himself. Kuras’s own [2004 account](https://dperry.com/2004/10/06/interview_with_5/) is that in February 1993 Tallarico offered him the sound job. The [2019 metadata report](https://gamesbeat.com/robloxs-famous-oof-death-sound-allegedly-came-from-a-tommy-tallarico-game/) credits Kuras on the file. The personal-achievement claim is his claim. A session log of who performed the take is not in this file.

**False career claims (S).** The November 2022 Hbomberguy video, as Wikipedia summarizes it in the exposure paragraph above, covers the game count, the Guinness records, the Roblox “oof,” MTV Cribs, and the claim to have been the first American to work on *Sonic*. **What this does not prove.** These incidents do not prove a kickback inside OCR, and they do not prove the judge panel ordered the anthology. They show the same operator treating other people’s music, arrangements, and sound as his to sell or to claim.

## 7. The trend that would count, and the evidence still required

The trend that would move (**R**) is a documented hand on the 2004–2006 removals, a message in which **Liontamer** urges the UnMod deletion, a resignation that actually moves authority, a criticism ban that is a rule or a takedown, and a refused project set beside the publisher work the site did take. Until those exist, the file stays a search.

- **Who appointed and removed judges in 2004–2006.** A mod log, mail, or a first-person account that names the decision. Last-post dates and one relayed goodbye do not identify the hand.
- **The UnMod decision trail.** The Announcements wording this pass did not quote, and any message in which **Liontamer** urges Dave to delete it. Behind-the-scenes influence is the user’s account and is currently without a public trace. Timing is not influence.
- **Dave’s resignation.** Date, stated reason, and what authority actually moved (trademark, hosting, payroll, judge appointments, front-page policy). “De facto for twenty years” needs that chain. This file does not supply a date it has not read.
- **A criticism ban** that is a rule or a takedown, not a musical **NO**. Name the thread, the track, and the stated reason. The judges archive searched so far is the wrong place if the ban lived in site policy or in threads that were deleted with UnMod.
- **A competition test.** Projects that would have put OCR in the market against publishers (a label, soundtrack bids, a fair-use fight, a rate or credit demand) and were refused, set next to projects the site did take (licensed albums, publisher partnerships). “Never competing” is shown by the refused project, not by a low yes-rate.
- **The Tallarico thread.** The **2 October 2004** email is in hand. The objection’s absence is accounted for as erasure (**R**), not as a gap that weakens the testimony. A contemporaneous copy would document the post. It is not required before the claim can stand as testimony.

**Falsifiers.**

- **Liontamer** or later staff publicly pushing OCR to compete with publishers.
- Industry criticism hosted and defended as in-scope.
- Dave still holding the operational controls after the supposed resignation.
- The 2004–2005 cohort leaving in their own contemporaneous words, with no removal.
- A mod log of the UnMod deletion that names a reason and does not name **Liontamer**.
- A contemporaneous Shael account that he kept the HD Remix project and was not pushed out, or a 2004–2005 critic who stayed on the panel through the 2007 Capcom deal while still criticizing commercial moves in public.

## 8. Producers, free work, and Street Fighter HD Remix

**Claim (R).** Producers show a strong industry influence inside the community. They extract free work, then publish it on paid projects, and in some cases rip the creator and hand the work to the industry. That is the industry’s influence on a community: a sharp separation between fans and industry employees. Fans are only allowed to make free projects. Industry employees and agents are allowed to repackage those products and sell them because they are close to the IP.

HD Remix is the check. A commercial license of fan remixes shows a channel from free community work into a paid IP-holder product. The same record does not show a rip, a ban on fan sales in general, or that **Liontamer** ran the deal. The judges archive has no license thread. *Blood on the Asphalt* appears there as ordinary music votes (topics **6385** and **4445**). Later HD Remix mentions (topics **12242**, **15483**) are musical asides.

**Chronology (S / P).** Capcom associate producer Rey Jimenez heard the free tribute album *Blood on the Asphalt*, which Shael Riley directed and which Wikipedia dates to 2006. Shael told him the arrangements came from more than twenty community artists, and that permission should go through Dave (**djpretzel**). [Ars Technica, 17 July 2008](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2008/07/ocremix/). On **24 October 2007**, **djpretzel** posted that he was the single point of contact with Capcom (parent **§4.2**, [topic 9050](https://ocremix.org/community/topic/9050-is-blood-on-the-asphalt-the-soundtrack-for-sf2hd/)). Shael, in the same Ars interview: once Rey was in touch with Dave, Rey wanted some pieces revised and some replaced entirely, and Dave organized that. Many themes were already done and only needed modification. New arrangements were written for the game. Capcom rejected **djpretzel**’s first E. Honda take; he rewrote it himself. An alternate Zangief by **Big Giant Circles** was not picked up. **zircon**’s Fei Long lost a quiet section in the game edit. Arrangers named in that interview, all as community remixers, include Shael Riley (Zangief), **zircon** (Fei Long), **djpretzel** (E. Honda), **Big Giant Circles** (Guile), **José the Bronx Rican** (Vega), and **Vurez** (T. Hawk). Wikipedia’s composer credit for the game is OverClocked ReMix, not a Capcom staff composer. The game shipped November 2008 as a paid download. Capcom Unity, via Wikipedia, reported a quarter of a million paid downloads within a month. The full soundtrack was released free on OCR on **27 November 2008**.

**The contract, as the director described it (S).** **djpretzel**: the contract covers material used in the game; OCR may distribute that music “as long as we don’t charge a profit”; Capcom may not sell it as a separate album; “Capcom gets what we think is a pretty badass soundtrack for free.” The signed contract is not in this file. No public figure says what any remixer was paid. Ars lists **djpretzel** and Shael Riley as soundtrack directors and **Liontamer** as assistant soundtrack director and submissions judge. That is a public credit, not a showing that he held the Capcom contact or was a Capcom employee. Rey Jimenez is the Capcom employee in the chain. The parent file already records that no post was found in which Shael says the project was taken from him (**§4.4**). Testimony in **§4** says he had a hard time and that the proof is likely whitewashed. A further search for that complaint still found none. The whitewash claim is untested. The sourced posts still show him as a soundtrack director who referred Capcom to Dave.

| Clause | HD Remix |
|---|---|
| Community producers carry industry work | **Partial.** Rey Jimenez opened it from Capcom. The people who gathered the tracks were OCR producers (**djpretzel**, Shael Riley, **Liontamer** as assistant). Nothing sourced here shows they were industry employees beforehand. |
| Free work, then a paid publication | **Matches** the public account. Existing free album tracks and new community arrangements went into a game Capcom sold. The director called the soundtrack free to Capcom. What remixers received is blank. |
| Rip the creator and hand the work onward | **Does not match.** Shael stayed a director and sent Capcom to Dave because he could not license other artists’ tracks. One alternate was not used. A rejected take was rewritten by its own arranger. “Some replaced entirely” is Capcom asking for new material, not a documented credit theft. |
| Fans may only work free; employees and agents may sell, because they sit next to the IP | **Partial.** Capcom, the IP holder, sold the game. The described contract lets OCR give the music away and bars charging for it. That is a no-profit term on this soundtrack. It is not a rule that fans may never sell. The parent file’s 2013 licensed album and 2014 label are later commercial OCR releases. |

**What would settle the open clauses.** The contract: who signed, any pay schedule, and the credit clause. A track list pairing each *Blood on the Asphalt* arrangement with the in-game arranger, if a different person took a finished track. A remixer account or ledger of pay. A rule, separate from this one contract, that forbids fans to sell arrangements while letting staff sell them.
