# Convention suite and publicist shunning (open)

**Last updated:** 2026-10-25 (conduct named as workplace bullying and power harassment; Japan, India, and South Korea)

**Privacy.** Jim Shepard is named because the game career is public. Everyone else in the room stays unnamed.

**Source.** One person's memories and the emails that person kept. That person's name is omitted. The sentiment is part of the account. It is not rewritten as a split down the middle.

**Tiers.** **R** is this account. The dollar figures and the order of the billing letters are what those kept emails say. The stare, the hour in the room, and the shunning are memory. Memory the emails do not spell out stays memory. The dated career below is the public record supplied for this file. The OC ReMix and MAGFest lines are one public web search on 25 October 2026.

## 1. Billing, early August 2013 (**R**)

A long-time community regular, not an established publicist, was asked mid-campaign by a mutual contact to help Jim Shepard, a newcomer to that circle, with publicity. Pay was presented as certain, at $25 an hour. She offered to work for less. She was trying to help and did her best.

She sent an invoice for seven hours and a friendly note congratulating the campaign and offering more help later.

He paid that night, $175, then wrote that he was dissatisfied: name errors in a press kit, a studio "we" after he had said he was one person, and a PR email he said was mostly his own example reordered. He then wrote that he felt taken advantage of. He did not ask for the money back.

That charge went past a professional note. He could have listed the errors, or taken responsibility for not reviewing the kit before it went out. The integrity accusation is the escalation.

She conceded the name errors, defended the hours as real and probably low, and returned the $175 the next day because the questioning of her ethic was not worth it. That reply was a defensive exit after the red flags, not an attempt to keep the job.

**The first email was horrible.** It is abusive unprofessional behavior, and the 2016 interview is the later admission. In August 2013 Jim Shepard paid a small publicity invoice the same night, then wrote that he felt taken advantage of, and the money was returned the next day. Three years later, in public, the hard part of the campaign is his own fund handling, and he says he does not think he did it well. In the [AUTOMATON interview of 8 April 2016](https://automaton-media.com/articles/interviewsjp/interview-the-man-who-challenged-indie-from-layoff-by-big-studio/), the interviewer says that even after Kickstarter funding succeeds, managing the funds is full of difficulties. Shepard answers: "Managing the money is hard! I don't think I did it well enough, either." The same answer then advises paying contracted developers fairly and says recruiting for next to nothing burns people out. The interview does not name the bill. The August email and this sentence are the behavior and the later admission.

> **Side hustle and broken promises (R).** Open claim, not a finding. Industry influence made video game development a side hustle, and that work became increasingly more difficult. That pressure encouraged the domineering insecurity in the August 2013 billing emails above, and similar psychologies that tear relationships apart and ruin communities. The broken-trust piece here is that billing fight and the later admission, "Managing the money is hard! I don't think I did it well enough, either." Dungeonmans funded and shipped. Eye of the Commando still being in Early Access is not proof a date was broken for budget; the store page said the target might slip. On this claim, these projects should have been led by the community that paid and did a lot of the work for free, not by for-profit leadership that weighs its own security against the promises it made and often breaks them as a matter of budget. The console case is Tommy Tallarico's Intellivision Amico, in [OC ReMix — judge panel and industry takeover](ocremix-judge-panel-industry-takeover-investigation.md) **§6**.

**Names for this conduct.** The core term is workplace bullying or psychological harassment. When it shows up as public anger or an excessive verbal assault under the guise of performance management, this file calls it abusive supervision, power harassment, or boss busting. Those names describe the August 2013 billing email and the suite approach. They are the description adopted here. They are not a court finding and not a clinical diagnosis. He was the paying client, not an employer of record. That clause does not soften the email or the 2016 admission.

**Japan (S, and the reading).** Japan is widely recognized as having this workplace problem, which is why the conduct has an official name there. The phrase power harassment (パワーハラスメント, パワハラ, pawa-hara) was coined in 2001 by consultant Yasuko Okada ([JILPT](https://www.jil.go.jp/english/reports/documents/jilpt-reports/no.12_japan.pdf); [Nikkei Business, 28 March 2022](https://business.nikkei.com/atcl/gen/19/00304/032500084/)). The government later made it a legal category. A 2019 amendment to the Labor Policy Comprehensive Promotion Act (Act No. 24 of 2019) requires employers to prevent remarks and behavior that use a superior position, go beyond what is necessary and appropriate for the work, and harm the working environment. The duty took effect on 1 June 2020 for large employers. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare dates the smaller-employer obligation to 1 April 2022 ([MHLW](https://www.check-roudou.mhlw.go.jp/study/roudousya_harassment.html); [Library of Congress, 22 June 2020](https://loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2020-06-22/japan-law-to-prevent-powa-hara-power-harassment-takes-effect)). Sexual harassment (sekuhara) is a separate category in that body of law. This file does not collapse the two. The overlap with these communities is a reading **(R)**. Game culture here was largely sourced from Japanese development, so the same supervisory dynamic is easy to see. Japanese labor law did not cause the billing emails or the suite visit. These communities were not Japanese companies.

**South Korea (S, and the reading).** Any discussion of this behavior includes South Korea. Gapjil (갑질) is the public name for abuse by someone in a superior position. On 15 January 2019 the country promulgated an amendment to the Labor Standards Act prohibiting workplace harassment (직장 내 괴롭힘), in force 16 July 2019. Article 76-2 defines it as an employer or worker using superiority of status or relationship at work, beyond the proper scope of the job, to cause another worker physical or mental suffering or to worsen the working environment ([Ministry of Employment and Labor, 15 January 2019](https://www.moel.go.kr/news/enews/report/enewsView.do?news_seq=9556)). The user's reading **(R)** is that South Korea is now the market for a lot of what used to be made in Japan, including the game culture these communities came from. No market-share figure is supplied here. Korean labor law did not cause the billing emails.

**India (R, against the surveys that were checked).** The user's comparison puts India with Japan: high in global studies of workplace stress and abusive management, and typically male-on-female. The surveys do not say that as one ranking. A Cigna TTK survey, as [NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/indians-most-stressed-globally-on-work-financial-issues-survey-1880867) reported it, put everyday stress in India at 89 percent against a global 86 percent and called India the most stressed of the countries in that round. The 2019 Cigna 360 Well-Being Survey, as [Express Healthcare](https://www.expresshealthcare.in/news/cigna-ttk-releases-surevey-on-stress/410521/) described the India cut, still called stress in India very high, at 82 percent, with work among the causes, and in that cut men at 85 percent and women at 82 percent. [Cigna's own 2019 write-up](https://www.cignahealthbenefits.com/en/newsroom/how-healthy-are-employees-around-world) of the same survey says South Koreans were the most stressed and that India ranked first on its overall wellbeing index. [Gallup's 2025 country page](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/705650/state-global-workplace-india-country-level-data.aspx) puts daily stress in India at 28 percent, under the global 40 percent. No global league table of abusive management was found that ranks India. The male-on-female pattern is this account's reading of the case: a man directed the billing assault and the suite approach at a woman. It is not a finding that Japanese power-harassment statistics, or the Indian stress surveys, establish that gender split.

## 2. The suite, early January 2014 (**R**)

The dispute was still open in the sense that she had refunded and heard nothing. At the next game convention he came to the shared suite and to her bed. She had just woken up, covered her face, and told him she was not comfortable talking.

He did not seem about to leave. He sat comfortably in the chair and stared at her under the covers. Then he noticed the witness staring at him from across the room. He noticed the witness, then he got out of the chair, then he said "ok" and walked away. The witness does not think he would have left had the witness not been staring.

He stayed in the room about an hour. He did not leave. She left the room until he was gone.

His later email reduced this to: she said she did not want to talk, he said ok, and he walked away. That is a whitewash of the fine points. The reliable account is the witness and the publicist, not his.

The witness called it unprofessional, aggressive, and violatory, and a continuation of the aggression in the email: an attempt to aggressively dominate and control a publicist he intended to keep.

He wrote that he skipped a friend's later event in case the two of them would be there. They also skipped it. They left for home immediately and did not say goodbye to that friend. His absence is not evidence that only he withdrew.

A friend who had been in the room answered only that the truth was somewhere in the middle and that he could not validate either side. He did not describe what he saw and did not ask them. Another person copied on the mail never wrote. The newcomer never answered the detailed sequence.

## 3. The shunning (**R**)

What made them leave was that everyone seemed to take his account as the truth without even asking them. That was the shunning.

They never wrote a reply after that, so the mailbox does not contain their answer to the shunning. Absence of that reply is what this account says happened. It is not a point against it.

She had been a regular for around 14 years. That span is this account's figure. He was new to that circle, with a commercial project, industry connections, and money potential. Old friends treated him as more valuable.

The witness's further account, kept here as testimony: they had already left after being removed from the judge panel. By this convention they had reconnected with everyone, including old enemies. They then had to leave again because of the newcomer's actions. Nobody came to their aid. It was not their choice.

The same account says it could have been handled better. A community can protect longstanding members by at least giving them an audience, hearing their part of the story, and building awareness to restrict for-profit influence when it becomes coercive or divisive. Their part of the story never went on record until this file, because nobody asked.

That paragraph is the witness's account. It is not a finding that the mailbox proves the whole community voted. The emails already show that the friend in the room would not describe what he saw, and that the detailed sequence was not answered. "Nobody asked" is why the record is one person's. The shunning stays open to other mail. The room and the billing tone stay the part more or less concluded. "Removed from the judge panel" stays a role.
