## Letter (draft text — not sent)

**Subject:** Formal resignation — Art Fair website; site deleted December 31, 2027

To Gordonston Neighborhood Association leadership, Juliette Gordon Low Park trustees, and Art Fair organizers:

This is the formal resignation I said would follow if nobody would even promise to try.

I wrote you earlier with a notice of intentions. I finished the 2027 fair cycle I owed the artists — booth fees collected, funds in the destination bank, jurying and late-entry mail handled. I was honest then: even with trustees fully on board, getting Georgia “purely public charity” property-tax status back might be hard or fail — Chatham can say no, and private trust ownership can stay forever. I did not demand a stamped exemption. I asked for a real promise to try for the Low / Scout / Savannah’s-children goal, with Twickenham included in that try. If we tried our best and had to quit, that would have been enough. What I got instead is the same picture as the history: no try, and no sign anyone will. So I’m done.

I resign from volunteer and underpaid professional work on [gordonstonartfair.com](https://gordonstonartfair.com) — site, artist checkout, jurying flow, and related outreach — effective with this letter, subject to the closeout below.

I’m not blaming the Art Fair committee or GNA volunteers. Most of you gave free labor out of goodwill and a hope the park could be community-managed somehow. The people I’m holding responsible are the park trustees. They own how this land is run. Everyone else was working on their property for free.

### What the paperwork shows (why faith is gone)

When I came on the Art Fair, I thought it was a charity. Neighbors said so. The old write-ups still talk that way. I believed it.

I had to dig to find 2011–2012. Chatham County Tax Assessor staff told the *Savannah Morning News* the park did not and never would have qualified as a “purely public charity.” Computer records they could still see went back to about 1985; older exemption paperwork was missing. By tax year 2012 the parcel is on the digest as taxable. Since then the trust has paid the bill.

How long that exemption lasted is a range, not a slogan:

| Bound | Years tax-free (charity exemption) |
|---|---|
| What the office could still see | ~1985 through 2011 — about 26–27 years |
| If it started with the 1928 trust conveyance | 1928 through 2011 — about 83 years |
| If it started with the 1948 cottage / Scout era | about 63 years |
| End | ~tax year 2012 (bill on the digest; paid receipts on the public site from 2014 on) |

83 years is the honest ceiling if the break began with the estate putting the park in trustees’ hands. 26 years is the floor we can still look up. Either way, staff said it never qualified — so however long it sat on the roll, it was wrong the whole time.

Anyone can look this up:

- PIN 20040 28001 — 10 Edgewood Rd — Juliette Magill Low Trust (care of the park trustee on the tax card).
- Chatham tax site ([chathamtax.org](https://www.chathamtax.org/)): no exemption on the exemptions tab. Paid about $700–$790 a year from 2014 through 2025.
- Fair-market value sat near $135,800 (2020) and was cut to $60,000 (2022) — about half — while houses on this block keep getting reappraised up. 2025 FMV is $67,500 on 3.0 acres listed as a park. That is park-priced tax on land run as a private club.

For at least ~26 years, and up to about 83 if the exemption rode with the 1928 gift, they enjoyed charity exemption while Residents Only (from ~2001), locked gates, and 2011 “call police on outsiders” were the practice. Girl Scout council had been without reliable park access for years by 2011. A Twickenham neighbor offered on the order of $100 a year plus volunteer cleanup to keep a life routine in the park. The answer was precedent — let one outsider in and you have to let all — not “too little money.”

Dues, cottage rental, and Art Fair booth fees are how this ground is monetized. Required club money and a guest list are the cover charge. Twickenham could not buy a seat at any price. Same unfair deal on the spending side: volunteers and the Art Fair raise the money; trustees and a small leadership circle decide where it goes, on private property, with no real community oversight. The cottage mini-split AC is a clean example — Art Fair and GNA each put in about $600 so the private cottage gets cooled and heated, outdoor unit locked in concrete and rebar. We provide the money. They tell us where it’s used. The benefit stays inside their building and their guest list.

That is the paperwork scam in plain English: charity tax, then park-cheap tax, without operating as a public charity. It’s on the roll.

There is even a “preservation” nonprofit on paper next to this setup. I am not claiming anyone is pocketing a yacht. I am saying the structure invites the exact risk charity law exists to police — private benefit and private inurement: a closed guest list, volunteer money, private control. That risk is common enough that federal and state rules treat it as a real class of abuse. A Residents Only park with a charity brand and a preservation association in the filing drawer is not a weird one-off. It is the kind of arrangement those rules are for.

### The 501(c)(3) call

Under that original impression I tried to put the Art Fair on a 501(c)(3)-supported network so artists could get free services — the public-charity route people had sold me. I called the park trustee. He hung up. Later it was explained as not trusting my Arizona area code. What I came to understand is he did not want the 501(c)(3) path at all. So I built the entire site myself at an extreme discount against 25 years of professional web work. That was little more than an act of faith. That faith is permanently shattered.

### Why the neighborhood part still hurts — and what actually triggered this

Corruption in a small trust is common. What got me was everyone else’s reaction — and then who was allowed to enforce the guest list on me.

The main person who attacked me online and ripped my notices off the park bulletin board lives across the street from the park trustee, in the center circle of Gordonston, and helps plan the social events. That is not a coincidence of geography. That is how private ownership of this ground turns into real neighbor abuse and exclusion: the trust sits in the middle, the enforcer sits next door, the calendars and cottage socials keep rolling, and the rest of the neighborhood treats it as normal. That pattern — online purge, board posters torn down, nasty notes back (lizard people, the carnival, because years ago there were Trump signs in my yard), Hollywood / filming dressed up as “no politics” — is what triggered this resignation. The paperwork made the faith die. The neighbor war made staying as volunteer labor impossible.

The responsibility to stop that lies with the rest of the neighborhood. Zero people came to my aid when it counted. People said that’s just the way it is. Private kindness after a purge does not count. So there is no community here — just a private club. I’ve already been cut out. This resignation is not really my choice in the sense people mean when they say someone “walked away.” Why stay where I am not welcome? Why keep building the website and checkout for a place that already showed me the door?

I live on the edge of an apartheid at Gwinnett — Twickenham on one side of the sign, Gordonston on the other — and I will not volunteer to paint it pretty. Volunteers are free labor on private trust land. Someone collects the money and decides who may stay. Posted hours and fair rules can exist on a park that is honest. Residents Only plus park-priced taxes plus “we’re a charity” talk cannot.

It isn’t only playtime. GNA brings Code Compliance, SPD, and City staff into meetings on this private park property and offers that face-to-face help to Gordonston. People across the street in Twickenham don’t get those specific gatherings. Other community centers don’t run city services behind a neighborhood guest list. That exclusive venue is supposed to feel like A+ treatment for living inside the line. I abhor it. I do not appreciate those services. I do not want the help, do not need the help, and will never need the help — not if it comes through a guest list that cuts people out based on which house they live in.

I had an illegally dumped fridge on my property. I used the ordinary city channel. As of yesterday the city got back to me: done; let us know if you have questions. I was happy with their timing and performance. I saw no evidence that the code compliance officer who appeared at the Gordonston Park GNA meeting had anything to do with it. The city did just fine on its own. I would rather deal with a slow city — even pay a fee if confusion or poor performance on the city’s part caused it — than accept exclusive help that excludes Twickenham and everyone else outside the fence. In the end that exclusivity amounts to little more than a feeling. One I will not share and do not feel.

### What I expect will happen without me

I said in the notice that full Georgia charity status might be unfeasible even if everyone meant well. That honesty still stands. Qualifying means acting like a children’s public charity — including Twickenham — not just talking about Daisy Low.

I also said the only financially feasible rebuild path is Eagle Scout–centered volunteer work — Plan A from the playground plans we already wrote. The existing wooden playground was an Eagle Scout Service Project; there is still a BSA plaque on a post. A full commercial replacement is on the order of $300,000–$750,000+, which this neighborhood is not going to raise. Plan A is nearly all volunteer labor and donated materials, with cash contingency in the low thousands. That only works if Scouts and donors hear the original children’s charity vision — including Twickenham — not Residents Only. I don’t believe they will rebuild a private guest-list park once they dig into lost charity status and the rest of this history. Without a try at that vision, there is no rebuild crew coming. The park keeps rotting.

Without a try, the more likely path is uglier: let the park rot, keep the token tax on a $60k–$67.5k appraisal, and wait. They’re already off the old charitable exemption. What they still have is the scam tax rate — park-priced land for club use. People will notice that bargain the way Twickenham noticed the exemption in 2011. Then it becomes a political fight. Elections matter. After elections, people are still answerable. I’m not staying on as volunteer labor while we wait for a try that never starts.

### Closeout (as flagged in the notice)

- Site stays up through December 31, 2027. No public countdown or kill date on the website.
- Free operational email for six months after this letter (late entries, “where is my invoice,” that class of mail).
- Instructions and advice for a new host and programmer. I will tell that person why I quit — private property, guest-list park, cheap or free labor unless they themselves believe in the fair as it actually is. Doing it for money is fine. Doing it as charity is a lie I will not help repeat.
- On December 31, 2027: entire website, all data, all code deleted. After that, no Gordonston Park labor from me. Hands washed.
- I’m not leaving this corridor. My daughter may still be in the park when access allows. That’s parenting, not volunteering for your brand.

It is nothing personal toward most people I built pages with. If you disagree, it may be personal for you. I did the work on faith, read the roll, and then lived the exclusion. The notice left a door open for a promise to try. This letter closes it — not because I found a warmer club elsewhere, but because this one already cut me out.
