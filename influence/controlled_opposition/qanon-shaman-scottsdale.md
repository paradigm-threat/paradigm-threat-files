## Scottsdale — Chansley, FOX 10, and the foiled association

On **Wednesday, June 24, 2020**, at **Scottsdale City Hall**, roughly two hundred people turned out for **“Unmask Us”** — an anti-mask mandate rally, **not** Stop the Steal. **Daniel McCarthy**, then a GOP Senate hopeful, spoke and framed the moment as resistance to **communist insurrection** ([azcentral](https://www.azcentral.com/story/news/local/scottsdale/2020/06/24/scottsdale-councilman-leads-protest-against-mask-mandates/3245724001/)). After he finished, **Jacob Chansley** — the man later branded the **QAnon Shaman** — emerged from the side of the grounds onto an **empty grass patch** the crowd had not filled. Shirtless. Horned headdress. Face paint. A spear sign: **“Q SENT ME.”** He never joined the main crowd. He staged a second act.

**One dedicated broadcast camera — FOX 10** on a tripod (**FOX 10 sticker on the leg**) held Chansley as the continuous subject. Two masked FOX 10 crew faced him. Other TV crews nearby ran **local interviews**. Handheld phones circulated on the lawn. Chansley yelled loud enough that his voice carried over those other on-camera interviews. The lens was not just collecting horns. In the same field of view sat **Republicans**, **mask protesters**, the **Daniel McCarthy** campaign signs and trailer, and the **Flat Earth** booth from [Part I](https://paradigmthreat.net/cosmos/flat_earth/the-flat-earth-trance.md). They wanted a package that welded **QAnon** onto McCarthy’s protest. That is what I saw on the lawn, and it is what the video still shows.

The framing was the whole point. Nobody had to say a word for it to work. Put the horned man with the “Q sent me” sign in the same shot as a Senate campaign sign and a trailer with the candidate’s name on it, add people holding anti-mask placards, keep a Flat Earth display running on the same grass, and the picture does the argument by itself. The horns get attached to McCarthy. McCarthy gets attached to the Republican Party. The party gets attached to the mask protest, and a few months later to the voter-fraud protests that filled the same plazas. One camera position, one composition, and every grievance in the frame inherits the costume.

I did not know whose camera it was while it was happening. I read the station sticker on the tripod leg years later, off my own frame grabs, going back through the footage. On the day it was simply the one professional camera that would not let go of him.

I walked up — too close, in front of that FOX 10 lens — to spoil that association, not merely to interrupt a take. First I signaled to the camera that he was crazy. Then I pointed at him and yelled **“Paid! Paid! Paid! money, money, money!”** He answered my Sons of Anarchy shirt. I did not answer when he told me to get out. I walked back toward Q supporters and pointed at the Flat Earth PVC booth — **they’re paid too**. At the end of my roll, the **same FOX 10 operator turns the camera away and packs it up** while Chansley is **still ranting**. They did not wait for him to finish. My reading is that the segment died because I ruined it: no clean adjacency, no agreeable crowd, a heckler in the middle of the shot. That reading can be argued with, and I want it argued with. A crew can pack up early because it already has enough footage, because it is due somewhere else, or because it decided the man was unusable on his own. Nothing I have found rules those out. What is on the roll is only this: the camera left before the subject did. The Short is that first confrontation. Frame grabs, dialogue transcript, and claim tiers live in the [Phoenix protest timeline investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/constitution/investigations/phoenix-protest-timeline-investigation.md).

### The second exchange

My second roll picks up a few minutes later. I am holding the phone, so I am off camera the whole time; the man on camera is one of the Q supporters, and he is following me around.

**Watch the second roll:** [Scottsdale, June 24, 2020 — second roll, unedited, with speaker-annotated captions](https://youtu.be/NhFky3v6eEI)

The argument goes nowhere and I decide to confront Chansley again. I walk away from the argument and back up to Chansley, who has not been watching it. He is still turned toward the few cameras that are left, and by this point there are no tripods on that lawn at all — just phones and handhelds. His crowd has thinned out badly and people are walking off. The cameraman leaving in my own frame a few seconds earlier is one of them. So he is performing to whatever is still pointed at him, and what is still pointed at him is somebody's phone. He says "What's up, dude?" and his voice is wrecked. He has been yelling for a while and you can hear it. I tell him his buddies insulted me, harassed me, and threatened me with physical violence. He says: "Buddies? I'm here alone." I tell him they are protecting him. He says "Who?" I point across the lawn — they're protecting you, right there. And he answers: "So, without my consent… on my behalf, without my knowledge."

There was a broadcast camera dedicated to him, a Flat Earth booth on the same grass, a billboard behind the whole thing, and a set of aggressive Q supporters running interference for him the moment anyone challenged the act. A man does not stand at the center of that and not notice it. Saying "without my knowledge" is the cheapest possible way to keep the structure and disown it at the same time.

Not "I don't know those guys." Instead: without my consent, on my behalf, without my knowledge. He said them in that order, to a stranger with a phone, while men with signs stood at his shoulder — which the frames further down show. I had brought the harassment back to its source: your people are doing this to me right now, what do you say. What I got back was that they were not his people. What I take from the phrasing is my own reading, not a proven fact about what was in his head.

So I asked him for the thing itself. I want to hear your Q. He starts: "Q is the highest levels of the military…" I ask him the only question that matters — what did Q ever leak? He starts to answer. "Q has leaked. Not only—"

And the video ends there, mid-sentence.

Going back through that roll years later I found things in the background I had walked right past at the time. One is a second cameraman. Not the one who had been holding Chansley — a different operator in a red shirt, broadcast camera up on his shoulder, tripod legs dragging behind him, crossing the grass toward the street and the parked cars. He is leaving. By that point the story on that lawn was a shouting match between me and a handful of Q supporters, and he had decided there was nothing in it. That is two professional cameras gone in under four minutes, and Chansley is still going the whole time, still in the headdress, still working the spear sign, now with nothing pointed at him but phones. If you want to argue the first crew just wrapped an ordinary segment, that is the part that needs explaining: the subject never stopped.

Look at the grass while you are in there. In both of those frames the middle and right of the lawn are empty, and the people left are in thin knots at the edges. The audience was draining. A thinning crowd is also a perfectly good reason for a news crew to pack up and find a story somewhere else, so that makes the innocent version of the camera turning away easier to argue, not harder. It does not explain him. He had lost the broadcast camera and he had lost the crowd, and he was still going, in full costume, to somebody's phone.

Another I had never noticed at all: the police. Three or four officers off to the side of the lawn, some of them standing with their bikes. A few seconds later in the roll there are more of them, masked, posted along the grass slope, and not a TV rig anywhere in the shot. They are not in the frame on the first roll. On this one they are close.

My read is that they had been there all along. Police work a protest like that from the edges, and you do not notice them until they matter. What changed was the distance. They closed it when it started to look like there was going to be a fight between me and Chansley.

I think that is what some people on that lawn were waiting for. The Q supporters were aggressive and looking for one — talk about harassment, arguing over who had the right to do what, following me. Chansley was screaming. My conjecture is that they wanted somebody to throw a punch or throw something, or to get angry enough at the things he was saying to make it physical, and to pull the police into it. A fair number of the cameras were there for the same thing.

It did not happen, and I think for two reasons. The picture was already spoiled — the crowd was plainly not on the shaman's side. And it became clear I was not going to supply the violence. I had gone at him twice, and the second time I asked him to explain Q, which is not a fight. I kept walking away from the taunts.

Somebody else might have swung eventually. I have been harassed enough in my life to know the shape of it, and the shape was a smokescreen: keep the exchange from going anywhere. I had plenty I could have said. Every time it got near an argument the man on my camera pulled it back to how close he was standing and what he could do about it — I could follow you around if I want to — and stopped just short of anything you could name. That is not a temper. That is a method, and it stays deniable.

The last thing is on my own roll, about a minute thirty-five in. Chansley is telling me he is there alone and that anyone acting for him does so without his knowledge. At the edge of the shot the man who had spent the previous minute following me around the lawn walks in with his yellow sign and takes up a position next to him. A second later an older man in a Trump cap is standing at Chansley's shoulder with a rolled sign, another man is filming on a phone, and there is a small cluster of them around him.

He said it on my recording, and the picture taken while he said it shows men standing at his shoulder. A crowded lawn does put strangers next to each other, and I cannot prove from a photograph that he knew those men or that anybody sent them. The claim was *alone*.

That is where my footage stops. Everything after it is my memory. He answered with time travel and the CIA. I told him I don't believe in time travel. He stormed off, into the crowd and then out of the whole scene — I stayed on that lawn about another half hour and never saw him again.

By then the TV cameras were packed away and my phone had stopped, so what happened next has no recording at all that I know of. It turned into a big argument between me and the Q supporters, and from the outside it must have looked like I was about to lose badly. Then it changed. They stopped arguing with me and started arguing with the other protesters who had been standing there watching the fight. After a while the Q contingent broke up and drifted off. That is when the headcount registered with me: there had always been far more ordinary protesters on that lawn than Q or Flat Earth people, and the two poison-pill crews were the ones leaving early.

What was left was a small group, ten or fifteen people, none of them Flat Earth or Q. They already suspected the Q presence had been staged, and they wanted to know what I made of the claims themselves. They asked how I knew he was wrong about pedophiles in Congress. I said I absolutely agree there are pedophiles in Congress and running the show. I do not doubt that. Their tone changed completely.

Then I told them the part that matters. Some of what these people say may well be true. That does not mean the rest of it is not said on purpose. It can be sponsored — and one of the cleanest ways to sponsor it is through a tax-exempt religious vehicle that takes deductible donations and sends people to protests to make everybody there look bad. That is why they are on your lawn. Not to persuade you. To make the whole protest look crazy by association. The paperwork side of that — a Flat Earth church with an IRS ruling going back to 1972, and the Arizona money trail nobody has closed — is laid out in the [Phoenix protest timeline](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/constitution/investigations/phoenix-protest-timeline-investigation.md#3f-tax-exempt-church-vehicle--arizona-funding-gap).

They wanted more than that. It fizzled when I would not tell them what I believed. A woman finally asked me straight: so what do you believe then? Back then I had barely done the research it would take to say what I can say now, so I said I was totally pagan and left it there, with a little about history being a lie. But the answer I was reaching for was that it does not matter what I believe. It does not matter what she believed either. A controlled-opposition element was standing on that grass with a broadcast camera pointed at it, and it has to be accounted for before any of the rest of the argument is worth having. Whoever turns out to be right about masks or votes or Congress, that element was in the frame, and leaving it out of the account is how the account gets discredited.

Someone from the campaign handed me a McCarthy sign after I said I never got one. It ended in good vibes. Some of them appreciated that I had called Chansley out. It was clear he was ruining the legitimacy of the protest, and they were glad someone roughly their age and demographic would challenge him. The real issue for that lawn was the mandates, the deep state, the attempt to control everyone. That is what I agreed with. I took the sign, got on my bike, and went home.

I cannot show you any of that. Other people probably can — there were cameras all over that lawn, including a man filming the Chansley exchange on his phone a few feet to my right, and you can see him in my own frame. Somebody has the rest of it. I do not.

McCarthy kept sharing the post-election circuit with Chansley through **November and December 2020** — Capitol, MCTEC, Executive Tower — without cleaning poison pills from rooms he headlined. That is why the stage hygiene reads as controlled opposition: not because every fraud claim was false, but because adjacent self-defeat was never expelled.

After Scottsdale I started to suspect McCarthy himself. The reason is not complicated. All of it was happening at his event. The Q presence, the man in horns running a television beat on his lawn, the Flat Earth booth a pan to the right, the supporters who spent the morning following me around — he disowned none of it. A candidate who lets that stand in his own frame is either not paying attention or does not mind.

Then there is the thing that struck me on that lawn and strikes me harder now. In the middle of 2020 there was a real hunger for a Republican willing to say *communism* out loud. The word is treated as effectively forbidden in mainstream American politics, and McCarthy said it — he called the mask mandates a communist insurrection, and that is in the local paper, not in my notes. So ask the question from the other side of the table. If you were choosing which man gets to carry that word into a protest, why would you not choose one whose surname is already welded to the overreaching and delusional persecution of communism?

Because that is what the name does. Joseph McCarthy was a Republican senator from Wisconsin who stood up in Wheeling, West Virginia in February 1950 and claimed to have a list of communists inside the State Department. He never produced it, the number kept changing, and within seven weeks the press had a word for the method. *McCarthyism* does not mean opposing communism. It means accusing people of treason without caring much about evidence. His own chamber condemned him for it by a vote of sixty-seven to twenty-two in December 1954, and he was dead at forty-eight three years later. That is the association a man named McCarthy brings to a stage before he opens his mouth, and everyone who stands behind him inherits it: red scare, witch hunt, paranoid excess. It is the same trick as the horns and the Flat Earth billboard, except the poison pill is the candidate's name.

I cannot prove any of this. There is no handler, no payment, no assignment I can put in front of you, and I have not found any family connection between Daniel McCarthy and Joseph McCarthy — nor do I need one, because the argument is about what a name already means to a listener, not about anyone's ancestry. Nobody picks their surname, and a man called McCarthy who believes every word he says fits these facts perfectly well. What I am saying is narrower and I want it stated plainly rather than dressed up: everything about that morning, including his name, points the same direction, and I have no evidence that anyone selected him.

He faded afterward, and I want to be clear that this had nothing to do with me. He lost the primary to Martha McSally about six weeks after Scottsdale by roughly three hundred and seventy thousand votes, which no protest interruption moved by a single ballot, and he stayed publicly active for another year and a half.
