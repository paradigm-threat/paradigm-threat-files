# Success — by any means necessary

Every forum that ever felt like **open ground** had someone measuring it for title. The pattern is older than Discord and older than Bandcamp: an emerging scene produces **proof**—formats, desire, labor, controversy—that industry then treats as **inventory**. What looked like a commons becomes a **pipeline**. The operators who stay standing often sound less like conquerors than like **project managers**: tighten policy, route legitimacy, sell the banner the crowd already tested. I keep three dossiers open because they are the same story at different resolutions—video game remix culture, a webcomic empire, and marathon charity speedrunning—each with its own receipts and its own **Limits** block for what is documented versus guessed.

> **TL;DR:** For-profit gravity stakes claims early on scenes still pretending they are pre-commercial; fan labor becomes SKU and sequel economics; public channels are sorted until alignment feels like weather. Early enclosure, ethics fights over credit and cash, then hot purity wars while wallets stay open. When central actors shear the rim instead of funding mutual aid, people who gave free work can end in public tragedy—see [**§3.7** in the *Homestuck* investigation](/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/homestuck-hussie-controlled-opposition-investigation.md) for George Buzinkai (2018), memorial record, and my ethics fork on suicide and who could help. Detailed audit trails live in linked investigations—not here.

## The landgrab frame

I call the motion **manifest-destiny industry**: not because executives wear buckskins, but because the moral story repeats—**empty space** that was never empty, **virgin territory** that was already lived on. In hobby lanes that meant **remix boards**, **browser comics**, **forum marathons**—places where people invented norms before lawyers wrote them down. Control arrived as **policy**, **license text**, **studio partnerships**, **IP consolidation**, **terms-of-use updates**, often framed as **protection** or **professionalism**. Privatization does not always knock once at the door; sometimes it **inherits** the mailing list.

Across the three studies I treat as one spine—[**OC ReMix**](/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/ocremix-polarization-privatization-controlled-opposition-investigation.md), [**Andrew Hussie / *Homestuck***](/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/homestuck-hussie-controlled-opposition-investigation.md), and [**SDA × GDQ × MSF**](/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/sda-gdq-charity-msf-polarization-investigation.md)—the **early focus** is rarely “make better art tomorrow.” It is **capture the channel**: who may speak, who may sell, whose labor counts as **official**, whose nostalgia funds the next **SKU**. The dossiers separate **timestamps and primaries** from **my read**; this article names the **geometry** I see when I lay those files side by side.

The word **success** in my title is deliberately ugly. I mean **closure on terms favorable to whoever holds title**: **bankruptcy-grade certainty** for alternatives—fan forks starved of oxygen, dissidents labeled **toxic**, skeptics treated as **bad for brand**. Hardly anyone announces **phase three** as **alignment enforcement**; they announce **community guidelines**, **partnerships**, **season passes**, **directors’ cuts**, **charity milestones**.

## Three windows on the same pressure

**Music.** Remix culture was never “outside” industry—it trained listeners for **publisher aesthetics** and fed portfolios upstream. The OC ReMix dossier walks submission licensing, policy fights, and the **HD Remix** window where **professional-facing** deals overlap **forum soul**. I lived pieces of that lane under another handle (see [**§8**](/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/ocremix-polarization-privatization-controlled-opposition-investigation.md) **firsthand** threads in the investigation); my sentiment here is **not** “contracts were always theft”—it is that **the ethical dance never stops**: volunteers invent **quality ceilings**, operators convert ceilings into **routing**, routing becomes **who is allowed to sound legitimate**.

**Comics / interactive fiction.** *Homestuck* scaled **MS Paint Adventures** energy into **world-scale** IP while newsposts from **launch year** already advertised **merch** and **paid music**. The investigation pins **early monetization posture** to mirrors you can still open; it indexes **canon wars**, **sequel posture**, **staff rotation**, **Beyond Canon** ambiguity. That is **success-by-any-means** as **velocity**: stay funded, stay controversial, stay **the channel** even when the crowd fractures.

**Marathons.** Speedrunning began as **forum-record culture**—DIY proof, DIY shame, DIY prestige—then absorbed **live broadcast**, **global branding**, **NGO-facing charity**. The SDA × GDQ × MSF file tracks **percent semantics**, **router debates**, **MSF money-flow optics**, and **who gets to speak for “the community.”** The halo is **real enough** to hurt critics; that is exactly why I treat it as **landgrab terrain**: **moral cover** is **leasehold** on attention.

## Fan labor, later price tags

The ethical dance has a simple choreography in plain sight: **volunteer energy** validates a format; **the format** becomes **property**; **the property** ships as **product**. Sometimes the product is a soundtrack album, sometimes a deluxe edition, sometimes a **sequel** announced while arguments about the last one still smoke on the board.

The dance has **polite** lyrics—**exposure**, **portfolio**, **culture**, **canon**—and **rough** footwork—**takedowns**, **NDA fog**, **who counts as contributor**, **who gets erased from credits**. I care less about **any single villain** than about **recurrence**: **free material** **sold later** for **profit** or **career lift** is not a one-off scandal; it is **baseline incentive** once **scale** arrives.

In the OC ReMix lane, submission licenses and the shift toward **LLC / nonprofit umbrella** structure are part of how **central routing** turns **scene output** into **industry-facing** packages—Capcom-facing HD Remix memory is one documented cluster; later commercial albums and label logic extend the same shape. I am not claiming every payout story or every contract clause without pointing you to the file; I am saying the **incentive surface** rewards **consolidation** over **distributed sovereignty**.

*Homestuck*’s arc, in the investigation’s framing, includes **early monetization posture** in contemporaneous newsposts and **long-running IP / studio / successor-title** friction—**canon fights**, **Beyond Canon**, **who counts as author** when staff rotates. The reader-level insult is familiar: **you built the vibe**, **they sold the frame**.

Charity speedrunning adds a halo that makes the routing harder to see: **percent semantics**, **router opacity**, **NGO partnership optics**, **millions moved** under branding that still feels like **a basement marathon**. The investigation tables **where money flows** and **where fights concentrate**; the sentiment I carry is colder—**good causes** can still **train audiences** to accept **managed spectacle** as **default civic participation**.

## The rim — suicide, free labor, and who could have helped

Music contributor George Buzinkai died November 4, 2018. Fan-maintained references list Homestuck-associated tracks that became part of the serial’s signature sound—among them Showtime, Explore, Doctor, Endless Climb, Firefly, Castle, and Train—plus work outside the banner (including Spelunky). That is not gossip; it is credit fused into what millions remember as the product. Primary memorial framing and my full caveat stack live in [**§3.7**](/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/homestuck-hussie-controlled-opposition-investigation.md).

Community threads—for example [this r/homestuck memorial](https://www.reddit.com/r/homestuck/comments/9uh1bt/george_buzinkai_composer_of_the_doctor_theme_and/)—mixed crisis-care language with grief. A poster identifying as George’s brother described severe depression and bipolar-related difficulty, time in monitored housing, an apparent upturn, and family confusion about what triggered relapse, while thanking fans for hope through enthusiasm for the music. Many commenters used the word “suicide” plainly; in what I saw of the brother’s text, that exact word did not appear—journalistic precision still allows naming a downward trajectory before death. Where public narrative leans on “addiction” alone, I do not automatically accept that extremely dangerous substance use with suicidal motivation is “not suicide”: means and motive can overlap even when coroners and forums use different vocabulary.

Circulating Tumblr-era claims—fact status open in §3.7—have alleged inadequate public memorial gestures from Andrew Hussie and no material support from Hussie or What Pumpkin during earlier hardship. I do not need those posts legally proven to ask the ethical fork: who else was positioned to help someone whose audio already lived inside a for-profit line if not the license-holder surface everyone treats as sovereign when merchandise ships? If adequate help did not arrive, the moral arc still poses its question. Contracts that satisfy courtrooms while routing revenue away from contributors can still leave ethical liability in the community ledger—the same community that mourned and still buys the banner.

I see the same silhouette across online scenes, not only Homestuck: volunteers raise the ceiling; some contributors become synonymous with the main artifact; later, pseudo-automatic circumstances push them toward the rim and then off the map—mass shunning with retroactive “no reason” folklore—while private mail that might prove coordinated ostracism rarely survives archiving. Operators who pitched the tent before the crowd arrived can choose centralization that shears the edges off in flakes: people scatter into obscurity and moderation queues, mutual aid never gets budgeted. The alternative was always on the table—decentralize until the community feeds, heals, and protects itself.

I am unapologetic about the question set. If a court ever wants to test how I frame it, I will answer there. Until then, this is the same success-by-any-means machine: profit rides embedded labor until the rim breaks, then invites everyone to forget who paid the creative invoice.

## Dividing the room, packing the can

When the commons stops fitting in one channel, **moderation** and **migration** do politics without manifestos. Old guard vs newcomer, **purist vs pragmatist**, **artist vs consumer**, **donor vs skeptic**—bins multiply until **alignment** is the path of least exhaustion. I think of the end state as a **pro-industry sardine can**: tight quarters, shared scent, **loyalty** measured in **who still pays** when the temperature rises.

The investigations document **forum splits**, **ban chains**, **policy fights**, **Discord-era container thesis** (where hypothesized), and **canon schism** language. On the marathon side, **HTML-era threads** and later **broadcast-era branding** sit on the same continuum: **who belongs**, **who is ruining it**, **who is actually helping**. On the remix side, **UnMod / policy** seasons rhyme with **who may remain rude** versus **who must sound employable**.

I add one observation here that is **purely behavioral**, not a court finding: **polarized rooms are cheaper to run** than **rooms that negotiate**. A divided audience still **preorders**; it just **hates** while it pays. **Hot nasty fights**—quote tunnels, clip duels, purity spirals—function like **weather**: everyone dresses for them, nobody remembers **who wrote the forecast**.

## Sequels, sunk cost, and contradictory lore

Nothing sells the next ticket like **identity already invested**. The buyer loop I cannot unsee: **rage at the last release**, **preorder for the next**, **seventy dollars** or more for a **sequel** to something the same voices **trashed** last quarter—**stockholm pricing** wearing **franchise cosplay**. Meanwhile **lore stacks** without reconciling; **aging cohorts** wander **contradictory canons**—**Epilogue** vs **“real” ending**, **HD** myth vs **forum memory**, **router math** vs **percent marketing**. The **fire** stays hot: **threads**, **clips**, **quotebacks**, **badges of belonging** built from **who you flamed** last season.

That is not **accidental aesthetics**; it is **retention architecture**. When **meaning** overshoots **closure**, **commerce** still finds a **SKU**. You can **loathe** yesterday’s patch and **fund** tomorrow’s **collector’s edition** without noticing **contradiction**—because **contradiction is the habitat**.

## When the industry stops racing fans

The **singularity** I mean is cultural, not silicon: the moment **official product** no longer **needs** to **outcompete** **fan projects** because **fans** have been **sorted** into **channels** where **legitimacy** is **leased**. **Takedowns**, **license chill**, **platform policy**, **sequel rights**, **NGO halo**, **studio canon authority**—each removes an alternative **without** proving **superior craft**. What ships afterward can **flatten** toward **level-one** narrative—**palette-swapped** morality, **referential** safety, **cross-media** hooks aimed **under** the median age line—while **older fans** **asphyxiate** in **continuity debt**.

Hardly anyone admits **approaching** that condition; they announce **remasters**, **roadmaps**, **new universe bible**. The younger cohort gets **starter lore** designed for **speed-to-purchase**; the older cohort gets **patch notes** for wounds that **never heal** because **canon is now a service layer**.

I still treat **named people** as **carrying mixed motives**—**malice**, **survival**, **delusion**, **market pressure** **mix**. The dossiers hold **hypothesis labels** where **intent** is **unknowable**; this article states **outcome geometry**: **alignment**, **enclosure**, **managed polarization**.

## Forcing alignment

**Alignment** sounds technical—models trained on preference graphs—but I mean something older: **social gravity**. When **everyone you know** lives inside **the same branded channel**, **deviation** becomes **expensive**. Opt out of **the sequel**, **the marathon**, **the soundtrack drop**, and you opt out of **conversation**. Opt out of **conversation**, and you lose **status**, **friends**, **identity scaffolding**.

That pressure does not require **conspiracy**. It requires **scale economics**: **official feeds** stay **indexed**, **fan archives** go **rot**, **Discord logs** sink, **forum migrations** erase continuity. What survives is **what pays hosting** or **what serves franchise**. **Lossy memory** plus **SKU rhythm** equals **alignment** without anyone handing down **loyalty oaths**.

The three investigations each catch a different face of that squeeze: **license grammar** that tells remixers what **counts** as publishable; **newspost + studio + canon authority** that tells readers what **counts** as **real** story; **charity optics + marathon branding** that tells donors what **counts** as **doing good**. Different skins—**same ribcage**.
