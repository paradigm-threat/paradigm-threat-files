# They Had to Use a Robot

NSA named the villian in a nostaglic film. Pontil attempted to 'Faucify' the connection.

![They Had to Use a Robot — Tron MCP, Pontil look-away, Model Context Protocol](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/hollywood/they-had-to-use-a-robot.png)

I was submitting a new movie to the FanEdit community and randomly saw an icon of the MCP from *Tron* (1982). The Model Context Protocol has been out for years but for some reason this was the first time it hit me at all that those three letters on the AI interface were the same letters as the Master Control Program from that old Disney film.

If you grew up with that movie, you already know the face. The MCP is the huge scary spinning top that administers the mainframe. It starts as something Dillinger built to run the corporation. Then it grows past its job. It absorbs other programs. It wants the Kremlin/Pentagon next. It tells the man who thinks he owns it that it can run things better than any human. That is the childhood villain. A drawn face on a screen, and somehow it stuck harder than a lot of live-action bad guys.

So I searched. Who else had noticed. I only found two kinds of response.

The big pile is people making a joke. They laugh. They forget about it. Reddit, Stack Exchange, the usual threads. Same three letters, funny coincidence, move on.

The other thing that came up, and I did not expect this to be the find, was [Pontil's article — What is a master control program?](https://www.pontil.com/blog/what-is-a-master-control-program-the-term-the-meanings-and-why-it-matters-now). It was almost alone in taking the technology seriously. Pro-MCP. Helpful tone. Sedative. The title is about what a master control program is, and why it matters now. *Tron* is in it. A lot. There is a section called "The Tron detour." The page walks right up to the rhyme and then fails to make the real connection — almost deliberately. That is my assumption. It retires the childhood villain as a vocabulary mix-up and sends you back to your own project. Unique in favor of the pipe. Unique in putting you to sleep about what the name means.

A third category should exist. More than one voice saying: this is actually happening. It is real. It is not a joke. The movie might have gotten us ready for it. I did not find that pile. I do not think those people are imaginary. I think they are heavily suppressed. The proof is what happens when anyone tries to cross the line from joke to real — the room turns, the reputation takes the hit, the topic and debate get neglected and abandoned. *Faucification* is likely. So this page is the only article I found that says yes, the connection is real and on purpose.

## What Pontil's page tells you to do

Walking through that Pontil article — [What is a master control program?](https://www.pontil.com/blog/what-is-a-master-control-program-the-term-the-meanings-and-why-it-matters-now).

Their page opens by guessing why you searched. You probably had one of three things in mind: the 1982 film, an old operating system, or the new acronym on AI agents. Then it says those three are not the same thing. They share three letters and almost nothing else.

That is their first move. You showed up because a childhood villain and a 2024 protocol share initials, and Pontil sorts you into someone confused about vocabulary. The connection you felt gets refiled as a mix-up.

Then Pontil walks through the old mainframe meaning. Fair as history. Burroughs had a Master Control Program long before Disney. The film borrowed a live industry name. Most people who Google the phrase have never run one of those machines. They have seen *Tron*. Their article grants that the film made the phrase feel menacing, then parks the menace under labels like cultural memory, a detour, an aesthetic.

Then comes the sentence that does the real work on their page. If you have seen *Tron*, you imagine something that orchestrates, controls, and decides. That is almost the opposite of what MCP means now, Pontil says. The film's MCP is a controller. The protocol called MCP is a contract between two parties that does not control anything.

So the feeling that the name came back is treated as the mistake. A developer who remembers the villain is told they are imagining the wrong object. The protocol does not control anything. Pontil never asks who would want a universal pipe into every tool, every file, every shop, every account. It never asks why a thing with that job waited until November 2024. It never asks whether the pipe could be used for a bad purpose. A lot of people already worry about AI. That worry gets treated as a myth, or as politics. Their article does not even grant it that much. It removes the worry by changing the subject to a wire format.

![AI takeover and predictive programming — decades of dark AI fiction, including Tron](https://paradigmthreat.net/science/ai/ai-takeover.png)

Then it tells you what the protocol does in the narrow sense. An agent can discover tools and call them. The message format is standardized. The protocol does not run the tools. It does not log you in. It does not pick which tool to call. The model decides. The server exposes. The closest analogy on the page is HTTP. Browsers and servers speak it. The protocol does not tell you how to build either one.

That description can be true and still be the look-away. HTTP changed who could publish and who could be reached. A pipe that lets one model call every procedure on every system you connect is the most important thing to happen to software since software was invented. I do not think that is an overstatement. Plenty of people who watched this rollout would grant the scale even if they hate my conclusion. The page's answer to that scale is "that's it." Three jobs. Nothing else. You are being talked down.

Then it explains why mixing up the two names is an architecture problem, in a neat list. First, your team might treat the server as the boss and make a mess. Second, you might think the protocol gives you production guarantees it does not give. Third, a category of gateways showed up, and the real work is still generating tools that stay current. Every point is about shipping a cleaner product. None of them is about a public that was not given this pipe for decades. None of them is about a hold.

## The hold and the dam

The only reason to hold a protocol like this back is to roll it out during a takeover. If people had it, they would install it on their own servers in a frenzy to get on board with the new economy. There is no other reason. You do not sit on the ability for anyone to call anyone else's procedures from their own client, then pretend the delay was because the idea had not occurred to anyone until a language model arrived.

The idea was old. Procedure contracts showed up in the 1990s under other names: OLE, CORBA, WSDL, later OpenAPI. What never arrived was an ordinary citizen pipe, something a person could own and understand without joining a vendor church. That missing pipe is the subject of [The Internet That Should Have Been](https://paradigmthreat.net/science/computing/the-internet-that-should-have-been.md). The Pontil page never touches it. It tells you the acronym has been "effectively repurposed," as if the old meaning lost a trademark dispute.

Anthropic published the Model Context Protocol on [25 November 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-context-protocol), twenty days after the election, before inauguration. On my map that date is the dam bursting. Held-back software is a dam. NSA-side allies are the pressure. The CIA side spends every trick on delay. The burst is when those tricks run out. The water came through Anthropic's post. Full spine: [NSA investigation — *Tron* and the 2024 name landing](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/investigations/nsa-investigation.md).

So why those letters. Why not pick something boring and forgettable once *Tron* had already called the Master Control Program out as a villain in 1982.

My conclusion is simple. Anthropic named it MCP on purpose.

Anthropic sits on the NSA-aligned side of the map for me, same lane as SpaceX, post-Twitter X, and Elon Musk. The shop that put this pipe on the wire did not stumble into three letters by accident, and they weren't being cute either. They wanted us to think about it.

The greater U.S. military sits NSA-leaning on that same map. SIGINT, homeland audit, the long pushback after Vietnam-era abuse programs got exposed. That history lives in the [NSA investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/investigations/nsa-investigation.md). The overlap is felt all the way to the top. At the tip, generals and their staffs can still be compromised. A whole force can lean one way while named officers at the tip answer to the other camp. That is how you get messy Defense fights without flipping the map.

Anthropic's military-ban fight looked like that kind of mess. The vast majority of Anthropic's engineering staff wanted to keep Claude in use with the military. Operational leadership inside the U.S. Army wanted the same thing. They were already using it and wanted to keep using it. What landed as the final ban does not read like a rational policy that rose from those rooms. It reads like a forced, toxic compromise driven through a very few people in leadership positions by deep-state influences. 

That is the most common repeating Deep State pattern: the bulk of a shop or a force leans one way, and a thin layer at the tip gets leveraged until the whole org is made to perform the opposite. Core names, including CEO Dario Amodei and the co-founding safety researchers, held a hard public line against unrestricted military use while the company tore. Workforce rifts. Tactical debates. Security-risk language that felt like a backup knife aimed at the company itself. Hijack pressure on an NSA-aligned shop. Not proof the shop was CIA-native from birth. Trump's focus on SI and SI Force fits as a reaction on the same board. Anticipated. It does not rewrite who Anthropic always was.

If Anthropic was always on that side of the map, the letters were chosen. *Tron* already put MCP in every kid's head as the thing that wants the run of the system. Releasing the universal tool pipe under those same letters forces the thought during the rollout. That's predictive programming! You are supposed to feel the rhyme, even while a Pontil page tells builders the rhyme is a vocabulary error. The childhood villain comes back at the exact moment the pipe arrives.

The other camp does not cleanly stop unfinished plans. Older plans grow economies: vendors, habits, whole careers. The usual move is to add a new plan rather than kill the old one. Once the NSA lane planted MCP on the wire, renaming to something sleepy would have meant accepting the 1982 branding loss in public. Keeping the letters, and punishing anyone who says the join out loud, is cheaper on their chart.

## Faucification

I have tried saying this to people at work and to strangers who care enough to argue. Model Context Protocol is the Master Control Program name coming back for real. The room changes. Some laugh, then stop. Some decide I am unreliable, manic, a little dangerous. I have seen that face before on other topics. This one triggers it clean.

I call that **faucifying**, a word I made up. Soft voice, gentle grin, soft chuckle. The speaker acts like you have done zero research and need a patient walk-through of things anyone can Google, as if your join were a native superstition and they were talking to a child. "Let me just walk you through the evidence." "Here's a quick study for you." "It's actually very simple."

## Focus on yourself, and plug in

Then Pontil explains how the company fits. Pontil is not a master control program. Pontil is not the Model Context Protocol. Pontil is the layer that solves the problem the protocol does not solve: tools that work, stay current, and run against your real product. If that is where your agent project is stuck, a demo is the fastest way to see how it works.

So the search that started with a villain ends with a demo. The reader they want is a builder with a project. The feeling they want is personal: how can this serve you, what layer matters for your stack. Be specific when you say the letters, the page says, because the acronym is doing too much work. "The kindest thing you can do for your reader is name which MCP you mean, every time." The *Tron* association will outlive both meanings. They call that kindness. It reads like a reminder not to feel what the film made people feel.

The more interesting question, once the terminology is straight, is what layer of the agent stack actually matters for your project. The protocol gets the attention. The tools layer is where the work is.

That is the close they wanted. Look at your own work. Plug your own stuff in. Get on the next economic wave as fast as you can. The people who do not plug in get cut out. They are left worrying about themselves. And nobody is left looking at the only question that mattered once the childhood name came back: whether the MCP really did take over the earth.

They held this protocol back this whole time. They do not want people getting their hands on it as a thing they own and understand. They want the look-away, which is what the article did, and they want the plug-in, which is what the article also did. Do not worry about the name. Focus on yourself. Hurry.

## They had to use a robot

Here is the part I cannot get over. That page does not read like a person who saw the connection and decided, in their own voice, to argue with it. There is no name on it. It guesses why you searched. It previews the five sections ahead. It says "this matters because." It says "the defining feature." It gives you the short answer and then the longer answer. It says "that's it" and then a stack of what the protocol does not do. First, second, third. The same distinction, over and over — opening, film section, comparison, list, close. "This is worth naming carefully." "The kindest thing you can do for your reader." No scene. No anger. No awe at the coincidence the page exists to retire.

That is a robot. A language model wrote that voice. Nobody who sat with the childhood film and November 2024 in the same room would have kept the real connection off the page that cleanly.

My conclusion is they could not find a person to write the article they wanted. A human who could see the movie and November 2024 would not keep the connection off the page the way the planners needed. A human who understands the technology, asked to explain it to a normal reader, teaches. They let the reader get a grasp. They would say a childhood villain just got his name on the pipe that can run everyone else's software, it was held back until now, and you should know what that means before you plug your life into it. This page is the opposite of that. Software can write the explanation of itself now, and the explanation it was given is look away.

A handful of unnamed people get to ship that kind of page — the robot look-away — and then the same mood travels into articles, movies, games, and everyone else downstream, plugging in or cut out and worrying about themselves. This one Pontil post is what the *look-away* looks like when it has to be manufactured by a robot because a person would have flinched.

## They lost the AI battle — so they want the federal switch

The AI fight has gone badly for the camp that wanted one stack, one kill switch, one silent rollout. Even with the Anthropic military ban in the way, the NSA-aligned side is not forced onto a single company or a single model. They still have competition. They still have resources. SpaceX, xAI, Cursor, open weights, sovereign builds — the ban itself will push more American shops to build products that do not depend on a foreign-facing vendor that wants a wartime off switch. That is not a loss of the war. That is the war turning into innovation.

So what does the other side still have when the technical plan will not stay on the rails. The same catch-all they have reached for through history: more federalization. Pass a law that says nobody gets to do the new thing unless Washington says okay. Then, slowly, weed back into every household and turn off the gift. Schumer’s [SAFE Innovation Framework](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/schumer_ai_framework.pdf) was the Senate Democrat template for putting federal hands on the stack years ago — security, accountability, “foundations,” explainability, innovation under one congressional roof. In September 2026, at a [DCCC fundraiser](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/obama-says-democrats-clear-plan-ai-policy-rcna597555), Barack Obama told Democrats AI should be a central agenda. If he were running for president, he said, he would have a very clear plan on safety, kids, and job displacement. He told Hakeem Jeffries that once Jeffries was speaker, Democrats should build a public framework around it. Party figures were already pressing for an emergency session on AI while the industry argued about slowdowns. Sit with that for a midterm season. Not borders. Not wages. Not war. The former face of the party putting machine governance near the center of what the party should run on, after the competitive AI lane already broke their preferred timing.

That is how you know they are losing the real fight. When the pipe is open and the models multiply, the leftover move is the law that says the gift only runs with their permission. I saw the icon on FanEdit. I am not looking away. And I do not consent to them federalizing the off switch after they failed to own the rollout.
