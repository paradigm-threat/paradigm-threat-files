# The Jedi Who Stare at Goats

Ronson’s joke title hid a real Jedi program — *Star Wars* already showed it

![The Jedi Who Stare at Goats — program stack, not-being-seen, 1994–95 timeline](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/hollywood/investigations/men-who-stare-at-goats-rv-satire-predictive-programming-investigation.png)

You have to be really bored to pick up a book or watch a movie called *Men Who Stare at Goats*. The title itself is a filter. The whole project seems designed so the majority looks away at first glance.

I only ended up watching the film because it came on automatically on Prime. Same experience most people had: weird quirky movie, interesting because Clooney is in it acting strange — and then a major revelation. There are apparently real people on this planet who think they were Jedi Knights trained by the U.S. government, especially around the time *Star Wars* came out.

That was an extremely hard disclosure to swallow. I really couldn’t believe it. I couldn’t believe someone would make a movie like that, lend it any credibility, and suggest it ties back to real testimony from U.S. soldiers and other people. I started this investigation because I wanted to see the truth of the movie and the book. What showed up pretty quickly is that both were designed to make people look away — and to mock the people who were originally part of this program.

Some of them might still be alive, living in cities somewhere. If they tried to tell anyone about the program they were in, they would look a lot like Clooney looks in the movie: absolutely crazy, without a shred of proof.

> **TL;DR:** The goats title and Clooney farce were built so most people never look. Behind them sat a real U.S. program that trained gifted people — often children — as Jedi-class operators on spy missions inside an MKULTRA-style bubble. Ronson mangled visibility, turned remote viewing into a nose-bang gag, and aimed the book’s ending at already-known Abu Ghraib instead of the declassified psychic material. That is controlled opposition, and it damaged the cadre. The French-hoax / Soviet-copycat origin fable is more insulting misdirection — as if whole governments only noticed psychic warfare after a magazine fake. Episode IV and V already showed the real physics. Everything in *Star Wars*, adjusted for a few costumes, is true on that map. Prequels and the Disney sequels then ruined the Force — midichlorians, resurrection lab plots, fatal remote projection, object-warp, cloning that strips spirituality from ability — containment, not bad casting. Later comedy kept survivors looking crazy until they die. Those agents were abuse victims who often never understood the agenda; they still deserve redemption while alive.

## The joke title and the real stack

Jon Ronson’s 2004 book is sold as nonfiction. The same year Channel 4 aired *Crazy Rulers of the World*, a companion documentary series — episode one shares the goats title. That is the nonfiction disclosure window: book plus television, before Hollywood finished converting the title into a Clooney farce. The 2009 film is sold as comedy. Together the book-and-film pair still do the same job: make psychic soldiers sound like a joke you stumbled into by accident.

The underlying stack is not a joke. By the early 1970s the U.S. intelligence lane already had remote-viewing work under names like SCANATE and SRI contracts. Army and DIA collection later ran under GRILL FLAME, CENTER LANE, SUN STREAK, and STAR GATE. MKULTRA sits earlier as the CIA mind-control research history everyone has heard of — drugs, hypnosis, behavioral control; the [CIA Reading Room still hosts the program paper trail](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/06760269). Jim Channon’s First Earth Battalion manual (late 1970s) pushed warrior-monk redesign language. Special Forces mind-body training in the 1980s shows up in secondary histories as Jedi Warrior / Jedi Project. Those lanes are adjacent and often conflated in pop culture. They are still real program names and real people — not Clooney improvisation.

Ronson’s own trail starts with **Uri Geller**. In October 2001 he meets Geller on a London rooftop; Geller’s long-standing claim is that he worked as a psychic for U.S. intelligence in the early 1970s. Inside the Army psychic culture Ronson documents, commanders run spoon-bending parties and metal psychokinesis demos as part of the same human-potential stack as walls and goat lore. I have no reason to deny that ability. It has been well reported for decades — even if later media spent just as much energy “debunking” it. Reporting and ridicule can both be true in public; neither erases why brass ordered officers to try.

The public wind-down story for the Fort Meade remote-viewing line lands in **1995** (AIR / STAR GATE closure narrative). About **eleven months earlier**, theaters got ***Stargate* (1994)** — the **program name on the marquee**, a **dying alien** master killed by the team, portal spectacle. On the current [Hollywood two-camp read](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/hollywood/investigations/hollywood-two-camp-war-investigation.md#thread-p--stargate-1994-film-nsa-aligned--program-name-dying-alien-1995-close) that 1994 title is **NSA-aligned**: antibody naming of STAR GATE, then the official dump and close. Timing matters: **film exposes/names in Oct 1994, program publicly closed in Sept 1995**, then leftover operators privatize, consult, and get mocked. The goats comedy in 2009 is a later harden — own the weird name, move the culture on.

Ronson interviewed that world and still got critical codes wrong. Visibility is the cleanest example. The operators were not talking about vanishing from cameras. They meant not being seen: not being noticed, not being identified, walking among people without the observer treating you as present. Ronson’s own sources say that adaptation out loud. He still lets the culture hear “invisibility” as a magic trick that failed. He screws that one up even while half-agreeing with the correct reading.

Walking through walls is worse. Moving through walls was code for remote viewing — the functionality that actually featured in the declassified programs. Framing that code as a general crashing his nose into drywall and failing is an extreme violation of the duty to tell the truth from the documents and testimony. For that reason Ronson pins as a controlled opposition agent. No doubt about it. Worse still: the declassified psychic material was never even the main punchline of his own book.

Another insult sits in the origin story itself. In the film — and in the wider Cold War folklore the goats product leans on — a general-class voice suggests the Russians only started psychic-spy research because they thought America was already doing it, after reading a **French magazine hoax** (the old *Nautilus* telepathy press lane). The implication is that the whole international stack happened by accident: paranoia, a fake article, copycat fear. That is clear containment and misdirection. It is rather insulting. It makes everyone in all the governments involved look very stupid — as if they could not detect this kind of capability in their own literature, their own people, or their own labs without having to spy on the enemy or get faked out by a magazine. Real programs do not bootstrap from a joke clip alone. The French-hoax fable is there so the audience can file psychic warfare under “Cold War silliness” and stop asking who trained whom on purpose.

Apparently the book is “about” Abu Ghraib. He fails, inside his own pages, to honestly explain why Abu Ghraib would have anything to do with the Jedi program or remote viewing. The First Earth Battalion loudspeaker hobbyhorse is not the origin of sexual humiliation in war. That abuse has a long institutional history. It did not start in the 1970s New Age Army manuals.

## What the comedy subtracted

Ronson’s book does not end on goat jokes. Late chapters dig into **Frank Olson** and **MKULTRA**. Olson was a Fort Detrick scientist tied to CIA work (Artichoke / MKULTRA lane). In 1953 he went out a New York hotel window after an LSD dosing at a Deep Creek Lodge retreat — the official story sold for decades as bad-trip suicide. His son Eric spent a lifetime arguing homicide and silence: that Frank had seen terminal “expendable” experiments in Europe and become a security risk. The CIA’s own later admissions about dosing, brothels, and Gottlieb’s shop are public; Eric’s murder thesis remains contested in press, but Ronson treats the Olson house, the exhumation, and the narrow gap between poisoners and psychic administrators as load-bearing. Sidney Gottlieb — the man tied to Olson’s dosing — also shows up in the psychic-spy administration history. Light side and dark side of the same intelligence culture sit one office apart. The 2009 film largely **subtracts** this spine. You get warm war comedy instead of a citizen’s autopsy of MKULTRA. That cut matters. Without it, people keep MKULTRA as a rumor word and never have to put a murder next to the Jedi ladder. Deeper program and disclosure context sits in the [RV / MKULTRA two-way investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/cia-reading-room-rv-two-way-iran-revival-investigation.md).

Ronson also follows the **privatization** of the psychic unit after the mid-1990s wind-down — retired viewers as consultants, rival schools, cut-throat civilian market. In that chapter he draws a line toward **Heaven’s Gate**: the 1997 mass suicide, the “Away Team” costumes, Hale-Bopp closure language. Critics argue over how tight that adjacency is. The book still puts it on the page. After the uniform program breaks up, the same human-potential and “exit” talk can slide into a cult and end in disaster. The comedy skips that part. It is easy to laugh at Clooney in goggles. It is harder to laugh at discarded operators and Heaven’s Gate.

## Abu Ghraib was strategy, not goat lore

The reason for Abu Ghraib was not Barney the Purple Dinosaur and it was not Jim Channon’s warrior-monk fax. Hard-core Muslim “terrorists” were sexually humiliated because a Christian-majority power was willing to do that in an ideological fight — not because the war’s logistics required it, and not because a few trailer-park MPs invented decadence on a night shift.

Humiliation was a strategy. Very likely NSA-aligned. Illegal in spirit, and sitting in a zone where ordinary law did not really cover the method. It was the same family of practice the CIA had refined for a long time: sexual humiliation proved most effective for breaking certain prisoners. Versions of that doctrine repeated at scale in Egypt and other places where the Muslim Brotherhood was put down. Agree with it or not — that is the history, and that is the reason.

Ronson’s book aims most of the anger at Abu Ghraib — a scandal everyone already knew in 2004. The rare stuff he actually had (Stubblebine, Project Jedi, goat lab, the coded abilities) gets used as warm-up for that ending. He did not need to break Abu Ghraib. That is the problem: he flipped the story. Chapter map and the counter-read are in the [goats / Jedi investigation](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-jedi-who-stare-at-goats).

The ideological arc did not end in a Baghdad prison. It runs toward the moment in 2026 when America smashed Iran’s military and killed the ayatollah leader. Abu Ghraib was one theater in a longer holy-war posture. It was never the afterbirth of men staring at goats.

## Promote first, contain leftovers later

*Star Wars* came out in the same era as this stack. That film was geared toward making people believe these powers were real, even if misunderstood. If the goal in 1977 had been mockery, they would have shipped a 2009-style comedy then — not a beloved space epic with Jedi shown in a noble, artistic way.

First came mass promotion. Then came the ops window: First Earth language, Project Jedi ladders, Fort Meade remote viewing. Then incomplete disclosure: Ronson’s 2004 nonfiction, which the market already read as satire because of his literal mistakes. Then containment harden: the 2009 film, so leftover real Jedi who speak out sound like crazy Clooney.

What shipped instead was a civilizational hit that makes Jedihood desirable. The comedy comes decades later — after *Stargate* (1994) already soft-contained portal and psychic-adjacent weirdness, after the 1995 STAR GATE wind-down story, after privatization and Heaven’s Gate hit the press — when the problem is leftover believers and operators, not recruitment.

Hollywood locked the goats title early. Rights were sold before Ronson finished the book. The screenwriter did not want much to do with him. The finished comedy warms the tone, renames the people, invents a buddy plot, and stamps fiction. The title card says more of this is true than you would believe — admit and inoculate in one line.

The movie made people up. Ronson’s book mostly names real operators (Stubblebine, Channon, Wheaton, Geller, Buchanan, and so on) and then misreads and mis-centers them. The film invents a cast and an Iraq road movie around that substrate. McGregor’s Bob Wilton is a fake small-town reporter writing up the story — a stand-in for Ronson, not a real person from the book. Clooney’s Cassady is a composite psychic soldier. Bridges’s Django rhymes with Channon. Spacey’s Larry Hooper — the dark-side rival — does not appear in Ronson at all. End titles admit the fiction. That is how containment hardens: keep a few real names as garnish, invent the plot so nobody has to treat the oral history as evidence.

That casting is not a wise artistic choice. McGregor is Obi-Wan in the *Star Wars* prequels. Putting him in a comedy that mocks real Jedi soldiers is a slap in the face of his own myth role. The better read is **containment typecasting**: keep the space / Jedi faces in a tight reuse pool so the same actors carry both the noble myth and the ridicule vaccine. Loyalty and message control get easier when the faces are familiar and under contract gravity.

## What Episode IV and V already showed

If the goats product teaches you to look away, *Star Wars* is the earlier disclosure. It taught a generation the skill list before the mockery hardened.

In the book, Project Jedi is told as a ladder: diet and body prep; invisibility adapted to finding a way of not being seen (“If you’re not observed, you are invisible. You only exist if someone sees you”); then kill a goat by staring — stop the heart. Stubblebine’s lane adds walls, levitation attempts, spoon-bending parties of the Geller class, cloud work, and a psychic spy unit. Map that onto the original trilogy and the rhyme is hard to miss.

Episode IV mostly gets it right. Obi-Wan’s “invisibility” works as failure of notice, not optical cloaking. He moves through Mos Eisley and the Death Star without being treated as a threat. The mind trick and the stormtrooper pass do the same job. That is exactly the adapted meaning from the real Project Jedi ladder — not being seen. Vader’s Force-choke is the lethal stare in costume. Luke destroying the Death Star without the targeting computer is intuition and Force aim under fire. Episode V adds levitation as a teaching spectacle: Yoda, the stones, the X-wing. Far-sense gets stronger too — Luke’s cave, the Luke–Leia call, Vader sensing Luke. The book’s own dark chapter covers the twin: same training stack turned toward coercion. Every serious *Star Wars* fan already felt some kind of real-life tie-in. There has to be an equivalence for what those films showed. Turns out there is.

Adjust a couple of costume facts and the basics hold. Everything you saw in *Star Wars* is actually true.

Levitation is true. The Force — the environment, the universe itself, the æther — guiding and constraining action is true. Mainstream talk calls related structure fields in vacuum; the substrate here is still named æther. The ability to be invisible and walk among people without being seen is definitely true. Not everyone can do it. It has far more to do with energy and spirituality than with physical motion. Those layers combine.

The way Obi-Wan dies and preserves his soul in the world without scattering into the Bardos is true, and it is easy to cover in Bardo mythology. Someone with strong focus can decide where the soul goes in the afterlife, and can stick around if they choose. They become far more powerful than they were while living. That power is hard to ascertain from the living side — it can sound godly — but reality has major limits, which is why spirits do not casually manipulate daily life. They mostly stay out of human affairs. It takes an extreme exception, like Obi-Wan’s, to remain and keep guiding someone. The physics behind it are real.

Jedi as a word shows up in Lucas’s papers years before the 1977 premiere. Public Army “Jedi Warrior” branding is easiest to cite in the 1980s. That floor is not a ceiling. It is exceedingly likely people in the psychic and warrior-monk lane were already told they were Jedi — or occupied that slot — before the theaters filled. Remote viewing and related work already existed before the premiere either way. The myth and the program drank from the same well.

## The real program under the joke

There was a real program to find gifted people — children, mostly — and train them as Jedi Knights in real life, then put them on missions to change the world. On those missions they thought they were heroes. Mostly they were used as spies without the political context needed to make a right choice. They lived in MKULTRA-style bubble lives: radical individual skill, thin news diet, almost no map of factions or consequences. Following orders blindly was the design.

Standard remote-viewing tradecraft already starves the viewer of meaning. You get a coordinate or a sealed envelope. You do not get the war, the coup, the hostage politics, or the legality. That can be sold as “reducing cueing.” It also means you cannot refuse on moral grounds, because you never learn what the task is for. Add false feedback, theatrical hits, and cover stories about who funds the product, and you have a sensor — not a citizen. Clooney’s Cassady is the public face of that isolation: jargon, confidence, and almost no ordinary political map.

Their actions were mostly spy actions. The dark-Jedi notion — which the goats film personifies as Hooper, and which the book’s dark half already documents — makes clear that the worst of these agents had the ability to stare at a goat or a hamster, and possibly even a person, and kill them. Of course that kind of power has limits. It had to stay in the shadows. Eventually the whole stack had to be contained so completely that nobody would even believe the powers were possible.

That is why Clooney’s face works as the vaccine. Speak the truth after 2009 and you sound like the crazy man from the comedy — not like a discarded intelligence asset asking for a hearing while still alive.

## *Star Wars* itself was contained

The notion that George Lucas alone “came up with the story” does not hold. He may have written an initial draft. Many hands touched it before final form, and it changed significantly. He then exited with little interest in the sequel, and only came back hard for the third film after the sequel’s major success. Mark Hamill himself may have been replaced after the holiday special — another contained object. Much of that special’s expansion work, including new-character energy around figures like Boba Fett, was lifted into Episode V without credit to the people who actually built those beats. Layer on layer of containment inside the project.

After the original trilogy, a long freeze kept sequels and mass cultural heat regulated mostly to models, cards, collectibles, and games — until Disney took over, which is simply more evidence of containment. Under Disney came the worst trilogy the franchise would ever see, now even discussed as something to decanonize. That is not “bad casting.” It is containment strategy taken to its end: ruin the Force on screen so it no longer rhymes with goat-stare / MKULTRA reality, and ruin the franchise with it.

The damage started earlier. Episode I already ruined the Force with midichlorians — blood-count biology instead of æther, training, and will. Episodes II and III put the Emperor on a resurrection hunt; that motive becomes the spine of Episode IX. Viewers complained about the Force being cheapened in the prequels. The signs were already there that the people steering *Star Wars* were wrecking the concept in front of the audience. Episodes VII–IX finished the job.

What the sequel reboot changed, away from the goat / MKULTRA stack Episodes IV–V had already shown:

- Episode VII reinforces **mind reading** — a true ability, still on the real ladder.
- Episode VII also shows a Force-user who can **stop a blaster bolt in mid-air** and hold it trembling for a long beat. That is comic-book / video-game stasis, not the quiet lethal stare or not-being-seen skill the original trilogy made feel almost real. It breaks the faith many viewers had that the Force obeyed some consistent, almost human-scale rules.
- Across the sequel trilogy, **remote Force appearing / projection** becomes a feat that can **kill the projector** (Luke’s Crait projection in Episode VIII). Real far-presence and far-sense were never sold that way in the program lore.
- Episode IX lets Force users **transfer or warp objects across locations** through a dyad link — teleport-adjacent stage magic, not remote viewing or goat-stare will.
- Episode IX reveals Jedi can be **cloned**, which detaches ability from spirituality, soul, and lived training — the opposite of æther, Bardo, and focus, and a clean cut away from MKULTRA-bubble operators as abused people.

That faith-break is not only a Paradigm Threat read. Fans and commentators already said the same thing in public, without needing the goats frame. Midichlorians were widely attacked for turning a spiritual Force into blood-count biology — even J.J. Abrams later told IGN he still preferred Obi-Wan’s “surrounds us / binds us” faith language over the microbe count ([IGN, 2019](https://www.ign.com/articles/2019/12/12/jj-abrams-and-daisy-ridley-on-why-there-are-no-midichlorians-in-the-star-wars-sequels)). Sci-fi readers called Kylo’s bolt-freeze nonsense even inside *Star Wars* physics — a plasma bolt that hangs, then resumes its old path — and noted the same stasis trick already lived in video games like Knights of the Old Republic ([Sci-Fi Stack Exchange](https://scifi.stackexchange.com/questions/125167/was-kylo-rens-blaster-bolt-freezing-really-force-telekinesis); [ability history thread](https://scifi.stackexchange.com/questions/110994/have-kylo-rens-force-abilities-been-shown-before)). After *The Last Jedi*, Business Insider summarized the room: between Luke’s projection and Leia’s space float, it seemed Lucasfilm was “just making up new rules for the Force” ([Business Insider, 2018](https://www.businessinsider.com/star-wars-the-last-jedi-how-luke-skywalker-used-force-projection-2018-1)). Unrealistic spectacle replaces the OT’s believable mysticism. That is how you ruin faith in the Force — and with it, any residual rhyme with real psychic tradecraft.

Promote first (1977). Contain leftovers with comedy (2009). Then scrub the myth OS itself so the Force no longer points at the real stack. That is the arc.

The agents trained in that program were victims of massive abuse — often for most of their lives. Many never understood the agenda behind crimes they may have committed while living inside the bubble. They still deserve a chance at redemption while they are alive. The goats book, the Clooney farce, and the later Force-ruin films were never built to give them that chance. They were built so the public would laugh first, then forget what the Force used to mean, and never ask second.
