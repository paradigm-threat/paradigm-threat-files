# Thrown Off the Train

Soft deletes, encyclopedia erasure, and the word that cannot be spoken

![Thrown Off the Train — cast from the mainstream narrative car; predictive programming papers fall into deleted/forgotten](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/censorship/thrown-off-the-train.jpg)

> **TL;DR:** Silent removal of posts and film listings is worse than a named ban — deniable, scalable, almost invisible unless people compare notes. “Predictive programming” is blocked because it implies conspiracy, and conspiracy has been a kill-word since the JFK era. Wikipedia deleted the dedicated page in 2015 after framing the topic as fringe first; academia and fact-checks mostly study believers, not the mechanism. FanEdit and peer hobby gates enforce Hollywood as sacred. That panic traces to Reformation grammar and the Cathar burn. Trump is a modern antibody who could not name the term and instead burst the dam — speech and agency so others can investigate until objective publication becomes possible. What does not match the programmed story is thrown off the train and forgotten.

A clean ban at least leaves a scar you can point at. Soft erase does not. A comment vanishes. A listing vanishes. A page that used to define a term is gone. Staff say they are following rules. The rules rearrange. The person who named the pattern looks unstable. Everyone else keeps scrolling. That is how you remove a vocabulary from public life without admitting you removed it.

## Soft erase beats a ban

![The shadow ban is worse — full ban is a visible rejection; shadow ban is silent erasure that looks like compliance](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/censorship/thrown-off-the-train-shadow-ban.jpg)

A formal ban creates a grievance event. Soft erase creates nothing. Posts and movies disappear without a notice that says you were banned. The venue still looks open. The hobbyist still has an account. The encyclopedia still has a search box. What left is the feeling that the subject never belonged there.

That design scales. You can run it across a hundred thousand users in a day. If those users do not know each other, none of them sees a trend. They each experience a local glitch, a local mod, a local “content policy.” Only people who talk to each other can map the same trigger words appearing in different rooms. That is why soft erase is more effective than a public purge. It is also why it is more dishonest.

## Framing the debate before it starts

Wikipedia, universities, and fact-check desks have a habit of deciding what a topic is allowed to mean before anyone may discuss it. On [25 April 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_deletion/Predictive_programming), editor **jps** opened an Articles for Deletion discussion on *Predictive programming*. The nomination already called the idea material from YouTube and from “David Icke or Alex Jones cults,” and said academics who study conspiracy theorists would have to go first. Soft dissent inside the delete pile still admitted the subject was real. One editor said the topic was **probably notable**. Another said a rewrite might work if two or three non-fringe sources appeared. [Short Brigade Harvester Boris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_deletion/Predictive_programming) voted **regretful delete** and wrote that predictive programming was very widely discussed and that it was regrettable Wikipedia could not have an article on such a well-known topic. Nobody voted Keep. The close on **2 May 2015** was delete. In 2020 a recreation titled *9/11 Predictive Programming* was [speedily deleted as a recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_deletion/9/11_Predictive_Programming). What remains is a short blurb under [List of conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_conspiracy_theories).

The same framing shows up wherever the phrase is “allowed.” Reuters debunks, psychology blogs explain why the idea is implausible, security shops study far-right users who use the phrase, and even a [2021 law-semiotics paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8043095/) that puts the words in the title still attributes the term to conspiracy theorists. That is not objective literature on a media-conditioning mechanism. That is literature that only gets published if the phenomenon is already filed as fringe belief. So it is fair to say there is effectively **no published academic study of predictive programming as a real practice** — only study of people who believe in it. The topic is forbidden. Permission to speak requires the fringe stamp first.

Alan Watt’s talks, John Carleton’s catalogs, Wikispooks, and independent books such as Valentini’s *Imagining 9/11* still exist outside that lane. They do not mint encyclopedia notability under rules that reject them by class. The catch-22 was stated out loud on the AfD page and then enforced.

## Conspiracy is the kill-word

Predictive programming is not banned because two ordinary English words are magic. It is banned because those words imply an arranged plan in culture — and that implication is filed under **conspiracy**. Since the [JFK era](https://paradigmthreat.net/events/jfk/jfk-was-never-assassinated.md), “conspiracy” and “conspiracy theory” have been treated as disqualifying labels. Once the label sticks, the content does not get a hearing. The hearing is over before it starts.

That is why a remote subject — a scene in a game, a symbol in a cut, a date rhyme in a passport — can still trigger the same removal machine. The challenge is never only about the scene. It hits faith in the establishment that produced the scene. God or Hollywood. Challenge that faith and you get a Reformation-style physical reaction: reject, ridicule, remove. Not an open investigation. A panic.

## Who is actually fringe

Forbidding two words, banishing the people who use them, and treating the possibility that the words have weight as unspeakable — that is fringe behavior. Asking questions is not fringe. Staying open-minded is not fringe. Challenging other people is not fringe.

Intro psychology classes still teach that personality and behavior can be read — that human motives are not a black box forever beyond analysis. People still live under societized instructions. They conform. They are rarely challenged. When someone finally suggests that a reaction can be deduced from past motives and training, the usual response is immediate reject-and-remove. That is not careful skepticism. That is a body that has never been pressed that way. The person who brought the challenge is treated as the problem so the faith never has to move.
