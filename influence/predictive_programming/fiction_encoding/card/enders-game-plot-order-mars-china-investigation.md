## Author’s originating thesis

Recorded from the request of 2026-09-28:

> Like the C. S. Lewis book, *Ender’s Game* encodes a reasoning behind why a planet would be destroyed. Everything is changed up and another planet is destroyed, not Earth. The story lines up with what happened with Mars. It is out of order. It starts with a scout from the alien worlds coming to Earth and gassing everyone in China. China in the late eighteenth or early nineteenth century would be the perfect territory, because the Great Wall would contain the gas so it could be eliminated later and escapees stopped. China was reset historically and organizationally in the nineteenth century by the British. They seem to have a direct connection with Mars, because *War of the Worlds* was also a real story. China may have been mass gassed, and survivors brought in under a new religion and a new-style government, with the belief that they were a separate ancient empire, cutting all ties to the Russian Horde.
>
> The scout ship is destroyed, and they send another. That contradiction is the giveaway. The author could not find a good plot point because the truth is the same event explained differently. The scout succeeded. It did not matter if it was destroyed. That is a heroic twist to invent a resistance. Instead of sending another scout to continue the gassing, the enemy sends their entire fleet, which makes no sense. That is another contradiction, portraying a resistance moment when the humans sabotage or kill the queen and the whole fleet stops following orders and the threat ends. A stupid point, which cannot be explained except that the invasion had to end. The aliens had to go back to their planet, and the story had to continue with us attacking them.
>
> The true beginning of the history is a planet anticipating another planet as a threat, though they are far apart and there is a relativistic time barrier, and though the other planet is neutralized and has no way to invade. The contradiction encodes the Pugachev–Peter III rebellion. It freezes the enemy fleet, which in history is the medieval armies on Earth in the eighteenth century. Under the deep state and monotheism they suddenly shatter. They no longer follow orders, because Peter III claimed to be the son of Christ and ruined the entire narrative. The queen of the fleet getting sabotaged is that moment. The fleet leaves.
>
> The story then puts the war on a child living in virtual reality, which is very likely what happened on Mars. It backfired when the kid used maximum attack to destroy the entire rebellion, the kingdom, and everything associated with the Russian Horde. Ender’s name comes from ending history as we know it, ending two planets. His attack destroyed the human fleet in the book, which is code for destroying Mars. Mars was never intended to be destroyed. The book explains this with yet another contradiction: the generals and the soldiers cried for joy when their own fleet was about to be destroyed in order to take out the enemy. The real tragedy is that the decision was left to a child, and that the generals and the soldiers thought it was not a child but an advanced artificial intelligence who had made an honorable and sound choice.
>
> Lewis’s encoding of the same history would be second-hand and a far weaker pattern. Look for whether any pattern is there, and what influenced him.

## Saga timeline

In-story order. The gassing is not in the first book. *Ender’s Game* (1977 story, 1985 novel) only **tells** the queen-ship as backstory, then shows the child destroying the planet. China is a 2013 prequel, written with Aaron Johnston.

| In-story | What happens | Book | Published |
|----------|----------------|------|-----------|
| 1 | Scout found far from Earth | *Earth Unaware* | 2012, with Johnston |
| 2 | Landers, **gas**, southeast China | *Earth Afire* | 2013, with Johnston |
| 3 | Scout ship stopped | *Earth Awakens* | 2014, with Johnston |
| 4 | Main fleet arrives anyway. Queen’s ship destroyed, fleet goes dead | Summarized inside *Ender’s Game*. Dramatized in *The Swarm* (2016) and *The Hive* (2019). Last volume, *The Queens*, not published as of 2025 | Backstory: **1977 / 1985**. Scene-work: 2016– |
| 5 | Child, simulation, own fleet spent, enemy planet destroyed | *Ender’s Game* | **1977 / 1985** |

After that, still in-story: *Ender in Exile* (2008) sits in the gap, then *Speaker for the Dead* (1986), *Xenocide* (1991), *Children of the Mind* (1996). Earth’s politics, parallel to the war and just after it: *Ender’s Shadow* (1999), *Shadow of the Hegemon* (2001), *Shadow Puppets* (2002), *Shadow of the Giant* (2005), *Shadows in Flight* (2012), *The Last Shadow* (2021).

Publication order of the five beats above runs **5 and 4 first** (1977), then **1–3** (2012–2014), then the unfinished dramatization of **4** (2016–).

## The long gap, and the Wells rhyme

**Author guess (2026-09-28):** the empty years between the first *Ender’s Game* and the backfill are the same shape as *The War of the Worlds* and the estate sequel. The later book has to explain why the aliens were such a threat if they could be stopped so easily. Instead of inventing a new war, the writer cites the older encoded history. That citation looks more like science fiction than the first book did.

| | First telling | The easy stop | The gap | The backfill |
|--|----------------|---------------|---------|----------------|
| **Card** | *Ender’s Game*, 1977 story, 1985 novel. The queen-ship is a paragraph of backstory. The child and the spent fleet are the story. | Mazer hits one ship and the whole invasion dies. A species that falls over when one queen dies is a weak reason to raise a generation of children and burn a planet. | Twenty-seven years from the story to *Earth Unaware* (2012). Thirty-six from the novel to the gas. | Aaron Johnston with Card. *Earth Unaware* (2012) finds the scout. *Earth Afire* (2013) puts the gas on southeast China. The first contact is a massacre, not a fleet that dies of a lucky shot. |
| **Wells** | *The War of the Worlds*, 1898. Cylinders, heat-ray, England. | The invaders die of bacteria. The war ends because Earth is already poisonous to them. | One hundred and nineteen years. | *The Massacre of Mankind*, Stephen Baxter, 19 January 2017, **authorised by the H.G. Wells Estate**. Not a descendant writing from a family desk. A second landing, fifty cylinders, no nineteen-hour wait, the microbes do not end it, Jovians called in by a changed sigil. The gas is the Card backfill’s citation of a mass attack from another planet. Baxter’s citation is a return wave that the bacteria cannot finish. Both later books add machinery the first book did not need (defoliant troops and a light-speed fleet; sigils, a buried city, a space gun). The author’s reading of that extra machinery is that it files the older history under a louder kind of fiction. The file on Baxter already notes that the sequel escalates the kinetic invasion and leaves the quieter questions alone. This section adds the shared calendar: a first book in which the threat collapses, a long silence, a twenty-first-century continuation that restores the threat by telling an earlier or a second blow.

**Working conclusion:** the two gaps are **L1**. The shared job of the later books — explain a threat that the first book had already shown how to stop — is **L3**. Johnston or Baxter holding a record of the eighteenth-century gassing, and choosing to cite it rather than invent, is **L4**.

## Which book contains which beat

The 1985 novel does not open on China. The China gassing is a **prequel**, written twenty-eight years later.

| Beat | Where it actually sits | In-universe order | Publication order |
|------|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Scout, three landers, **southeast China**, yellow defoliant gas, Chinese government refuses outside help | *Earth Afire* (Card and **Aaron Johnston**, 4 June 2013), second book of the First Formic War | First | 2013, after the child story was already famous |
| One lander destroyed; the scout ship boarded | Same book, continued in *Earth Awakens* (2014) | First war | 2013–2014 |
| One ship destroyed and the **whole fleet goes dead** | *Ender’s Game* (story 1977, novel 1985): **Mazer Rackham** hits the queen’s ship on the invasion the novel treats as already won | Taught as backstory inside the child’s book | 1977, the original encoding |
| A world far away is still an existential threat across a **time lag**, so a preemptive attack is launched | The International Fleet’s reason for Battle School and the “Third Invasion” | After the queen-ship victory | 1977 / 1985 |
| A **child** in a simulation orders the maximum strike, spends **his own fleet**, cracks the enemy planet; the command weeps and cheers; he learns it was real | *Ender’s Game* ending | Last | First thing Card published |

Card’s in-universe patch for “we killed the scout, why is a fleet still coming” is the **light-speed gap**: a fleet already launched cannot be recalled. That is the explanation the author reads as the patch. It is on the page. It is not an absence of a reason. The author’s claim is that the reason is doing the work of hiding a different sequence.

The landing in *Earth Afire* is **southeast China**, not the northern marches. The gas in that book is a **defoliant** misted by troops, killing plants and, in the war’s course, people. The Chinese state **refuses outside help**, which closes the territory politically. The Great Wall is not the reason the novel gives.

## The contradictions, in the author’s order

### 1. The scout that gasses China

**On the page (2013):** three landers come down in the southeast. Gas strips the region. China will not let the world in. A lander is nuked from underneath. A boy, Bingwen, is in the sequence with Mazer. Heroic sabotage is the climax.

**Author’s history:** the scout **succeeds**. Destruction of the ship is the resistance scene added so the landing can look repelled. The real selection of China is geographic and political: a large population, a state that can be sealed, a wall the author reads as a barrier to hold gas until it can be cleared and to catch people leaving. Survivors are then organized by an outside power into a people who believe they were always a separate ancient empire, and the older tie to the **Rus-Horde** is cut. The British nineteenth-century reorganization of Qing China is the documented institutional layer the author puts under that story. *War of the Worlds* is, on this site, the same Mars war told as an invasion of England. Detail and the open historical test: [China gassing](/history/chronology/investigations/china-wall-gassing-late-18th-early-19th-investigation.md).

### 2. The queen dies and the fleet stops

**On the page (1985):** the buggers have no independent minds. Kill the queen’s ship and every vessel stops. Mazer is the man who did it. The invasion ends because the biology requires it to end.

**Author’s history:** medieval armies under one religious-political order **stop following orders** when **Peter III**, remembered by the Romanov record as the impostor Pugachev, claims to be the **son of Christ** and breaks the monotheistic story those armies were obeying. The site’s statement of that claim is [MudFlood — why the event happened](/history/mudflood/page.md): in 1774 he was in a position to claim the Tartarian empire, and Mars would not allow the Reformation project to be smashed. The hive-queen is the plot device that lets every unit fall still when one figure is struck. The author calls the device stupid as biology and exact as a command-break. After it, the story is free to turn around: the aliens go home, and the next act is **our** attack on **their** planet.

### 3. They are far away, already beaten, and still the threat

**On the page:** Earth spends a generation building a fleet of children because the buggers might return, across a gap of years in which a message and a ship are not the same thing. The enemy has shown no second capacity that the command can point to. The attack is preemptive.

**Author’s history:** this is Mars looking at Earth after the armies have already shattered. Earth is neutralized and has no invasion fleet. Mars still treats the **Pugachev** claim as an existential threat to the narrative, the orders, and the centuries of religious-military arrangement. The relativistic barrier is the novel’s way of saying “they cannot reach us, and we are going anyway.” The rebellion itself never enters the science-fiction plot. The contradiction stands in for it.

### 4. The child, the maximum strike, the fleet that was not supposed to die

**On the page:** Ender is **Andrew Wiggin**. The short name is a child’s nickname. The book is also built so that he is the one who **ends** the war and the world. He believes the last battle is a game. He spends the fleet to put the Molecular Disruption Device on the planet. The command weeps and cheers. Then he is told the fleets were real.

**Who knows, in the first book.** The 1977 story and the 1985 novel both keep the lie on Ender: he thinks the last battle is an examination, he breaks it on purpose, and the adults in the room already know he has been commanding the real fleets. Mazer’s line in the novel is that it had to be a child, because someone old enough to understand war would not have gone in with a whole heart, and they made sure he did not know. His fellow child commanders are inside the same game. The books do not say that the crews who died were told a child was giving the orders. They also do not say those crews thought the choice came from an advanced intelligence. That voice question appears later, in *Ender’s Shadow* (1999): Bean, taking the last two ships, does not know whether the crews heard a child’s voice or a voice changed to sound older. The first book leaves the crews’ belief unstated. The irony it does contain is in the room: the people cheering know; the child who gave the order does not.

**Author’s history, kept distinct from that sentence:** on Mars the war against the Horde was planned inside a virtual command, and the maximum setting was left to a child. The intended target was the rebellion and the kingdom. The name, in this reading, is the end of history as it stood: two planets’ order, and the Horde with them. The joy in the first book is the adults in the room. The belief that the choice came from an advanced intelligence is something the author **read on the web**. It is not a sentence in the 1977 story or the 1985 novel.

**The cheer, then the delay (2026-09-28).** Card does not show a reason the fleet would be willing to die and cheer the dying. The author’s one explanation is that the cheer in his head was only for the enemy’s defeat. The people cheering were disconnected, in that moment, from the result that would land on themselves. They were not cheering their own death. They had not met it yet.

On the history this site already tells, that moment is the strike from Mars that breaks the Rus-Horde: the rebellion defeated, its main figure gone, the threat gone on a numbers level. Tears and cheering belong there. [MudFlood — how the event happened](/history/mudflood/page.md) then has the energy build inside the hollow Earth and leave again through the points of least resistance, the volcanic belt Andrew Hall reads as a negative electric scar, the Ring of Fire, with the return stroke hitting Mars because the planets were in opposition. The author’s timing, added here, is the gap between those two ends. Hours, possibly days. The heat gathers in the inner Earth, coalesces, and bursts out as volcanoes around the targeted region. Those vents later cool into the hotspots of the present map and leave the range of volcanoes around the ground that was hit. Only after that delay does the energy, with nowhere else to go, leave Earth and strike Mars.

The 22 May 1984 remote-viewing session is the picture of what that return stroke left. The declassified transcript ([Mars Exploration 1984](/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/cia-reading-room-mars-exploration-1984-rv-investigation.md)) moves from a before-state of megalithic walls, aqueducts, a basin, and plants, through a geologic storm, into huge interior spaces and a boat that arrives among volcanoes, gas, and a volatile ground. The card’s “one million years” is treated in that file as a misdirection. The author’s sentence for the same session is the one the transcript approaches and does not spell: a watery, living Mars becomes the mostly dead planet, and whatever life remains is underground in the hollows or at the poles, where it is still warm. The poles are the author’s placement. The transcript’s own refuge is the interior, and its far destination is volcanic.

The book collapses the cheer and the loss into one scene because Card had the emotion and not the delay. The history, on this reading, does not. The command cheers a defeated Horde. Mars is still alive. The secondary discharge is still in the ground.

The index file already holds the narrower parallel: a child, a telepathic enemy, a weapon that consumes the attacker’s own force, told as the secondary discharge. This section adds the author’s reorder, the China beat, and the hours-or-days gap between the cheer and the return stroke. It does not replace that index.

## Lewis, second-hand

Lewis published *Out of the Silent Planet* in **1938**. He died in **1963**. He cannot be encoding Card. If both sit on one history, he is the earlier and thinner telling, which is the author’s guess.

| Author’s beat | Lewis |
|---------------|--------|
| China gassed, Wall as a lid, British reset, Horde tie cut | Absent. No China, no gas, no wall, no empire installed afterward. |
| Scout succeeds; the “we destroyed it” scene is the heroic twist | Partial, and inverted in tone. The cold death **lands**. The highland is killed. The binding of the Bent One comes **after** the damage. The victory does not undo the wound. He does not pretend the blow missed. |
| One ruler struck, and every unit stops obeying | Partial. The Bent One is bound in the air of Earth and the heavens go silent. One figure’s fall ends his freedom to operate. There is no hive, no queen-ship, no armies of Earth going still in 1774. |
| A distant, already-beaten world is still attacked | Partial, and the direction is Earth’s. **Weston** would take Malacandra for humanity’s necessity after Mars has already been wounded. The “why attack them when they cannot reach us” shape is his speech. The Pugachev claim is not in it. |
| A child in a simulation spends his own world | Absent. Ransom is an adult philologist, kidnapped, not in command. No simulation. The handramits are opened so the world will **not** be unpeopled. Lewis stops at a rescue. |
| Mars killed **before its time**, by a blow linked to Earth, against the era’s story that Mars simply cooled | Present. This is the one strong rhyme. Lowell, Wells, and the cooling argument said Mars died of age. “Before its time” refuses them. |

**What influenced the scrap he does have.** Named in his own record: Wells (the form, and a dying neighbor world), Stapledon’s *Last and First Men* read in 1937, Haldane’s master-race essay as Weston’s mouth, Lindsay’s spiritual voyage, and the summer 1937 wager with Tolkien that the space story would uncover the **fall of Satan**. A rebel who only spoils Earth does not use Mars, so the second hand falls on the harandra. That stack builds a killed planet without China, without a child, and without a command celebrating the loss of its own fleet. Full score of those sources: [Lewis Mars wound](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/cslewis/investigation-narnia-planetary-encoding.md).

**Working conclusion:** Lewis is second-hand relative to this reading. The pattern that survives is a world struck early by a power tied to Earth, life left in the cuts, the striker then bound. The China scout and the queen as Peter III are Card’s and Johnston’s layer. The belief that the crews took the order for an advanced intelligence is a later web reading, not the first book, and it is not in Lewis either.
