## *Gideon v. Wainwright* — a plea is not the exhibit

*Gideon v. Wainwright*, 372 U.S. 335 (1963). Florida refused Clarence Earl Gideon a lawyer on a felony breaking-and-entering charge. He defended himself. The conviction stood until the Supreme Court held that the Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies to the states. At the retrial, with a lawyer, he was acquitted. The first result was an artifact of a proceeding with no real defense.

Sims’s week of 17 Mar 2015 has that shape. David Gallaher warned him that someone was about to expose the old posts because of the Marvel solicit. The first note went to Gallaher, not to D’Orazio. The public apology followed the same day she tweeted. He did not put the original page in the record. He did not contest the wording. He pleaded to a category: “jerky comments,” “personal attack,” “the aggressor and a complete jerk,” and later, in Ask Chris, “Between 2007 and 2010, I harassed and bullied Valerie D’Orazio online.” ComicsAlliance then told readers he had admitted a specific comment. The Mary Sue’s November 2017 sexual-harassment timeline cited the episode as if the category were sexual. That is the conviction traveling without the trial record.

Log the apology as his words under a pile-on. Do not use it to prove a sentence that is not on the page. Do not use it as a gendered-insult finding. There has been no retrial, because the thread is still missing.

## Did he clean up before the hire?

The posting stopped about five years before Marvel. The apology did not.

- He, ComicsAlliance (“ended his harassment several years ago”), and she (pulled back before the *Butterfly* threat week) put the stop around 2010.
- 2010 to early 2015: no second incident of this kind shows up in the trade record. He spends those years as the Ask Chris columnist on a site that wins the 2015 Eisner for comics journalism.
- The apology is the week of the solicit. She called it sudden and rejected it. ComicsAlliance says throwaway accounts were built to wreck the Marvel news and to discredit the site, and that those messages called her “David’s wife.”
- He stepped away from ComicsAlliance briefly (reported around 23 Mar 2015) and was back. WWAC in 2018 says he stayed until the site shuttered in 2017. Marvel kept *X-Men ’92*.

Behavior change predates the contract. The public plea happens because the hire made the old posts useful again. That is not the same as cleaning up in order to get the job, and it is not a cancel.
