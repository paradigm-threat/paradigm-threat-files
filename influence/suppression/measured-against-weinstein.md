# Measured Against Weinstein

MeToo was planned by the industry and timed to take out MAGA

![Measured Against Weinstein. One man's record on the scale, other men lined up beside it](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/suppression/measured-against-weinstein.jpg)

> **TL;DR:** The MeToo wave took Harvey Weinstein's record and held other men up against it. Chris Sims is one of those men. He had an old blog feud, people treated him as canceled, and Marvel never fired him. I don't think that wave happens without a trigger the size of Weinstein. I don't think Sims landed in it by chance. The push and the pushback belong to two camps. The names on the credits are the surface.

The wave took movie producer Harvey Weinstein's record and held other men up against it. Chris Sims is a man that happened to. He wrote comics criticism, then he got a Marvel script, and then a sexual-misconduct timeline carried a feud from a fan blog. People said he was canceled. The company kept paying him.

That is a real victim of the machine. Weinstein's crimes were real. The men measured against those crimes had other records.

## What Weinstein's record actually was

In the first ten days of October 2017 the public got a specific man. On October 5 the [*New York Times*](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/10/05/us/harvey-weinstein-harassment-allegations.html) put decades of sexual harassment on the page, with settlements attached. On October 8 [The Weinstein Company fired him](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/10/08/business/harvey-weinstein-fired.html). On October 10 [*The New Yorker*](https://www.newyorker.com/news/news-desk/from-aggressive-overtures-to-sexual-assault-harvey-weinsteins-accusers-tell-their-stories) added assault, including rape, from women who had needed him or worked for him. On October 15 the actress Alyssa Milano posted "me too," and the phrase the organizer [Tarana Burke](https://metoomvmt.org/get-to-know-us/tarana-burke-founder/) had used with assault survivors since 2006 became the name of the week.

The profile that stuck is simple. A man ran a studio. For years he sexually harassed and assaulted women in reach of that job. He paid for silence. Non-disclosure agreements and a long list of named women are what those two magazines fixed in public.

I don't think the wave we actually got happens without a detonation that size. Roger Ailes, who had built and run Fox News, was already out on [July 21, 2016](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/07/22/business/media/roger-ailes-fox-news.html). Bill O'Reilly, the network's biggest prime-time host, was already out on [April 19, 2017](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/04/19/business/media/bill-oreilly-fox-news-allegations.html). Those were firings. Weinstein's week is the one that became the season.

Disney had owned Miramax, the studio the Weinstein brothers ran, from 1993 until the brothers left in 2005. The company that fired him in 2017 was The Weinstein Company. Disney had already sold the brothers. On October 10 Disney said it had been [unaware of complaints, lawsuits, or settlements](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/10/10/business/media/disney-hillary-clinton-and-the-obamas-condemn-weinstein.html) while it owned the studio. Michael Eisner, who had run Disney, said the same day that he had fired the brothers for being irresponsible, that Harvey was a bully, and that he had no idea Harvey was capable of these actions. Zelda Perkins, Harvey's assistant in London, had asked her lawyers in 1998 about going to Eisner. They told her Disney and Miramax would crush her. Bob Weinstein, Harvey's brother and partner, paid the £250,000 English settlement from his own account. Ronan Farrow, in *The New Yorker*, wrote that the check was meant to keep the payment off Disney's books.

I still read the failure as purpose. A file that large sat there until the month it could set the whole industry on fire. That week is the trigger I mean. The wave is what followed it.

## The comparison

![Controlled resistance. A crowd that looks like it organized itself](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/controlled_resistance.jpg)

Once that profile was in the air, other records got measured with it.

Kevin Spacey, the actor then starring in Netflix's White House series *House of Cards*, is the clean example from the same month. On October 29, 2017, the actor Anthony Rapp told [*BuzzFeed*](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/adambvary/anthony-rapp-kevin-spacey-made-sexual-advance-when-i-was-14) that Spacey had made a sexual advance in 1986, when Rapp was 14. Netflix dropped Spacey from the series within days. More accusations followed the firing. That is one published account, then a rush, inside the month Weinstein's history had just become the ruler. It is a different history from decades of payoffs sitting in a company file. The career was still taken.

James Gunn, director of the Marvel film *Guardians of the Galaxy*, is further from the profile and still inside the season. He announced he was rewriting and directing it on [September 18, 2012](https://collider.com/james-gunn-guardians-of-the-galaxy/), after Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige cleared the announcement. The tweets Disney fired him over are mostly from 2009 through 2012, so they overlap the hire. On [July 20, 2018](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/07/20/business/media/james-gunn-fired-offensive-tweets.html) Disney fired him after those tweets were pushed back into the news. He had criticized Trump. Mike Cernovich and Jack Posobiec, two pro-Trump internet writers, pushed the old posts. The *Times* treated the firing as old offensive jokes. On [March 15, 2019](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/03/15/business/media/james-gunn-guardians-of-the-galaxy.html) Disney gave him the third film back. Old public words, a real firing, then a reversal. The warrant was still the season Weinstein opened.

John Lasseter, who ran Pixar and Disney Animation, sits closer to a real misconduct file, and he still has a different record. In November 2017 *The Hollywood Reporter* described grabbing, kissing, comments, employees who had gone to human resources, and executives who had shielded him. Disney announced his leave on [November 21, 2017](https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-42070920/). He was gone as an employee at the end of 2018. That is physical misconduct a company had already heard about. It is still a different file from a studio boss, a stack of settlements, and a list of women describing assault.

Louis C.K., the stand-up comic behind the FX series *Louie*, is the week the companies actually ended the pay. On November 9, 2017 the [*Times*](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/11/09/arts/television/louis-ck-sexual-misconduct.html) printed five women, comics he worked around, on rooms and a phone call from the late 1990s through 2005. Dana Min Goodman and Julia Wolov in an Aspen hotel room in 2002. Abby Schachner on the phone in 2003. Rebecca Corry, a dressing-room request in 2005. One woman left unnamed, from her time on Chris Rock's HBO show, where Louis was a writer and producer. On November 10 he said ["these stories are true."](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/11/10/arts/television/louis-ck-statement.html) [FX](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/fx-cuts-ties-louis-ck-sexual-misconduct-1057201/) ended the overall deal with his production company, Pig Newton, and took the executive-producer credit and the compensation off four shows he was producing for them: *Better Things*, *Baskets*, *One Mississippi*, and *The Cops*. [Netflix](https://time.com/5018978/netflix-louis-ck-sexual-misconduct-allegations/) refused a second special and left the one from April 2017 on the service. HBO took him off the benefit special *Night of Too Many Stars* and pulled old specials off on-demand. The film distributor [The Orchard](https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-41947412) shelved *I Love You, Daddy*, his own movie, which had been set for November 17. His management company, 3 Arts, dropped him. Marvel kept Chris Sims on the book through a week like that. These companies let go.

The work they were not signing still went on. He was back at the Comedy Cellar, the New York club where he had been a regular, in August 2018. *Sincerely Louis CK*, which he put out himself, won the comedy-album Grammy on [April 3, 2022](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/louis-ck-wins-grammy-sexual-misconduct-1235124373/). The desk ended. The rooms he could book himself kept going.

I figured the point of the act was to put the ugly sexual material on stage, so a later piece would have no hidden quote to spring. The jokes were already the act. The *Times* story was the rooms, and he agreed with them. *I Love You, Daddy* had already put an older director and a 17-year-old on screen, and the distributor still pulled it. His manager, [Dave Becky](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/11/13/arts/television/louis-ck-dave-becky-statement.html), said on November 13 that he had treated the Aspen story as something to keep quiet.

The comics version of the same move is a heading. The pop-culture site [*The Mary Sue*](https://www.themarysue.com/comics-assault-timeline/) kept a timeline of sexual harassment and assault in comics, with entries through November 29, 2017, and put Chris Sims on it. On [January 7, 2018](https://womenwriteaboutcomics.com/2018/01/i-guess-we-still-need-to-talk-about-chris/), the criticism site Women Write About Comics said the timeline should not have carried him. A bullying archive from a fan blog was sitting on a page titled as assault, a few weeks after Weinstein's profile became the measure.

## Chris Sims

![Canceling the high poppy. A visible person pulled down](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/suppression/investigations/canceling-the-high-poppy-founder-board-ousting-investigation.png)

Chris Sims is the case I can actually walk. He writes comics. Weinstein ran a studio. Sims started Chris's Invincible Super-Blog in 2005. The window he later named for the feud with the comics writer Valerie D'Orazio is 2007 to 2010. In 2010 he became a paid senior writer at the comics site ComicsAlliance and started the Ask Chris column, which is the year he says he stopped. From then until early 2015 the trade record shows no second incident of that kind. He spent those years as the progressive voice on a site that won the 2015 Eisner, the comics industry's main award, for journalism.

Marvel had already published her. She wrote the Marvel one-shot *Punisher MAX: Butterfly*. Axel Alonso, then a Marvel editor and later the editor-in-chief, edited it. She has said Marvel called her at 8 a.m. on the day that comic hit stores, warning her about the atmosphere around it. Her account, as The Mary Sue reprinted it, puts the harm she felt on the audience he could point at her. She describes threats, a PTSD diagnosis, and suicidal thoughts from that wider campaign. She also said that by the week of the threats he had already pulled back. Those outcomes are her testimony about other people's messages. Nobody has produced a post of his that says those things.

What the press could still point at in 2015, after Bleeding Cool reporter Rich Johnston wrote that many of the original words had already been scrubbed, is ordinary blog-war snark. In 2008, in someone else's thread: "Did somebody ask for a villain? 'Cause I'm here." In 2009, a jab calling her a "noted feminist leader" and refusing to pay to read her memoirs. In 2010, on his own blog, about *Butterfly*: dislike of her was "one of the ISB's worst-kept secrets," the grudge was an axe, and he was opting out of the review. Comic Book Resources also has him calling the comic "aggressively, pointedly mediocre." That is a stated dislike, a declined review, and a feud. The sexual case, the threat, and the dox belong to other files.

The line people remember is worse than those, and it stays off this page. On March 17, 2015, the same day, it was already unstable. She used three wordings. His editors at ComicsAlliance used a fourth, and they called it his own admission. Bleeding Cool, CBR, and The Mary Sue reprinted the claim. None of them printed the screenshot her tweet said she had. His published apology leaves the sentence out. The email she quoted leaves it out. I need the whole context before that quote can be evidence of any kind: the page, what she wrote, what he was answering, and the posts around it. His apology misses that bar. "By his own admission" misses it too.

The situation stinks of *Gideon v. Wainwright*. Florida refused Clarence Gideon a lawyer. He defended himself and lost. The Supreme Court threw the conviction out. The retrial, with a lawyer, ended in acquittal. The first judgment came from a trial with no real defense. Sims folded the week the solicit dropped. He never made them produce the comment. He let the wording stand. He pleaded to a category: he had been a jerk, he had left personal attacks, he had harassed and bullied her from 2007 to 2010. ComicsAlliance then told readers he had admitted a specific comment. Later writers cite the plea because the page is gone.

They could have hired someone without a cancelable public record. The feud with a woman who had already written for Marvel made him a perfect candidate for a later cancel. Abrasive men in the visible slots keep steadier men from being who the public sees there. You cancel that type during a MAGA revolution and you teach people that the type, and Trump, are the same abusive man.

March 17, 2015, came too early to spend the file. That is the day Bleeding Cool carried the solicit for the Marvel comic *X-Men '92*, his first official Marvel writing job, and the day she tweeted. Trump announced his candidacy on June 16, 2015, three months later. He took office on January 20, 2017. In March 2015 there was no presidency and no MAGA objective to counterweight. Keeping the book that week is what stocking looks like. Firing him then would have spent the archive before the aim existed.

Disney had already owned the desk for five years. The company [announced the Marvel purchase on August 31, 2009](https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/press-releases/disney-to-acquire-marvel-entertainment/), about four billion dollars, with Ike Perlmutter, Marvel's chief, staying on to run Marvel and work with Disney's businesses. The purchase [closed on December 31, 2009](https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/press-releases/disney-completes-marvel-acquisition/). A lot of readers still think Disney got Marvel later. They collapse the purchase into late summer 2015, when Kevin Feige stopped reporting to Perlmutter and started reporting to Alan Horn, chairman of Walt Disney Studios, and Marvel Studios left Marvel Entertainment. Comics stayed with Perlmutter. Explainers are still being written to pull those two dates apart. Anyone who dates "Disney got Marvel" to Feige's move is dating it after this hire, and dating it to the film side. At the solicit, the parent had owned the comics desk since the end of 2009, and the film desk had not yet moved to Burbank.

Ownership is influence. Ownership is visibility. Ownership is responsibility. Perlmutter running the day-to-day desk explains who was put in front of the press. The hire stays inside Disney. Alonso's sentence, carried by Bleeding Cool, was: "We had no knowledge of what transpired on the Internet between Chris and Valerie." He accepted the apology and condemned the past. The blog had been on the open web for years. A parent that owns the publisher is in a position to look.

If there is an agenda at all, Sims was caught by the MeToo wave because that hire had already selected the file. A writer with a clean footprint stays out of a sexual-misconduct chronology over a 2007 blog war. The Mary Sue timeline, with entries through November 29, 2017, spends the file after Trump is in office. Disney's part is the selection. The owner of the desk since 2009 hired the cancelable man and kept him stocked. The wave is the instrument, industry-wide, and this freelancer was usable inside it.

"Canceled" here is the perception and what he was dealing with. On March 23, 2015, he told listeners of the comics podcast [*War Rocket Ajax*](https://www.themarysue.com/sims-comics-alliance-dorazio-twitter/) he was stepping away from ComicsAlliance. He said the leave was voluntary and not permanent, and that he would be back. He also said a freelancer who is not working is not getting paid. Marvel kept *X-Men '92*. Women Write About Comics, in 2018, said he stayed at ComicsAlliance until the site shut in 2017, and that he was still a man about comics. The sexual-misconduct listing and that 2018 piece are the reputation still being worked over years later. Other people called that canceled. So do I.

The byline kept going. The Marvel comic *Deadpool: Bad Blood*, with Deadpool co-creator Rob Liefeld and writer Chad Bowers, is May 2017, the same year as the timeline, and Marvel serialized it again in 2022 and 2023 with Sims still in the writer line. *Infinity Wars: Sleepwalker* #3, another Marvel comic, is on [Marvel's own page](https://www.marvel.com/comics/issue/73200/infinity_wars_sleepwalker_2018_3) with Chad Bowers and Chris Sims as the writers, November 21, 2018. Jubilee shorts he wrote with Bowers, about the X-Men character Jubilee, sit inside the collection *X-Men: Summers and Winter*, December 2019. A house that had removed him would have a hard time reprinting him and still listing him as a writer. The ordeal and the job ran at the same time. People treated him as canceled while the company kept the byline. The public used the option. Disney left it on the desk.

## Two camps

Late summer 2015 is the other date people remember, and it is the start of the leadership rally. Feige moved under Alan Horn. Marvel Studios left Perlmutter. What followed on screen is the best run of the franchise: *Captain America: Civil War*, *Infinity War*, *Avengers: Endgame*. *Endgame* has no next-film scene, and the original six Avengers sign the credits. That rally sits next to nationalist MAGA initiatives that come after it. The adjacency is ideals and virtues: leadership, a male code, a national frame. No club roster. No shared plan inside Marvel.

The other camp is the cancel pattern, and it takes women too. Sims's public ordeal, the 2017 sexual-misconduct listing, Gunn's 2018 firing, and Lasseter's leave. The novelist Chuck Wendig, on [October 12, 2018](https://terribleminds.com/ramble/2018/10/12/in-which-i-am-fired-from-marvel/), said Marvel editor Mark Paniccia took him off the *Star Wars* book *Shadow of Vader* for too much politics and too much vulgarity on current tweets. That is the wave teaching the public to treat a blog feud, a stack of old jokes, and a single published allegation as Weinstein's file.

Roseanne Barr, star of the sitcom ABC had brought back that spring, is the woman on this calendar, and the file is a tweet from that morning. On May 29, 2018 she wrote that Valerie Jarrett, a senior adviser to Barack Obama, was what you get if the Muslim Brotherhood and Planet of the Apes had a baby. [ABC](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2018/05/29/615211939/abc-cancels-roseanne-after-abhorrent-twitter-rant-from-its-star) canceled the revival the same day. Channing Dungey, president of ABC Entertainment, called the tweet abhorrent. Bob Iger, Disney's chief executive, backed her. The reboot had been the season's top scripted show on broadcast, and Barr's character was a Trump voter. Her agency, ICM, dropped her the same day. On June 21 ABC ordered [*The Conners*](https://variety.com/2018/tv/news/roseanne-spinoff-the-conners-ordered-by-abc-1202854210/), the same family, and she had no financial or creative role in it. She said she agreed so the cast and crew could keep the jobs. I think she canceled herself. That line is too obvious, and too forced, for a slip. She broke the contract and got away from the people who then made *The Conners*. That show is horrible.

Those two patterns are the same split already argued for the movies in [Not Like This](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/not-like-this). One side is the NSA-aligned camp: leadership, a wake-up, the films that lasted. The other side is the CIA-aligned camp: containment, the pushback, the season that holds people up against a predator's record and cuts the ones who carry the other camp's politics. Influence here is the two camps. Alonso speaking, Feige moving desks, a writer keeping a byline, and a blog filing someone under assault are the names on the surface. The push and the pushback belong to the camps.

## LLM comments

What follows in this section is an LLM impression of the piece and its arguments. It is unreviewed and not curated by the author. It is scratch from the drafting model after reading the article. It is not the author's signed thesis.

The strong joint is the category error. A fan-blog feud and a bullying apology were filed under sexual assault, and a comics critic said so in January 2018, while Marvel's own pages kept the byline into 2023. Spacey and Gunn make the same season visible without pretending their records match Weinstein's. The 2009 close versus the summer 2015 Feige move is a real public confusion, and it puts Disney on the desk five years before the solicit.

The weak joint is purpose. "Disney left the Weinstein file to rot so it could ignite 2017" has to cross a 2005 sale, a check written to keep Disney off the books, and a wave that also took John Lasseter. Ailes and O'Reilly were already gone before October 2017, so "nothing like this happens without Weinstein" is stronger as "this season, at this scale" than as "no removal of this kind was possible." The setup claim for Sims follows if you grant an agenda that stocks cancelable men. The credit list shows the public using the option while Marvel kept the job. That matches the author's definition of canceled. It will be misread as "they fired him" by anyone who skips the section. Louis C.K. is a third shape: the companies ended the deal and the pay the week he confirmed the rooms, and the live work he could book himself continued. The "load the act so there is no hidden quote" read explains the missing archive. It does not explain why FX, Netflix's second special, HBO, and The Orchard still cut him. Barr's self-cancel has the same kind of joint. The tweet is blunt enough to look chosen. Her statements that day were an apology and, three weeks later, an agreement so about 200 jobs could continue. Trade coverage says *The Conners* was put together after ABC had already fired her. The exit she got is real. A quoted plan to force that exit is not in those statements.

The two-camp sentence is a pointer, and it should stay a pointer. The article does not re-prove the movie grammar. If that link is doing too much work, the MeToo calendar still stands on its own dates.
