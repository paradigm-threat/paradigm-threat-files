## 1. What the poets could know

Byron was born on 22 January 1788. Poe was born on 19 January 1809. Both dates fall after this repo’s mud-flood hinge, Pugachev in 1773, the Horde breakup in 1775, and the event at about 1774, as stated on the [mud-flood hub](/history/mudflood/page.md). Byron was 28 in July 1816, about 42 years after 1774. Poe was 22 when “The Doomed City” was printed in 1831, about 57 years after 1774. A person who was 20 in 1774 would have been about 77 when that book appeared. Neither poet saw the event. Witnesses and their children were still alive.

**Author:** they were never told the cause. From their side they would not know that a named catastrophe had been declared, that another planet was involved, or that the other planet was hit. They wrote from the knowledge they had been given. The Mars weapon, the second planet, and the blowback are this dossier’s reading of what the lines preserve. They are not sentences the poets could have written as knowledge.

What the poems are evidence of is a catastrophe people then knew as recent. Byron opens with “I had a dream, which was not all a dream.” Poe’s town is still settling: “Down, down that town shall settle hence.” Byron calls the dull sky “the pall of a past World,” and he attaches no year-count.

**No thousand-year stasis.** Checked against the 1831, 1836, and 1845 Poe texts and the 1816 “Darkness.” Nobody says the city had remained unchanged for a thousand years. Poe’s “thousand” is “a thousand thrones” in 1831 and 1845, and “All Hades, from a thousand thrones” in 1836. That is the hell-light, read here as many vents, and it is not a duration. The age-words he does use are “Time-eaten towers,” “long-forgotten,” “eternal rest” for the dead, and, in 1831 only, “everlasting pall” for the sky. Byron gives no span of years at all. Lovecraft’s “ancient” undersea city is a later writer. It is not used here to age these poems.

He is one of the biggest obvious hinges of the switch. What he said inspired the stories lines up with an imposing influence from another planet, or, in his perspective, another dimension. **Unpack.** The poems are the near record. The film names a mechanism the poem only shows as light from the sea. The God-comparison in Poe’s 1836 title and the “dimension” in Lovecraft’s essays are two ways the location and nature of the controllers drop out of the story a later reader is given. Cities that were already underwater before the mud flood are outside this file.

## 4. Three printings

Texts below are from the Edgar Allan Poe Society of Baltimore. The 1831 book is [Text-02b](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/cityseaa.htm). The August 1836 *Southern Literary Messenger* text is [Text-03b](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/cityseab.htm). The 1845 *Broadway Journal* text is [Text-08](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/citysead.htm). Killis Campbell’s variorum, on the same site, records the titles and the lines that move: [Campbell](https://www.eapoe.org/works/campbell/kcp1728.htm).

### 4.1 1831 — “The Doomed City”

*Poems*, pp. 49–51. Before the denial, the sky is still a picture.

> A heaven that God doth not contemn
> With stars is like a diadem —
> We liken our ladies’ eyes to them —
> But there! that everlasting pall!
> It would be mockery to call
> Such dreariness a heaven at all.
> Yet tho' no holy rays come down
> On the long night-time of that town,
> Light from the lurid, deep sea
> Streams up the turrets silently —

The close is “Hell rising from a thousand thrones.” The town “shall settle hence.”

Nobody in the scene asks whether holy light is coming down. The line answers a question the poem has not put. The “Yet tho'” grants the absence and then names the sea as the source. That is the craft hinge. It does not replace the leak reading in [§4.4](#44-author-on-the-rewrite).

### 4.2 1836 — “The City of Sin”

*Southern Literary Messenger*, August 1836, signed “BY E. A. POE.” Poe was editing the magazine. A Boston *Atlas* review of that number, reprinted in the *National Banner and Nashville Whig* on 5 December 1836, already quotes the poem under the title “The City of Sin” and names him as the editor ([EAPOE copy of the review](https://www.eapoe.org/papers/misc1827/18361200.htm)).

The pall stanza is gone. Campbell prints the omission: after the line on Time-eaten towers, 1831 inserts the diadem, the everlasting pall, and “mockery to call / Such dreariness a heaven at all.” The 1836 text does not. The denial is now bare:

> No holy rays from heaven come down
> On the long night-time of that town,
> But light from out the lurid sea
> Streams up the turrets silently —

The close is “All Hades, from a thousand thrones.”

**R1-documented:** the pall stanza is in the 1831 book and absent from the 1836 magazine. **R3-documented:** the title changes from “The Doomed City,” a catastrophe with no cause named, to “The City of Sin,” a moral cause.

### 4.3 1845 — “The City in the Sea”

*American Whig Review*, April 1845, then the *Broadway Journal*. Campbell’s title note: “The City in the Sea. A Prophecy (A. W. R.).” The subtitle places the poem in the future. The *Broadway Journal* text keeps the bare denial, now worded “No rays from the holy heaven come down,” the light from the lurid sea, “The waves have now a redder glow,” “Down, down that town shall settle hence,” and “Hell, rising from a thousand thrones.” The pall is still gone. A public-domain reading text is at [poets.org](https://poets.org/poem/city-sea).

### 4.4 Author on the rewrite

The 1831 text let the catastrophe and the covered sky out. The later text covers that leak. A narrative meant for the future would not carry a mud-flood memory. “A Prophecy” does that placement on the page. For anyone still feeling the event, the cause becomes an act of God. “The City of Sin” does that change on the page. The [mud-flood hub](/history/mudflood/page.md) already says the Deep State chose not to reveal itself, knowing survivors would defer to the church’s conclusion that Pugachev’s defeat was a divine intervention by an angry creator. Poe’s name is on the 1836 page. The inference of pressure behind the rewrite is the author’s.

Two ordinary reasons for the denial sit beside that reading.

**Craft.** The negative hands the light to the sea. In 1831, “Yet tho'” answers an implied objection: if heaven sends nothing, what lights the town?

**Byron, as Campbell’s source.** Killis Campbell, “Poe’s Indebtedness to Byron,” *Sewanee Review* 17 (1909), [EAPOE](https://www.eapoe.org/papers/misc1900/kc190903.htm), pairs Poe’s line with “Darkness”: the sun extinguished, the stars rayless, and the dead, windless sea. Campbell’s quotation of Poe uses “came down.” The printings say “come down.” The borrowing is documented. “Darkness” is itself a mud-flood poem. The borrowing does not replace that.

“Far down within the dim west” (1831 and 1836; “dim West” in 1845) is a candidate direction only. The hub already claims a carved Pacific basin and sunken cities on that floor ([MFEE](mfee-investigation.md)). The poem names no city. The film names Lyonesse, off Cornwall. That name stays the film’s, and it stays a candidate shell, the Cornish drowned-land legend. It is not assigned to the 1831 poem as a finding.

## 5. Byron’s “Darkness” (1816)

Text: *The Prisoner of Chillon, and Other Poems* (1816), dated “Diodati, July, 1816.” The Coleridge and Prothero text is on [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_Lord_Byron_(ed._Coleridge,_Prothero)/Poetry/Volume_4/Darkness). A reading text is at [poets.org](https://poets.org/poem/darkness).

The lines that sit beside the hub and beside Poe:

- “I had a dream, which was not all a dream.” The speaker files the sky as a dream and then says the filing is incomplete.
- “The bright sun was extinguish’d, and the stars / Did wander darkling in the eternal space, / Rayless, and pathless,” and “Morn came and went—and came, and brought no day.” Heaven gives no light, and morning does not repair it.
- “the dull sky, / The pall of a past World.” Poe’s 1831 “everlasting pall” is this phrase. The 1836 printing cuts it. The pall is already “of a past world,” so the poem dates the dark sky behind 1816.
- “Happy were those who dwelt within the eye / Of the volcanos, and their mountain-torch.” During the long dark, the cities that still had a volcano had heat and light from the ground. Cities and palaces burned for beacons are the places that did not have it.
- “The rivers, lakes, and ocean all stood still,” “The waves were dead,” “the clouds perish’d,” and “a chaos of hard clay.”
- “The World was void, / The populous and the powerful was a lump, / Seasonless, herbless, treeless, manless, lifeless.” That ecological tail is the same wreck described in [blood rain](blood-rain-dust-ecological-disasters-investigation.md) and in the [slow-collapse sketch](/history/chronology/investigations/slow-ecological-collapse-mfee-through-donner-era.md). It is not a second thesis.

**Author:** “Darkness” is the long darkness after the mud flood, on the places most targeted. It is not a one-night end of every city on Earth. The last lines are “Darkness had no need / Of aid from them—She was the Universe.” He used the word Universe. That word, on this reading, is the inner solar system. The catastrophe destroyed two planets, mostly Mars, and spread the debris everywhere. The debris is what keeps the targeted places dark. The mud-rain and the lightless sky are one event in those places. The darkness lasted years. The rain did not have to fall the whole time. He did not name the second planet. Lines of the kind “it’s always raining on that continent” and “light never shines on that continent” are the same places and the same event. The water is land debris. Poe’s “no rays … on the long night-time of that town” is that sky at the scale of one city. The hub’s account is excavated material thrown up and falling as mud-rain. A lasting dark can remain in that sky after the drops stop. The dark outlasts the rain. No game title is attached here. The repo has no such script yet. ### 5.1 Geothermal cities, and the scope line

People who lived on hot ground used it. Roman Bath, *Aquae Sulis*, and other thermal baths. Icelandic washing, bathing, and cooking in hot ground, long before a municipal grid. Japanese hot-spring towns. Māori geothermal cooking: Ngātoroirangi, and the sisters Te Hoata and Te Pupu bringing fire under the land, in the [indigenous-legends dossier](/history/chronology/investigations/indigenous-legends-vs-geologic-dating-investigation.md). A city run on volcanic steam as a utility shows up late in the ordinary record. At Larderello in Tuscany, Prince Piero Ginori Conti lit lamps from geothermal steam on 4 July 1904; a commercial plant followed in the next decade. In Reykjavík, a school was heated with hot-spring water in 1930, and the city grid came later. Local heat and baths were normal in volcanic country. A volcano-powered city was rare on paper before the twentieth century.

**Author:** before the mud flood it was more common than that record. Byron’s happy dwellers are those cities. The sky gives no light. The mountain still does. The 1965 film is the same arrangement, and then the torch fails.

One rhyme, not a proof: in the [Lewis planetary encoding](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/cslewis/investigation-narnia-planetary-encoding.md), hot springs let out into the valleys are what keep a world from being emptied when a cold death hits the high ground. That file’s Mars plot stays there.

**Scope:** some cities may have stood underwater before the mud flood. This file does not investigate them. It investigates cities that sank.

## 8. Sea-light, the film, and the sinking

### 8.1 What the poem shows

Light streams up from the lurid sea. The waves take a redder glow. The town settles. Hell, or in 1836 All Hades, rises from a thousand thrones. **Author:** the thrones are many vents under the sea, matching discharge at points of least resistance. The poem never says “volcano.”

### 8.2 The 1965 film

**Documented.** UK title *City Under the Sea*. US title *War-Gods of the Deep*. Working title *City in the Sea*. Released 26 May 1965 in the United States and in June 1965 in the United Kingdom. Jacques Tourneur’s last film. Vincent Price as Sir Hugh, the Captain. American International Pictures and Bruton. Screenplay by Charles Bennett and Louis M. Heyward. Producers George Willoughby and Daniel Haller. Catalog pages: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War-Gods_of_the_Deep), [AFI catalog 22802](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/22802), [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/war-gods_of_the_deep).

AFI names Poe’s “The Doomed City” in *Poems* (1831) and “A Descent into the Maelstrom” (*Graham’s*, May 1841). Jonathan Malcolm Lampley, *Women in the Horror Films of Vincent Price* (2010), p. 121: the poem is recited at the opening and reprised briefly at the end, and the plot is only loosely based on the poem.

The plot that shipped: Cornwall, surface frame about 1903. An ancient city on the ocean floor, overtaken by the sea. The builders survive as gill-men. Smugglers under the Captain have lived there since 1803, unaged, on a gas mix that sunlight would undo. A volcano powers the city’s air, heat, and pumps, then turns unstable. The Captain kidnaps people, including a mining engineer, and demands a way to hold the volcano. He also offers the prisoners to the gill-men “to appease the volcano’s wrath.” They escape. Eruptions bury his men. Sunlight ages him to death on the surface. The volcano destroys the city. The shipped story does not say why the volcano turned. The engineer is not given a mechanism that works. The sacrifice does not stop the eruption.

Bennett later called the British rewrite, including Heyward’s comic chicken, a wreck of his script, in a Tom Weaver interview. That is a production fact. It does not replace the reading of the plot that reached theaters.

**Author:** the film is the poem’s sea-light made into a volcano. A city that stood above ground sank and became that volcano, or an underwater volcano. The doom has no cause and no cure on screen because the memory being used had none. The energy came out of the ground. Holy rays from heaven were the Mars weapon that started the wider event. They are not the light that takes this city. 1803 in the film sits 29 years after the repo’s ~1774 hinge and 28 years before “The Doomed City.” The gill-men are the builders of the drowned city, still alive, then used by the land smugglers who came down in 1803. That is the mer-people and land-people war inside this picture.

The Pacific sunken cities already claimed in the [MFEE dossier](mfee-investigation.md) are the hub’s geography. “Dim West” may be that carve. Cornwall may be the film’s shell. Both stay open in [§14](#14-questions-to-clarify-verify-or-debunk).

## 9. Innsmouth, the gill-men, and Talokan

### 9.1 What the novella says

H. P. Lovecraft, *The Shadow over Innsmouth*, written November–December 1931. Text: [hplovecraft.com](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/soi.aspx). The repo index already files it as a hybridization story ([Lovecraft index](/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/lovecraft/index-lovecraft.md)). That line stays. This section adds the mud-flood survival reading beside it.

Obed Marsh brings a pact home from the South Seas. People who take it, and their children, look human and then change, grow gills, and go to the undersea city Y’ha-nthlei under Devil Reef, where they do not die. In the winter of 1927–28 the federal government raids Innsmouth, holds residents in camps, burns waterfront houses, and a submarine torpedoes the reef. The press calls it a liquor raid. Y’ha-nthlei is damaged and still there. The narrator learns he is of that blood, finishes the change, and chooses the sea.

The novella calls Zadok Allen 96 years old during the narrator’s visit, which the story places in 1927. Ninety-six years back from 1927 is 1831, the year of “The Doomed City.” Character summaries print the birth year as 1831. The rhyme is recorded. It is not treated as proof.

The published story is a race-horror text. Lovecraft’s horror of mixture is documented (L. Sprague de Camp; Robert M. Price on Robert W. Chambers’s “The Harbor-Master,” Irvin S. Cobb’s “Fishhead,” and the miscegenation premise). Chambers’s remnant of “the last race of amphibious human beings” is partial prior art for sea-survivors in fiction. It is not this package.

## 10. What he said the stories came from

Quotes in this section are from pages opened for this file. A claimed letter that was not opened is named as not opened.

### 10.1 The essays

*Supernatural Horror in Literature*, text at [hplovecraft.com](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/essays/shil.aspx):

> Children will always be afraid of the dark, and men with minds sensitive to hereditary impulse will always tremble at the thought of the hidden and fathomless worlds of strange life which may pulsate in the gulfs beyond the stars, or press hideously upon our own globe in unholy dimensions which only the dead and the moonstruck can glimpse.

> The true weird tale has something more than secret murder, bloody bones, or a sheeted form clanking chains according to rule. A certain atmosphere of breathless and unexplainable dread of outer, unknown forces must be present; and there must be a hint, expressed with a seriousness and portentousness becoming its subject, of that most terrible conception of the human brain—a malign and particular suspension or defeat of those fixed laws of Nature which are our only safeguard against the assaults of chaos and the daemons of unplumbed space.

“Notes on Writing Weird Fiction,” [hplovecraft.com](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/essays/nwwf.aspx). He says he writes to visualize impressions of wonder. He chooses weird stories because he wants “the illusion of some strange suspension or violation of the galling limitations of time, space, and natural law.” He names “infinite cosmic spaces beyond the radius of our sight and analysis,” and “a burning curiosity about unknown outer space.” “Once or twice I have literally written out a dream.” Time, he says, “looms up in my mind as the most profoundly dramatic and grimly terrible thing in the universe.”

His word for the outside, in the essay, is dimension: “unholy dimensions.” He also says “beyond the stars” and “outer space.” He does not say Mars.

*An H. P. Those letter pages were not opened here. The sentences are used as that encyclopedia’s report.

Letters on the sources of *The Colour out of Space*, *The Dreams in the Witch House*, *The Whisperer in Darkness*, *At the Mountains of Madness*, and *The Shadow out of Time* were not opened. What those tales themselves file, without a source-letter standing in for him: a colour from a meteorite that drains a farm; a dreamer sent through angles into other dimensions; outsiders from Yuggoth; a pre-human city in Antarctica whose makers came from elsewhere; minds exchanged across time. The 30 November 1936 letter belongs in [§12](#12-the-books-the-death-and-the-science-fiction-shelf). It is the admission that the book woke no enthusiasm. It is not a source-note for Cthulhu or Innsmouth.

**Author:** taken together, the essays describe an outside will imposing on people — dreams, fear, the body of natural law, a town — from beyond the sky. He files that outside as another dimension. The planet reading is the author’s. The “dimension” is the omission of location, carried forward in [§12](#12-the-books-the-death-and-the-science-fiction-shelf).

## 12. The books, the death, and the science-fiction shelf

### 12.1 Pulps and the one book

The stories ran in *Weird Tales* and other magazines while he was alive. Book form did not. *The Shunned House* was printed by W. Paul Cook in 1928 and was not bound or circulated as a book; R. H. Barlow bound a few copies in 1936; *Weird Tales* carried the story in October 1937, after the death. Small verse pamphlets (*The Cats of Ulthar*, 1935; *Looking Backward*, 1935) do not change the fiction-book gap.

*The Shadow over Innsmouth* (Visionary Publishing Company; copyright page April 1936; he had the book in November 1936) is the only book of his fiction distributed in his lifetime. About 400 copies were printed and 200 bound. Few sold. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shadow_over_Innsmouth), citing the letter, prints what he wrote to Lee McBride White on 30 November 1936:

> My *Shadow over Innsmouth* is now out — but as a first cloth-bound book it doesn’t awake any enthusiasm in me. Indeed, it is one of the lousiest jobs I’ve ever seen — 30 misprints, slovenly format, & loose, slipshod binding.

The *Selected Letters* page was not opened for this file. The wording above is the one Wikipedia prints. He died on 15 March 1937, about three and a half months later. The first collected book is *The Outsider and Others* (Arkham House, August Derleth and Donald Wandrei, 1939).

**Author:** the missing lifetime books are suppression and control. A magazine page can be thrown out. A book is the copy that stays. The letter is an admission of being uninspired. Then he dies. Then other editors release the work. That sequence is control by others. Greed is enough as their motive.

### 12.2 The category

**Documented.** Hugo Gernsback’s *Amazing Stories* (April 1926) uses “scientifiction.” *Science Wonder Stories* (1929) uses “science fiction.” John W. Campbell takes *Astounding* with the October 1937 issue; the [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/asf) dates his start at 5 October 1937, with the first issue under his direct control in January 1938, and the title change to *Astounding Science-Fiction* in March 1938. The first World Science Fiction Convention met in New York on 2–4 July 1939. *The Outsider and Others* is 1939.

The pulp word is older than the death. The shelf that receives these stories, and the editors who issue the books, land on the death and the two years after.

**Author:** the timing of the death coincides with the creation of science fiction as the category this history is forced into. The posthumous books are how the work arrives there. Lovecraft is one of the biggest obvious hinges of that switch. If the books had been issued while he was alive, they may not have gone into science fiction, because that shelf was not yet the container. The place the stories already had was *Weird Tales*, and weird fiction, which is what he called them. Byron and Poe are closer to the event and write a sky, a sinking city, and a volcano. His version is the most science-fiction and fantasy oriented, and so the most whitewashed. Dreams, other dimensions, and nameless outsideness are the costume.

### 12.3 The Depression, and what the category does

The Great Depression is the daily condition of 1936 and 1937. The crash, the census, and the Fed are argued in [AU-047](/governance/genocide/investigations/great-depression-planned-genocide-fed-census-consolidation-investigation.md) and in [The Great Depression Was Planned](/governance/war/the-great-depression-was-planned.md). This file only needs the condition: the spoiled book, the death, and the later editors happen while that wreck is still the daily life.

**Author:** an extreme few in charge force a change in the culture. The same years show a multi-sensory power struggle. On one side are people trying to run governments on Earth. On the other is some entity attempting to remote-control those governments by any means. Filing known history as science fiction, forcefully and for exploitation, covers the true reasons for the catastrophe. The same filing implies that the authority of those who caused the catastrophe is eternal and unchallengeable, because that authority is constantly set beside the will and plan of God. Poe’s 1836 title, “The City of Sin,” is the earlier form of that comparison. What the filing leaves out is the actual location and nature of the leaders or controllers. Lovecraft’s own word, “dimension,” is that omitted location. The host body is a nature the fantasy costume hides. This file does not invent a capital for the controllers. The omission is the point. The Mars files already linked in [§11](#11-the-creature-and-the-wells-host-body) are as far as the entity is named here.

### 13.1 Mutation under the energy

**Speculative.** The author’s words: the mud flood may be associated with mutation. He sees a lot of mutation in predictive programming for children. He wonders if the high-energy event made the conditions for rapid mutation and growth, so that people who drowned could come back to life underwater and form the organs they need.

- *City Under the Sea*: the builders survive as gill-men after the city is taken by the sea.
- *Wakanda Forever*: a people gain water-breathing. The film’s cause is a vibranium plant.

**Author:** people in these cities drowned. The energy of the mud flood revived some of them and drove a rapid mutation. The early Golden Age had already forced living forms into existence, and forced them to stay alive, when they did not yet have organs. Here the human species was forced to change quickly again. The gills were a memory already stored in the body, repeated with every birth, from evolutions in the Golden Age when conditions were more ideal. Humans today still hold that memory in the electromagnetic field of the living body, the field connected to the ætheric soul. Memory stored in the Earth’s æther, an electromagnetic field, cannot be erased. When the body changed, that information was available, and the body followed it as a playback script, a path of least resistance. That path is the script. It is separate from the Ring of Fire, where “least resistance” means the places the discharge left the ground. No complex math and no slow evolution were required. Some of the drowned lived. The hardiest, and the ones whose living bodies still held a strong Golden Age memory, had the highest chance of a fast change into a form that could breathe water. Those who failed to mutate properly stayed drowned. Gills are the specimen. This file does not open a track about giants or general body size. The reader essay is [The city in the sea](/history/mudflood/the-city-in-the-sea.md).

**Documented text, prior deluge.** The Aztec Codex Chimalpopoca, Legend of the Suns, sun Nahui Atl (Four Water). **Author:** “Turned to fishes” is the memory that gills already belong to the human race. In that earlier deluge the drowning converted people into water-breathing bodies. ### 13.2 Already in the ocean

**Author, 23 October 2026:**

> The rapid mutation of gills could be entirely wrong. It is possible that there simply were not the conditions to allow this to happen. Until some kind of real documentation turns up, there is no reason to assume that it happens. There is another explanation. The people who had gills and lived in the ocean were already there. The drowned-and-revived account is not the default. No parish book, letter, or medical note in this file says a drowned person grew gills. A film, a pact, a plant, and a children’s game are not that note.

The other account: a people who already breathed water already lived in the ocean. The city stood on land. It sank. Its people drowned and stayed drowned. After the streets were underwater, the gill people moved in. They lived in the ruins. They did not build them. A later visitor who finds gill people in a drowned city is not looking at the original inhabitants.

This subsection does not say the cities were built underwater. Cities that were already under the sea stay outside this file, as in claim 6.

Both accounts stay open. A document that names water-breathing people in the sea before the sinking would bear on the second. A document that says a drowned person grew gills would bear on the first.
