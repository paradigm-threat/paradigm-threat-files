# The city in the sea

Memories of cities sinking into the ocean was recorded prevailently in the 18th century and then suppressed as science fiction in the 19th during the Great Depression.

![The doomed city still settling, light and energy from the earth, some of the drowned revived with gills, the memory filed as science fiction](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/mudflood/the-city-in-the-sea-hero.jpg)

On the night of 22 October 2026 I watched *City Under the Sea*, the 1965 film with Vincent Price. A lot of it lines up with the mud flood. What interests me most are the contradictions the story cannot explain.

Edgar Allan Poe’s poem leaves a blank about what happens to the city. The movie fills that blank. It puts a volcano under the drowned city, and it makes the doom unavoidable. That is another contradiction. The plot then hangs on a miner who is lured down into the sea to fix the volcano at the eleventh hour. He knows nothing about how a city under the sea stays alive. He has no real way to apply mining to what is in front of him. He has no way to shut down a volcano that is drawing its energy from the earth itself.

The plot is an attempt to connect those dots and tell them as a science fiction story. A very subtle predictive programming runs through it. The people who made the movie may not have known it was there. To get at the truth, I have to go back to the sources that inspired the film.

In 1831 Edgar Allan Poe printed a poem called “The Doomed City.” He wrote the denial himself: “Yet tho' no holy rays come down / On the long night-time of that town.” That is an explicit statement that holy light did not come down on that town. I find that very interesting. Nobody in the scene asks whether heaven is shining. The line answers a question the poem never puts. The light that does come rises out of the sea.

The town is still going down. “Down, down that town shall settle hence.” Fifteen years earlier, at the Villa Diodati in July 1816, Lord Byron had already written that morning came and brought no day, and that the dull sky was the pall of a past world. People then were still repeating what they had heard about a catastrophe in distant lands. These poems are that talk, set in lines.

## What they could have known

Byron was born on 22 January 1788. Poe was born on 19 January 1809. The mud flood that wrecked the targeted places sits around 1774, in the years of the Pugachev war and the breakup of the Horde. Both poets were born after it. Byron was 28 when he wrote “Darkness.” Poe was 22 when the first printing appeared. A person who had been 20 in 1774 would have been old, and still alive, when Poe’s book came out. The poets did not see the strike. They were not told the cause. They were not told that another planet had been hit.

What they wrote is evidence that the catastrophe was still recent in people’s mouths. A dream that was not all a dream. A town that has not finished settling. A pall over a past world, with no count of years attached.

Nobody in either poem says the city under the water had stood unchanged for a thousand years. Poe’s “thousand” is a thousand thrones. In 1831 and 1845 hell rises from them. In 1836 it is “All Hades, from a thousand thrones.” That is light from many places under the sea. The thrones do not measure how long the city stood. The age-words he does use are time-eaten towers, long-forgotten bowers, eternal rest for the dead, and, in 1831 only, an everlasting pall over the sky. Byron gives no number of years at all.

The one later writer who files the city under the sea as ancient is H. P. Lovecraft. He comes last in this account, and his telling is the one that got pushed furthest into fantasy.

## Three printings

The 1831 text still shows the sky. A heaven God does not contemn, stars like a diadem, then:

> But there! that everlasting pall!
> It would be mockery to call
> Such dreariness a heaven at all.
> Yet tho' no holy rays come down
> On the long night-time of that town,
> Light from the lurid, deep sea
> Streams up the turrets silently —

The “yet” grants the dark and then names the sea as the source. The [1831 text](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/cityseaa.htm) is on the Edgar Allan Poe Society site.

In August 1836 the same poem appears in the *Southern Literary Messenger*, the magazine Poe was editing, under the title “The City of Sin,” signed by him. The pall stanza is gone. The denial is bare: “No holy rays from heaven come down.” The close is “All Hades, from a thousand thrones.” The [1836 text](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/cityseab.htm) is the one to set beside 1831. A doomed city, with no cause named, has become a city of sin. Sin makes the wreck God’s plan. Anyone still living with the memory is handed an act of God.

In April 1845 the title is “The City in the Sea,” and the *American Whig Review* prints it with the subtitle “A Prophecy.” The pall is still gone. The *Broadway Journal* text keeps “No rays from the holy heaven come down,” the light from the lurid sea, waves with a redder glow, the town settling, and hell rising from a thousand thrones. A prophecy puts the event in the future. A future story does not have to carry a mud-flood memory.

The 1831 printing let the covered sky out. The later printings cover that. Poe’s name is on the 1836 page.

## The long dark

Byron’s “Darkness” is dated Diodati, July 1816, in *The Prisoner of Chillon*. The [1816 text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_Lord_Byron_(ed._Coleridge,_Prothero)/Poetry/Volume_4/Darkness) is public.

He opens with a dream which was not all a dream. The bright sun is extinguished. The stars wander rayless. Morning comes and brings no day. Happy were those who dwelt within the eye of the volcanos and their mountain-torch. The rivers, lakes, and ocean stand still. The waves are dead. The end of the world on that page is “a chaos of hard clay.” The last lines say darkness had no need of aid from the dead winds and the dead clouds. She was the Universe.

That dark lay on the places most targeted. It did not sit on every city on Earth for one night. It lasted years. The rain that carried land debris lasted much less, and it did not have to fall the whole time. A dark sky can remain after the drops stop. The rain and the lightless places are the same event.

“She was the Universe” is the inner solar system. The catastrophe destroyed two planets, mostly Mars, and spread the debris. That debris is the dark and the mud-rain. He did not name the second planet.

Mount Tambora had erupted in April 1815. The summer of 1816 is the summer he wrote in. The poem is larger than that summer. In the long dark, the cities that still had a volcano still had heat and light from the ground. The mountain-torch is that supply. Cities that burned their own palaces for beacons did not have it.

Hot ground had been ordinary for a long time where people lived on it. Roman baths. Icelandic cooking in hot earth. Japanese hot-spring towns. A city run on volcanic steam as a utility shows up late on paper. At Larderello, lamps were lit from geothermal steam on 4 July 1904. A Reykjavík school took hot-spring water in 1930. Before the mud flood, a volcano as the heat of a city was more common than that late record.

## The volcano under this city

The poem never uses the word volcano. It shows light streaming up from a lurid sea, waves turning a redder glow, the town settling, and hell rising from a thousand thrones. The thrones are many vents.

In 1965 the film *City Under the Sea* reached theaters. The American title is *War-Gods of the Deep*. Jacques Tourneur directed. Vincent Price plays the Captain. The story is set off Cornwall, with a surface frame around 1903. An old city sits on the ocean floor. A volcano powers its air, its heat, and its pumps, and then turns unstable. The Captain kidnaps a mining engineer and demands a way to hold the volcano. He offers prisoners to the gill-men “to appease the volcano’s wrath.” The sacrifice does not stop the eruption. The engineer is not given a mechanism that works. The volcano destroys the city. Nobody on screen can say why it turned, or how to stop it.

The credits disagree about the sources, and both lines are on the record. One names Poe’s poem and Lovecraft’s *The Shadow over Innsmouth*. Another names the 1831 poem and “A Descent into the Maelstrom.” The poem is spoken at the start and again at the end. The plot is only loosely the poem. The plot that reached theaters is the one that matters here. A city that stood above ground sank and became that volcano, or an underwater volcano. The energy that took it came out of the ground. The holy rays were the weapon that started the wider event. The light that takes this town rises from the sea.

Mars fired more than once. The weapons heated the surface. The energy built up inside the hollow Earth. It broke out at the weak places, the secondary discharge, and left the volcanic belt called the Ring of Fire. Mars was in opposition. The same energy went back and struck Mars. The catastrophe there was worse. That return is already written on the [mud-flood page](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/mudflood/page.md), in the section on the secondary discharge.

![The Ring of Fire as the scar of a secondary discharge out of the Earth](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/mudflood/secondary_negative_discharge.jpg)

The film’s smugglers have lived in the drowned city since 1803. That date belongs to the film. The gill-men are the builders, still alive, then used by the land people who came down. That war between sea people and land people shows up again in Lovecraft, and again in *Black Panther: Wakanda Forever* (2022), where the people of Talokan breathe water and fight the surface. The film’s own cause for Talokan is a vibranium plant and a flight from the Spanish. The mirror is the survival, and the war.

## Gills after drowning

People in these cities drowned. The energy of the mud flood revived some of them and drove a rapid change. The early Golden Age had already forced living forms into existence, and forced them to stay alive, when they did not yet have organs. Here the human species was forced to change quickly again.

The gills were a memory the body already held, repeated with every birth. That memory is of evolutions from the Golden Age, when conditions were more ideal. Humans today still carry it in the electromagnetic field of the living body, the field connected to the ætheric soul. Memory stored in the Earth’s æther cannot be erased. When the body changed, that information was there. The body followed it as a playback script, the easiest path available. No long calculation was required. No slow breeding program was required.

Some of the drowned lived. The hardiest had the best chance, and so did the ones whose living bodies still held a strong Golden Age memory. They had the highest chance of a fast change into a form that could breathe water. Those who failed to change stayed drowned.

The stories file a different cause. In *The Shadow over Innsmouth* the change grows over years from a pact and from blood, and the people go to the city of Y’ha-nthlei under Devil Reef, where they do not die. In the 1965 film the builders are already gill-men after the sea takes the city. In *Wakanda Forever* the plant does the work. Children’s games train the same bodily change as play. *E.V.O.: Search for Eden* (1992) lets a child buy new body parts, and crystals speed the change. The energy of the flood is the cause in this account. The pact, the plant, and the game are later costumes.

## Buildings found underwater

Popular magazines keep announcing that buildings have been found underwater, or on the ocean floor. I hear that announcement all the time.

[National Geographic, 19 September 2007](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/yonaguni-jima-japan-underwater-city), ran “Japan's Ancient Underwater ‘Pyramid’ Mystifies Scholars.” A marine geologist said the stones off Yonaguni were a city sunk about 2,000 years ago, and he dated the ruins to 5,000 years. A Boston University professor who had dived the site said the major shapes were natural. The magazine printed both, and the age stayed ancient.

[National Geographic, 25 July 2024](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/sunken-underwater-anicent-cities), ran “These ancient cities sunk to the ocean floor. Here's how they were rediscovered.” The opening line is that lost cities under the waves are not just myths. The piece then walks through coastal places swallowed in the ancient world, and through the machines that map them now.

The finds are real enough to print. The cause, in these pages, is an old earthquake, a slow rise of the sea, or a legend. The announcement comes around again. That repetition is gaslighting. It prepares people for a day when the sunken cities can be told as fact, and the people telling it do not lose control. The city can be admitted. The recent catastrophe, and who fired, stays out. The 1836 title “The City of Sin” did the same work with God’s plan. The science-fiction shelf did it later by filing the memory as fantasy.

## The most whitewashed telling

Byron and Poe write a sky, a sinking city, and a light from the sea. Lovecraft writes dreams, other dimensions, and creatures. His stories ran in *Weird Tales* while he was alive. A book a person could keep did not exist for the body of the work until after he was dead.

*The Shadow over Innsmouth* was written in November and December 1931. William L. Crawford’s Visionary Publishing Company issued it as a book with an April 1936 copyright page. Lovecraft had copies in November 1936. About 400 were printed and 200 bound. Few sold. On 30 November 1936 he wrote Lee McBride White, in the wording [printed with the publication history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shadow_over_Innsmouth):

> My *Shadow over Innsmouth* is now out — but as a first cloth-bound book it doesn’t awake any enthusiasm in me. Indeed, it is one of the lousiest jobs I’ve ever seen — 30 misprints, slovenly format, & loose, slipshod binding.

He died on 15 March 1937, about three and a half months later. In 1939 August Derleth and Donald Wandrei issued *The Outsider and Others* through Arkham House. Other editors released the work. Greed is enough as their motive. The sequence is control by others.

The word “science fiction” was already in the pulps. Hugo Gernsback’s *Amazing Stories* used “scientifiction” in April 1926. *Science Wonder Stories* used “science fiction” in 1929. While Lovecraft lived, his stories had a place. That place was *Weird Tales*, and the weird fiction he named himself. John W. Campbell took *Astounding* in October 1937. The first World Science Fiction Convention met in New York on 2–4 July 1939, the year of the collected book. If those books had come out while he was alive, they might have stayed on the weird-fiction shelf. The shelf that later held them was not yet the container. He is one of the biggest hinges of that switch.

1936 and 1937 are still the Great Depression. The spoiled book, the death, and the later editors happen while that wreck is the daily condition. The [Depression essay](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/the-great-depression-was-planned.md) carries the crash. This page only needs the years.

An extreme few in charge force a change in the culture. The same years show a struggle between people trying to run governments on Earth and some entity trying to remote-control those governments by any means. Filing known history as science fiction covers the true reasons for the catastrophe. Setting the causers’ authority beside the will and plan of God makes that authority look eternal and unchallengeable. What the filing leaves out is the actual location and nature of those leaders. Lovecraft’s own word for the outside, in *Supernatural Horror in Literature*, is dimension: worlds beyond the stars, or forces pressing on this globe in unholy dimensions. The planet is the reading. The dimension is the omission.

His imposing creature matches the body in H. G. Wells. *The War of the Worlds* describes a rounded bulk, a beak, and sixteen whip-like tentacles, and says the Martians had become brains wearing different bodies as men wear suits of clothes. That body is the host. The occupant is not what the witnesses are shown. Cthulhu, in [“The Call of Cthulhu”](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/cc.aspx), is a vaguely human outline with an octopus-like head whose face is a mass of feelers, a scaly body, claws, and long narrow wings. The fish-people of Innsmouth stay the sea survivors. They are a different figure from the suit.
