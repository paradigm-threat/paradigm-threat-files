## 1. Author’s originating thesis

> **Author (21 November 2026):** An extremely convincing version of history is available, and politicians worldwide, including in America and Russia, still disregard it. Russia would be the country expected to champion this alternate history. Putin constantly calls Russia the Third Rome, which on Fomenko’s chronology is why they are called that. The phrasing is confusing. He says the spiritual successor in the West. Fomenko was probably the person who coined that, and probably the first person, besides the people he researched, who suggested there was more than one Rome to begin with. He says it often. He said it this morning, or earlier today. It seems to serve a dual track. It highlights how close they hold Russian history, and it shows the limit on even that president: he cannot outright say they support New Chronology. If he does, the West has that much more ferocity in accusing and blaming Russia for trying to take over the world, invade Europe, claim Europe, and the rest of the bucket that comes with it. The presidents of America and Russia cleanly avoid that. The American analogy is the same thing. It produces the best leadership outcome and a psychological defeat for the enemies. It is not sustainable. As part of the whole sin-eater MAGA thing, Trump is a sin eater for the history of America. He eats the sins of America and leaves it clean, so the next president can be a lot more honest without sacrificing the ideological advantage and losing the demographic base. That is exactly what Putin is doing too. This file is for the political parallels and the strategies. It stays separate from the fact-checking articles.

That conflict gets in the way of real peace negotiations between countries at war. It is one of the things they cannot afford to say at a publicity event, because they cannot have the enemy exploit those statements to imply that Russia is after something other than peace. That challenge is going to have to come from individuals who live in those countries, and not from their leaders, who are hamstrung on this.

**What this means.** The author is not asking this file to prove New Chronology. He is asking it to watch what officeholders do with a history they will not name. The history lesson cannot come from the state. It has to come from private people who live in the countries whose textbooks would change.

## 2. Putin, 20 November 2026 — the sentence

**Documented.** Channel One aired the documentary *Our Baptism. The Patriarch* (*Наше крещение. Предстоятель*) on **20 November 2026**, Patriarch Kirill’s 80th birthday. Putin’s interview in it was with the Serbian director Emir Kusturica. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the conversation was recorded before the president’s foreign trip, so the air date is the release, not necessarily the hour of the taping. Press on **21 November 2026** (RIA, Komsomolskaya Pravda, WION, and RT’s English article, updated in the early hours and headlined on the slogan) carried the Third Rome passage. That is the cycle the author heard as “this morning or earlier today,” and the RT piece is what he marked as the occasion of [Weep Not for Memory](/history/chronology/weep-not-for-memory.md). [RT, 20–21 November 2026](https://rt-english.com/russia/649179-moscow-third-rome-putin-kusturica/).

Kusturica asked whether Russian civilisation can become an ark for those who believe in Christ. Putin said it can. The English Kremlin transcript:

> I am saying it with such confidence not just because the Russian theological and philosophical thought is centred around one concept that dates centuries back: Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be.
>
> What does it mean? It means that Christianity came into existence in ancient Rome, then Rome fell, and Christianity moved to Byzantium, the Eastern Roman Empire. Constantine became the first emperor to convert to Christianity. The Byzantine Empire also fell, but by that time, Christianity had already spread to Russian soil and reached Moscow. Thus, Moscow inherited this faith and these traditions. In a way, it became the Third Rome, the spiritual keeper of those holy values, that heritage.
>
> Yet this theological, or philosophic, idea, which, as I said, Russia has cherished for ages, is not the main point here. It is all about today.

Source: [Kremlin transcript, 20 November 2026](http://www.en.special.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/81318). RIA’s Russian rendering the same night: Christianity arose in ancient Rome, Rome fell, Christianity moved to the eastern part of the Roman Empire, Byzantium, under the first Christian emperor Constantine; Byzantium fell; by then Christianity had already been carried onto Russian soil, to Moscow; Moscow became the continuator of that faith, and in that sense the Third Rome, the spiritual keeper of those holy things ([RIA, 20 November 2026](https://ria.ru/20261120/putin-2133048332.html)).

He then moved the weight off the formula. People moving to Russia from Europe, he said, come to keep families and traditional religious values. The ark is for the four traditional religions he names — Buddhism, Judaism, Islam, and Orthodox Christianity — not for Orthodox Christians alone. He added that Russian civilisation “never took over others” and provided conditions for other peoples inside the empire.

**What this pass did not find.** A speech, interview, or article in which Putin names Fomenko, Nosovskiy, or New Chronology. A search of that pair against his name returns other people’s inferences. Felix Philipp Ingold’s reading, circulated in Russian in August 2023, treats the Kremlin as glad to use New Chronology because it props a Eurasian space under Russian leadership, and treats Putin’s “Crimea is Russian holy land” line as already an appeal to New Chronology ([inosmi.info summary, 9 August 2023](https://inosmi.info/exclusive/36227-istorija-po-putinu-novaja-hronologija.html)). That is a critic’s join. Crimea as the site of Vladimir’s baptism is ordinary Orthodox textbook history. It does not require Fomenko.

**Adjacent, and still mainstream.** On **18 February 2024**, explaining the history lecture in the Tucker Carlson interview, Putin told Pavel Zarubin that he had given “the most well-known theory, the Norman origin,” beginning in **862**, that the roots of Russian statehood are deeper than that, and that Mikhail Lomonosov criticized the Norman theory ([RIA, 18 February 2024](https://ria.ru/20240218/intervyu-1928042728.html)). When he does early history for a Western audience, the frame he speaks is the textbook one.

## 3. Two Third Romes

### 3.1 The slogan Putin quoted

**Documented.** “Two Romes have fallen, a third stands, and a fourth there shall not be” is the monk **Filofei** (Philotheus) of the Pskov Eleazarov monastery, in the letter against the astrologers to the grand-princely secretary Mikhail Misiur-Munekhin, end of **1523** or start of **1524**. Aleksandr Gol’dberg’s textual study treats that letter as the one that can be pinned on Filofei; later “Filofei cycle” texts borrow from it. The formula was little used as official policy until the 1589 charter establishing the Moscow patriarchate, which speaks of the great Russian tsardom as the Third Rome. Nineteenth-century historians then read the letter as imperial ideology, and that reading entered the schoolbook. Marshall Poe’s survey is the English entry point: [“Moscow, the Third Rome: The Origins and Transformations of a ‘Pivotal Moment’”](https://ir.uiowa.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1019&context=history_pubs) (Iowa history papers). Short reference: [Encyclopedia.com, “Third Rome”](https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/christianity/christianity-general/third-rome).

“More than one Rome” is older than Filofei and much older than Fomenko. Constantinople was already **New Rome** in the third canon of the Council of Constantinople (**381**). Filofei’s step was to put a third and last Rome in Muscovy after 1453.

Putin’s gloss matches that schoolbook: ancient Rome, then Byzantium, then Moscow as spiritual keeper. He calls it a theological idea Russia has cherished for ages, and he says it is not the main point.

### 3.2 Fomenko’s several Romes

**Documented, and a different object.** In *History: Fiction or Science?* and the empirico-statistical volumes, “Second Roman Empire” and “Third Roman Empire” are labels for Scaligerian dynastic blocks (roughly the principate, and the late empire of the third through sixth centuries). Fomenko stacks those blocks on each other and on later states — the Holy Roman Empire of the tenth through thirteenth centuries, the Habsburgs — by shifts he gives as about **330**, **720**, **1053**, and **1400** years. Contents: [chronologia.org, Chronology 2](https://chronologia.org/en/chronologia2/index.html); Russian contents of the 2005 *Methods* volume: [chronologia.org/content/index2.html](https://chronologia.org/content/index2.html).

A separate book, *Tsar’s Rome in the Oka–Volga mesopotamia* (*Царский Рим в междуречье Оки и Волги*, AST, 2009; Fomenko text revision 2013–2015), identifies regal Rome with the Second and Third Roman Empires and with the Great “Mongol” Empire, and places that Rome between the Oka and the Volga ([chronologia.org/charskii_rim/index2009.html](https://chronologia.org/charskii_rim/index2009.html)).

So Fomenko does argue that the Romes of the textbook are duplicates, and that a Tsar’s Rome stood in Rus. He did not coin “Moscow is the Third Rome,” and he was not the first to say there was more than one Rome. A reader of his books can hear Putin’s slogan as a memory of that reconstruction. The sentence Putin spoke to Kusturica is the Filofei succession: faith moved west to east, and Moscow keeps it. Fomenko’s sentence is that the western “ancient” Rome is a misdated copy of a later empire whose original seat he puts in Rus.

**Author note on the coinage guess.** The “probably coined it” line in [§1](#1-authors-originating-thesis) was the reason to open the file. The documents above close that particular guess. The political question stays open: why a president who says the slogan so readily never steps from Filofei into Fomenko.

## 5. Author — dual track, and the American sin eater

**Author (AU-088).** The 20 November sentence does the dual track in one breath. He says the slogan, which keeps Russian history close and sounds, to anyone who has read Fomenko, like the reason the slogan exists. He then defines it as spiritual succession from a real ancient Rome, and he says the formula is not the main point. The Horde map, the claim that western antiquity is a copy, and the territorial consequence Fomenko himself named in 2009 stay off the podium.

That omission is the strategy, on this reading. A president who adopted New Chronology as state history would be heard in the West as claiming Europe, because that is what the reconstruction says the fourteenth through sixteenth centuries were. The accusation bucket — takeover, invasion, a Russian title to European ground — would no longer be a war aim the Kremlin can deny. It would be a history the Kremlin had just signed. Filofei lets him hold the maximal phrase. Scaliger lets him stop before the map. He names the lessons and skips the dates: the 20 November speech gives the Rome–Byzantium–Moscow succession and leaves the year-numbers off, because that order of houses is already the Russian telling, and the years are what another capital can use at a peace table (C9). Reader essay: [Weep Not for Memory](/history/chronology/weep-not-for-memory.md).

A person who says ancient events happened in the Middle Ages — New Chronology, the phantom-time hypothesis, or the rest of that class — is easy to dismiss while the parties are still at the table. The fight over the timeline then sits in the way of a peace. A leader cannot say it at a public event. The other side can take the sentence and imply that Russia wants something other than peace.

The documented neighbor is already in [§4](#4-who-has-said-they-support-it). The author adds the use at the table: the chronology sentence is a tool the other side can use to throw the speaker out of a peace talk, not only a map claim after the fact.

The posture he assigns to Russia is practical. It has to be ready to work with the rest of the world in a non-threatening, non-authoritarian, and non-patronizing way. Ordinary sales and commerce can continue. Teaching other countries their own history back cannot be a Russian state job. That challenge has to come from individuals who live in those countries. The sin-eater term, already used on this site for the political cost Trump’s camp absorbs, applies here to the **history**. He eats the contradiction. ## Limits and disclaimers

> **Prisca sapientia (epistemic foundation):** This investigation assumes [**prisca sapientia**](/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md)—the historical and philosophical belief that the ancients possessed a vast, profound understanding of the universe, nature, and theology that was subsequently lost or degraded. Modern consensus science and institutions are not treated as default truth; evidence tiers below adjudicate specific claims.

Putin’s interview, Filofei’s letter, Glazyev’s article, Medvedev’s Novgorod remarks, and Kasparov’s foreword are public texts. They are quoted for what they say.
