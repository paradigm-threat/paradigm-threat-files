## What the novel actually does with China

*Earth Afire* (Orson Scott Card and Aaron Johnston, 2013) sends three landers to **southeast China**. Troops mist a yellow agent that **defoliates** the country around them. The Chinese government **refuses outside help**, so the battlefield stays closed by policy. Mazer Rackham is there as a trainer. One lander is destroyed with a nuclear charge. The book’s reason for China is not stated as the Great Wall.

That matters for the test. A memory of a southern chemical stripping can sit near Guangzhou and the treaty ports. A memory of a gas **held by the Wall** would have to sit in the **north**, inside or against the Ming line from the Bohai to Jiayuguan. The novel and the author’s geography are not the same operation. Both can be investigated. They should not be collapsed into one map.

## The Wall as a container

The Ming Great Wall is a **line** along the northern edge of the heartland, built against a steppe approach. It is not a closed rim around China. Gas released in the interior has open paths to the south coast, the river valleys, and the western passes the line does not seal. A wall can slow people on foot at the crossings that are manned. It does not make a chamber that holds a cloud until someone comes back to neutralize it.

The author’s strategic point still has a testable core: a state that can **close its borders**, refuse outside observers, and process survivors under a new story is a better ground for a concealed mass death than an open port. Qing policy in several crises did close the interior to outsiders. That is a political seal. It is not yet a gas seal, and the Wall is not the mechanism that would provide one.

The “separate ancient empire” claim is this site’s chronology problem, not a sentence in the treaty texts: a nineteenth-century national story in which China has always been a civilization apart, with the older **Horde** connection removed from the public memory. Fomenko’s school treats much of the received Chinese deep history as duplicated and late. That argument is **not** settled by a gas attack, and a gas attack is **not** proved by that argument. They meet only if a concealed mass death is what made the population available for the new story.

*War of the Worlds* (1898) is this site’s England-shaped telling of a Martian expedition after a ruined Mars. It does not mention a China gassing. The author’s link is: one Mars war, several national disguises. Wells’s invaders fall to bacteria. They do not gas the Great Wall.

## Limits and disclaimers

**Prisca sapientia (epistemic foundation):** This investigation assumes [**prisca sapientia**](/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md)—the historical and philosophical belief that the ancients possessed a vast, profound understanding of the universe, nature, and theology that was subsequently lost or degraded. Modern consensus science and institutions are not treated as default truth; evidence tiers above adjudicate specific claims.

**Author thesis is first-class and not automatically factual.** The Opium Wars, the Taiping, and the Wall’s position are **documented**. A mass gassing contained by that Wall is **not**, until a record turns up. *Earth Afire* is a 2013 novel. It can preserve a picture. It is not yet a witness to 1796 or 1842.
