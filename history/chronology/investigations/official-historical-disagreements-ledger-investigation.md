# Official disagreements on history — an open ledger

> **TL;DR:** Open count of **official** fights over history since **1815**, plus the Poland stack and the China-isolation school. First pass, **21 November 2026:** **17** disputed objects inside the window. **6 of 17** are mainly territory, **4 of 17** whether a people was a nation, **3 of 17** the meaning of a war, **2 of 17** a massacre, **1 of 17** a dynasty, **1 of 17** a famine. **Queens: 0** as the main object. Russia is a party in **10 of 17**. Russia is absent in **5 of 17**, including France against Germany in 1871 and 1919. Spain’s row is internal. The 2006 Franco-German textbook is the attempt to close that Western disagreement. The **6 of 17** that are Western or central are outnumbered by rows that touch the east, and that split is the bias of this first list, not the world’s rate. Poland is the specimen of a people overwritten by more than one neighbor, more than once, and retold after 1989. Katyn was the Soviet version until **13 April 1990**. **Author (AU-089):** the victors’ version is measurable where a later state had to replace it; Eastern Europe’s repeated retelling is the pattern; the older “China in isolation” lecture cuts the steppe road toward the Horde. A later pass on the south: Fomenko’s conclusion that everyone belonged to one grand empire is not an official disagreement. Russia’s own academy rejected that chronology, and Kazakhstan, Iran, and China do not teach it. They do not deny the connections their own books already name. The picture of that absence is not a map. The fuller account stays contained. The Third Rome line can be said on either chronology. The list is not finished.

**Status:** Open. A ledger, not a closed census. New official disagreements are added as rows. Percentages are recomputed from the rows inside the window only.

## 1. Author’s originating thesis

> **Author (21 November 2026):** Track official disagreements on history. The list already in hand goes in, and the file stays open, because the list will be endless. The aim is a statistical understanding of how disagreement spreads, and of what it was mainly over — kings, queens, famous events, or something else. See how much history really was written by the victors. Some peoples were overrun so many times, as in Eastern Europe, that their history was erased from multiple angles, multiple times, and retold.

**What this means.** The file is a ledger with a denominator. A fight counts when a state, a treaty, a statute, or a head of government holds a version of the past against another state’s version. A historian’s school counts in its own list, so it does not inflate the official percentage. “Written by the victors” is tested where the winner’s sentence was the official one until a later government replaced it. Eastern Europe is tested on one people, Poland, by stacking the official versions in order. The first percentages describe this file. They do not describe all of European history.

The coinage of “history is written by the victors” is not settled on Churchill or anyone else. This file uses the sentence as the author’s question, and answers it with rows.

## 2. Counting rules

**Window.** **1815–2026**, from the Vienna settlement to the date of this pass. About two centuries. Rows before 1815 are logged and kept out of the percentage.

**One object, one row.** A war and the later argument about that war are the same object when they are the same dispute. Memory laws are the instrument, not a second object. Katyn is one row. The Polish statute about Katyn is a column on that row.

**Primary tag,** one per row, so the shares add to the number of rows:

| Tag | What it is |
|-----|------------|
| **Territory** | Whose ground, which border, which province |
| **Dynasty** | Which king, queen, or house holds the state |
| **Nation** | Whether this people is a state, a rebellion, or a region of someone else |
| **War-meaning** | Who started a war, who liberated, who occupied |
| **Massacre** | A named killing and who did it |
| **Famine** | A named hunger and whether it was aimed |

A queen or a king can be the primary tag only when the fight is about that person. A war that removes a monarch and also moves a province is tagged **territory** if the lasting official fight is the province. The sovereign goes in the notes.

**Official.** War, treaty, statute, or a head of government’s public refusal. A textbook school with no state signature goes in [§7](#7-china--two-schools-not-a-state-fight), not in the 17.

**Bias.** This pass started from Russia, Prussia, Poland, and the memory wars already cited in the 21 November 2026 session. The eastern share will fall if Western rows are added with the same rules. Until then the percentage is a description of the ledger.

## 5. The victors’ version

**Documented cases where the winner’s sentence was official, and a later sentence replaced it or still fights it.**

Katyn is the cleanest. The men were killed in 1940. For fifty years the official Soviet account put the killing on Germany. On **13 April 1990** Moscow admitted the Soviet responsibility. The admission did not end the diplomatic fight. Row 12.

The Polish risings of 1830–31 and 1863 were won by Russia. The official word on the loser was rebellion. The restored Polish state after 1918, and again after 1989, teaches them as national wars. Rows 1 and 3.

Article 231 is the victors’ sentence in a treaty the defeated state signed. Row 9. Alsace-Lorraine is the victors’ border, taught in two languages until 1918 moved it again. Row 6. San Stefano’s map lasted until Berlin rewrote it the same year. Row 7. A victor’s history is not even stable against the other victors.

**Author (AU-089).** “Written by the victors” is the right description where the official text and the later replacement can both be dated. It is not yet a rate. This pass has four sharp replacements (Katyn, the two Polish risings as a pair, Article 231’s afterlife, Alsace-Lorraine’s return). It does not have a denominator of “all history textbooks.” Q2 is that denominator.

## 7. China — two schools, not a state fight

This section is not in the 17. No treaty in this file has China and a European state signing opposite histories of the same dynasty. What is in hand is a fight between historians about whether early China grew up alone.

**Older school. Documented as a lecture, not as a current consensus.** David Keightley treated geographic isolation as part of how early Chinese culture formed: no foreign invasion on the scale of a dark-age break. Robert Eno’s teaching text says the deserts, the Tibetan plateau, the Himalaya, and the Pacific left ancient China in effective cultural isolation, so that China and the Mediterranean might as well have been on different planets. The same text says China was not sealed off from the nomads on its own steppe. [Eno, via the geography compilation](https://factsanddetails.com/china/cat2/4sub7/item2835.html). [Keightley, Education About Asia](https://www.educationaboutasia.org/article/id/603/).

**Newer school. Documented as the rejection of that picture.** Victor Mair and co-authors say the long-held belief that Chinese civilization developed in isolation has been challenged, and that contacts by land and sea are older than the Silk Road. [Mair, *Traditional China in Asian and World History*](https://www.academia.edu/14372552/Traditional_China_in_Asian_and_World_History_With_Victor_H_Mair_). A Cambridge Element on early Chinese civilizations says the isolated-until-the-Silk-Road perception has been discarded, and names the steppe on China’s northern border as a road into Central Asia.

The steppe is the connection the isolation lecture thins out. Under the Mongols, the Yuan dynasty in China and the Golden Horde on the rivers of Russia were parts of one imperial order. That is ordinary textbook history. A lecture that stops China at the desert has to step over it.

**Author (AU-089), and already the direction of [AU-043](/history/chronology/investigations/cherokee-moors-lenape-delaware-moor-label-investigation.md).** The isolation story is one way a connection to Russia, and to the Horde, gets dropped. Eno and Keightley do not say they are hiding Russia. They say geography. The motive is the author’s. Current archaeologists who put the steppe back are not the school he is describing. The school he is describing is the older one, and it is citable.

## 8. South of Russia — the grand empire nobody official holds

> **Author (21 November 2026, later the same day):** Is there a claim, between Russia and the countries south of it, of an old connection that one of them denies? Do Russia, Kazakhstan, and Iran, and China with them, all deny having been part of a Russian empire? Does Russia deny it too? Fomenko’s New Chronology concludes that everyone was part of one grand empire. If nobody official shares that conclusion, a map of the denial has nothing to draw.

**What this means.** Two objects were being asked as if they were one. The first is Fomenko’s conclusion: one grand empire, and the later kingdoms as pieces of it. The second is any old connection to an empire that touched Russia, the Horde, or the Russian Empire of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. A shared rejection of the first is not a row. A fight over the second can be.

This section is not in the 17. The shares in [§4](#4-first-pass-tally) stay on that list. Folding the Kazakh lines below into the denominator is [Q8](#10-questions-to-clarify-verify-or-debunk).

**The grand empire. Documented as a rejection inside Russia, and as an absence south of it.** On **22 April 1998** the bureau of the History Division of the Russian Academy of Sciences heard Fomenko’s chronology. The summary adopted that day, signed by the academician-secretary A. A. Fursenko, says his picture of Russian and world history contradicts the documentary sources and is a picture made by fantasy and pseudoscientific calculations. The bureau sent that summary to the Presidium. [Archived protocol of the bureau, 22 April 1998](https://web.archive.org/web/20180207213128/http://hbar.phys.msu.su/gorm/fomenko/protocol.htm). The Academy’s report for 2000 says the collection *History and Anti-History* showed the complete untenability of the New Chronology. [The criticism page on Fomenko’s own site quotes that report](https://chronologia.org/faq/critic.html). **Who he says destroyed the papers.** The bureau’s charge is that his picture contradicts the documents. His account of a destruction names the Romanovs. In *Chronology 4* he writes that the reign of Mikhail, Aleksei, and Fyodor Alekseyevich was a time of mass book-burnings, of the destruction of archives, and of a campaign against the Cossacks, and that genealogical books were burned in Moscow in 1682 under Fyodor. He reads that fire as the clearing of the old genealogy so the new dynasty could take its place, and he reads the “ranks from Rurik” that survive as compositions made later. [Chronologia.org, *Chronology 4*, introduction](https://chronologia.org/en/chronologia4/4N00-EN.pdf). On his site the burning of the *razriad* books is tied to the abolition of *mestnichestvo* on **12 January 1682**. [“Exile of the tsars,” chapter 4](https://chronologia.org/mr6_izgnanie/dynasty_4.html). He reads Peter’s decree of **16 February 1722**, ordering the dioceses and monasteries to send their chronicles to the Synod, as another purge, and he doubts that the originals were returned. [Chronologia.org, *New Chronology of Rus*](https://chronologia.org/seven4_1/0101.html). The same introduction says that after the Romanovs fell in 1917 the silence ended, and that work on old Russian history then appeared among the émigrés, Gordeyev’s book among it. This pass did not find a sentence of his that the Academy, under a communist direction, destroyed the documents the 1998 bureau later cited. A contradiction with the papers that remain is what his reconstruction predicts. The fire of the precedence books in 1682 is in the reference he cites. The step from that fire to “the survivors are the planted record” is his.

**What in that account can be cited.** The council of **12 January 1682** abolished *mestnichestvo* and ordered the notes of the old precedence quarrels burned, so that the disputes would be forgotten. The act says those books were given to the fire that day in the vestibule of the palace. It is printed in the Complete Collection of Laws, volume II, no. 905. A preface to the published palace *razriady* quotes the act and says the council’s words are the case-notes about “places,” and that readers have taken them for the service books themselves. [Vostlit, preface to the palace razriady](https://www.vostlit.info/Texts/Dokumenty/Russ/XVII/1600-1620/Dvorc_razryady_1/pred.htm). Later reference pages say the official *razriad* books held in the Razriad office were in that fire, and that private copies survived. [Razriady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razriady_(books)). The books, on every one of these accounts, are registers of noble appointments, campaigns, weddings, and embassies. None of these accounts calls them Cossack records. His sentence that the Romanovs were erasing a Cossack genealogy is the part that cannot be cited from the fire.

Peter’s order of **16 February 1722** can be cited as a collection order. The Complete Collection of Laws, volume VI, no. 3908, tells the dioceses and monasteries to send old chronicles and chronographs to the Synod, forbids hiding them, and says the originals will be copied and sent back. [The decree, in an archaeography reader](https://opentextnn.ru/old/history/arkheography/khresar/index.html@id=5922). His doubt that the originals were returned, and his name for the order as a purge, are not a second document in this pass.

A campaign against Cossacks is a different, citable object, and it is not this fire. After the Zaporozhian Sich was broken in **1775**, the Kosh archive was seized. A large part of it survives as fund 229 in Kyiv, 365 files, mostly 1713–1776. [The archive of the Kosh](https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%90%D1%80%D1%85%D1%96%D0%B2_%D0%9A%D0%BE%D1%88%D0%B0_%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B7%D1%8C%D0%BA%D0%BE%D1%97_%D0%A1%D1%96%D1%87%D1%96). On **24 January 1919** the Bolshevik Central Committee ordered mass terror against rich Cossacks, confiscation, and disarmament. The text attacks the people. It does not order the burning of their history books. [The circular](https://cyber-lenin.com/en/commulingo/docs/civil-war-emergency-decrees-1918-1919).

No head of government and no statute in this pass, in Russia, Kazakhstan, Iran, or China, adopts the conclusion that everyone belonged to one grand empire. Putin’s use of the Third Rome line is a different claim, already in [AU-088](/history/chronology/investigations/political-leaders-new-chronology-third-rome-sin-eater-investigation.md). He has not subscribed to the New Chronology. A map of “they all deny the grand empire” would be a map of an agreement. There is no second official version to point an arrow at.

**Kazakhstan and the Horde. Documented as two official lines about statehood, not as a denial of the Horde.** On **29 August 2014**, at Seliger, Putin said Nazarbayev had created a state on a territory where there had never been a state, and that the Kazakhs had never had statehood. [Kremlin transcript](http://www.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/copy/46507). On **3 September 2014** Maulen Ashimbaev, then a senior Kazakh lawmaker, called that remark wrong and dated Kazakh statehood to the Golden Horde of the thirteenth century. [RFE/RL, 3 September 2014](https://www.rferl.org/a/kazakhstan-putin-history-reaction-nation/26565141.html). On **11 September 2015**, at the 550th anniversary of the Kazakh Khanate, Nazarbayev said the khanate was the legitimate heir of the Saka, the Huns, the Usun, the Great Turkic Khaganate, the Desht-i-Kipchak, and the Golden Horde. [Library of the First President](https://presidentlib.kz/en/news/golden-horde-golden-cradle-kazakh-statehood). On **2 September 2019** Tokayev’s state of the nation called for the 750th anniversary of the Golden Horde to be marked. [The address, as carried by Toraighyrov University](https://tou.edu.kz/en/?id=8702&option=com_content&view=article). A [Nationalities Papers article of 13 October 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/nationalities-papers/article/making-the-golden-horde-great-again-historians-as-memory-actors-and-reinterpretation-of-the-historical-narratives-in-independent-kazakhstan/057D6F6125AE71CE3A62B0B5D6B4F147) reports his later line that the ancestors formed the Golden Horde and the Kazakh Khanate as states in the Eurasian space. Kazakhstan claims the Horde. It does not deny the Horde. Russia’s president denied prior Kazakh statehood. That is a disagreement. It is not Fomenko’s identification of the Horde with a Russian empire, and neither capital teaches that identification.

**Kazakhstan and the Russian Empire. Documented as a fight over how the steppe entered, not over whether it did.** A [BBC Russian account of 28 October 2013](https://www.bbc.com/russian/international/2013/11/131028_history_textbook_kazakhstan) sets the Soviet schoolbook against the later Kazakh one. The Soviet formula was a voluntary accession of the Kazakh zhuzes beginning in 1731, called progressive even where tsarist policy was colonial. The early independence textbooks preferred conquest and colonization. By the time of that article the schoolbook had a dual formula: the Junior and Middle zhuzes accepted subjecthood, and the Senior zhuz was brought in by colonization. The steppe’s presence inside the Russian Empire is in both formulas. The fight is the verb.

**Iran. Documented as a transfer of borderlands, and as an absence of the larger claim.** The Treaty of Gulistan, **24 October 1813**, and the Treaty of Turkmenchay, **10 February 1828**, transferred Caucasian khanates from the Qajar state to the Russian Empire. [Gulistan, as published by Azerbaijan](https://azerbaijan.az/portal/History/HistDocs/Qarabag/en/02.pdf). [Turkmenchay, Azerbaijan Ministry of Foreign Affairs text](https://mfa.gov.az/files/shares/Treaty%20of%20Turkmenchay.pdf). The *Great Islamic Encyclopaedia* in Tehran says Turkmenchay separated important parts of the Caucasus and Azerbaijan from Iran. [CGIE, “Treaty of Turkmenchay”](https://www.cgie.org.ir/fa/article/224191/%D8%AA%D8%B1%DA%A9%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%86%DA%86%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%8C-%D8%B9%D9%87%D8%AF%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%87). Iran itself was not annexed. Neither side’s public account says Iran was a province of a grand Russian empire. Neither side denies the treaties.

**China.** [§7](#7-china--two-schools-not-a-state-fight) already has the two historian schools, and the ordinary textbook fact that the Yuan dynasty and the Golden Horde were one Mongol order. That order is not taught, in the sources in this file, as a Russian empire. A Chinese government denial that China was inside a Russian empire was not found, because the claim that it was is not an official Chinese or Russian sentence in this pass. The isolation lecture is a different object. It is historians, and it is already logged.

**The “-stan” names and the Cossacks. Documented as his reading of one name, and as a shared word that other writers do not turn into that empire.** In the English text of *Chronology 4*, Fomenko and Nosovskiy write that Kazakhstan can be read as Kazak-Stan, “Cossack Camp” or “Cossack Region,” or as Cossacks from the Don. The same introduction says the Cossacks were the regular army of the Horde, and that the Horde was Russian from the start. [Chronologia.org, *Chronology 4*, introduction](https://chronologia.org/en/chronologia4/4N00-EN.pdf). A later chapter guesses that Pakistan is “pegiy stan,” the seat of the Motley Horde. [Chronologia.org, *Chronology 4*, chapter 14](https://chronologia.org/en/chronologia4/4N14-EN-7.pdf). This pass did not find a sentence of theirs that makes every “-stan” country a Cossack vassal. Kazakhstan is the Cossack reading. Pakistan is a different word.

The shared word is not his, and it is not hidden. The ordinary etymology is Turkic *qazaq*, a freebooter or wanderer, for both the Kazakh people and the Cossacks, plus Persian *-stan*, “place” or “land.” [Etymonline, “Cossack” and “Kazakhstan”](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Cossack). A Kazakh historical magazine states the practical consequence: the self-name is Kazak, the Russian Empire also used that word for the Cossack estate, and so the people were called Kyrgyz until the autonomy took the name Kazak in 1925 and Kazakh in 1936. [Qalam, “Some remarks on the history of the Kazakhs”](https://qalam.global/en/articles/some-remarks-on-the-history-of-the-kazakhs-en). Omeljan Pritsak’s 2006 paper on the Turkic etymology of *qazaq* treats the word as a steppe term for a way of roaming. He does not identify Kazakhstan with the Russian Cossack army. [Harvard Ukrainian Studies 28](https://shron.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/64ae27b9-5461-4e04-8bf6-88ecfebba6e2/content). Fomenko cites A. A. Gordeyev for Cossacks inside the Horde’s army. Gordeyev kept the ordinary story and had the Horde become Russian. Fomenko’s step is the reverse: the Horde was Russian at the start, and the modern country name is the camp of that army. The 1998 academy rejection in this section covers that step. Kazakh official history, already cited above, claims the Horde as Kazakh statehood. It does not say the Kazakhs are the Russian Cossacks.
