# Official disagreements on history — an open ledger

> **TL;DR:** Open count of **official** fights over history since **1815**, plus the Poland stack and the China-isolation school. First pass, **21 November 2026:** **17** disputed objects inside the window. **6 of 17** are mainly territory, **4 of 17** whether a people was a nation, **3 of 17** the meaning of a war, **2 of 17** a massacre, **1 of 17** a dynasty, **1 of 17** a famine. **Queens: 0** as the main object. Russia is a party in **10 of 17**. Russia is absent in **5 of 17**, including France against Germany in 1871 and 1919. Spain’s row is internal. The 2006 Franco-German textbook is the attempt to close that Western disagreement. The **6 of 17** that are Western or central are outnumbered by rows that touch the east, and that split is the bias of this first list, not the world’s rate. Poland is the specimen of a people overwritten by more than one neighbor, more than once, and retold after 1989. Katyn was the Soviet version until **13 April 1990**. **Author (AU-089):** the victors’ version is measurable where a later state had to replace it; Eastern Europe’s repeated retelling is the pattern; the older “China in isolation” lecture cuts the steppe road toward the Horde. The list is not finished.

**Status:** Open. A ledger, not a closed census. New official disagreements are added as rows. Percentages are recomputed from the rows inside the window only.

## 1. Author’s originating thesis

> **Author (21 November 2026):** Track official disagreements on history. The list already in hand goes in, and the file stays open, because the list will be endless. The aim is a statistical understanding of how disagreement spreads, and of what it was mainly over — kings, queens, famous events, or something else. See how much history really was written by the victors. Some peoples were overrun so many times, as in Eastern Europe, that their history was erased from multiple angles, multiple times, and retold.

**What this means.** The file is a ledger with a denominator. A fight counts when a state, a treaty, a statute, or a head of government holds a version of the past against another state’s version. A historian’s school counts in its own list, so it does not inflate the official percentage. “Written by the victors” is tested where the winner’s sentence was the official one until a later government replaced it. Eastern Europe is tested on one people, Poland, by stacking the official versions in order. The first percentages describe this file. They do not describe all of European history.

The coinage of “history is written by the victors” is not settled on Churchill or anyone else. This file uses the sentence as the author’s question, and answers it with rows.

## 2. Counting rules

**Window.** **1815–2026**, from the Vienna settlement to the date of this pass. About two centuries. Rows before 1815 are logged and kept out of the percentage.

**One object, one row.** A war and the later argument about that war are the same object when they are the same dispute. Memory laws are the instrument, not a second object. Katyn is one row. The Polish statute about Katyn is a column on that row.

**Primary tag,** one per row, so the shares add to the number of rows:

| Tag | What it is |
|-----|------------|
| **Territory** | Whose ground, which border, which province |
| **Dynasty** | Which king, queen, or house holds the state |
| **Nation** | Whether this people is a state, a rebellion, or a region of someone else |
| **War-meaning** | Who started a war, who liberated, who occupied |
| **Massacre** | A named killing and who did it |
| **Famine** | A named hunger and whether it was aimed |

A queen or a king can be the primary tag only when the fight is about that person. A war that removes a monarch and also moves a province is tagged **territory** if the lasting official fight is the province. The sovereign goes in the notes.

**Official.** War, treaty, statute, or a head of government’s public refusal. A textbook school with no state signature goes in [§7](#7-china--two-schools-not-a-state-fight), not in the 17.

**Bias.** This pass started from Russia, Prussia, Poland, and the memory wars already cited in the 21 November 2026 session. The eastern share will fall if Western rows are added with the same rules. Until then the percentage is a description of the ledger.

The tilt is real. **10 of 17** have Russia on one side of the official text. It is not the whole list.

The five with Russia absent are Western or central, and two of them are France against Germany. Row 6 is Alsace-Lorraine, the Treaty of Frankfurt, **10 May 1871**. Row 9 is Article 231, **28 June 1919**, the Allies’ sentence on Germany. Soviet Russia was not a party to Versailles. Row 4 is Prussia and Austria against Denmark. Row 5 is Prussia against Austria over who leads Germany. Row 17 is Spain against itself: the 2007 and 2022 memory laws, parties inside one country fighting over the Civil War and Franco. No row in the 17 has France against Spain, or Germany against Spain.

France and Germany also sit inside row 13, where Russia is a party. [Siddi 2012](http://www.exact.uni-koeln.de/fileadmin/home/siddim/Studia_Diplomatica_LXV-4__2012__Siddi.pdf) keeps the French, German, and Polish accounts of the Second World War as three narratives, not one Western narrative set against Russia.

They treated that disagreement as a problem to close. On **21 January 2003**, the fortieth anniversary of the Élysée Treaty, students at the Franco-German youth parliament in Berlin asked Jacques Chirac and Gerhard Schröder for one history book with the same content in both countries. The first volume, *Histoire / Geschichte*, Europe and the world since 1945, was published in **2006** by Nathan and Klett, five historians from each side, and unveiled by the French education minister at Péronne ([BBC, 10 May 2006](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/4972922.stm); [EuroClio factsheet](https://euroclio.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Histoire-Geschichte-2006-Factsheet-PDF.pdf)). A joint official textbook is evidence the two states did not already share one history. It is not evidence they have stopped disagreeing about every earlier war. Alsace-Lorraine and Article 231 remain in the ledger.

**What the test does to the guess.** A clear split with Russia is the largest share of this list, **10 of 17**, and the nearest-country rows add **2 of 17**. The West does disagree with itself. France and Germany disagree on the 1871 border and on the 1919 war-guilt sentence, and they commissioned a shared schoolbook because the separate books were the problem. Spain’s recorded fight is internal. A uniform Western history, with Germany, France, and Spain all telling one story, is not what these rows show. Whether France and Spain, or Germany and Spain, have an official history fight of their own is still an empty cell. That cell is Q7.

## 5. The victors’ version

**Documented cases where the winner’s sentence was official, and a later sentence replaced it or still fights it.**

Katyn is the cleanest. The men were killed in 1940. For fifty years the official Soviet account put the killing on Germany. On **13 April 1990** Moscow admitted the Soviet responsibility. The admission did not end the diplomatic fight. Row 12.

The Polish risings of 1830–31 and 1863 were won by Russia. The official word on the loser was rebellion. The restored Polish state after 1918, and again after 1989, teaches them as national wars. Rows 1 and 3.

Article 231 is the victors’ sentence in a treaty the defeated state signed. Row 9. Alsace-Lorraine is the victors’ border, taught in two languages until 1918 moved it again. Row 6. San Stefano’s map lasted until Berlin rewrote it the same year. Row 7. A victor’s history is not even stable against the other victors.

**Author (AU-089).** “Written by the victors” is the right description where the official text and the later replacement can both be dated. It is not yet a rate. This pass has four sharp replacements (Katyn, the two Polish risings as a pair, Article 231’s afterlife, Alsace-Lorraine’s return). It does not have a denominator of “all history textbooks.” Q2 is that denominator.

## 6. Poland — layers on one people

Poland is the Eastern European specimen because the same country was overwritten by more than one neighbor, and then retold. These layers are not extra rows in the 17. They are the count of versions on one people. [Redaction](/docs/REDACTION.md) is the name for a version that drops the previous one. Here the versions are public and successive.

| Layer | When | Who held the pen | What the official story became |
|-------|------|------------------|--------------------------------|
| 1 | 1772, 1793, 1795 | Russia, Prussia, and Austria | The commonwealth is divided. Three states teach three possessions. Before the window |
| 2 | 1830–31 | Russia | The November Uprising is a revolt. Nicholas I remains the sovereign |
| 3 | 1863 | Russia, with Prussia’s convention of **8 February 1863** | The January Uprising is a revolt. A second empire signs the pursuit |
| 4 | 1918–21 | The restored Polish republic and Soviet Russia | A state again, then a war over the border. Row 10 |
| 5 | 1939 | Germany and the Soviet Union | Two occupations, two official erasures, one season. Row 11 |
| 6 | 1940–90 | The Soviet Union | Katyn blamed on Germany, then admitted. Row 12 |
| 7 | 1945–89 | The communist state | 1945 as liberation. The Western and later Polish story says a new occupation. Row 13 |
| 8 | After 1989 | The Polish republic, still in dispute with Russia | Martyrdom and Katyn as the public canon. Russia has not adopted that canon. [Siddi 2012](http://www.exact.uni-koeln.de/fileadmin/home/siddim/Studia_Diplomatica_LXV-4__2012__Siddi.pdf) |

**Eight layers. Three empires on the first one.** The retelling after 1989 is real, and it is not peace about the past. Row 12 is still a foreign-policy fight.

**Author.** “Erased” here means the previous official story was replaced, not that every local memory died. The claim that Eastern Europe as a whole matches this stack, people by people, is not yet counted. Q3 is the next country, with the same layer rules. Ukraine’s famine row and the 2022 war are the obvious second stack. They are not written out as eight layers in this pass.

## 7. China — two schools, not a state fight

This section is not in the 17. No treaty in this file has China and a European state signing opposite histories of the same dynasty. What is in hand is a fight between historians about whether early China grew up alone.

**Older school. Documented as a lecture, not as a current consensus.** David Keightley treated geographic isolation as part of how early Chinese culture formed: no foreign invasion on the scale of a dark-age break. Robert Eno’s teaching text says the deserts, the Tibetan plateau, the Himalaya, and the Pacific left ancient China in effective cultural isolation, so that China and the Mediterranean might as well have been on different planets. The same text says China was not sealed off from the nomads on its own steppe. [Eno, via the geography compilation](https://factsanddetails.com/china/cat2/4sub7/item2835.html). [Keightley, Education About Asia](https://www.educationaboutasia.org/article/id/603/).

**Newer school. Documented as the rejection of that picture.** Victor Mair and co-authors say the long-held belief that Chinese civilization developed in isolation has been challenged, and that contacts by land and sea are older than the Silk Road. [Mair, *Traditional China in Asian and World History*](https://www.academia.edu/14372552/Traditional_China_in_Asian_and_World_History_With_Victor_H_Mair_). A Cambridge Element on early Chinese civilizations says the isolated-until-the-Silk-Road perception has been discarded, and names the steppe on China’s northern border as a road into Central Asia.

The steppe is the connection the isolation lecture thins out. Under the Mongols, the Yuan dynasty in China and the Golden Horde on the rivers of Russia were parts of one imperial order. That is ordinary textbook history. A lecture that stops China at the desert has to step over it.

**Author (AU-089), and already the direction of [AU-043](/history/chronology/investigations/cherokee-moors-lenape-delaware-moor-label-investigation.md).** The isolation story is one way a connection to Russia, and to the Horde, gets dropped. Eno and Keightley do not say they are hiding Russia. They say geography. The motive is the author’s. Current archaeologists who put the steppe back are not the school he is describing. The school he is describing is the older one, and it is citable.

## 10. Weak points

- Row 10’s “each side’s textbook” is not yet quoted.

France against Spain, and Germany against Spain, are not in the 17. That absence is Q7, not a finding that those pairs agree.
