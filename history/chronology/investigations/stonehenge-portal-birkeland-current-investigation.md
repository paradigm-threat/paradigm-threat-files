## 5. Twisted pair — Bell (1881) vs Cat 6 vs Stonehenge

### 5.1 What Bell discovered

Alexander Graham Bell’s **US Patent 244,426** (**1881**, “Telephone-circuit”) introduced **twisted pair** wiring: two conductors **helically twisted** so each wire occupies **equivalent inductive relation** to external fields—**crosstalk** and **induced noise** drop because disturbances arrive as **common-mode** and **cancel** at the receiver.

Sources: [US244426A — Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US244426); [Twisted pair — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_pair).

Bell’s insight was **geometric**: **rotation along the run** beats **parallel untwisted** runs for **signal clarity**. By **1900** the American telephone plant was largely **twisted pair** or **transposed open wire**.

### 5.2 Cat 6 as industrial miniaturization

Modern **Category 6** (and higher) cable **inherits** the same principle at **manufactured twist rates** (tens of twists per meter), **pair-to-pair** different pitch, and **impedance control**—enabling **hundreds of MHz** of **clean bandwidth** on copper.

| **Cat 6 pair** | Millimeter-scale **helical** copper | **Reject EMI**; **raise SNR**; **carry data** |

| **Stonehenge ring** | Meter-scale **raised circular** stone **torus** | **Couple** telluric + sky column; **stabilize** portal **rotation** |

Cat 6 is **Bell’s patent in a jacket**; Stonehenge is **Bell’s patent in sarsen**—aimed at **inter-world bandwidth**, not **telephone calls**.
