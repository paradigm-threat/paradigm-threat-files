## 1. Author thesis — person → word → rebrand → forget

When establishment history **rewrote** the Rus–Horde and Crusade layers, **personal names** in oral memory could:

1. **Crystallize** into **nouns**, **surnames**, or **place-names**
2. **Detach** from the **forgotten event** via **Protestant / Latin** spelling drift (**Lane A**)
3. **Re-attach** to a **modern celebrity**, **brand**, or **commodity** (actor, food company, city) — **oscillating** public memory  
4. **Toxic-name whitewash (author):** vilified **historical** bearer of a name → **beloved modern celebrity** with the **same** surname or given name — establishment **re-popularizes** the token so the old charge cannot stick (flagship: **Francis Bacon** → **Kevin Bacon**; see §2c)  
5. **Seal** under **handbook etymology** (“from Old French…”, “from Germanic…”) with **no person** in the chain

The file began as a search lane, and an earlier limit said it did **not** claim every word was an eponym. On **24 October 2026** the author replaced that limit. The default is now the family name. A word coined by a profession has to be shown as the exception. [§5](#5-family-name-as-the-default-au-081) holds that claim, the Poe row, and the lists.

## 5. Family name as the default (AU-081)

### 5.0 Primary frame — final Latin is not ancient (AU-028)

**Author, 24 October 2026, stated as the primary theory:**

> My primary theory is that Latin is not nearly as old as it's claimed to be. If that's true, then a lot of old Latin words were coined during medieval times, during events. Or even possibly later. There's vulgar Latin, and then there's the final Latin. We can assume that the final version of Latin was not ancient at all, and that that is one of the major deceptions of the Scaligerian timeline.

**Documented.** Handbooks already separate vulgar Latin, the spoken language, from classical Latin, the written standard they date to the late Republic and the early Empire. Medieval Latin kept receiving new words for the church, the law, and the schools. Lorenzo Valla’s *Elegantiae linguae Latinae*, written in the 1440s and printed in 1471, called medieval Latin corrupt and rebuilt a correct Latin from selected books. Joseph Scaliger’s *De emendatione temporum* (1583) and *Thesaurus temporum* (1606) are the chronology that dates that standard to antiquity.

**Author.** “Old Latin words,” in the sentence above, means words the dictionary treats as ancient because they are Latin. It does not mean only the handbook stage called Old Latin, the Latin of Plautus. **Final Latin** is the author’s name for the finished textbook version. That version was not ancient. Many of its words were coined at medieval events, and some later, and the finished language was then placed in a remote past. That placement is one of the major deceptions of the Scaligerian timeline. The reader essay states the same split in [Latin as Breakaway Language](/history/chronology/the-hidden-history-of-words.md).

The family-name default below is the next step, not a second origin story. A handbook line “from Latin,” or “from Greek” dated by the same timeline, is the filing. The family remembered for the event is what the filing covers.

**Author, 24 October 2026, the family-name default under that filing:**

> Nearly all words that we know today came not from being coined by events or by professionals, but from family names that were remembered for events. Patsy and moron are the kind of family name that became a word later. The pattern fits almost completely, and the exceptions are few.
>
> Edgar Allan Poe’s family affected the word poet and changed its meaning. The family name Poe describes somebody proud, flamboyant, or vain, and later became poet. The common thing is major influence. That does not mean the person was telling poetry. It does not mean they wrote anything down. The family name and the word poet are both associated with a massive influence a family had at some point. Speeches, money, marriage, or an actual poem could all be the instrument.
>
> These people tended to be the keepers of the great secrets of the past, in the original literature. Burns was that, in public. The family name was a harbinger of the only public knowledge available. The city in the sea is the specimen. Poe was aware of the real context. He condensed it into a poem. He held the first impression many people would ever have of the topic.

**Handbook text, kept on the row.** Greek ποιητής (*poiētēs*) is “maker,” from ποιέω, “to make.” Latin *poeta* is the form the handbooks place centuries before Edgar Allan Poe was born on 19 January 1809. French *morgue* is a haughty look, then the prison room where new prisoners were stared at so the guards would know their faces, then the room where unnamed bodies were laid out to be recognized. *Moron* as a clinical grade was proposed from Greek μωρός (*mōros*), “foolish,” and the American Association for the Study of the Feeble-Minded adopted Henry H. Goddard’s term in 1910.

Those lines do not all work the same way. The “centuries before 1809” on *poeta* are Scaligerian centuries. If final Latin, and the Greek filed on the same timeline, were finished in the Middle Ages or later, that priority is the deception named above, not a measured exception. The 1910 vote is a modern dated act. It can still be a professional coinage of the clinical word. It does not decide whether Greek *mōros* was already a late filing of an older name. French *morgue* is a vernacular history of a room. It is not a Latin root. It stays as the joining of a haughty look and the dead-room, which [§5.1](#51-morgan-morgue-and-mor) uses.

**Author, refined.** Edgar Allan Poe did not invent the syllable. The claim is that a house named Poe carried pride and display, and that the public word for a massive influence over what people are allowed to know settled on poet. Verse is one instrument. It is not the definition. [The city in the sea](/history/mudflood/the-city-in-the-sea.md) is the late specimen: he had the sinking, the covered sky, and the light from the sea, and the poem is still the first form many readers meet. **Burns**, as the author named it, is the public face of the same keeping. Robert Burns is the poet that name points to. The secrets stay with the house. The name is what the public is given.

### 5.1 Morgan, morgue, and mor

Two readings, both open. They are not the same claim.

1. A **Morgan** house sat where the unnamed dead were recognized, and the family name became **morgue**.
2. The syllable **mor** is a death family. **Moran**, **Morgan**, **morgue**, **moron**, Latin *mors*, all show up when the event is death. The common piece is the sound, not one household inventing the French room.

The French word already joins the two senses the author split: a haughty stare, and the room where a face is matched to a body. Pride and the dead-room are one word in that language before any Morgan genealogy is attached. **Moran** is already a seed row in [§2](#2-candidate-table-seed--open). **Moron** has a professional baptism in 1910. That date is an exception on the record. It does not decide whether Greek *mōros* was itself a family name that had already been forgotten.
