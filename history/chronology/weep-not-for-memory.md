# Weep Not for Memory

*Teaching history is a double-edged sword, and it can hurt as much as it can heal*

The three paths are the truth, the fiction, and the power that comes from knowing the truth and operating by the strict guidelines of the fiction. The left door is the truth. Truth is the natural path to peace, and it has been unavailable through the last few centuries. Attempting it in this lifetime brings a fierce reaction from the environment, from peers, and from governments. It is simply not an option yet. The middle door is the fiction, the lie we were taught in history class. That lie leads directly to the repeating of mistakes like a world war. The right door is knowing the truth and operating by the strict guidelines of the fiction, in order to avoid collateral damage and achieve political victories and the important goals that actually matter.

He has not subscribed to Fomenko's New Chronology. The school dates were set in 1583 and 1627 by men who were arguing with each other, and the other account of the Horde stays unsaid. Putin told Emir Kusturica that Moscow is the Third Rome, and that a fourth there shall not be. It inspired me to write an article about the dangers of true history.

## This morning

["Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be"](https://rt-english.com/russia/649179-moscow-third-rome-putin-kusturica/) is the headline on the English article that carried the interview overnight. Channel One aired it on 20 November 2026, for Patriarch Kirill’s 80th birthday. RT updated the piece in the early hours of 21 November, and I read it this morning. The Kremlin spokesman said the conversation was recorded before a foreign trip, so the air date is the release.

The [English transcript](http://www.en.special.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/81318) has the walk he gave Kusturica:

> I am saying it with such confidence not just because the Russian theological and philosophical thought is centred around one concept that dates centuries back: Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be.
>
> What does it mean? It means that Christianity came into existence in ancient Rome, then Rome fell, and Christianity moved to Byzantium, the Eastern Roman Empire. Constantine became the first emperor to convert to Christianity. The Byzantine Empire also fell, but by that time, Christianity had already spread to Russian soil and reached Moscow. Thus, Moscow inherited this faith and these traditions. In a way, it became the Third Rome, the spiritual keeper of those holy values, that heritage.
>
> Yet this theological, or philosophic, idea, which, as I said, Russia has cherished for ages, is not the main point here. It is all about today.

He then put the weight on the present. People from Europe, he said, come to Russia to keep their families and their traditional faith. The ark is not only for Orthodox Christians, and not only for Christians. He named Buddhism, Judaism, Islam, and Orthodoxy together. “Russian civilization never took over others,” he said, and he called that a unique feature. “We are not trying to limit anyone in anything.” Western churches, he said, have departed from the canons, and that departure has not strengthened them.

He has not subscribed to Anatoly Fomenko's New Chronology. I have not found him saying Fomenko's name, or the name of the chronology. He gave the old church succession, and he said the formula is not the main point.

## Lesser known phrases

He said Moscow is the Third Rome, and a fourth there shall not be, and he said the formula is not the main point. The chronology he did not name was lesser known. A hollow Earth, or a civilization on Mars, drew the same dismissal. "Deep state" is another name that was lesser known, and then became a party name. A [history of the phrase](https://warontherocks.com/how-the-deep-state-came-to-america-a-history/) says Peter Dale Scott used it for the United States in *The Road to 9/11* in 2007, and that daily American news picked the phrase up in February 2017. A [Monmouth poll](https://www.monmouth.edu/polling-institute/reports/monmouthpoll_us_031918/) of 803 adults, taken from 2 to 5 March 2018, asked who knew the name. Thirteen percent said they were very familiar with it, 24 percent somewhat familiar, and 63 percent not familiar. The same poll then described unelected government and military officials who secretly direct national policy. Twenty-seven percent said that group definitely exists, and 47 percent said it probably does.

The [Library of Congress guide](https://guides.loc.gov/hollow-earth/additional-works) lists Martin Gardner's 1957 book, which put the hollow Earth in one chapter with the flat Earth. On 14 July 1965, [Mariner 4](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-4/) returned the first close pictures of Mars. NASA's account says the cratered surface ended the expectation of a lost civilization there. [Astronomers](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/what-did-astronomers-believe-about-mars-before-the-space-age) had already left Percival Lowell's canals behind from about 1909. A [2022 review of repeated polls](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0270429) found no steady rise in the share of people who believe particular claims of this kind. That review's search of the Roper archive found 10 such national questions from before 2010. The dismissal was already the record. The wide audience is the newer part.

By now people have heard all of these topics through some compromised channels. Those channels demand allegiance to certain agendas, and people cannot separate the agenda from the topic anymore. Everything has become red versus blue. Today the same talk can cost the job, the friendship, the relationship, and the family table. Truth in this lifetime is dangerous. It gets a person in trouble with peers, with parents, with the person they are with, with friends, with associates, and with coworkers. He could say Moscow is the Third Rome. The chronology he left off is one of the names that still costs the room.

## What the wars spent

Countries gave up the virtuous history that had defined them. That history was the account of their own conduct, and people knew the country by it. The wars that followed spent those virtues and nearly eliminated them. The story is still told, and the conduct it praised is gone. Freedom of speech is one of the few I still count, and I count it in America.

Russia's laws forbid kinds of speech. On 4 March 2022 the state made it a crime to publicly spread what the statute calls [knowingly false information about the armed forces](https://kremlin.ru/acts/bank/47616). Public actions aimed at discrediting that use begin as an administrative case, and a repeat of them is a crime. On 14 July 2022 a separate law put a person under foreign influence on a [register](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_421788/) when that person engages in political activity or spreads material to an unlimited audience. The register took effect on 1 December 2022. The person on it is marked. In America the deep-state presentation is a social cost. In Russia a public sentence the state calls false, when the sentence is about the army, is a charge under the March law. That is the difference in the virtue I still count.

## What the truth does

What the truth is, and who is right, does not move the room. A story about space lizards and a story about a hidden Russian chronology get the same reaction. The knee-jerk is the loss. The room drops the person either way, lizards or chronology. The truth causes a knee-jerk in real time. The job, the friendship, and the family table move in that moment. That is what the truth does. Thom Yorke sings the same charge in the refrain of ["Ful Stop,"](https://radiohead.bandcamp.com/track/ful-stop) on Radiohead's *A Moon Shaped Pool*, released on 8 May 2016. The line is "Truth will mess you up," and he sings it over and over. Right now the truth does more damage than good. A true sentence that sets the reaction off hurts people on the spot, and that hurt is larger than the good of having said it. True things get suppressed by bad people, and by good people who are trying not to cause that damage. A good person can see the knee-jerk coming. The damage they are refusing is the job, the friendship, and the family table. They know the sentence, and they will not pay the cost of saying it. Bad people want the same sentence gone for their own reason. The quiet from the good people is enough to keep it out of the room.

The result is a species pointed at the future only. People can sit and wait. They cannot talk about the past, and they cannot talk about the truth, without hurting someone.

## The old dates

Anatoly Fomenko is a Russian mathematician. His New Chronology says the famous ancient empires are later events stacked on the wrong years. A war, a reign, or a city that the schoolbook puts in antiquity is, on his pages, a medieval event written again and moved backward. A Rus-Horde empire, in the same account, held a much wider world than the schoolbook yoke.

![Anatoly Fomenko, the mathematician behind New Chronology](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/anatoly_fomenko.jpg)

The yoke in the schoolbook is a Mongol occupation of the Russian lands. English speakers meet the word Horde as a swarm. Students learn that Russia came out from under that occupation into the modern state. In this account the same word is the Russian empire those occupation stories were taken from. His pages turn the picture over. The Horde is a Russian empire, and its reach runs far past one occupied country. The ancient kingdoms and that empire are the same events under two dates. A person who has never met this can hold that much and follow the rest. The tables that shift a given battle by a set number of centuries are a separate job.

A date is a claim about who came first. If Rome is ancient and the Horde is medieval, Rome acts and the Horde inherits, and the stories about law, faith, and war run from the first to the second. If the ancient Rome is a copy of a later empire, the inheritance runs the other way. The people called the heirs become the source, and the people called the source become the copy. Who followed whom is the chain, and a year that is off by centuries changes the chain.

In the Russian telling that order is already understood. It was a long fight of houses over the seat. Who married whom, who took the seat, who was pushed out, who came back under another name. People know that kind of succession from Game of Thrones, and the Russian version runs a long time. The audience that grew up on that telling already has the order. They do not need a new count of centuries to know who followed whom.

They still cite the old dates. The lesson in the date is the part they want, and the lesson holds if the event was early and it holds if the event was late. A speaker can give the lesson in the year the schoolbook prints. The same speaker can give the lesson and skip the year. Once the order of the houses is known, the year-number is not what carries the meaning.

Putin can skip the dates. The sentence from this morning is the lesson. The years are what he left off the podium.

## Europe and Asia

Official, on this count, means a war, a treaty, a statute, or a government refusing the other country's account. A first count of those fights since 1815 comes to 17. Russia or the Soviet Union is a party in 10 of them. Two more are next door, and Russia does not sign them. The Balkan states and the Ottomans fought over the same ground in 1912 and 1913. Poland and Ukraine do not share one official account of the Volhynia massacres of 1943 to 1945.

The other five are inside the West. Prussia and Austria fought Denmark in 1864. Prussia fought Austria in 1866 over who would lead Germany. France and Germany fought over Alsace-Lorraine, and the Treaty of Frankfurt on 10 May 1871 wrote the German border. On 28 June 1919 the Allies wrote that Germany and its allies were responsible for the war. Soviet Russia was not at Versailles. Spain's fight is Spaniards against each other. The [memory law of 26 December 2007](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2007-22296) and the [memory law of 20 October 2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-17099) are parties inside one country fighting over the Civil War and Franco. France against Spain, and Germany against Spain, are still an empty place on the list.

France and Germany treated their own split as a problem to close. On 21 January 2003, the fortieth anniversary of the Élysée Treaty, students asked Jacques Chirac and Gerhard Schröder for [one history book with the same pages in both countries](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/4972922.stm). The first volume came out in 2006. The shared book is the two states admitting the separate books were the problem. It leaves 1871 and 1919 where they were.

China's place is a different kind of fight. No Chinese government and European government on this list sign opposite histories of the same dynasty. A Chinese statute against a European account of events outside China is not in the 17. The written fight is among historians, about whether early China grew up alone. [David Keightley](https://www.educationaboutasia.org/article/id/603/) treated the geography as part of how early Chinese culture formed, with no foreign break on the scale of a dark age. [Robert Eno](https://factsanddetails.com/china/cat2/4sub7/item2835.html) taught that the deserts, the Tibetan plateau, the Himalaya, and the Pacific left ancient China effectively cut off, so that China and the Mediterranean might as well have been different planets. However, the same text says China was not sealed off from the nomads on its own steppe. [Victor Mair](https://www.academia.edu/14372552/Traditional_China_in_Asian_and_World_History_With_Victor_H_Mair_) and the newer school say the isolation claim does not hold, and that the steppe on China's northern border is a road into Central Asia. Under the Mongols, the Yuan dynasty in China and the Golden Horde on the rivers of Russia were parts of one imperial order. A lecture that stops China at the desert leaves that order out. I read the older lecture as a cut in the connection to Russia. Keightley and Eno say geography.

Britain is on a side that was fought. In the Crimean War, from 1853 to 1856, Russia fought France, Britain, the Ottoman Empire, and Sardinia over the Ottoman lands, the Black Sea, and the holy places. Prussia stayed out. Austria did not back Russia. That is Britain's disagreement on this list. The wider schoolbook English speakers use today was not checked country by country. France and Germany already disagree with each other on the same list.

## The dates they share

The seventeen are wars, borders, and the meaning of a war. A grand empire that held the Stan countries, China, and Iran is not among them. Kazakhstan claims the Golden Horde as its own statehood. On 26 August 2005, at Kazan's thousandth anniversary, [Putin](http://en.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/23140) said a number of Russian historians already count that Horde as part of Russia's history, and that the Russian state took the Horde's tax, military, and administrative offices. He named Lev Gumilev on the culture of the steppe. Iran's encyclopaedia and the treaties of 1813 and 1828 teach a transfer of Caucasian lands. The Yuan dynasty and the Horde are taught as one Mongol order. [Kazakh and Cossack](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Cossack) are the same Turkic word, and the Persian ending means the place. Fomenko reads Kazakhstan as the Cossack camp of a Russian Horde. Putin has not said that. Kazakhstan has not said that.

The dates in the schoolbook were built in that early modern window, and the builders were on opposite sides. Joseph Scaliger published *De emendatione temporum* in Paris in 1583. Denis Petau, a Jesuit, published *De doctrina temporum* in Paris in 1627 as a rebuttal of Scaliger, because a Catholic answer was wanted to a Protestant chronology. [A 2025 account of that rebuttal](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783112223345-004) is the source for the motive. The governments in this count still teach the dates that came out of that work. They do not teach the other empire.

I read the book they share as a long record of slaughter, repeated, for greed, for tyranny, and for conquest. I place the loss of a different account in the Reformation, the Renaissance, and the years after the Inquisition. Those centuries hold Scaliger and Petau. They do not hold a document, in this pass, of a better history being burned. The fire of 12 January 1682 burned the notes of noble precedence quarrels. It is not, on the council's own act, a Cossack archive.

Lewis Milestone's *[All Quiet on the Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Quiet_on_the_Western_Front_(1930_film))* (1930) has two classrooms. It opens on the first. A parade is passing the window. Professor Kantorek tells the boys that the war is their duty to the fatherland. The class stands and enlists. The film follows them to the front. Later Paul comes home on leave. Kantorek puts him in front of a new class and asks him to send them too. Paul tells the children that the first bombardment taught them otherwise. The teacher and the boys call him a coward.

Edward Berger's [2022 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Quiet_on_the_Western_Front_(2022_film)) leaves both classrooms out. A [2024 comparison](https://doi.org/10.17680/erciyesiletisim.1352595) of the two films says the enlistment speech and Paul's answer on leave are absent from the remake, and that the 1930 leave speech is the criticism of volunteering. Jamelle Bouie, in the New York Times on 17 December 2022, wrote that cutting the return home turns the story from a condemnation of the war into a simpler picture of soldiers and the leaders who sent them. The same reception notes call the 2022 picture a spectacle of blood. Peter Bradshaw, in the Guardian on 12 October 2022, called it an anti-war nightmare of bloodshed and chaos.

I find that cut highly suspicious, and the later scene most of all. The lesson is the part a soldier brings back to the next children. Leaving both scenes out is the clearest specimen I have that history is being taught so the war will repeat, by dropping the lesson. The remake keeps the slaughter and drops the hour when someone says what it did. The 2022 film is so horrible, and so bloody, that I read it as warmongering. It is not a caution. I read the two 1930 rooms as the school preparing the children to repeat the war, and the 2022 cut as the same job with the lesson removed. An academy that keeps this agenda will not teach the history that could make peace. That history is one origin for the species, and a time when it agreed. The academy hides that account. The history it teaches is the other one, a hundred thousand years of war that has never stopped, with no end in sight.

A person can see the other account and still not say it in a public room. The cost is already on this page. A public sentence about the Russian army that the state calls false is a charge under the law of 4 March 2022. A president who says the old empire was Russian hands the other capital the map. The job, the friendship, and the family table move in the room where the sentence is said. Saying the whole of it at once is the dangerous act. The only honest way I see to disclose it is obscure, and extremely gradual. It has to sit for years, the way a seed sits until the sun is on it again.

## Why he does not take the chronology

I had thought Fomenko coined "Moscow, the Third Rome," and that he was the first, besides the sources he used, to say there was more than one Rome. That guess does not hold. A monk at Pskov, [Filofei](https://ir.uiowa.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1019&context=history_pubs), wrote the line at the end of 1523 or the start of 1524, in a letter to the grand prince's secretary: two Romes have fallen, a third stands, and a fourth there shall not be. Constantinople was already called New Rome in 381. Putin's walk matches that letter. Faith moved from ancient Rome to Byzantium to Moscow, and Moscow keeps it. The slogan is centuries older than Fomenko's books.

Fomenko’s several Romes are a different object. He stacks the textbook empires on later centuries, and he puts a Tsar’s Rome between the Oka and the Volga, in the Russian heartland. A person who has read him can hear Putin’s slogan as a memory of that reconstruction. However, the sentence Putin spoke to Kusturica is the monk’s succession. It is not a subscription to the new dates.

The line does two jobs at once. It keeps Russian history close. It also shows the limit on the man who says it. He cannot say the state supports New Chronology. If he does, the West has a louder charge that Russia means to take Europe, to invade, to claim the ground. An open endorsement would turn that charge into a history the state had just accepted. The slogan lets him hold the maximal phrase, and the standard ancient dates let him stop before the map. The dispute is not a border, and a map cannot show it. The fuller account stays contained, because saying it is the dangerous act. The Third Rome line is the part he can wield. It holds if the years are the schoolbook's, and it holds if the years are the other count. A listener who rejects the new chronology still has the monk's sentence in front of him. I hold that he cannot really deny it. The years are what Putin leaves off.

## Three doors

I picture the choice as three doors, and only three.

The first door is the truth. Truth is the natural path to peace, and it has been unavailable through the last few centuries, and it is unavailable now. Attempting it in this lifetime brings a fierce reaction from the environment, from peers, and from governments. It is simply not an option yet. The country beyond the door is quiet. The broken stone is the picture of a path that cannot be walked.

The second door is the fiction, the lie we were taught in history class. It leads directly to the repeating of mistakes like a world war. It stands open, and it is easy. The last few centuries have used it widely. A redacted history sends the children, as Kantorek's class was sent, and the 2022 film keeps the blood and drops the lesson. The same redaction consolidates the power of the people who hold the schoolbook. I read that door as the one this age walks through.

The third door is the power that comes from knowing the truth and staying inside the guardrails of the fiction. It is shut and locked. A great power glows behind it and does not come out. The truth is wielded there in a small enclosed way. The sentence that gets through is one an enemy cannot answer, as the Third Rome line cannot be answered by picking a timeline. I hold that this is how the peace of the first door can be reached. That peace has not arrived. The third door is the method that does not require opening the first.

In September 2020 [Sergey Glazyev](https://www.apn-spb.ru/opinions/article32413.htm) did say it. He was a minister of the Eurasian Economic Commission, and he had been an economic adviser to the president until 2019. He wrote that a Rus-Horde, not Mongol nomads, held Eurasia in the fourteenth through sixteenth centuries, and that New Chronology gives a logical basis for restoring the historical memory of the Russian world. In September 2009, when Dmitry Medvedev was president, he told cultural workers in Veliky Novgorod that innovative history must not become the textbook. [Garry Kasparov](https://www.chesshistory.com/winter/extra/newchronology.html), in a 1999 foreword, wrote that the attack on the standard dates is solid and that the reconstructions can still be challenged. He was not a head of government. Putin has the slogan. He does not have Glazyev’s subscription.

When he does early history for a Western audience, the frame is the textbook. In February 2024, explaining the lecture he gave Tucker Carlson, he told Pavel Zarubin that he had used the [Norman theory from 862](https://ria.ru/20240218/intervyu-1928042728.html), and that the roots go deeper, and that Lomonosov criticized that theory. He did not move the ancient dates.

The year-number does a different job at a peace table. A person who says the ancient events happened in the Middle Ages is easy to dismiss. The fight about the timeline then sits in the way of the peace. If the speaker says the old empire was a later Russian one, the other side does not have to answer the peace. It can answer the map. The accusation is that Russia wants the ground the chronology would hand it, which is something other than peace. In 2009 Fomenko himself said the work stops before contemporary politics, and that [territorial arguments still rest on who lived somewhere first](https://chronologia.org/interview/090713vvestnik.html). Leaving the year off is how a president stays in the talk.

Russia has to be ready to work with the rest of the world in a non-threatening, non-authoritarian, and non-patronizing way. Sales and commerce can continue. Teaching another country its own history is not a job for the Russian state. Private people who live in those countries can carry the chronology. A head of government who carries it hands the other side the sentence. The leaders are hamstrung. He names the lesson and skips the dates, because the order of the houses is already the Russian telling, and the years are what another capital can use.

Trump and much of the GOP do the same thing with America. The public sentence is that the country is the golden case of virtue and human rights. It is, for the most part. Those virtues have been cut down since the Civil War. Saying the golden sentence anyway sets the standard and hands the other side a psychological defeat. It is not sustainable as a description. The [sin eater](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-sin-eater) is the name for the man who eats that contradiction and leaves the patriotic case intact, so the next president can be more honest without losing the base. Putin's Third Rome paragraph is the same job in the other capital: the continuity stays maximal, and the correction stays unspoken.

## Security is still in the future

People are told their memories are wrong. The chronology is one of those memories. Calling it wrong takes the past off the list of things a person may insist on, so the species is pointed forward even while that future is being fought over. The security they are promised sits in that future, and the future is not here yet. Quitting is available. A person can stop the argument and leave the room. Tuning out is available. A person can stop taking in the news of the fight. Relaxing is not available, and retiring is not available. Both assume the peace has arrived and the person may live in it. That peace is still the prize under dispute. The fight stays hot, and it stays global.
