*Teaching history is a double-edged sword, and it can hurt as much as it can heal*

[Putin told Emir Kusturica](https://rt-english.com/russia/649179-moscow-third-rome-putin-kusturica/) that Russian thought had for centuries held one concept: ["Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be."](http://www.en.special.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/81318) He then named Rome, Byzantium, and Moscow, and called Moscow "the spiritual keeper" of that heritage. He did not name which century the events happened. He is not describing movie Rome. He is saying that Russia today still embodies the spiritual center of a Christian empire that once held together — the part of the story that is almost never talked about. He did not suggest, as Fomenko has, that Russia's Rome predates Italy's Rome on a corrected New Chronology timeline. The key word he used was "spiritual," and that word represents the real fight within our species — not physical, and not over "the truth." We are in a spiritual war. Putin's remark to Emir Kusturica is what got me writing about the cost of saying the truth.

Knowing the real timeline, Fomenko's, does not unlock the power of truth. Knowing the sequence of events does, regardless of which century the West insists they happened in. The West needs everyone to believe Rome traces back to Italy in ancient times as a starting point, so that they were first, and the Russia that starts in the 11th century is next. If the secret, that history was duplicated, is left out of the conversation entirely, one can have a sustainable and powerful debate strategy that could outwit any westerner who tries to dislodge the connection from Rome to Christianity to Russia. That is why Putin makes the spiritual Rome claim. The actual does not matter. What matters is the 'why', the real sequence of events from the 11th century until now, which explains the 'why'. Only in the West is history taught out of order, and in spots isolated by countries. Why? It is the only way to hide the lessons. It is deliberate. What else could it be?

## The Three Paths for Historians

Putin's intent is to save Mother Russia. ## The Spiritual Third Rome

["Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be"](https://rt-english.com/russia/649179-moscow-third-rome-putin-kusturica/) is the headline on the English article that carried the interview. Channel One aired it on 20 November 2026, for Patriarch Kirill’s 80th birthday. RT updated the piece in the early hours of 21 November, and I read it that morning. The Kremlin spokesman said the conversation was recorded before a foreign trip, so the air date is the release.

The [English transcript](http://www.en.special.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/81318) has what he told Kusturica:

> I am saying it with such confidence not just because the Russian theological and philosophical thought is centred around one concept that dates centuries back: Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be.
>
> What does it mean? It means that Christianity came into existence in ancient Rome, then Rome fell, and Christianity moved to Byzantium, the Eastern Roman Empire. Constantine became the first emperor to convert to Christianity. The Byzantine Empire also fell, but by that time, Christianity had already spread to Russian soil and reached Moscow. Thus, Moscow inherited this faith and these traditions. In a way, it became the Third Rome, the spiritual keeper of those holy values, that heritage.
>
> Yet this theological, or philosophic, idea, which, as I said, Russia has cherished for ages, is not the main point here. It is all about today.

He then put the weight on the present. People from Europe, he said, come to Russia to keep their families and their traditional faith. The ark is not only for Orthodox Christians, and not only for Christians. He named Buddhism, Judaism, Islam, and Orthodoxy together. “Russian civilization never took over others,” he said, and he called that a unique feature. “We are not trying to limit anyone in anything.” Western churches, he said, have departed from the canons, and that departure has not strengthened them.

What he is naming is larger than the costume Rome of film. The Roman world became Christian. It became a vast Christian empire. Schoolbooks say that empire did not span the globe. Count Russia inside it, and the globe claim is easier to hold. Count the West Coast holdings where Russia later had a claim — not the 1492 Atlantic landings — and the same empire reaches farther still. I read that Christianity once held together and did not fight itself. Then it split into factions that are at war today. Putin’s claim is that the spiritual center did not move to someone else’s capital. It remains in Russia. That is the big piece for Christians, for historians, and for anyone who cares why the present looks the way it does: the peace of a united Christian order broke, and not every split was even or fair. Some blows came from outside the kingdom to invade it. Protestantism is the clear case. Russia is the place where old Orthodox tradition stayed closest to intact. There are exceptions. Those exceptions still pale beside the religions built outside Russia on redaction, deception, confusion, and mockery.

Sebastian Brant’s *[Ship of Fools](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/a-tale-told-by-an-idiot.md)* is a specimen of that East–West fight over Christianity — ridicule aimed at the imperial order until the order could be split. The odd modern result is that the West may be practicing not the original Christianity, but the mockery version that was used to attack it. In Fomenko’s terms, that is Protestant Christianity used against the royal, court-and-king Christianity, pushed by successive waves of mass publishing and stories in the line of *The Emperor’s New Clothes*. Ask whether America was founded on Christian principles and the room splits. As an American I know how hard that argument is to win, even when the record can be shown. Most people hear flight from persecution and stop there. The flight was real. It was also Christianity already breaking into pieces and fighting itself. Putin’s “spiritual keeper” line is aimed at that puzzle: the center that did not relocate.

Putin's short slogan is the older Third Rome teaching, compressed. At the end of 1523 or the start of 1524, in a letter to the grand prince's secretary, a monk at Pskov, [Filofei](https://ir.uiowa.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1019&context=history_pubs), wrote: "two Romes have fallen, a third stands, and a fourth there shall not be." Constantinople was already called New Rome in 381. He has not subscribed to Anatoly Fomenko's New Chronology. He recited the old slogan as a concept that "dates centuries back," walked Rome to Byzantium to Moscow, and said the formula is not the main point. He did not name Filofei.

## The phantom thousand years

[Fomenko's New Chronology](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/page.md) treats a large stretch of schoolbook antiquity as a phantom — roughly a thousand years duplicated or inserted so the Christian empire that begins in the eleventh century looks as if it sits on a much older Rome. One of Fomenko's suggestions is that a letter *I*, once written for the name of Jesus (Latin *Iesus*), was later read as the Roman numeral for a thousand. That reading stretched the timeline by ten centuries. On the corrected clock, the abundance that followed a solar Lord — the sun as the Lord who came and opened a new age — belongs to the eleventh century and after, not to a phantom antiquity ten centuries deeper.

That stretch was not an honest clerical slip. It was the path of least resistance for Protestant chronology-builders who needed a foundation from which to dismiss the true history of Europe and Asia. The bias ran against a map whose center ran through Russia. Italy, Palestine, London, and Egypt had to be the source. Russia could not be. On that map, Fomenko's claim that those same events run back to Russia is blasphemy.

That is why the word "spiritual" in Putin's explanation is not ornament. If he embraced New Chronology in public, the West would heap a caravan of debunkability on every statement — Russia claiming the center of a rewritten order, the map charge ready-made. He has spent his life staying out of that danger zone. The Third Rome line he can say. The phantom thousand years he will not.

## Lesser known phrases

That review's search of the Roper archive found 10 such national questions from before 2010. The dismissal was already the record. The wide audience is the newer part. Not everyone has heard of a hollow Earth, and fewer still have heard it outside the same paragraph as the flat Earth. The [Library of Congress guide](https://guides.loc.gov/hollow-earth/additional-works) lists Martin Gardner's 1957 book, which put the hollow Earth in one chapter with the flat Earth. ## The old dates

Anatoly Fomenko is a Russian mathematician. His New Chronology says the famous ancient empires are later events stacked on the wrong years. A war, a reign, or a city that the schoolbook puts in antiquity is, on his pages, a medieval event written again and moved backward. A Rus-Horde empire, in the same account, held a much wider world than the schoolbook yoke.

![Anatoly Fomenko, the mathematician behind New Chronology](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/anatoly_fomenko.jpg)

The yoke in the schoolbook is a Mongol occupation of the Russian lands. English speakers meet the word Horde as a swarm. Students learn that Russia came out from under that occupation into the modern state. In this account the same word is the Russian empire those occupation stories were taken from. His account reverses the schoolbook order. The Horde is a Russian empire, and its reach runs far past one occupied country. The ancient kingdoms and that empire are the same events under two dates. A reader new to the theory can hold that much and follow the rest. The tables that shift a given battle by a set number of centuries are a separate job.

A date is a claim about who came first. If Rome is ancient and the Horde is medieval, Rome is the actor and the Horde is the inheritor, and the stories about law, faith, and war run from the first to the second. If the ancient Rome is a copy of a later empire, the inheritance runs the other way. The people called the heirs become the source, and the people called the source become the copy. Who followed whom is the chain, and a year that is off by centuries changes the chain.

In the Russian telling that order is already understood. It was a long fight of houses over the seat. Who married whom, who took the seat, who was pushed out, who came back under another name. People know that kind of succession from Game of Thrones, and the Russian version runs a long time. The audience that grew up on that telling already has the order. They do not need a new count of centuries to know who followed whom.

They still cite the old dates. The lesson holds if the event was early and it holds if the event was late. Once the order of the houses is known, the year-number is not what carries the meaning.

## Classrooms that prepare the war

Lewis Milestone's *[All Quiet on the Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Quiet_on_the_Western_Front_(1930_film))* (1930) has two classrooms. It opens on the first. A parade is passing the window. Professor Kantorek tells the boys that the war is their duty to the fatherland. The class stands and enlists. The film follows them to the front. Later Paul comes home on leave. Kantorek puts him in front of a new class and asks him to send them too. Paul tells the children that the first bombardment taught them otherwise. The teacher and the boys call him a coward.

Edward Berger's [2022 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Quiet_on_the_Western_Front_(2022_film)) leaves both classrooms out. A [2024 comparison](https://doi.org/10.17680/erciyesiletisim.1352595) of the two films says the enlistment speech and Paul's answer on leave are absent from the remake, and that the 1930 leave speech is the criticism of volunteering. Jamelle Bouie, in the New York Times on 17 December 2022, wrote that cutting the return home turns the story from a condemnation of the war into a simpler picture of soldiers and the leaders who sent them. The same reception notes call the 2022 picture a spectacle of blood. Peter Bradshaw, in the Guardian on 12 October 2022, called it an anti-war nightmare of bloodshed and chaos.

That cut is highly suspicious, and the later scene most of all. The lesson is the part a soldier brings back to the next children. Leaving both scenes out is the clearest specimen on this page that history is being taught so the war will repeat, by dropping the lesson. The remake keeps the slaughter and drops the hour when someone says what it did. The 2022 film is so horrible, and so bloody, that I read it as warmongering. It is not a caution. The two 1930 rooms are the school preparing the children to repeat the war, and the 2022 cut is the same job with the lesson removed. An academy that keeps this agenda will not teach the history that could make peace. That history is one origin for the species, and a time when it agreed. The academy hides that account. The history it teaches is the other one, a hundred thousand years of war that has never stopped, with no end in sight.

A person can see the other account and still not say it in a public room. A president who says the old empire was Russian hands the other capital the map. The disclosure that remains is obscure, and extremely gradual. It has to sit for years, the way a seed sits until the sun is on it again.

## Europe and Asia

Official, on this count, means a war, a treaty, a statute, or a government refusing the other country's account. A first count of those fights since 1815 comes to 17. Russia or the Soviet Union is a party in 10 of them. Two more are next door, and Russia does not sign them. The Balkan states and the Ottomans fought over the same ground in 1912 and 1913. Poland and Ukraine do not share one official account of the Volhynia massacres of 1943 to 1945.

The other five are inside the West. Prussia and Austria fought Denmark in 1864. Prussia fought Austria in 1866 over who would lead Germany. France and Germany fought over Alsace-Lorraine, and the Treaty of Frankfurt on 10 May 1871 wrote the German border. On 28 June 1919 the Allies wrote that Germany and its allies were responsible for the war. Soviet Russia was not at Versailles. Spain's fight is Spaniards against each other. The [memory law of 26 December 2007](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2007-22296) and the [memory law of 20 October 2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-17099) are parties inside one country fighting over the Civil War and Franco. France against Spain, and Germany against Spain, still do not appear as official disputes on the list.

France and Germany treated their own split as a problem to close. On 21 January 2003, the fortieth anniversary of the Élysée Treaty, students asked Jacques Chirac and Gerhard Schröder for [one history book with the same pages in both countries](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/4972922.stm). The first volume came out in 2006. The shared book is the two states admitting the separate books were the problem. It leaves 1871 and 1919 where they were.

China's place is a different kind of fight. No Chinese government and European government on this list sign opposite histories of the same dynasty. A Chinese statute against a European account of events outside China is not in the 17. The written fight is among historians, about whether early China grew up alone. [David Keightley](https://www.educationaboutasia.org/article/id/603/) treated the geography as part of how early Chinese culture formed, with no foreign break on the scale of a dark age. [Robert Eno](https://factsanddetails.com/china/cat2/4sub7/item2835.html) taught that the deserts, the Tibetan plateau, the Himalaya, and the Pacific left ancient China effectively cut off, so that China and the Mediterranean might as well have been different planets. However, the same text says China was not sealed off from the nomads on its own steppe. [Victor Mair](https://www.academia.edu/14372552/Traditional_China_in_Asian_and_World_History_With_Victor_H_Mair_) and the newer school say the isolation claim does not hold, and that the steppe on China's northern border is a road into Central Asia. Under the Mongols, the Yuan dynasty in China and the Golden Horde on the rivers of Russia were parts of one imperial order. A lecture that stops China at the desert leaves that order out. The older lecture reads as a cut in the connection to Russia. Keightley and Eno say geography.

Britain is on a side that was fought. In the Crimean War, from 1853 to 1856, Russia fought France, Britain, the Ottoman Empire, and Sardinia over the Ottoman lands, the Black Sea, and the holy places. Prussia stayed out. Austria did not back Russia. That is Britain's disagreement on this list. The wider schoolbook English speakers use today was not checked country by country. France and Germany already disagree with each other on the same list.

## The dates they share

The seventeen are wars, borders, and the meaning of a war. A grand empire that held China, Iran, and the Central Asian republics whose names end in -stan is not among them. Kazakhstan claims the Golden Horde as its own statehood. On 26 August 2005, at Kazan's thousandth anniversary, [Putin](http://en.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/23140) said a number of Russian historians already count that Horde as part of Russia's history, and that the Russian state took the Horde's tax, military, and administrative offices. He named Lev Gumilev on the culture of the steppe. Iran's encyclopaedia and the treaties of 1813 and 1828 teach a transfer of Caucasian lands. The Yuan dynasty and the Horde are taught as one Mongol order. [Kazakh and Cossack](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Cossack) are the same Turkic word, and the Persian ending means the place. Fomenko reads Kazakhstan as the Cossack camp of a Russian Horde. Putin has not said that. Kazakhstan has not said that.

The dates in the schoolbook were built in that early modern window, and the builders were on opposite sides. Joseph Scaliger published *De emendatione temporum* in Paris in 1583. Denis Petau, a Jesuit, published *De doctrina temporum* in Paris in 1627 as a rebuttal of Scaliger, because a Catholic answer was wanted to a Protestant chronology.
