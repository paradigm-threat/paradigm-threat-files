# Weep Not for Memory

*Teaching history is a double-edged sword, and it can hurt as much as it can heal*

![Three doors in a stone wall. The left opens on a domed city and a river, and the stone under it is broken away. The middle is open, and a crowd walks toward smoke and red banners, with torn books on the stones. A man at a lectern holds a blank page. The right door is shut, locked, and glowing, and a blank sheet leans against it.](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/weep-not-for-memory.png)

The three paths are the truth, the fiction, and the power that comes from knowing the truth and operating by the strict guidelines of the fiction. The left door is the truth. Truth is the natural path to peace, and it has been unavailable through the last few centuries. Attempting it in this lifetime brings a fierce reaction from the environment, from peers, and from governments. It is simply not an option yet. The middle door is the fiction, the blindness we were taught in history class. That blindness is the omission of the lessons the past already taught, and the classroom version of the same lesson drops them, and that is what leads to the repeating of mistakes like a world war. The right door is knowing the truth and operating by the strict guidelines of the fiction, to reach peace without warfare, without losing civil rights, and without handing authority to a remote corporate-government gray zone that rules every part of life. The other direction is people holding the tools to run their own lives, with government and corporations competing for better products instead of better ways to control them. The narratives in movies, books, and predictions keep suggesting there has to be a major cataclysm, even a depopulation event, before peace can arrive. Marxism implies the same: a bloodletting that brings down the government before the peace. Truth is a virtue, and people have died for it. Peace is never worth surrendering. But is it worth giving up the fight for truth to preserve those victories and revolutions without tainting them with the dangerous virtue of disclosure? Would you trade away the chance to see truth arrive in your own lifetime, if that is the price of keeping everything else intact?

Putin wants to save Mother Russia. He is willing to sacrifice its actual history in the most harmless way available — a sin he will repeat across his lifetime — to keep the other virtues intact: peace, stability, a mutual understanding with the West built on a shared history even when that history is not the true one. He can afford the omission because he believes the truth will surface on its own once the peace holds. That is the right door as a governing philosophy, not just a personal choice.

> **TL;DR:** Teaching a true history cuts the person who says it. Putin told Emir Kusturica that Moscow is the Third Rome, and a fourth there shall not be. Putin's remark to the filmmaker is what got me writing about the cost of saying the rest. He has not subscribed to Fomenko's New Chronology. The school dates were set in 1583 and 1627 by men who were arguing with each other, and the other account of the Horde stays unsaid. These topics were lesser known, and a hollow Earth or a civilization on Mars already drew the same dismissal. Right now the truth does more damage than good, from bad people and from good people who will not pay the cost. The peace they are waiting on is still not here.

## This morning

["Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be"](https://rt-english.com/russia/649179-moscow-third-rome-putin-kusturica/) is the headline on the English article that carried the interview overnight. Channel One aired it on 20 November 2026, for Patriarch Kirill's 80th birthday. RT updated the piece in the early hours of 21 November, and I read it this morning. The Kremlin spokesman said the conversation was recorded before a foreign trip, so the air date is the release.

The [English transcript](http://www.en.special.kremlin.ru/events/president/transcripts/81318) has the walk he gave Kusturica:

> I am saying it with such confidence not just because the Russian theological and philosophical thought is centred around one concept that dates centuries back: Moscow is the Third Rome, a fourth there shall not be.
>
> What does it mean? It means that Christianity came into existence in ancient Rome, then Rome fell, and Christianity moved to Byzantium, the Eastern Roman Empire. Constantine became the first emperor to convert to Christianity. The Byzantine Empire also fell, but by that time, Christianity had already spread to Russian soil and reached Moscow. Thus, Moscow inherited this faith and these traditions. In a way, it became the Third Rome, the spiritual keeper of those holy values, that heritage.
>
> Yet this theological, or philosophic, idea, which, as I said, Russia has cherished for ages, is not the main point here. It is all about today.

He then put the weight on the present. People from Europe, he said, come to Russia to keep their families and their traditional faith. The ark is not only for Orthodox Christians, and not only for Christians. He named Buddhism, Judaism, Islam, and Orthodoxy together. "Russian civilization never took over others," he said, and he called that a unique feature. "We are not trying to limit anyone in anything." Western churches, he said, have departed from the canons, and that departure has not strengthened them.

He has not subscribed to Anatoly Fomenko's New Chronology. I have not found him saying Fomenko's name, or the name of the chronology. He gave the old church succession, and he said the formula is not the main point. That is the only position he has ever taken on it, and the absence may be the evidence. Sponsoring New Chronology would hand Europe the cartoonish villainy it already wants — Russia claiming the center of a new order, the Hitler comparison ready-made. He is not walking into that trap.

## Lesser known phrases

He said Moscow is the Third Rome, and a fourth there shall not be, and he said the formula is not the main point. The chronology he did not name was lesser known. A hollow Earth, or a civilization on Mars, drew the same dismissal. "Deep state" is another name that was lesser known, and then became a party name. A [history of the phrase](https://warontherocks.com/how-the-deep-state-came-to-america-a-history/) says Peter Dale Scott used it for the United States in *The Road to 9/11* in 2007, and that daily American news picked the phrase up in February 2017. A [Monmouth poll](https://www.monmouth.edu/polling-institute/reports/monmouthpoll_us_031918/) of 803 adults, taken from 2 to 5 March 2018, asked who knew the name. Thirteen percent said they were very familiar with it, 24 percent somewhat familiar, and 63 percent not familiar. The same poll then described unelected government and military officials who secretly direct national policy. Twenty-seven percent said that group definitely exists, and 47 percent said it probably does.

The [Library of Congress guide](https://guides.loc.gov/hollow-earth/additional-works) lists Martin Gardner's 1957 book, which put the hollow Earth in one chapter with the flat Earth. On 14 July 1965, [Mariner 4](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-4/) returned the first close pictures of Mars. NASA's account says the cratered surface ended the expectation of a lost civilization there. [Astronomers](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/what-did-astronomers-believe-about-mars-before-the-space-age) had already left Percival Lowell's canals behind from about 1909. A [2022 review of repeated polls](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0270429) found no steady rise in the share of people who believe particular claims of this kind. That review's search of the Roper archive found 10 such national questions from before 2010. The dismissal was already the record. The wide audience is the newer part.

By now people have heard all of these topics through some compromised channels. Those channels demand allegiance to certain agendas, and people cannot separate the agenda from the topic anymore. Everything has become red versus blue. Today the same talk can cost the job, the friendship, the relationship, and the family table. Truth in this lifetime is dangerous. It gets a person in trouble with peers, with parents, with the person they are with, with friends, with associates, and with coworkers. He could say Moscow is the Third Rome. The chronology he left off is one of the names that still costs the room.

Most people hear hollow Earth or Mars civilization and feel no cost, because it does not touch their paycheck or their street. The cost is upstream. A history that gets hidden stops being a history and becomes a silence, and the silence is what the next generation inherits as normal. That is the chain from the buried archive to the classroom to the war that follows.

## What the wars spent

Countries gave up the virtuous history that had defined them. That history was the account of their own conduct, and people knew the country by it. The wars that followed destroyed those virtues and nearly eliminated them. The story is still told, and the conduct it praised is gone. Freedom of speech is one of the few I still count, and I count it in America.

Russia's laws forbid kinds of speech. On 4 March 2022 the state made it a crime to publicly spread what the statute calls [knowingly false information about the armed forces](https://kremlin.ru/acts/bank/47616). Public actions aimed at discrediting that use begin as an administrative case, and a repeat of them is a crime. On 14 July 2022 a separate law put a person under foreign influence on a [register](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_421788/) when that person engages in political activity or spreads material to an unlimited audience. The register took effect on 1 December 2022. The person on it is marked. In America the deep-state presentation is a social cost. In Russia a public sentence the state calls false, when the sentence is about the army, is a charge under the March law. That is the difference in the virtue I still count.

## Red versus blue

It is not a clean cut. Democrats accuse Republicans of corruption and deception, and Republicans accuse Democrats of the same thing. The accusation has the same shape on both sides. Russian history walks into that fight, and it is red versus blue now, the same as everything else. A person who brings the dates is heard as a party. The other party already has a name for that person.

The [break of the GOP](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/world-war-3-anyway) in 2026 was meant to [end that cycle](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-big-surprise). One side split, and that broke the lock of mutual accusation. Democrats vote in lockstep. That party cannot be broken from the outside. An attack from the other party becomes one more reason to vote the same way. Communism, socialism, the Democratic Party, and the short violent liberal causes have not been beaten from outside either. They fall from the inside, when their own people turn on the line they were holding. The argument runs a long time. Often there is blood. When it turns, the universities take the fallout, and the schoolbook that follows drops the people who disagreed. Grade school gets Einstein. The people who argued with him do not make that book. A child can finish school and never learn that the argument happened.

## What the truth does

Whether a claim is true does not change how people react. A story about space lizards and a story about a hidden Russian chronology produce the same result. The person who says either one loses the job, the friendship, or the place at the family table in that moment. Thom Yorke sings the same charge in the refrain of ["Ful Stop,"](https://radiohead.bandcamp.com/track/ful-stop) on Radiohead's *A Moon Shaped Pool*, released on 8 May 2016. The line is "Truth will mess you up," and he sings it over and over. Right now the truth does more damage than good. A true sentence that sets off that reaction hurts people on the spot, and that hurt is larger than the good of having said it. True things get suppressed by bad people, and by good people who are trying not to cause that damage. A good person can see it coming. The damage they are refusing is the job, the friendship, and the family table. They know the sentence, and they will not pay the cost of saying it. Bad people want the same sentence gone for their own reason. The quiet from the good people is enough to keep it out of the room.

They look backward and see only war repeating, with no solutions carried forward, so they face the future with no idea what to expect. People can sit and wait. They cannot talk about the past, and they cannot talk about the truth, without hurting someone.

## The old dates

Anatoly Fomenko is a Russian mathematician. His New Chronology says the famous ancient empires are later events stacked on the wrong years. A war, a reign, or a city that the schoolbook puts in antiquity is, on his pages, a medieval event written again and moved backward. A Rus-Horde empire, in the same account, held a much wider world than the schoolbook yoke.

![Anatoly Fomenko, the mathematician behind New Chronology](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/anatoly_fomenko.jpg)

The yoke in the schoolbook is a Mongol occupation of the Russian lands. English speakers meet the word Horde as a swarm. Students learn that Russia came out from under that occupation into the modern state. In this account the same word is the Russian empire those occupation stories were taken from. His account reverses the schoolbook order. The Horde is a Russian empire, and its reach runs far past one occupied country. The ancient kingdoms and that empire are the same events under two dates. A reader new to the theory can still follow. The tables that shift a given battle by a set number of centuries are a separate job.

A date is a claim about who came first. If Rome is ancient and the Horde is medieval, Rome is the actor and the Horde is the inheritor, and the stories about law, faith, and war run from the first to the second. If the ancient Rome is a copy of a later empire, the inheritance runs the other way. The people called the heirs become the source, and the people called the source become the copy. Who followed whom is the chain, and a year that is off by centuries changes the chain.

In the Russian telling that order is already understood. It was a long fight of houses over the seat. Who married whom, who took the seat, who was pushed out, who came back under another name. People know that kind of succession from Game of Thrones, and the Russian version runs a long time. The audience that grew up on that telling already has the order. They do not need a new count of centuries to know who followed whom.

They still cite the old dates. The lesson in the date is the part they want, and the lesson holds if the event was early and it holds if the event was late. A speaker can give the lesson in the year the schoolbook prints. The same speaker can give the lesson and skip the year. Once the order of the houses is known, the year-number is not what carries the meaning.

Putin can skip the dates. The sentence from this morning is the lesson. The years are what he left out.

## Europe and Asia

The disputes here are over succession, origin, and vassalage — whose king came from where, who was the original, who was the vassal. A separate section or investigation file holds the battle list.

Official, on this count, means a government or its statute refusing the other country's account of who held the seat or who came first. Russia or the Soviet Union is a party in most of them. Poland and Ukraine do not share one official account of the Volhynia massacres of 1943 to 1945, which is a dispute over who did what and under whose authority. The Balkan states and the Ottomans fought over the same ground in 1912 and 1913.

The other disputes are inside the West. Prussia and Austria fought over who would lead Germany. France and Germany fought over Alsace-Lorraine, and the Treaty of Frankfurt on 10 May 1871 wrote the German border. On 28 June 1919 the Allies wrote that Germany and its allies were responsible for the war. Soviet Russia was not at Versailles. Spain's fight is Spaniards against each other over the Civil War and Franco, written into the memory laws of 26 December 2007 and 20 October 2022. France against Spain, and Germany against Spain, are still missing from the list — no official dispute between those pairs appears in the record.

France and Germany treated their own split as a problem to close. On 21 January 2003, the fortieth anniversary of the Élysée Treaty, students asked Jacques Chirac and Gerhard Schröder for [one history book with the same pages in both countries](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/4972922.stm). The first volume came out in 2006. The shared book is the two states admitting the separate books were the problem.

China's place is a fight among historians rather than between governments. No Chinese government and European government on this list sign opposite histories of the same dynasty. The written fight is about whether early China grew up alone. [David Keightley](https://www.educationaboutasia.org/article/id/603/) treated the geography as part of how early Chinese culture formed, with no foreign break on the scale of a dark age. [Robert Eno](https://factsanddetails.com/china/cat2/4sub7/item2835.html) taught that the deserts, the Tibetan plateau, the Himalaya, and the Pacific left ancient China effectively cut off, so that China and the Mediterranean might as well have been different planets. However, the same text says China was not sealed off from the nomads on its own steppe. [Victor Mair](https://www.academia.edu/14372552/Traditional_China_in_Asian_and_World_History_With_Victor_H_Mair_) and the newer school say the isolation claim does not hold, and that the steppe on China's northern border is a road into Central Asia. Under the Mongols, the Yuan dynasty in China and the Golden Horde on the rivers of Russia were parts of one imperial order. A lecture that stops China at the desert leaves that order out. The older lectures did not just leave the steppe out by accident. Encyclopedia Britannica grew out of the same Renaissance and Reformation window that produced Scaliger and Petau, and when the British Empire rose it carried that encyclopedia's version of history to the world. The desert isolation claim fit that frame because it kept China separate from the Russian lands and from any story of a steppe order centered elsewhere. Later historians excavated inside that frame and found what it was built to let them find. The steppe road into Central Asia stayed covered because covering it served the frame. Keightley and Eno say geography.

Britain is on a side that was fought. In the Crimean War, from 1853 to 1856, Russia fought France, Britain, the Ottoman Empire, and Sardinia over the Ottoman lands, the Black Sea, and the holy places. Prussia stayed out. Austria did not back Russia. That is Britain's disagreement on this list.

Two open questions for a later pass. First: does Europe actually disagree with Russia on these succession questions, or do they mostly agree, with Russia the outlier? Second: is Putin deliberately staying compatible with the Western Renaissance timeline while omitting the dates, in order to remain legitimate in the eyes of the West he is trying to make peace with?

## The dates they share

The seventeen are wars, borders, and the meaning of a war. A grand empire that held the Stan countries, China, and Iran is not among them. Kazakhstan claims the Golden Horde as its own statehood. On 26 August 2005, at Kazan, Kazakhstan's president marked the 750th anniversary of the Horde and called it a statehood event. The schoolbook keeps that empire out of the seventeen.

## Tina Peters

The American case is Tina Peters, and her sentence shows what the cost looks like when the state writes it down. In 2021 she was a county clerk in Mesa County, Colorado. She gave a hard drive with voting-machine files to people who said they were journalists. She was charged with tampering and attempting to influence a public servant. In August 2024 a jury found her guilty. In November 2024 the judge sentenced her to nine years in prison. The files themselves were never shown to prove that any vote changed. The charge was the access, not a found fraud.

## The Office

The show is not about history. It is about a workplace where the rules are known and nobody follows them out loud. The boss who breaks the rule is protected by the people who benefit. The person who names it is the one who leaves. That is the room the article has been describing, translated into comedy. The boot was on the neck the whole time. It did not always feel that way. School and movies taught that the other history, the other science, and the other medicine were impossible. They were suppressed. They were not impossible. The rules allow almost anything. That anything is up to the people who are here. Focus on the future. Weep not for memory.
