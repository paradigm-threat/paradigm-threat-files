## Semmelweis: the easy win, the wrecked man, and the inversion

**Ignaz Semmelweis** (1818–1865) links Napoleon’s corpse theater to today’s germ orthodoxy. At Vienna’s **Allgemeines Krankenhaus** in the **1840s**, students and physicians went from **autopsy** to **maternity** exams unwashed; **childbed fever** ravaged the doctors’ clinic, not the midwives’. After **Jakob Kolletschka** died in **1847** from an autopsy wound matching mothers’ deaths, Semmelweis ordered **chlorinated lime**; mortality **collapsed** (~**18%** toward **~1%** in **1848**). When Vienna dropped handwashing after he left (**1850**), deaths rose again.

Peers **refused** the conclusion; Vienna **blocked the corpse work**. **20 March 1849:** **Johann Klein** installed **Carl Braun**; Semmelweis **left the ward**. Docent petition **denied at first**; **10 October 1850** brought a **“theoretical”** obstetrics post—**no cadavers**, **leather mannequins** only. His proof required **autopsy**; they still dissected; he could not continue in Vienna and quit within days. **Experiment on corpses** here means **dissection and cadaverous inquiry**, not a ghoulish side protocol.

He spoke of **cadaverous particles**, not **germs**. The ward win was **keep autopsy residue off the living**—not “believe Pasteur.” Colleagues mocked the **dirty killer** charge; in **Pest** from **1851** he ran wards again. **1865:** lured to a Vienna asylum, beaten, dead **13 August** (age **47**) from **sepsis** on an infected hand—the institution killed the messenger.

Later canon made him patron saint of **universal handwashing** under **germ theory** and **Flexner-era** licensing—one true practice **weaponized** to bury terrain medicine and sort dissent into **supporter** vs **denier** camps around a name.
