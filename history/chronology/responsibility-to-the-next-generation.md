## Autodidactic by default — class is supplementary

For most of history, serious learning happened **one child, one book, one problem** — with a tutor or craft master when luck allowed. Mass schooling inverted that: **same age, same bell, same score**, and the child who will not perform for the chart gets the label. Installment I documented that **backward** crime. Installment II refuses a **forward** mistake: treating LLMs as a cheat to ban, or a headset to mandate, instead of the **library every child was always owed**.

**Every child should be autodidactic by default.** The LLM is not the teacher — it is the **sidecar**: search, explanation, drill, translation, accessibility layer, narrative hook. The child chooses the thread; the adult holds **ethics and verification**. Class becomes **what cannot be done alone at the kitchen table** — not the place where a district pours reading and listening into thirty seats because that is how accreditation was built in 1910.

That is how you deliver a **complete education** and **accessibility** without forcing every child through **conformist grading**: the canon is **available**, not **mandated as performance for strangers**. Grades measure compliance with a packet; they do not measure whether the child **knows**. When lookup is cheap and models are everywhere, **packing volume** matters less than **honest QA** and **exit** when a vendor degrades (switch models; run local weights; do not beg one platform for transparency).

## What class is actually for

A **classroom lecture** — thirty children, one voice, one pace — works for **certain** children in **certain** subjects. For many others it is **noise**: fear, boredom, performance without retention. Arguably it **rarely works at all** as the primary engine of understanding; it persists because it is **cheap to administer** and easy to **grade**.

**Invert the budget:**

| **At home / async (LLM + books + human QA)** | **In physical class (non-negotiable)** |
|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Reading, writing, listening, research, language drill | **Laboratory** — matter, measurement, falsification |
| Narrative history, civics texts, math explanation | **Gym, court, field** — motor skill, coach's eye |
| Accessibility (TTS, translation, adaptive pacing) | **Workshop equipment** — tools, chemicals, instruments, safety supervision |
| Ethics discussions with a trusted adult | **1-on-1 or very small group** — mentor reads affect, catches performed understanding |

**Software will never replace a laboratory.** Simulations are preparation; **ground truth** is what touches the bench. The same rule applies outside science: a thousand free-throw videos do not replace the **gym**. **One-on-one education cannot be replaced by AI** — a model scales worksheets; it does not reliably notice that a child is **performing** comprehension, that fear is driving the answers, or that ethics must be named **in the moment**.

Foundational literacy — **reading, writing, typing** — is especially dangerous to outsource to automated validation alone. A clever kid can paste a paragraph, game a fluency timer, let autocomplete write while they nod. A **human** changes the prompt, asks the follow-up, listens, watches fingers on keys. The LLM **helps**; the adult **verifies**. That is quality assurance, not red tape.

**Do not** burn scarce class hours on what the sidecar already does better at home: long listen-and-copy sessions, worksheet factories, one-size reading assignments everyone "finished" but nobody understood.

## The headset and the sidecar

In René Laloux's ***Fantastic Planet*** (1973), Draag children learn through a **headset** that pours knowledge into the brain. Terr, an Om slave, steals the headset; wild Oms learn literacy and revolt. Critics read Soviet allegory; the film also says plainly: **monopolized knowledge is power**; **shared knowledge is insurgency**.

On Earth the visible lane is implant evangelism — Neuralink's mission to "**unlock human potential tomorrow**," ethicists calling for **moratoriums** on implantable enhancement and **bans for children** ([PLOS Biology](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3002899), [Springer Neuroethics 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12152-025-09612-6)), K-12 consultants fantasizing about BCI "personalized learning." That is the **Fantastic Planet headset**.

My path is different: **knowledge at your side** — LLMs as **library**, accessible and inspectable where possible, **without drilling into your skull** and **without** replacing the human in the room for ethics, labs, or tutoring.

## Rethink education — ethics and quality assurance

These tools **demand that we rethink education entirely** — not to pack the old syllabus tighter, but to **shrink the educator's scope** to what only humans should own.

The responsibility shifts — like developers who use AI — toward **quality assurance**. The **highest priority is ethics**. Ethics must be taught **from the get-go** and **imposed at all times**, so that when they're older they follow good ethics on their own and **do not take advantage** of people or technology. That is a **much reduced scope** from what educators do today.

I don't think we have any **right to choose what topics** children learn. What we **do** have the right to do is **dangle** opportunities before them in the **most presentable and accessible way** — a vast library where **every child finds their spot**, their tool, their narrative, their muse **on their own**, with technical details (math, science, the rest) **at hand** when needed.

**Ethics is the one mandatory imposition** — guardrails, not syllabus. Topical exploration is **curated presentation**, not **mandated curriculum**. LLMs belong in the stack as **library and drill assistant** — sidecar, not substitute teacher, not compliance chart. Rhymes with Installment I: you may not own the child's curiosity, but you **do** own whether the channel is honest.

## Housebroken society and reduced scope

In a society where many problems are **already handled** and survival often reduces to being **housebroken** and fed, packing a **full volume** into every child correlates weakly with life skills they will actually need. The question is not whether children **can** absorb more content — they can, especially with sidecar tools — but whether **mandated volume** still serves a world where lookup is cheap and character is scarce.

That is not an argument against learning. It is an argument against **syllabus-as-identity**: the child as a vessel for an accredited canon, graded on compliance, diagnosed when they refuse. Installment I documented the **backward** crime. Installment II refuses the **forward** replacements — Neuralink as curriculum, chatbot as teacher, automated literacy badges without a witness, **and** pretending LLMs are optional extras in a lecture hall that never worked for half the room anyway.

## What to insist on (plain checklist for parents and schools)

When a district buys the AI package, treat these as **non-negotiable**:

- **LLMs enabled** for every child who can use them — accessibility, language, pacing — with **inspectable** tools where possible; teach **provider exit** when quality drops  
- **Autodidactic default** — child-led inquiry with adult **QA**, not lecture-first  
- **Class time** reserved for **lab, gym, workshop, mentor** — not bulk reading/writing/listening that belongs at home  
- **Human** verification of reading, writing, and typing — not timer-gamed fluency alone  
- **1-on-1 or small-group** blocks where affect and ethics can be read  
- **No implantable** cognitive enhancement for minors — moratorium lane per neuroethics consensus  
- **No conformist grading** as the definition of "educated" — competence and ethics over chart performance  

If the institution cannot name who performs QA, who runs the lab, and who sits 1-on-1, it has bought a **headset** with a school logo.
