# A Tale told by an Idiot

That idiot is Shakespeare, who was not a person, but a Renaissance project designed to create a new religion — Nihilism.

![A Tale told by an Idiot — Folio in the museum, lost chronicles, Shakespeare bust, and the void as Nihilism](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/a-tale-told-by-an-idiot.jpg)

> **TL;DR:** Shakespeare was not a man who invented Italian costume dramas. He was a Renaissance print project that costumed **world-empire** chronicles, then sealed English culture with **Nihilism** — the creed that the tale signifies nothing. Fomenko’s New Chronology reconstructs those plays as shared history the whole planet heard because the whole planet lived it: conflicts, contradictions, and hard-won agreements in the evolution of the species. The Renaissance redaction kept the plots and dropped the conclusions — especially faith, and Christianity linked to government. School and entertainment flooded the void. France was conquered after rejecting the Bard. Science later wore the same religion in a lab coat.

Last night I watched [*The Comedy of Terrors*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0056943/) — the 1963 Jacques Tourneur farce with Vincent Price, Peter Lorre, Boris Karloff, and Basil Rathbone. Rathbone’s undying Mr. Black keeps spouting the *Macbeth* lines, including the walking-shadow speech, while the movie treats him as a corpse that will not stay dead. For the first time I heard those lines spoken in **mockery** rather than reverence. Just that mood shift alone is enough to make the mind critical. The same words school taught me to kneel to sounded like a joke the culture had been forbidden to laugh at.

Fomenko says Shakespeare wasn’t one single person, and I agree. In the end, the only thing anyone will remember Shakespeare for will not be the stories, but the line “A tale told by an Idiot,” and they’ll stop there, because Shakespeare was that useful idiot — any other person alive at the time would have told the stories correctly, in their true origin countries, and on their own merit, without forcing the pro-establishment Nihilism agenda.

## The creed

Everyone who sat through high-school English knows the lines, even if they cannot name the act. Life is a walking shadow. A poor player who struts and frets his hour upon the stage. A tale told by an idiot, full of sound and fury, signifying nothing.

That speech sits in *Macbeth*. On stage it belongs to a murderer at the end of his rope. In culture it left the play. Quote banks, titles, sermons that do not admit they are sermons, and classroom poster walls lift it out as wisdom. Once detached, it stops being character psychology. It becomes liturgy. Reverence is the install method. Mockery is the crack.

The sacrament of the new religion is simple: the tale means nothing. Sacred story means nothing. Imperial chronicle means nothing. Your life is noise between two silences. Memorize that in blank verse and you have been confirmed.

## Not a person — a Renaissance project

There was no generative William Shakespeare inventing the modern English mind from a Stratford desk. The name brands a project. The inputs were older chronicles, plays, and sacred narratives. The operation was translation and costume into English print — not blank-page invention.

Mainstream source study already knows the weaker half of this. *Macbeth* and the histories lean on Holinshed. The Roman plays lean on Plutarch. *Hamlet* sits on the Amleth story. *Romeo and Juliet* sits on Brooke. Specialists sell “originality” as language and character, not as plot invention. Public school still spends weeks on the Bard and usually never installs that floor. In my lifetime of classroom Shakespeare, that evidence never arrived. The graduate leaves believing one genius invented the tales.

The stronger half is the Renaissance package itself. Older eastern and imperial memory was costumed as Rome, mythical Britain, Tudor gossip. School never teaches the erased world-empire underneath. The Folio arrives as island genius. The trail is cut at the brand.

Hunting Bacon versus Oxford versus Stratford keeps the invention myth alive under a different signature. The fight over which person wrote the verse presupposes a person. The project does not need one.

## World empire stories — what New Chronology reconstructs

School sells Shakespeare as isolated theater: Denmark, Scotland, Rome, Verona, a little Italy of the mind. Private tales. Local color. One island genius inventing psychology for modernity.

Anatoly Fomenko and Gleb Nosovskiy’s New Chronology reads the famous plays differently. In [*What Shakespeare Actually Wrote About*](https://chronologia.org/en/shakespeare/index.html), the plots are based on real events of the twelfth through sixteenth centuries inside the **Great Empire** — the Rus–Horde world order — then wrongly dated and relocated by Scaligerian geography. *Hamlet*, *Macbeth*, *Lear*, *Henry VIII*, *Timon*, *Titus* and the rest are not random Italian novellas. They are **world-empire stories**. The entire world heard them because the entire world was affected by them.

That is the reconstruction’s context in plain speech. These were not boutique entertainments for a city-state audience. They were the shared memory of a planetary civilization working through new contradictions and conflicts — and coming to agreements on their merit. A true path of evolution of the species: blood, succession, betrayal, sacred kingship, gospel-shaped chronicle, the cost of splitting a realm. People argued about what the stories meant because the stories named what had just happened to everyone.

The Renaissance Shakespeare project kept the costumes and dropped the conclusions. Messages that mattered — especially where people still have faith in their religion, or believe that Christianity is linked to government — did not survive the English redaction as usable teaching. What survived was psychology, rhetoric, and the void creed. Empire chronicle became “a tale told by an idiot.” Faith-linked sovereignty became stage intrigue. The agreements the species had already paid for in history were filed as fiction from a faraway country.

Fomenko’s reconstruction revives what the redaction buried: the plays as **encoded chronicle**, not invented isolation. The reconstruction does not ask you to worship a Stratford man. It asks you to put the stories back on the map of a world empire and hear the conclusions again — including the ones Nihilism was designed to erase.

A few documented joins from that book’s English foreword make the scale obvious. *Hamlet* and several other titles encode Christ-reflections under different names. *Macbeth* maps onto the Herod / Baptist / Christ lane. *King Lear* and *Henry VIII* encode Ivan IV-era empire drama, not merely Tudor gossip. Once those maps are on the table, “Italian story” stops being a serious description. The globe was the stage because the globe had lived the plot.

## Nihilism as the new religion

**Nihilism** here is not a teenage mood. It is a religion.

It is the rejection of all religion. Of all structured order to the universe. Of all hope for humanity. Of faith in anything.

People say that is not a religion — that it is anti-religion, or atheism, or “just realism.” The dodge fails. You cannot fill the gap of human history with simple notions of dismissing. Multitudes of intersecting themes stay essential in anyone’s value system or belief system. People who say they believe in nothing still have a belief system. Even if the content is nothing. Believing in nothing is still belief.

Between Christianity and nothing, English print culture chose nothing and made the choice sound like art. The Renaissance project did not hand out pamphlets titled *How to Stop Believing*. It required literature. It required stage and later film. It required exams. It omitted the traditions and religions of the existing and old world, then filled the silence with void speeches, suicidal deliberation, mockery of conscience, and wonder at a “brave new world” that readers later recognized as managed order.

That is high-culture obligatory liturgy. Not a sect with a building. A creed with a curriculum.

*Hear now a little costume, then plain speech again:*

> What is a man, if all his yesterdays light fools the way to dusty death? What is an empire, if the chronicle be but sound and fury? Signify nothing, and thou shalt be counted wise; keep faith with the old world, and thou art provincial.

That took half a minute. The point is not that blank verse is magic. The point is that the “high style” is a costume you can put on. The religion underneath is the void.

## What was omitted

The old world had traditions, religions, imperial Christianity, eastern memory, and a cosmos that was not random noise. Reformation cultural war first made that order look foolish. The *Ship of Fools* satire and carnival “hell wagon” trained Europe to laugh at the ship and storm it. When the empire was split, the same shows were filed as medieval humor.

Then the cleared century needed “correct masterpieces.” Brand names — painters, cartographers, and the English playwright — received later works in the spirit of the new chronology. Shakespeare is the English seal of that replace phase: ridicule first, then obligatory genius.

What dropped out of the Shakespeare redaction was not only place-names. It was **value**. Conclusions the empire stories carried — about faith, about sacred order, about whether Christianity and government belong in one body of memory — were the parts least likely to be translated intact. A play that still encodes Herod and Christ can still teach a classroom that the tale signifies nothing. That is the point of the seal. Keep the sound and fury. Lose the merit of the agreements.

The graduate who never heard of Holinshed also never heard that the Bard corpus sits downstream of world-empire narrative. Empire silence and Bardolatry travel together. One classroom withholds the world. The other installs the void. New Chronology reconstruction is one path back to the lost conclusions; Nihilism is what filled the gap while they were gone.

## Museum irony

Here is the first hard contrast.

The West built a **museum** around Shakespearean metaphor. School makes you kneel to it. Entertainment refreshes it. Talking ill of Shakespeare will not win you many friends. The same West spent the last three to five centuries of the Renaissance Revolution teaching living speakers that metaphor, deep abstraction, and surreal density are inappropriate — a bit much — uncool — unserious.

Eastern languages spoke that way as ordinary high speech. Metaphor was not a museum stunt. Abstract contemplation was not a social mistake. Cyrillic and eastern literature carried poetic register as how people thought on the page. After Latin prestige won and eastern tongues were filed as ancient or dead, Anglophones met that density mainly inside the Folio brand — and credited a Stratford person. They should have been impressed by Cyrillic literature in general. The [Hidden History of Words](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-hidden-history-of-words) already pins Shakespeare, Bacon, and the King James print elite as the seal on English after the Horde fracture. Eastern origin under the pin is the sister claim.

Modern English, after that war, is rigid. It names the world like a form. It distrusts image. It rewards thin clarity and punishes the speech that still sounds like it is touching something. The museum keeps one frozen sample of metaphor as compulsory genius. The street is told not to talk that way.

*Another costume, then the contrast in ordinary words:*

> Soft! What light through eastern casements breaks? It is the tongue that named the heavens as kin, and man as more than dust rehearsing nothing. Yet in the West we cage that light in folios, and call the living speaker fool.

The museum irony is the load-bearing beat. The Revolution that installs the Bard empties the street of the eastern register. Sacred costume upstairs. Living metaphor downstairs marked unserious. That is not taste evolving. That is a culture war with a trophy case.

## France: war, not taste

Here is the second hard contrast.

Shakespeare’s impact on France did not begin as worship. It began as trouble. Voltaire helped introduce him in the 1730s, then turned. He called the English dramatist strong and fruitful — and without taste, without the rules of drama, a ruin of the English stage, an “histrion barbare.” He translated passages, then spent decades warning Europe that English excess would wreck French theater. Eighteenth-century France adapted and censored “monstrous” scenes to fit classical unities. Gravediggers and cobblers on stage with kings offended a theater that wanted elevation and moral clarity. Ducis rewrote *Hamlet* until it behaved. For a long stretch, rejection was the institutional posture — not a fringe blog, the house style of polite letters.

Then the Romantic century reversed the field. Letourneur’s translations and Anglomania opened the door. Shakespeare became the liberating model against the Académie. The battle of *Hernani* and Victor Hugo’s campaign turned excess and the grotesque into a banner. Critics who had treated Shakespeare as the enemy of taste found themselves on the losing side of a fashion that became an orthodoxy. By the end of the arc, the same figure who had been barbaric was canonized as universal genius.

That sequence is not organic taste discovering a timeless classic. Taste that needs a century of institutional hostility, then a Romantic street fight, then total school-and-stage acceptance is not “we finally noticed beauty.” Rejection first, then obsession, then one-hundred-percent acceptance means somebody won. Winner-take-all cultural war, sponsored by major influences of the time, occupies the field until rival canons starve. France is the continental specimen of the same war English schools and stages ran at home.

French-style Nihilism — literary despair, void philosophy, the continental mood that later looked native to Paris — sits downstream of that English project once France absorbed and sacralized the Bard. The creed crossed the Channel as literature, then returned as “French” despair with English roots. When later readers blame Paris for inventing nothingness, they are often reading an export that France first refused and then over-installed.

France proves war, not taste. Taste does not require a century of institutional hostility followed by forced conversion. Victory does.

## How it reached nearly everyone

The Renaissance / Shakespeare package flooded **both** education and entertainment. Classroom time made it obligatory. Stage, film, tourism, and quote culture made it feel like leisure. Societies reached by English literature treat Bard literacy as non-optional civilization. Not every person reads the Folio cover to cover. Nearly all are touched by the brand.

That is control by preemption. Install one summit of wisdom so rival chronicles never get a fair hearing. The establishment does not always need to ban competing opinions. It floods the channels until competing opinions have no status and no friends.

Talking ill of Shakespeare still costs status in Anglophone polite society. That soft policing is what remains after school and entertainment finish the install. France’s rejection-to-canon arc shows the same war when the field was still contested. English dual flood shows the war after victory — when dissent reads as anti-culture rather than anti-propaganda.

## Science as a second liturgy

Here is the third hard contrast.

Before the modern popular picture hardened, many cultures treated the heavens as ordered, meaningful, inhabited, or at least structured. Nineteenth- and twentieth-century science — as public culture, not as every working equation — popularized void as realism. Vacuum of space as the default room the stars sit in. Black holes as the glamorous nothing that eats light. A chaotic Newtonian picture in which the human race is alive purely by chance and could be wiped out by an asteroid at any time. From there it is a short step to the lonely-universe mood: no other life, nowhere, ever — the planet as accident. Lars von Trier’s [*Melancholia*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt1527186/) is a late pop sacrament of that feeling: beauty, depression, and a world ending because the cosmos does not care.

This is **science as a second liturgy**. Same religion, lab coat instead of blank verse. The Macbeth creed — signifying nothing — starts to feel like what educated people “know” about physics and astronomy. Ordered cosmos is provincial. Chance void is mature. Hope becomes a counseling problem. Faith becomes a private hobby. The asteroid is the new Last Judgment with no judge.

Newton did not write for Stratford. The claim is not a forged committee memo linking the Principia to the Folio. The claim is parallel liturgy and shared outcome. Once English literature trained the hemisphere to rehearse nothingness as wisdom, French literary Nihilism and scientific void-cosmology had a prepared congregation. Realism helped replace an ordered universe with a universe where hope looks childish. School teaches both liturgies: the quote from *Macbeth*, then the poster of empty space.

Museum irony froze metaphor in a trophy case. France proved the culture war. Science baptized the void as fact. Three doors, one creed: **Nihilism**.

---

## The human cost

Through literature the project filled people with Nihilism while omitting the traditions and religions of the existing and old world. The reachable human race became largely disillusioned, depressed, warlike, evil, and lost.

The canon itself rehearses the symptoms. Hamlet’s world is weary, stale, flat, and unprofitable. Flesh would melt. A bare bodkin ends the coil. Macbeth grows aweary of the sun and wishes the estate of the world undone. Edmund worships Nature against custom. Iago invents divinity of hell. Richard is himself alone. School treats these as character studies. Culture treats the most quotable void lines as insight.

A population that rehearses meaninglessness becomes easier to rule. Ignorance and obscurity feel adult. Sovereignty looks naive. The brave new world — the phrase lifted from *The Tempest* and later made dystopian title — is what remains when the old world is filed as idiot noise or fantasy. Mud flood and empire fracture destroy physical and institutional memory. The Renaissance project teaches survivors that the tale never meant anything anyway.

That is the cost. Not a bad play. A civilization trained to accept nothing as its faith.

## Framing and limits

This article assumes [prisca sapientia](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md): older order and knowledge were degraded or captured; later classroom consensus is not default truth.

Shakespeare’s plays are literary texts. France’s rejection-to-Romantic-canon path is ordinary reception history (Voltaire’s hostility, classical adaptation, nineteenth-century cult). Fomenko and Nosovskiy’s play-to-Empire maps are New Chronology reconstruction, not mainstream dating. The reads that those maps recover **world-empire** shared history (not isolated Italian tales), that Renaissance redaction dropped faith and Christianity–government conclusions while keeping void pedagogy, that France proves sponsored culture war, that science void-cosmology is a second liturgy, and that “Shakespeare” was not a person but a Renaissance project creating **Nihilism** are hard interpretive claims. Literary critics who call *Macbeth* nihilist are not thereby proving a single intentional committee. Tiers, quote banks, and play maps live in the [AU-083 companion](https://paradigmthreat.net/history/chronology/investigations/shakespeare-project-nihilist-redaction-investigation.md).
