> **TL;DR:** After a late-nineteenth-century Mars attack on Earth left flying-machine technology in human hands, a secret war between the planets was fought and then covered up. [*The War of the Worlds*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36) (1897) and [*Edison’s Conquest of Mars*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/19141) (1898) are how that story got filed into public culture — not the full ops calendar. The fighting itself ran for decades, roughly Civil War through World War II. The canal maps faded from about 1909; the [Dead Planet](https://paradigmthreat.net/timeline/evt-mars-surface-dismantling-dead-planet-protocol) story sealed into the early 1960s. Things still broke into public — [mystery airships](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/mystery-airships-1896-1897-vocabulary-bridge.md), the [Battle of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Los_Angeles), [Roswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident), a clumsy [Philadelphia](https://paradigmthreat.net/events/philadelphia/the-philadelphia-experiment-was-not-time-travel.md) reverse-engineering story — and then got shut down. That pattern points to **two camps at war**, not aliens already owning every government. Soft history keeps each event in its own folder so you never join them. The publics on both worlds were kept dark. The poison was never a single base to bomb. It was a culture that could flip anyone from virtue to desperation.

H. G. Wells opened *The War of the Worlds* by saying no one would have believed that worlds were being watched. The harder claim is this one. A war between planets could be fought, answered across decades, and buried so thoroughly that later generations inherited only novels, canal “illusions,” and a dead rock in the sky. Once you put the pieces next to each other, they stop looking like separate mysteries.

## This explains everything

Stop treating Earth and Mars as two sealed worlds that only meet in pulp. Treat them as one political neighborhood with a thin command channel at the top and mass publics kept unaware on both ends. The war that matters is secret by design: send force to the far side, and keep the record from becoming public memory.

Once that is on the table, the leftovers stop looking random.

![The Secret War — timeline from MFEE through London incursion, Earth reply, canal kill, Dead Planet Protocol, and media echoes](https://paradigmthreat.net/cosmos/mars/the-secret-war-timeline.png)

The invaders left a great flying-machine on Primrose Hill — already under atmospheric test when disease stopped them. That kit is not a prop. It is a ladder. Earth factions that could finish or copy it suddenly had lift, and a reason to use it.

Wells’s novel serialized in 1897. The next year Garrett P. Serviss published *Edison’s Conquest of Mars* — Earth builds electrical ships that defy gravity, strikes Mars, and wrecks the canal waterworks. Read that as how Earth’s reply got told in print, not as proof the fleet finished the war in one publishing season. The real fighting ran longer: roughly the 1860s through the 1940s, Civil War into World War II, with strikes, occupation pressure, resource grabs, and suppression on both worlds. [Schiaparelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Schiaparelli) and [Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percival_Lowell) were not hallucinating first. They were reading a working irrigation grid that still looked alive into the early 1900s. Turning the water off was not a Martian courtesy. We did that. From about 1909 the establishment sold “optical illusion.” The [Dead Planet Protocol](https://paradigmthreat.net/timeline/evt-mars-surface-dismantling-dead-planet-protocol) scrub and camera seal ran on into 1962.

An earlier catastrophe — the [Mudflood Energetic Event around 1774](https://paradigmthreat.net/timeline/evt-1774-ce-the-mars-catastrophe) — devastated Mars before the London attack. That is prelude, not the secret war itself. It is easy to misfile as Mars hitting itself. It is easier still to overlook if the order came from Earth through the same thin channel that already linked the two worlds. Who gave the order matters as much as the beam.

Stories of breakaway civilizations and “lost worlds” do not require sealed planets. They require compartmentalization. The publics never get the map. The command caste does.

Valve’s [*Half-Life*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_(video_game)) ends with the G-Man claiming the far side while wrecked Abrams tanks, Marines, and aircraft sit in the tableau. The Hazardous Environment Combat Unit was sent into Black Mesa to contain the outbreak and silence witnesses. Heavy armor went through the aperture. That is the same dual motive as the planetary secret war: project force, erase knowledge.

Science fiction keeps teaching planets run like military bases with a commander. That is less random pulp laziness than leftover org chart. The people who could reach Mars never demobilized the command.

“Martians ruled Earth” is often the wrong noun. The rulers can be humans who could reach Mars — lift, portal, leftover flyer lineage — then sell the public an alien-invasion novel so the human side of the ledger stays invisible.

Dead Planet is not a separate later conspiracy. Mars as a rock, canals as a mistake, the war as fiction — that is the cover half of the same operation, running roughly 1909 to 1962 and sealed for the public by [Mariner 2 in 1962](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-2/). Soft history’s cratered-desert postcard is [Mariner 4 in 1965](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-4/). Do not mash those two craft into one date. Suppress hard enough and even the operators’ grandchildren forget there was a war.

That is why this does not need nine conspiracies. Same leftover kit. Same double job — hit hard, stay unowned. Same two camps.

## What the narrator never saw

Wells’s narrator watches the pit. He sees the rounded fungoid forms crawl from the cylinders. He sees the machines. He does not see living bipeds climb out of suits.

Later, in a retrospective digression that mixes memory with later science, the book mentions shrivelled biped remains that “had fallen into human hands” — about six feet tall, flimsy siliceous skeletons, round heads, large eyes in flinty sockets — two or three per cylinder, “all killed before earth was reached,” filed as provisions from Mars. The narrator never watched them eaten in the pit. He never attended an autopsy that opened a host and pulled an occupant free. He trusted the museum and the anatomists the way a survivor trusts the institutional caption. The Epilogue even points the general reader at the magnificent specimen in the Natural History Museum and says the rest is purely scientific.

Those bipeds are the actual Martians who occupy the host bodies. The four-foot fungoid “Martian” witnesses saw is the suit — armor, life support, and interface in one. Occupants ride curled inside. The suit owns locomotion and the senses. Wells already said they wore different bodies according to their needs, just as men wear suits of clothes. The food label is the cheapest way to keep a casual reader from joining the two layers. Display the suit as the organism and the biped as livestock, and nobody asks what was supposed to wear the equipment.

[*Independence Day*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/everything-in-independence-day-is) later stages the public opening Wells’s survivor account never performs — the autopsy that extracts the occupant. Wells’s printed text did not need a deleted confession. Silence and an unasked question already sealed the secret.

That is how the book survived the great burning into the new science-fiction shelf. It required no manuscript redaction. It contained the important secrets. It was allowed to live as an amazing tale because the culture had already decided amazing tales were imaginary. The biggest secret hid in plain sight because the narrator never witnessed it himself.

Once that is clear, the next question is obvious. What about the books that came after — especially the one that describes Earth’s counterstrike?

## The unfinished flyer

At the end of Wells’s war the invaders are building on Primrose Hill. The artilleryman sees lights and something in the air and believes they have built a flying-machine and are learning to fly. The narrator finds it across the farther lip of the pit — flat and vast and strange — the great flying-machine they had been experimenting with upon Earth’s denser atmosphere when decay and death arrested them. Death had come not a day too soon. Sight-seers still gather around Martian machines left standing after the war.

London was not a random target. It was a dogmatic pinnacle of human culture in that era — the British Empire’s way of ruling the whole Earth made visible. An attempt on that peak wakes anyone watching. The attackers die of disease before they finish the flyer. Their technology falls into human hands. The people who inherit it learn not only how the machines work, but what kind of goal is worth fighting for across worlds.

Hiding that kit from nearly every human is less plausible than factions studying it. The Epilogue already shows public machines and museum specimens. Partial compartmentalization is one thing. Universal silence is not the default.

## Earth’s reply

Serviss’s 1898 sequel does not read like empty fantasy once the Primrose Hill flyer is on the table. In the novel’s frame, humans under a wartime presidency mount a secret war with Mars. They build electrical ships that fight gravity — not glowing cartoon saucers — take the fight to the other planet, and wreck the canal dams so the inhabited basins flood. That is the finishing move you would use against a civilization whose open signature was irrigation. Treat 1898 as the filing date, not the ceasefire. The book shows what kind of reply was possible. The real work took decades and stayed off the books.

Schiaparelli mapped *canali* from 1877. Lowell kept insisting on artificial irrigation while the war and its aftermath were still underway. From about 1909 the establishment pushed the fade. Surface scrub for camera readiness belongs roughly 1918 to 1962. This site’s public seal is Mariner 2 in 1962; soft history’s Mars postcard is Mariner 4 in 1965. Soft history says the lines were never real. The better read is that the waterworks were killed, then denied. The Martians did not turn the water off. We did.

That lift technology did not vanish from human politics the day the novel ended. It was used for decades inside the same war window. After World War II the public remembered flying saucers without clear owners, without a clear why, without a clear chain of custody. That residue is explainable if the Primrose Hill lineage was real, compartmentalized, and fought over.

That double job never leaves the operation. Hit Mars. Keep the war unowned. Those are not two conspiracies. They are two arms of one brief.

## Public flashes, then the lid

The secret war did not stay invisible every night. Searchlights, press releases, and mass sky waves kept breaking into view, then getting shut down the next morning. Soft history files each flash as its own curiosity. Put them together and you see the same move: something real enough to fire guns or announce a disc, then a cover story and a specialty wall.

The [1896–97 mystery airship wave](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/mystery-airships-1896-1897-vocabulary-bridge.md) ran across America before the Wright brothers, before Roswell, before the CIA. Witnesses described structured craft in the language of the day — *airship*, not *UFO* — then came ridicule and no lasting official answer. The [Mars contacts timeline](https://paradigmthreat.net/timeline/evt-mars-contacts-timeline) keeps that wave next to the later sky episodes. That is the public seeing contact during the same years the novels were encoding it.

On the night of 24–25 February 1942, ten weeks after Pearl Harbor, U.S. batteries fired more than 1,400 anti-aircraft rounds at unidentified targets over Los Angeles — the [Battle of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Los_Angeles). No bombs dropped. No Japanese wreckage. Japan later said it flew nothing over the city that night. Army and Navy contradicted each other in public. The tidy later story was weather balloon and war nerves. Searchlight photos still show beams meeting on something luminous. This site’s [Pearl Harbor investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/investigations/pearl-harbor-hijacked-imperial-japan-investigation.md) treats that night as a possible staging rhyme in the same post–Pearl Harbor panic: expect a Japanese raid, watch an anomalous object, remember an attack, then deny. Whatever survived that barrage was not a weather balloon.

In July 1947, [Roswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident) Army Air Field announced recovery of a “flying disc,” then took it back within a day and said weather balloon — the same kind of lid used after Los Angeles. [Kenneth Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Arnold)’s “saucers” the month before gave the era its name. A military press office does not invent a disc recovery for fun and then scramble to unsay it unless camps are fighting over who owns the story.

Those flashes do not read as one alien empire already owning every human government. If a single off-world caste already ran Earth as a closed farm, you would not need public anti-aircraft nights, competing service statements, disc announcements, and panicked retractions. What fits is two camps already at war — human factions with Mars-reach kit, plus breakaway players on the far side — colliding in ways the public briefly sees before the cover half seals the file. Historians are trained to keep airships, Los Angeles, Roswell, and Depression-era Halloween in separate books so the join never appears.

## How nobody remembers

You do not need psychic erasers for planetary amnesia. Marvel’s [2004–05 *Secret War*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_War_(comics)) stages a memory wipe of the operators after Nick Fury’s unauthorized Latveria strike. That is an outcome parallel. It is not the mechanism Earth needed.

A real Mars secret war only needs a strike that cannot be traced home and almost nobody who saw the ships leave. Far-side ops, deniable propulsion, no public telemetry. No victory parade. No after-action report in the clear. People who stayed home have a hole in the sky, not a war story. Then the cover myths fill the hole: bacteria saved us, it was only a novel, the canals were an illusion, Mars is dead.

Anyone who only saw ships depart and vanish can be told anything — accident, deep mission, never happened. The war stays unowned. The quiet half of the job is ships that never come home. The loud half is silencing witnesses. Same operation.

Ordinary history uses “secret war” for covert theaters inside a known conflict — Laos, proxy sponsorship, escalation management. That almost never means a war with another world that neither public remembers. Soft scholarship will not hand you that second sense. Most historians are trained to disconnect Depression from radio Halloween, canal fade from Serviss, Los Angeles from Roswell, Tonkin declass from a comics delay, so each shelf stays a specialty and the join never shows.

## Two camps

Once humans hold Mars-reach technology, the fight on Earth is not geography. It is camp.

One camp wants sovereignty for every nation and person it can extend that instinct to — call it the **pro-national** or sovereignty camp. The other wants the opposite — full control over nations and persons under puppet governments, controlled media, plagues, famines, and economic strife. The Great Depression and World War II were accomplishments of the **conquering** camp. They could not have been the goals of the sovereignty camp. The reader essay [*The Great Depression Was Planned*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-great-depression-was-planned) walks the farm-pen method on the American and Holodomor boards. Soft history wants a single king with a stop switch. The mechanism is cages, process, and off-books pressure riding dogma. Keep the calendar in view: by the late 1930s that Depression machine was still grinding when American living rooms got the other half of the lesson — Martians as costume. Historians usually file economics in one book and radio folklore in another.

You have to rebuild how you look at the last three centuries around those two camps. Mudflood aftermath, Mars novels, Depression, world wars, canal fade, Dead Planet, airships, Los Angeles, Roswell, portal folklore, comics containment — none of that holds together as one alien empire, one deep-state monolith, or a string of accidents. Soft history’s specialties are how the disconnect is done. Without two camps fighting over lift, portals, and public memory, each next fact breaks the last explanation.

The [Philadelphia Experiment](https://paradigmthreat.net/events/philadelphia/the-philadelphia-experiment-was-not-time-travel.md) is a useful fingerprint. The legend puts a 1943 Navy-yard trial that moved a ship between harbors — Philadelphia to Norfolk — not through time. This site reads it as ordered reverse engineering of offworld or Golden Age hardware without a full operator manual: rotating fields, a place jump, crew burned or frozen when the coil shut off. The [investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/events/philadelphia/investigations/philadelphia-experiment-portal-precursor-investigation.md) walks the Allende–Jessup–Varo leak chain. That kind of sloppy military yard work, with thousands of civilians nearby and injuries that leak into letters, is hard to square with the conquering camp. A faction that plans Depressions and world wars does not casually leave portal-class kit with the open Navy for a burn-the-crew science fair, and it is not usually that clumsy when it owns the stack. What fits better is the pro-national camp grabbing recovered or contested technology and trying to reproduce it under wartime urgency — incomplete manuals, a catastrophic learning curve — then the conquering camp’s lid and the time-travel movie mockery sealing the file. Roswell and Los Angeles rhyme the same way: contested custody, not finished alien ownership of every capital.

The deep state people have been hunting does not have to “start on Mars” as a foreign species planting a flag. It has to be political for a long time — family power struggles the majority on any planet cannot see or trace. An unseen fight culminates into a secret war between planets. Standing on Mars does not give lasting authority over Earth. You cannot attack a planet without the risk that your failed mission rebounds to your other doorstep within years. The London attack is that rebound: a desperate dying Mars trying to take advantage, and Earth waking up hard enough to reply. Los Angeles, Roswell, the airships, and Philadelphia are the same pattern — something public or almost public, then the lid. That is contested ownership between camps, not a finished alien takeover of all capitals.

After a triumphant counterstrike the horrible final reality is that you can find no single source to the great poison of the other camp. It is deep in the culture. It can affect any person at any time and drive them from virtue to desperation. Identification happens at the level of actions and motives — what a faction builds, what it suppresses, what outcomes it accepts — not at the level of passport stamps from a red planet.

## Bodies as equipment

![Body as equipment — host and occupant across War of the Worlds, Independence Day, Half-Life 2 Stalkers, and related Mars encodings](https://paradigmthreat.net/cosmos/mars/investigations/body-as-equipment-host-occupant.jpg)

![Occupant biped, fungoid host (head + tentacle-hands, no arms/legs), brazen-hood fighting-machine with fisherman’s-basket rear and Heat-Ray case](https://paradigmthreat.net/cosmos/mars/the-secret-war-host-bodies.png)

The domineering camp needs a slave culture deep inside it. Even at the high levels of that society, people know they are using real human slaves as tools — controlled on a transhuman level, made dependent, forced to work. That is the nature of the original War of the Worlds invasion once the bipeds are read as riders rather than rations. Tall thin human-stock occupants locked into Earth-variant hosts, program-driven, building the Primrose Hill return ship or dying painfully on Earth with no rescue. Rebellion is hard when your body is owned by the masters. The suit is life support. Without home servicing it fails. The workers complete the industrial mission or perish.

In Wells the layers are clear if you stay with the [text](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36). The first host layer is the fungoid suit the public mistook for the organism — oily brown bulk about the size of a bear, immense eyes, sixteen whip-like tentacles in two bunches of eight, no arms or legs. The fighting-machine is not a glass cockpit with a pilot’s chair. It is three towering legs, an aluminium body, glittering metallic tentacles on the chassis, and a rear white-metal mass Wells compares to a gigantic fisherman’s basket, with the host nested in a brazen hood that turns like a cowled head. A wrecked operator crawls out of that hood as a small brown figure to repair a leg. When a shell bursts the hood, witnesses see red flesh and glittering metal — organism inside a metal cowl, not a sealed bridge. The Heat-Ray is not held in soft tentacles like a pistol. A kind of machine arm carries a complicated metallic case with a funnel; Wells also calls it the camera that fired the Heat-Ray. Green flashes around the case; the beam itself is invisible. Soft tentacles run the handling-machines. The war chassis mounts the weapon. Either way the rider stays dependent: the suit is life support, the masters own the body, and recall is built in.

[*Half-Life 2*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_2) shows the same grammar as mass culture. Stalkers are humans surgically stripped into Combine slave labor — attenuated bodies, little remaining voice, bodies owned by the machine hierarchy. That is not a “food biped” joke. It is the enslaved rider made visible for a game audience.

The secrets exposed in England in the failed incursion were captured and contained. One Earth camp tried to use them. Another struggled to do something about the inevitable plan — to strike the other planet and find the root. The flyer lineage did not stay on Primrose Hill as a tourist curiosity. It entered that fight.

## Tanks on the far side

The Xen tableau itself belongs to the original ending. The point does not move. There was a human force that fought a secret war on another world that almost nobody was supposed to find out about. The game shows conventional vehicles through the aperture — tanks inside the far-side grammar, whether or not anyone ever bolted an Abrams into a saucer in real life.

## The phrase on the comics shelf

Marvel already owned “Secret Wars” as a 1984 toy-event title — heroes fight on Battleworld for a toy line. In 2004–05 Brian Michael Bendis and Gabriele Dell’Otto published *Secret War*, singular — a different product. Nick Fury learns a Latverian prime minister is funding villains with advanced tech for terror on American soil. The President denies authorization. Fury runs an unauthorized strike, levels Castle Doom, memory-wipes his own hero team, and goes underground. Blowback returns as a citywide antimatter bomb. His exit hope is that they will understand what he did was necessary. The series was meant to ship bimonthly. Delays stretched it from April 2004 to issue five on 14 December 2005 — the same month the NSA’s Gulf of Tonkin SIGINT pack hit the public record. That is the post-9/11 strength window for NSA-aligned media. Most historians will never join a comics ship date to a SIGINT release; the disconnect is the method. The book does two jobs at once. It contains “secret war” as cartoon memory. It teaches the necessity of the sin eater — someone who crosses norms, bends rules, and eats the dirt so the rest of the case can stay pure — with Fury as a major face. [*The Sin Eater*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-sin-eater) says the job out loud for adults through *The Bourne Legacy*. Bendis staged it for the comics aisle first.

In 2015 Jonathan Hickman’s *Secret Wars* re-owns the brand as Battleworld multiverse cosmic event — Doom as god-emperor, Incursions, toy-scale spectacle. That is the exact opposite of Fury’s grounded spy story. Keep the words. Bury the lesson. That is CIA-camp containment of an NSA-aligned deposit. Kids who only know 2015 never meet the sin eater who was the point of 2004–05.

The planetary secret war still stays unthinkable. The shelf is occupied. The phrase routes to comics. The ships that left and never returned stay off the evening news.

## Why nothing else fits

Try explaining this pile without two camps fighting over Mars-reach technology, and the story falls apart.

If *War of the Worlds* is only a novel, why does it leave a nearly finished flying-machine sitting in the open, and a museum culture that labels the bipeds as food when the narrator never saw them alive? If the canals were only an optical trick, why does Serviss’s 1898 book climax on destroying the waterworks while Lowell’s maps still look alive into the early 1900s, then get laughed off from about 1909? If Mars was always a dead rock, why do [*Half-Life*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_(video_game)) and Marvel keep teaching the same double job — send force to the far side, then shut the witnesses up? You do not need a psychic eraser for that amnesia. Ships that leave and never come home already do the work. Soft history’s “secret war” means Laos and proxy wars. It does not mean a war with another world that neither public remembers.

Put the calendar next to itself and the specialty walls look deliberate. Around 1774 the [Mudflood](https://paradigmthreat.net/timeline/evt-1774-ce-the-mars-catastrophe) hits Mars hard. In the 1890s London gets invaded and leaves a flyer on Primrose Hill. In 1898 Serviss encodes Earth’s reply — that is filing, not the ceasefire. The real fighting stretches from the Civil War years through World War II. The canals get killed; by 1962 the Dead Planet story is sealed. In between, the public actually sees sky flashes: [airships](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/mystery-airships-1896-1897-vocabulary-bridge.md), the [Battle of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Los_Angeles) in 1942, a clumsy [Philadelphia](https://paradigmthreat.net/events/philadelphia/the-philadelphia-experiment-was-not-time-travel.md) reverse-engineering story that fits the sovereignty camp better than a conqueror who never slips, [Roswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident) in 1947 — and then the lid every time. Depression and world war are what the conquering camp builds. Slave grammar lives inside the host culture. Comics teach the [sin eater](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-sin-eater) in 2004, then bury him under Battleworld in 2015. None of that is a string of unrelated hobbies. Historians are trained to keep the folders separate so the join never shows.

You have to rebuild how you look at the last three centuries around those two camps. One alien empire already running every capital does not need public anti-aircraft nights and panicked press retractions. One accidental deep state does not explain both the leftover flyer and the Halloween that told a Depression-era country the Martians were a costume. The pieces only lock when you stop treating each shelf as its own religion.

## It’s just Halloween!

Forty years after Wells, [Orson Welles](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/orsonwellswaroftheworlds.htm) put the invasion on American radio and then stepped out of character to tell the country none of it was real. The Mercury Theatre’s [30 October 1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_of_the_Worlds_(1938_radio_drama)) broadcast had just annihilated the world before people’s ears. Remind yourself what else was raging. The Great Depression was still the weather of American life — unemployment, farm collapse, the farm-pen politics walked in [*The Great Depression Was Planned*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-great-depression-was-planned) — while the conquering camp’s next act, World War II, was already on the doorstep. Into that stress, Welles’s closer delivered the containment line: holiday offering, sheet-and-boo, we didn’t mean it, CBS is still open for business. That is how a living room learns to file Martians under pumpkin patch while the economic war on the public continues in a different vocabulary. Soft history treats the broadcast as a quirky media anecdote and the Depression as a separate macroeconomics chapter. Most historians disconnect the two on purpose — specialty walls, not coincidence. The novel had already survived as science fiction. The broadcast taught a mass audience that the terror itself was a costume, at the exact moment when belief in far-side politics was least convenient.

> So goodbye, everybody, and remember, please, for the next day or so, the terrible lesson you learned tonight. That grinning, glowing, globular invader of your living room is an inhabitant of the pumpkin patch, and if your doorbell rings and nobody's there, that was no Martian; it's Halloween.
