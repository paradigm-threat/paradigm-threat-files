> **TL;DR:** A secret war between Earth and Mars was fought and covered up after a late-nineteenth-century incursion left lift technology in human hands. Wells (**1897**) and Serviss (**1898**) are the **encode**, not the ops calendar: the real war window runs roughly **Civil War through World War II**; canal fade and Dead Planet burial run **~1909 into 1962**. Public flashes — airships, **Battle of Los Angeles**, **Roswell**, and a sloppy Navy-yard **Philadelphia** reverse-engineering read — show the war breaking into view before the lid; they imply **two camps at war**, not aliens already owning every government. **Nothing in the last three centuries makes sense without that two-camp frame** — soft history’s single-king, single-alien, single-accident shelves are the disconnect. The publics on both worlds were kept dark. The poison was never a single base to bomb — it was a culture that could flip anyone from virtue to desperation.

H. G. Wells opened [*The War of the Worlds*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36) by saying no one would have believed that worlds were being watched. The harder claim is this one. A war between planets could be fought, answered across decades, and buried so thoroughly that later generations inherited only novels, canal “illusions,” and a dead rock in the sky. When the pieces locked, they locked as one story — not a pile of separate mysteries.

## This explains everything

Stop treating Earth and Mars as two sealed worlds that only meet in pulp. Treat them as one political graph with about one degree of separation at the top — a thin command channel — and mass publics kept unaware on both ends. The war that matters is secret by design: project force to the far side, and keep the record from becoming public memory.

Once that frame is on the table, the leftovers stop looking random.

![The Secret War — timeline from MFEE through London incursion, Earth reply, canal kill, Dead Planet Protocol, and media echoes](https://paradigmthreat.net/cosmos/mars/the-secret-war-timeline.png)

The invaders left a great flying-machine on Primrose Hill — already under atmospheric test when disease stopped them. That kit is not a prop. It is a ladder. Earth factions that could finish or copy it suddenly had lift, and a reason to use it.

Wells’s novel serialized in **1897**. In **1898**, Garrett P. Serviss published [*Edison’s Conquest of Mars*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/19141) — a public Earth fleet of electrical ships that defy gravity, a strike on Mars, and a climax that wrecks the canal waterworks. That is an **encode of Earth’s reply**, not proof the fleet finished the war in the same publishing season. The operational window is wider: roughly the **1860s through the 1940s** — Civil War to World War II — expeditionary strikes, occupation pressure, resource control, and suppression on both worlds. The canal maps of Schiaparelli and Lowell were not a collective hallucination first. They were a running irrigation signature that still peaked in the early **1900s**. The finishing move did not turn the water off on Mars for the Martians’ convenience. We did. Canal “illusion” talk hardens from about **1909**; Dead Planet Protocol burial runs on through surface scrub and camera seal into **1962**.

An earlier catastrophe — the Mudflood Energetic Event around **1774** — devastated Mars before the London incursion. That layer is **prelude**, not the secret war itself. It is easy to misfile as Mars attacking itself. It is easier still to overlook if the engagement was ordered from Earth through the same political channel that already linked the two worlds. Agency matters as much as the beam.

Stories of breakaway civilizations and “lost worlds” do not require sealed planets. They require compartmentalization. The publics never get the map. The command caste does.

Valve’s [*Half-Life*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_(video_game)) ends with the G-Man claiming the far side while wrecked Abrams tanks, Marines, and aircraft sit in the tableau. The Hazardous Environment Combat Unit was sent into Black Mesa to contain the outbreak and silence witnesses. Heavy armor went through the aperture. That is the same dual motive as the planetary secret war: project force, erase knowledge.

Science fiction keeps teaching planets run like military bases with a commander. That is less random pulp laziness than leftover org chart. The people who could reach Mars never demobilized the command.

“Martians ruled Earth” is often the wrong noun. The rulers can be humans who could reach Mars — lift, portal, leftover flyer lineage — then sell the public an alien-invasion novel so the human side of the ledger stays invisible.

Dead Planet Protocol — Mars as a rock, canals as optical error, the war as fiction — is not a separate later conspiracy. It is the suppression half of the same operation, running roughly **1909–1962** and sealed for the public by **[Mariner 2 (1962)](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-2/)**. Soft history’s Mars close-up is **[Mariner 4 (1965)](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-4/)**; do not collapse those two craft into one date. Suppress hard enough and even the operators’ descendants forget there was a war.

That is why the stack lines up. Same leftover kit. Same dual motive. Same political graph. Not nine conspiracies.

## What the narrator never saw

Wells’s narrator watches the pit. He sees the rounded fungoid forms crawl from the cylinders. He sees the machines. He does not see living bipeds climb out of suits.

Later, in a retrospective digression that mixes memory with later science, the book mentions shrivelled biped remains that “had fallen into human hands” — about six feet tall, flimsy siliceous skeletons, round heads, large eyes in flinty sockets — two or three per cylinder, “all killed before earth was reached,” filed as provisions from Mars. The narrator never watched them eaten in the pit. He never attended an autopsy that opened a host and pulled an occupant free. He trusted the museum and the anatomists the way a survivor trusts the institutional caption. The Epilogue even points the general reader at the magnificent specimen in the Natural History Museum and says the rest is purely scientific.

Those bipeds are the actual Martians who occupy the host bodies. The four-foot fungoid “Martian” witnesses saw is the suit — armor, life support, and interface in one. Occupants ride curled inside. The suit owns locomotion and the senses. Wells already said they wore different bodies according to their needs, just as men wear suits of clothes. The food label is the cheapest way to keep a casual reader from joining the two layers. Display the suit as the organism and the biped as livestock, and nobody asks what was supposed to wear the equipment.

[*Independence Day*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/everything-in-independence-day-is) later stages the public opening Wells’s survivor account never performs — the autopsy that extracts the occupant. Wells’s printed text did not need a deleted confession. Silence and an unasked question already sealed the secret.

That is how the book survived the great burning into the new science-fiction shelf. It required no manuscript redaction. It contained the important secrets. It was allowed to live as an amazing tale because the culture had already decided amazing tales were imaginary. The biggest secret hid in plain sight because the narrator never witnessed it himself.

Once that is clear, the next question is obvious. What about the books that came after — especially the one that describes Earth’s counterstrike?

## The unfinished flyer

At the end of Wells’s war the invaders are building on Primrose Hill. The artilleryman sees lights and something in the air and believes they have built a flying-machine and are learning to fly. The narrator finds it across the farther lip of the pit — flat and vast and strange — the great flying-machine they had been experimenting with upon Earth’s denser atmosphere when decay and death arrested them. Death had come not a day too soon. Sight-seers still gather around Martian machines left standing after the war.

London was not a random target. It was a dogmatic pinnacle of human culture in that era — the British Empire’s way of ruling the whole Earth made visible. An attempt on that peak wakes anyone watching. The attackers die of disease before they finish the flyer. Their technology falls into human hands. The people who inherit it learn not only how the machines work, but what kind of goal is worth fighting for across worlds.

Hiding that kit from nearly every human is less plausible than factions studying it. The Epilogue already shows public machines and museum specimens. Partial compartmentalization is one thing. Universal silence is not the default.

## Earth’s reply

Serviss’s **1898** sequel does not read like empty fantasy once the Primrose Hill ladder is on the table. In the novel’s frame, humans under a wartime presidency mount a secret war with Mars. They build electrical ships that defy gravity — not glowing blue cartoon saucers, but charge against gravitation. They take the fight to the other planet. The climax wrecks the canal dams and floods the inhabited basins. That is not random pulp color. It is the logical finishing move against a civilization whose surface signature was irrigation. Treat **1898 as the filing date**, not the ceasefire. The real war window stretches across the **Civil War through World War II** band: the encode tells the public what kind of reply was possible; the ops take decades and stay off the books.

Schiaparelli mapped *canali* from **1877**. Lowell insisted for years on artificial irrigation — still readable while the war and its aftermath were underway. From about **1909** the establishment pushed the “fade.” Surface dismantling for camera readiness belongs roughly **1918–1962**. Author seal for the public “dead Mars” ontology is **[Mariner 2 (1962)](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-2/)**. Soft history’s cratered-desert postcard is **[Mariner 4](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mariner-4/) in 1965**. Soft history says the lines were never real. The better read is that the waterworks were killed, then denied. The Martians did not turn the water off. We did.

That lift technology did not vanish from human politics the day the novel ended. It was used for decades inside the same war window. After World War II the public remembered flying saucers without clear owners, without a clear why, without a clear chain of custody. That residue is explainable if the Primrose Hill lineage was real, compartmentalized, and fought over.

The dual motive never leaves the operation. Hit Mars. Keep the war unowned. Those are not two conspiracies. They are two arms of one brief.

## Public flashes, then the lid

The secret war did not stay invisible every night. It kept **breaking into public** — searchlights, press releases, mass sky waves — and then getting **suppressed the next morning**. Soft history files each flash as a separate curiosity. Joined, they are the same pattern: something real enough to mobilize guns or announce a disc, then a cover myth and a specialty wall.

The [1896–97 mystery airship wave](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/mystery-airships-1896-1897-vocabulary-bridge.md) ran at national scale before Wright, before Roswell, before the CIA — structured craft reported in period **airship** language, then ridicule and no durable adjudication ([Mars contacts timeline](https://paradigmthreat.net/timeline/evt-mars-contacts-timeline)). That is public contact grammar during the encode window.

On **24–25 February 1942**, ten weeks after Pearl Harbor, U.S. batteries fired **over 1,400 anti-aircraft rounds** at unidentified targets over Los Angeles — the [Battle of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Los_Angeles). No bombs dropped. No Japanese wreckage. Japan later confirmed **no overflight**. Army and Navy contradicted each other in public; the tidy later story was **weather balloon** and war nerves. Searchlight photos still show beams converging on something luminous. This site’s Pearl Harbor dossier treats the night as a possible **staging template** in the same post-PH panic machine — expected “Japanese raid,” anomalous object, spectacle remembered as attack, then denial ([Pearl Harbor investigation §15](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/investigations/pearl-harbor-hijacked-imperial-japan-investigation.md)). Whatever survived that barrage was not soft history’s weather balloon.

In **July 1947**, [Roswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident) Army Air Field announced recovery of a **“flying disc,”** then retracted within a day to **weather balloon** — the same class of lid used after Los Angeles. Kenneth Arnold’s “saucers” the month before named the era. A military press office does not invent a disc recovery for fun and then scramble to un-say it unless **camps** are fighting over custody of the narrative.

Those flashes do not read as **one alien empire already owning every human government**. If a single off-world caste already ran Earth as a closed farm, there would be no need for public AA nights, competing service statements, disc announcements, and panicked retractions. What fits is **two camps** already at war — human factions with Mars-reach kit, plus breakaway / far-side players — colliding in ways the public briefly sees before the suppression half of the dual motive seals the file. Historians disconnect airships from Los Angeles from Roswell from Depression Halloween so the political graph never appears.

## How nobody remembers

You do not need psychic erasers for planetary amnesia. Marvel’s [2004–05 *Secret War*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_War_(comics)) stages a memory wipe of the operators after Nick Fury’s unauthorized Latveria strike. That is an outcome parallel. It is not the mechanism Earth needed.

A real Mars secret war only needs attribution failure and a thin witness set. The strike cannot be traced back to Earth — far-side ops, deniable propulsion, no public telemetry. Almost nobody knows unless they saw ships leave and not return. There is no victory parade. There is no after-action in the clear. Survivors who stayed home have absence, not a war story. Cover myths fill the gap: bacteria miracle, “just a novel,” canals as illusion, Dead Planet.

Anyone who only saw ships depart and vanish can be told anything — accident, deep mission, never happened. The war stays unowned. The quiet edge of the dual motive is ships that never come home. The aggressive edge is silencing witnesses. Same operation.

Ordinary history uses “secret war” for covert theaters inside a known conflict — Laos, proxy sponsorship, escalation management. That shelf almost never means a war with another world that neither public remembers. The planetary amnesia war is a different sense of the word. Soft scholarship will not hand it to you. Most historians are trained to **disconnect** such moments — Depression from radio Halloween, canal fade from Serviss, Battle of Los Angeles from Roswell, Tonkin declass from a comics delay — so each shelf stays a separate specialty and the political graph never appears.

## Two camps

Once humans hold Mars-reach technology, the fight on Earth is not geography. It is camp.

One camp wants sovereignty for every nation and person it can extend that instinct to — call it the **pro-national** or sovereignty camp. The other wants the opposite — full control over nations and persons under puppet governments, controlled media, plagues, famines, and economic strife. The Great Depression and World War II were accomplishments of the **conquering** camp. They could not have been the goals of the sovereignty camp. The reader essay [*The Great Depression Was Planned*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-great-depression-was-planned) walks the farm-pen method on the American and Holodomor boards. Soft history wants a single king with a stop switch. The mechanism is cages, process, and off-books pressure riding dogma. Keep the calendar in view: by the late 1930s that Depression machine was still grinding when American living rooms got the other half of the lesson — Martians as costume. Historians usually file economics in one book and radio folklore in another.

**You have to redefine reality around that two-camp graph.** The last three centuries — mudflood aftermath, Mars encode, Depression, world wars, canal fade, Dead Planet, airships, Los Angeles, Roswell, portal folklore, comics containment — do not cohere as one alien empire, one deep-state monolith, or a string of disconnected accidents. Soft history’s specialties are the disconnect method. Without two camps fighting over lift, portals, and public memory, nothing in that span has a single political explanation that survives contact with the next bead.

The [Philadelphia Experiment](https://paradigmthreat.net/events/philadelphia/the-philadelphia-experiment-was-not-time-travel.md) (legend **1943**, ship-scale harbor routing under Navy cover — not time travel) is a useful camp-fingerprint. This site reads it as **ordered reverse engineering** of offworld / Golden Age class hardware without full operator literacy: rotating fields, Philadelphia↔Norfolk place jump, crew Flame and Freeze when the coil shut off ([investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/events/philadelphia/investigations/philadelphia-experiment-portal-precursor-investigation.md)). That kind of **sloppy military yard trial** — civilians in the thousands nearby, injuries that leak into the Allende–Jessup–Varo chain — is hard to square with the conquering camp. A faction that plans Depressions and world wars does not casually leave portal-class kit with the open Navy for a burn-the-crew science fair, and it is not usually that clumsy when it owns the stack. What fits better is the **pro-national camp** grabbing recovered or contested technology and trying to reproduce it under wartime urgency — incomplete manuals, catastrophic learning curve, then the conquering camp’s lid and mockery layer (time-travel film deposit) sealing the file. Roswell and Los Angeles rhyme: contested custody, not finished alien ownership of every capital.

The deep state people have been hunting does not have to “start on Mars” as a foreign species planting a flag. It has to be political for a long time — family-oriented power struggles the majority on any planet cannot see or trace. An unseen struggle culminates into a secret war between planets. Standing on Mars does not give lasting authority over Earth. You cannot attack a planet without the risk that your failed mission turns into a rapid rebound that reaches your other doorstep within years. The London incursion is that rebound grammar: a desperate dying Mars trying to take advantage of the situation, and Earth waking up hard enough to reply. Battle of Los Angeles, Roswell, airships, and Philadelphia are the same grammar: **public or semi-public collision**, then **lid** — evidence of contested ownership between camps, not of a finished alien takeover of all capitals.

After a triumphant counterstrike the horrible final reality is that you can find no single source to the great poison of the other camp. It is deep in the culture. It can affect any person at any time and drive them from virtue to desperation. Identification happens at the level of actions and motives — what a faction builds, what it suppresses, what outcomes it accepts — not at the level of passport stamps from a red planet.

## Bodies as equipment

![Body as equipment — host and occupant across War of the Worlds, Independence Day, Half-Life 2 Stalkers, and related Mars encodings](https://paradigmthreat.net/cosmos/mars/investigations/body-as-equipment-host-occupant.jpg)

![Occupant biped, fungoid host (head + tentacle-hands, no arms/legs), brazen-hood fighting-machine with fisherman’s-basket rear and Heat-Ray case](https://paradigmthreat.net/cosmos/mars/the-secret-war-host-bodies.png)

The domineering camp needs a slave culture deep inside it. Even at the high levels of that society, people know they are using real human slaves as tools — controlled on a transhuman level, made dependent, forced to work. That is the nature of the original War of the Worlds invasion once the bipeds are read as riders rather than rations. Tall thin human-stock occupants locked into Earth-variant hosts, program-driven, building the Primrose Hill return ship or dying painfully on Earth with no rescue. Rebellion is hard when your body is owned by the masters. The suit is life support. Without home servicing it fails. The workers complete the industrial mission or perish.

In Wells the layers stack cleanly if you stay with the text. The first host layer is the fungoid suit the public mistook for the organism — oily brown bulk, immense eyes, sixteen whip-like tentacles in two bunches. The fighting-machine is not a glass cockpit with a pilot’s chair. It is legs, aluminium body, glittering metallic tentacles, and a rear white-metal mass “like a gigantic fisherman’s basket,” with the host nested in a **brazen hood** that turns like a cowled head. A wrecked operator **crawls out of his hood** as a small brown figure to repair a leg. When a shell bursts the hood, witnesses see **red flesh and glittering metal** — organism inside metal cowl, not a sealed spaceship bridge. The Heat-Ray is not a soft-tentacle pistol. **A kind of arm** carries a complicated **metallic case** with a funnel — Wells also calls it the **camera** that fired the Heat-Ray — green flashes around the case, beam itself invisible. Soft tentacles actuate handling-machines; the war chassis mounts the weapon. The control cycle is the same either way: contain, depend, direct, recall.

[*Half-Life 2*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_2) shows the same grammar as mass culture. Stalkers are humans surgically stripped into Combine slave labor — attenuated bodies, little remaining voice, bodies owned by the machine hierarchy. That is not a “food biped” joke. It is the enslaved rider made visible for a game audience.

The secrets exposed in England in the failed incursion were captured and contained. One Earth camp tried to use them. Another struggled to do something about the inevitable plan — to strike the other planet and find the root. The flyer lineage did not stay on Primrose Hill as a tourist curiosity. It entered that fight.

## Tanks on the far side

The biggest eyebrow this topic ever raised in predictive programming was at the end of *Half-Life*. Behind the G-Man, as he explains that the protagonist was used for a useful purpose and can be put away now, sit destroyed Abrams tanks and other military wreckage on the far side. The backstory of the counterinsurgency team is deepened in the expansions. Blue Shift puts an Abrams on a Black Mesa freight yard — Earthside, not Xen. The Xen tableau itself belongs to the original ending. The point does not move. There was a human force that fought a secret war on another world that almost nobody was supposed to find out about. The game shows conventional vehicles through the aperture — tanks inside the far-side grammar, whether or not anyone ever bolted an Abrams into a saucer in real life.

HECU’s brief is the dual motive in miniature. Contain the outbreak. Silence the witnesses. Project force. Bury the record. The people who can send main battle tanks off-world are the same people tasked with making sure no one talks.

## The phrase on the comics shelf

Marvel already owned “Secret Wars” as a 1984 toy-event title — Battleworld slugfest, focus-group words. In 2004–05 Brian Michael Bendis and Gabriele Dell’Otto published *Secret War*, singular — a different product. Nick Fury learns a Latverian prime minister is funding villains with advanced tech for terror on American soil. The President denies authorization. Fury runs an unauthorized strike, levels Castle Doom, memory-wipes his own hero team, and goes underground. Blowback returns as a citywide antimatter bomb. His exit hope is that they will understand what he did was necessary. The series was meant to ship bimonthly. Delays stretched it from April 2004 to issue five on 14 December 2005 — the same month the NSA’s Gulf of Tonkin SIGINT pack hit the public record. That is the post-9/11 strength window for NSA-aligned media. Most historians will never join a comics ship date to a SIGINT release; the disconnect is the method. The book does two jobs at once. It contains “secret war” as cartoon memory. It teaches the necessity of the sin eater — someone who crosses norms, bends rules, and eats the dirt so the rest of the case can stay pure — with Fury as a major face. [*The Sin Eater*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-sin-eater) says the job out loud for adults through *The Bourne Legacy*. Bendis staged it for the comics aisle first.

In 2015 Jonathan Hickman’s *Secret Wars* re-owns the brand as Battleworld multiverse cosmic event — Doom as god-emperor, Incursions, toy-scale spectacle. That is the exact opposite of Fury’s grounded spy story. Keep the words. Bury the lesson. That is CIA-camp containment of an NSA-aligned deposit. Kids who only know 2015 never meet the sin eater who was the point of 2004–05.

The planetary secret war still stays unthinkable. The shelf is occupied. The phrase routes to comics. The ships that left and never returned stay off the evening news.

## Why nothing else fits

Try explaining this pile without two camps fighting over Mars-reach technology, and the story falls apart.

If *War of the Worlds* is only a novel, why does it leave a nearly finished flying-machine sitting in the open, and a museum culture that labels the bipeds as food when the narrator never saw them alive? If the canals were only an optical trick, why does Serviss’s 1898 book climax on destroying the waterworks while Lowell’s maps still look alive into the early 1900s, then get laughed off from about 1909? If Mars was always a dead rock, why do games and comics keep teaching the same double job — send force to the far side, then shut the witnesses up? You do not need a psychic eraser for that amnesia. Ships that leave and never come home already do the work. Soft history’s “secret war” means Laos and proxy wars. It does not mean a war with another world that neither public remembers.

Put the calendar next to itself and the specialty walls look deliberate. Mudflood damage around 1774. London and the leftover flyer in the 1890s. Serviss encoding Earth’s reply in 1898 — not the ceasefire. The real fighting stretched across the Civil War through World War II window. Canal kill, then Dead Planet burial into 1962. Sky flashes the public actually saw — airships, the Battle of Los Angeles in 1942, a clumsy Philadelphia reverse-engineering story that fits the sovereignty camp better than a conqueror who never slips, Roswell in 1947 — then the lid every time. Depression and world war as what the conquering camp builds. Slave grammar inside the host culture. Comics that teach the sin eater in 2004, then bury him under Battleworld in 2015. None of that is a string of unrelated hobbies. Historians are trained to keep the folders separate so the graph never shows.

You have to rebuild how you look at the last three centuries around those two camps. One alien empire already running every capital does not need public AA nights and panicked press retractions. One accidental deep state does not explain both the leftover flyer and the Halloween that told a Depression-era country the Martians were a costume. The pieces only lock when you stop treating each shelf as its own religion.

## It’s just Halloween!

Forty years after Wells, [Orson Welles](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/orsonwellswaroftheworlds.htm) put the invasion on American radio and then stepped out of character to tell the country none of it was real. The Mercury Theatre’s [30 October 1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_of_the_Worlds_(1938_radio_drama)) broadcast had just annihilated the world before people’s ears. **Remind yourself what else was raging:** the Great Depression was still the weather of American life — unemployment, farm collapse, the farm-pen politics of [*The Great Depression Was Planned*](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/the-great-depression-was-planned) — while the conquering camp’s next act, World War II, was already on the doorstep. Into that stress, Welles’s closer delivered the containment line: holiday offering, sheet-and-boo, we didn’t mean it, CBS is still open for business. That is how a living room learns to file Martians under pumpkin patch while the economic war on the public continues in a different vocabulary. Soft history treats the broadcast as a quirky media anecdote and the Depression as a separate macroeconomics chapter. Most historians disconnect the two on purpose — specialty walls, not coincidence. The novel had already survived as science fiction. The broadcast taught a mass audience that the terror itself was a costume, at the exact cultural moment when belief in far-side politics was least convenient.
