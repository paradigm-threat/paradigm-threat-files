## 1. Mainstream model (Track 1 — logged)

Institutional virology defines a **virus** roughly as:

- An **obligate intracellular** agent — replication requires **living cells** (not free-living bacteria).  
- **Virion** = protein capsid ± envelope, often with **lipid envelope** from host membrane.  
- **Genome** = RNA or DNA (institution word for nucleic acid in the agent).  
- **Disease** in the host = agent replication **plus** **immune/inflammatory** response — institutions rarely claim genome **alone** causes every symptom with **no** host factor.

**Distinction for this file:** **Virion** (particle) vs **infection** (process in host/culture) vs **detection** (PCR antibody antigen) — institutions often **collapse** these in press copy; this dossier **does not**.

**Primary anchors (B tier unless noted):**

- [CDC — clinical overview, laboratory diagnosis](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/professionals/diagnosis/index.htm) (representative viral diagnostic workflow); national lab manuals describe cell-culture isolation steps.

- [Wölfel et al., *Nature* 2020 — virological assessment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2196-x) — culture vs RNA in clinical samples (A tier for COVID culture claims).
