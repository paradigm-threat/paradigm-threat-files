# Get Ready for Trumpcare

The fundamental difference between the two is — where the money goes

![Federal healthcare money plumbing — valve turns insurer pressure tank flow into household HSA vaults](https://paradigmthreat.net/science/health/get-ready-for-trumpcare-hero.jpg)

Obamacare sent most of the federal help to the insurance companies. The pitch now called Trumpcare tries to send it to the people. That is the load-bearing hinge on one track. Under the Affordable Care Act, the [premium tax credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/premium-tax-credit-ptc-overview) belongs to the person and is reconciled at tax time; most households still take it as an **advance** credit paid to the insurer so the monthly bill drops. Under [Trump’s Great Health Care Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/The-Great-Healthcare-Plan.pdf) and the [spoken release line](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/01/president-trump-unveils-the-great-healthcare-plan-to-lower-costs-and-deliver-money-directly-to-the-people/), the White House says stop sending those extra subsidy payments to big insurers and put money with Americans — including into a healthcare savings account in their name. That is a **pitch**, not finished law. Under [current HSA rules](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), account cash generally cannot pay insurance premiums, and an HSA still pairs with a high-deductible plan from an insurer. The name “Trumpcare” will make people expect another signup system. What the money-path track is trying to start is moving purchasing power off the insurer tip.

There is a second track under the same name. On 12 November 2026, Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed posted a gold card mockup branded TrumpCare, with a seal that said Medicare for All, and told Donald Trump the offer was on the table: guarantee healthcare for every American from cradle to grave. Trump reposted the pitch. On 24 November 2026, in an [Oval Office interview with the New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/11/24/us-news/trump-vows-to-meet-with-abdul-el-sayed-on-golden-trumpcare-tells-the-post-he-and-dems-could-do-great-things/), he called El-Sayed’s rebranding “very smart,” said he expects to meet with him to see if there is middle ground, and said he and the incoming Democratic majorities could do “great things together,” while keeping veto power as leverage. That brand offer is not the HSA bill. Medicare-for-All economics and HSA / high-deductible economics pull opposite ways. Both headlines wore “Trumpcare” in November 2026. That sequence is what drove this article.

> **TL;DR:** Two different products share the Trumpcare label. **Track B** is the White House money-path pitch — stop routing extra subsidy cash to insurers; put purchasing power with households, including a stated HSA destination. **Track A** is El-Sayed’s offer to brand a Medicare-for-All / public-guarantee architecture as Trumpcare; Trump praised the branding and floated middle ground. Obamacare dangled a public option, then shipped a private-insurer marketplace that Obama put on his name. The 2026 subsidy cliff is the latency bomb. Trump is eating the naming sin on the cut and the response while an antibody strategy waits on midterm geometry.

## A short history of American health insurance

The modern prepaid hospital plan in the United States is usually dated to 1929, when Justin Ford Kimball at Baylor University Hospital in Dallas set up a group plan for schoolteachers — a fixed annual fee for a set number of hospital days. That line grew into what became Blue Cross. During World War II, wage controls made cash raises hard, and employer-paid health benefits were treated as fringe rather than wages, so firms used coverage to hold workers. By mid-century, employer insurance was the default path for working families. [Medicare](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/RL32237) arrived in 1965 for seniors. The late twentieth century was a long fight over who pays, how much, and whether Washington or employers sat in the middle.

In 2003, the Medicare Prescription Drug, Improvement, and Modernization Act authorized [Health Savings Accounts](https://www.kff.org/wp-content/uploads/2011/03/5-02-13-history-of-health-reform.pdf) — tax-advantaged accounts paired with high-deductible coverage, so households could park money for care without feeding every care dollar through an insurer first. That was a buffer against premium chaos. On this site that household-security habit is read as **NSA-aligned** — the antibody / cleanup lane that hardens American household security while Congress keeps the domestic theater ([*The Sin Eater*](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/the-sin-eater.md)). Then came the Affordable Care Act in 2010, sold as reform and built as a private-insurer marketplace with subsidies.

## The public option they dangled and snatched

The public wanted a public option — a government plan that would compete with private insurers. [Hillary Clinton’s American Health Choices Plan](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/hillary-clinton-campaign-press-release-the-american-health-choices-plan-ensuring), launched 17 September 2007, said Americans would get “the choice of a public plan option similar to Medicare,” and she was still selling that line through the 2008 primary. [Kaiser tracking](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/kaiser-health-tracking-polloctober-2009/) in 2009 showed majority favor for a government-administered public option similar to Medicare — **59%** in August and **57%** in October — even though the wording could move the numbers. The House kept a public option in its bills. The Senate path and the final law did not.

That was the sellout. It was not that America somehow chose corporate medicine after a fair fight for rates and household-side rules. It was a badly negotiated handoff to the medical-industrial stack — the insurers got the architecture, and the public option the Democrats had dangled was pulled at the last stretch. The heart of the problem was never socialized medicine. Socialized medicine would have been the public option under Hillary’s brand of that fight. Democrats dangled it, then snatched it away and gave the country Obama and Biden on the promise that they would do what the rest of the party wanted. They did not. Obama took the major sin of naming the whole debacle after himself. [Enemies coined “Obamacare”](https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/archive/2011/02/obamacare-more-than-just-a-word/71519/); he later [embraced the word](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/2013/09/26/remarks-president-affordable-care-act); a [February 2017 Morning Consult poll](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/02/07/upshot/one-third-dont-know-obamacare-and-affordable-care-act-are-the-same.html) found **35%** of respondents either thought Obamacare and the Affordable Care Act were different policies (**17%**) or were unsure (**18%**), while **72%** of Republicans knew they were the same. It is poetic justice that Trump would take another bite of that naming sin by putting the response under Trumpcare.

## Where the money goes

Under Obamacare, most people who get help take the credit as an advance premium tax credit paid to the insurance company. The household can refuse the advance and settle at tax time; the usual path never puts the cash in the household first. Trump’s first-term **2017** halt of cost-sharing reduction payments fed **silver loading** — insurers raised silver premiums to cover the gap, and premium games followed inside the leftover ACA shell. That is part of the latency stack, not a side note.

Under the Great Health Care Plan, the White House says stop sending big insurers those extra taxpayer subsidy payments and send money directly to eligible Americans. In the spoken line he names a healthcare savings account in the person’s name. The leading Senate vehicle, [S.3386](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3386/text) from Sens. Crapo and Cassidy, would have deposited into an Exchange-plan HSA for eligible enrollees. It [failed cloture **51–48** on 11 December 2025](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/5644280-senate-gop-health-care-plan-cassidy-crapo/) — **0** Democratic yes votes. What did pass on the HSA file was narrower: [P.L. 119-21](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1) and [IRS Notice 2026-05](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-05.pdf) made bronze and catastrophic ACA plans HSA-eligible starting in 2026. That expands who can open an HSA beside an Exchange plan. It is not the full direct-subsidy deposit pitch.

Trumpcare is a misnomer when the ear hears a new system everyone signs up for, the way people signed up for marketplace plans under the ACA. Track B is trying to begin giving purchasing power back — money with the household, not only a discount on an insurer’s invoice. Critics of converting enhanced premium credits into HSA-style deposits document who loses under that swap: low-income households that leave silver cost-sharing for bronze face deductibles jumping from about **$80**-class silver cost-sharing into bronze averages near **$7,476** ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/the-new-aca-repeal-and-replace-health-savings-accounts/)); older and sicker buyers lose age- and income-adjusted subsidy shape; healthier people leaving the risk pool can push premiums up for those who stay ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/converting-aca-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-into-hsa-contributions-would-worsen)). Those costs are real. The load-bearing fight this page tracks is still who holds the cash and who eats the naming sin. The denser scoreboard stays in the [companion write-up](https://paradigmthreat.net/science/health/investigations/obamacare-trumpcare-nsa-cia-alignment-investigation.md).

## The 2026 cliff

Enhanced marketplace credits built in 2021 and extended through 2025 expired at the end of that year. [KFF](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/aca-marketplace-premium-payments-would-more-than-double-on-average-next-year-if-enhanced-premium-tax-credits-expire/) projected average subsidized enrollee payments would more than double if the same plan were kept — about **+114%**. [Observed 2026 figures](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/what-we-know-so-far-about-2026-aca-marketplace-enrollment-premiums-and-deductibles/) show average monthly payments rising from **$113** to **$178** (**+58%**), with many buying down or exiting, and effectuated enrollment falling on the order of about **3 million** from the 2025 peak. That is the latency bomb going off — the addiction dose ending on schedule.

This is not a rocking red-versus-blue theater where each side just reverses the last bill for the cameras. It is an [antibody strategy](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/the-sin-eater.md). A **sin eater** takes the moral cost — the cut, the blame, the ugly months — and buries it so the rest of the cause can stay cleaner. Trump will be the one who looks like he cut people off when the subsidy addiction hits the cliff. He also has a plan for what rises after Obamacare falls to pieces.

![The Sin Eater — moral dirt buried so the cause can stay pure](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/war/the-sin-eater.png)

The fight was never only about which branded service sits on the ID card. It was about the economic problems behind medicine. During Trump’s first term he pushed hard on drug prices, and he is doing the same again now, including most-favored-nation style pressure in the Great Health Care Plan. A few years ago I went to the dentist and she told me I really could not afford the nitrous oxide right then — the price had gone up about ten times, and it was not something I should spend money on. That is how a price spike lands on ordinary care. Equipment and drug prices skyrocketed, and the press blamed a CEO, while households and clinics ate the bill. Clinical access, wait times, and staffing strained in the years after the ACA install and again around the 2025–2026 cliff; denser receipts and open counters live in the [companion write-up](https://paradigmthreat.net/science/health/investigations/obamacare-trumpcare-nsa-cia-alignment-investigation.md). The aim on Track B is getting back toward a place where households hold more of the purse.
