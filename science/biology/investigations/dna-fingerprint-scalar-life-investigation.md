A watershed in imperial Christianity is the Second Council of Constantinople (553 CE; [Fifth Ecumenical Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Council_of_Constantinople)), convened under theological policy associated with Emperor Justinian I. The council condemned Origenist teachers and positions; among the anathematized matter was the doctrine of soul pre-existence (together with other theses grouped under Origenism—scholars dispute fine attribution, but the juridical condemnation is documented). For this investigation the load-bearing point is structural: continuity-of-soul doctrines were ruled impermissible by 553 through conciliar and imperial action, not because new physics or biology had disproved them. Forbidden is not the same as false; conflating the two skews the weight given to modern single-body orthodoxy when judging field-based embryology and conception-as-junction above.
