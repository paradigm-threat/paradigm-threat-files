# Lifelike human robots — centered on JFK

> **TL;DR:** The picture that puts a U.S. lifelike robot in front of reporters and calls him a man is ***Frankenstein Meets the Space Monster*** (22 September 1965), staged at the John F. Kennedy Space Center. The spoken lines stay in [JFK §2.3 item 5](/events/jfk/investigations/jfk-rfk-staged-assassination-hoax-investigation.md#5-frankenstein-meets-the-space-monster-1965). §3 is the known shelf of a machine passed off as a person: *Metropolis* (1927), *The Creation of the Humanoids* (1962), *The Stepford Wives* (1975), *Futureworld* (1976), *Alien* (1979), *Screamers* (1995), *Impostor* (2001), *Ex Machina* (2014), *Terminator Salvation* (2009), and *Companion* (2025), among the others in the table. A labeled robot president is not the thing this file is looking for.

**Status:** Open. One government specimen at the Kennedy center. §3 lists the known shelf so a later reveal is not silently treated as that specimen.

## 1. The gate

A film is on this shelf when a machine is passed off as a human being. The people in the room, the machine itself, or the audience is led to take the body for a person, and the reveal is that the body is a robot, an android, a replicant, or a manufactured duplicate. Pod people, demons, and a mask with a man inside are a different shelf.

The JFK center is narrower. All four are true.

1. A government, military, or standing scientific office is in the room.
2. It puts a lifelike machine in front of people and calls the machine a named human.
3. The machine can be controlled, or it carries orders the room does not hear.
4. The picture is built so a later audience files the hardware as fiction.

*Frankenstein Meets the Space Monster* is the specimen of that narrower gate. The rest of §3 can sit on the wide shelf and still fail the government test. Visible machines — Robby, Gort, C-3PO — are not this list. [*Capricorn One*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capricorn_One) (1977) fakes a Mars mission with a living crew. It is the realistic twin of a faked flight, filed in [JFK §2.3](/events/jfk/investigations/jfk-rfk-staged-assassination-hoax-investigation.md#5-frankenstein-meets-the-space-monster-1965). It is not a robot.

## 2. Exact — *Frankenstein Meets the Space Monster* (1965)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Release** | 22 September 1965, Allied Artists, double bill with *Curse of the Voodoo*. Also sold as *Mars Invades Puerto Rico*. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenstein_Meets_the_Space_Monster) |
| **Stage** | Drive, press conference, and launch at the John F. Kennedy Space Center. The center’s name was already law under [Executive Order 11129](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/codification/executive-order/11129.html) (29 November 1963). Spoken words in the car: “the Space Center.” |
| **Full lines** | [JFK §2.3 item 5](/events/jfk/investigations/jfk-rfk-staged-assassination-hoax-investigation.md#5-frankenstein-meets-the-space-monster-1965) |

Colonel Frank Saunders is introduced as the astronaut for a solo trip to Mars. A reporter who has covered the space program says he has never heard of him. The room is closed with “He really is shy.”

Dr. Steele, with the general in the room:

> We have here, for all practical purposes, a normal human being created out of normal parts transplanted except for his synthetic skin and the electronic sensory control system of course.
>
> But the significant factor here is that we can control him with this simple mechanism.
>
> His brain can record the data that we need and transmit it to us.

The general: “Doctor Steele, I've been against this project from the beginning.” The launch still happens. At liftoff he tells Steele, “Let's hope your creation doesn't fall apart.” After the capsule is shot down: “An astro-robot without a control system.” At the repair, Adam to Karen: “Remember, he's a robot, not a man, Karen.”

The other half of the picture is the breeding raid. That list is the [Mars needs women investigation](/influence/predictive_programming/investigations/mars-needs-women-recent-cataclysm-investigation.md). This file keeps the robot.

## 4. Author’s originating thesis

**Author (2026-11-18),** also filed under [JFK §2.3 item 5](/events/jfk/investigations/jfk-rfk-staged-assassination-hoax-investigation.md#5-frankenstein-meets-the-space-monster-1965):

> I never said the movie would show a robot president. That would give away too much. Nothing in this investigation looks for a smoking gun like that. We look for patterns, and this pattern is too hard to ignore. It lines up with too much, and the message is deliberate. All the things in the movie are supposed to be dismissed as science fiction. The hokiness, done in the Ed Wood style, lends itself to that outcome. So does the topic. Most women watching will feel alienated from a science-fiction genre that treats them like objects to be captured and bred on another planet.

> Anyone can watch this movie and see a general supporting a deception of a fake astronaut human, and might assume that the robot is possible and that it is run by the persistent layer of government — unelected, and lasting through many presidential administrations. There was little reason, beyond the science-fiction genre, for anyone at the time to debunk or disbelieve this kind of technology. It could have been presented in a real fashion by a real authority: a reanimated corpse, using an electric-impulse system, remotely controlled. Instead it was presented in a hokey movie, and for that reason we are told that such technology is not possible, or just made up, imaginary. In the 1950s, people did not automatically think that any of this technology was not real. This is very similar to *Capricorn One* (1977), which showed the faking of a Mars mission in a very realistic sense, but is meant to be taken as fiction only.

**What that means here:**

The 1965 press conference is the specimen because a standing office puts a controllable body in front of reporters and the reporters are told the body is a colonel. A picture of a robot sitting in the Oval Office would name the method. The shelf in §3 is the same method said again: a wife, a science officer, a boy, a soldier, a weapons designer, a servant, a resistance fighter, a girlfriend. *Futureworld* is the one that puts the copies in the chairs of world leaders. The cheap bill on the 1965 picture, and the breeding plot beside the robot, are what get that hardware laughed out of the room. The breeding plot itself is the other file.

## 5. Author’s open claims

1. The recent pattern is a lifelike substitute passed off as a person, run by a standing unelected layer. The 1965 film shows the general in the room at the Kennedy center.
2. A robot president on screen would give the method away. This file does not score the shelf by that missing exhibit.
3. As of this pass, the only obvious theatrical film that meets the four-part government gate is *Frankenstein Meets the Space Monster*.
4. §3 is the known shelf of the wider deception. *Futureworld* and *Impostor* are the later pictures that aim the substitute at leaders and at a weapons office.
5. The hokey double bill is how the 1965 hardware is later called imaginary.
