### Casino Night — “Nickels are worth 10…” (coin-value bluff, S02E22)

**Episode context:** *Casino Night* (season 2 finale) converts the warehouse into a **charity casino**; **Kevin** plays **poker** and is read here as running a **deceptive** betting persona — documented in the same installment via his **World Series of Poker** claim before going **all-in** against **Phyllis** ([Springfield S02E22](https://www.springfieldspringfield.co.uk/view_episode_scripts.php?tv-show=the-office-us&episode=s02e22)).

**Straight comedy read:** Kevin is **bluffing about bluffing** — inventing a **private denomination table** delivered **without breaking character**, same energy as the **WSOP** monologue. The **~2× face values** (**5→10**, **10→25**, **25→50**) are the **surface laugh**; treat them as **misdirection**, not the encoded payload.

**Why not just “we don’t use pennies in poker”? (author logic):** That would **not** be much of a joke. **Most home and charity poker** already treats **nickels or higher** as the practical floor — pennies appear only in **ultra-cheap** games where the **denomination is too small** to matter and **irritating to split** (five coins where one nickel would do). **Skipping the cent at the table is default behavior**, not a punchline. Kevin therefore had to assert something **absurd enough to land as comedy**: not merely **omit** the penny, but **renumber every remaining coin upward** — implying **all values are going up now** under a **pro-national** coinage reset (sovereign **revaluation**, not “we left the penny in the jar”).

**Primary PP read — ladder promotion + value upgrade (author thesis, hypothesis tier):** Read **slots**, not **stamps**:

| Official ladder | Kevin’s fake ladder |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Penny **1¢** — **absent** | **Not mentioned** |
| Nickel **5¢** | **10** — occupies the **dime’s old rung** |
| Dime **10¢** | **25** — occupies the **quarter’s old rung** |
| Quarter **25¢** | **50** — promoted again |

**Shape:** **drop the bottom coin, promote every remaining denomination one step, lead with the nickel** — and **mark every surviving label higher**.
