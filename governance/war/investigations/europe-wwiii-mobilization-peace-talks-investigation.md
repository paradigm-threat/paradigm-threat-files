## 2. What mobilization means in Europe in 2026

It is not a continental call-up. It is the legal machinery that makes a later call-up a vote instead of a new constitution.

### Germany — registry first, conscription by a second law

The **Wehrdienst-Modernisierungsgesetz** entered into force on **1 January 2026**. The German government and a Bundestag written answer describe the pieces:

- Men must complete a conscription questionnaire. Women are not obliged.
- Medical examination (*Musterung*) is back. A man who does not appear can be brought in by police or examined on the file. The parliamentary answer says compulsory examination of the post-2007 male birth cohorts is the design, with full cohort processing as capacity allows.
- Actual service is still voluntary. Pay for volunteers has been reported around €2,600 a month.
- Article 12a of the Basic Law still authorizes conscription of men. The 2011 peacetime suspension remains. It can be lifted by a **new statute** (*Bedarfswehrpflicht*) if the security situation requires it and volunteers are not enough. The government’s draft would have allowed activation by ordinance with Bundestag consent. The committee changed that to a statute. The executive cannot flip the switch alone.
- Since the start of 2026 the statute, § 3(2) WPflG, says men after their 17th birthday need approval for a stay abroad longer than three months, up to the year they turn 45. On **9 April 2026** the Defence Ministry issued a general exemption (BAnz AT 16.04.2026 B3, in force **17 April**). No application is required. The [Foreign Office](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/2755596-2755596) says so. Euronews on **27 Apr 2026** still described the statutory duty. The waiver is the November fact.

Sources: [Federal Foreign Office — military service](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/2755596-2755596); [Bundestag Drucksache 21/7894](https://kleineanfragen.info/drucksache/21/7894/); [Euronews, 27 Apr 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/04/27/defence-which-european-countries-have-mandatory-and-voluntary-military-service).

That is not Ukrainian busification. It is the list and the exam, with the foreign-travel rule written down and currently waived, and the street phase left to a later vote. The author’s claim is that the later vote is the easy part once the list exists and the public is already against the war. The statute makes the vote sufficient. It does not make the vote inevitable.

### France — “we need to mobilise,” still a volunteer intake

The BBC reported Macron’s announcement of a paid 10-month national service, mainly for 18- and 19-year-olds, at least €800 a month. Initial intake **3,000**, rising to **50,000 by 2035**. France’s professional force was given as about 200,000, plus about 47,000 reservists. Macron, at an infantry base near Grenoble:

> The only way to avoid danger is to prepare for it.

> We need to mobilise, mobilising the nation to defend itself, to be ready and remain respected.

[BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c0edw7g7z79o). Conscription itself, abolished about 25 years earlier, was not restored in that announcement. The verb he used was mobilise. The headcount he funded was a few thousand volunteers.

### States that already compel service

Euronews (27 Apr 2026) counted at least ten EU states with conscription in place, among them Austria, Croatia, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Greece, Latvia, Lithuania, and Sweden. Denmark and Sweden include women. Croatia’s term is the short one: two months.

Reuters, **6 Mar 2026**: Croatia resumed mandatory service after 17 years. Parliament had amended the defence law the previous October. The piece said the first 800 recruits were starting two-month training that Monday. The government planned about **4,000 conscripts a year**. The public reasons given were European security and the Western Balkans, in a climate of tension with Russia. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/croatia-re-launch-mandatory-military-service-after-17-years-2026-03-06/).

The BBC’s NATO survey (professional armies versus conscript armies) still described the United Kingdom, Spain, Italy, and several smaller members as professional forces, and said the UK had no plan to restore conscription. Britain recruits at 16 with parental consent. That page and the March 2026 Croatia start should be read together: the restart is real, and it is not universal.

### NATO’s job description

The 10 Nov POLITICO story is the alliance-level sentence. Camille Grand, former NATO assistant secretary-general for defence investment, described the draft as “a very clear understanding that no matter what, for NATO, a major contingency in Europe will overtake any other priorities.” Oana Lungescu, former NATO spokesperson, said the alliance had come full circle: deter Russia, especially as the United States reduces its commitment. Washington’s name for the refocus, in that piece, is **NATO 3.0**. A Pentagon review of the U.S. troop presence in Europe was expected the following month and could withdraw some forces.

**Where Europe can stop a deal without announcing an attack.** The five capitals that want a seat are the same capitals offering troops after a ceasefire. Kushner has described NATO as the provider of Ukraine’s security guarantees. Moscow’s stated reason for keeping Europe out is that Europe finances and arms Ukraine. A settlement that requires European soldiers on Ukrainian ground is a settlement Russia has already said it will not sign with those soldiers’ governments in the room. A settlement that gives up the disputed territories without those soldiers is the settlement European leaders have spent four years calling a prelude. Either rule lets Europe hold the veto while the press still says Europe is “stepping up peace talks.”

That is the documented choke point. The claim that someone **ordered** it, as a command to attack so the deal dies, is [§7](#7-authors-originating-thesis).

## 4. The narrative on offer

The public story is not “Russia wants Paris.” It is a stack of smaller claims. Each one is checkable. Together they are the reason a government can mobilize during a peace round and still say it is preventing a war.

### 4.1 A deal is a pause, and the pause is the trap

Both sides already say this about the other.

- European line, in office since the Minsk agreements: a ceasefire lets Russia rearm and come again. Merz on 3 Nov said he did not see serious ceasefire talks yet. McEntee on 16 Nov said Russia’s position had not changed, and paired “renewed talks” with 113 sanctions and more military support.
- Russian line, Peskov, 17 Nov: a temporary ceasefire would let Ukraine recover from the damage. Moscow says it wants a peace that lasts centuries, and that the talks will take time.

The author’s question — are they afraid the peace deal is only a pretense for a Russian invasion? — is the European line, said in public. It does not require a secret memo. It is the reason given for keeping the weapons flowing while the envoys fly.

### 4.3 What “beyond the disputed territories” actually consists of

The territories in dispute are Crimea and the Donbas land Russia holds and demands, plus whatever else the maps freeze. The European story past that line has four steps. None of them is “the Russian army is on the Rhine.”

1. **Precedent.** If borders can be moved by force and then ratified, the next demand is cheaper. The Baltics and the Suwałki corridor are the places named, because they are small, exposed, and full of Russian-speaking populations the Kremlin already claims to care about.
2. **Article 5 as a bluff.** Tusk’s version: a limited strike whose purpose is to show that the treaty will not be honored. The prize is the alliance, not the village.
3. **Rearmament after a bad peace.** If the war ends with Russia in possession of the east and with its army intact, the 2029 stockpile story becomes the reason not to sign. The peace is treated as the start of the countdown.
4. **Security architecture.** Jamestown’s account of the Moscow position: a Ukraine settlement is tied to limits on NATO. Europe hears that as a demand to disarm the continent, not as a border deal. That hearing is what turns a territorial concession into an existential one.

**Author’s answer to that stack**, kept here so it is not flattened into the press account, is in [§7](#7-authors-originating-thesis). The short form: the invasions that reached Russia in 1812 and 1941 were attacks **on** Russia. A Russia that has just taken or been ceded the disputed Ukrainian territories has the same historical reason not to open a war into NATO that it had after surviving Napoleon and Hitler. The 2022 invasion is the case the press uses to break that pattern. This repo already files 2022 as the inverted crusade — a Western-built figurehead, Russia cast as the aggressor — in [reverse-crusades](/governance/war/investigations/reverse-crusades-comparison.md). On that filing, 2022 does not prove a program to march past the territories now under negotiation.

What the narrative **cannot** show, on the sources in this file, is a Russian plan, dated and ordered, to invade Germany, France, or Britain if Ukraine cedes the east. What it **can** show is a professional military class that has been told to be able to fight tonight, a planning cycle aimed at 2029, and a political class that treats any pause as the enemy’s trick.
