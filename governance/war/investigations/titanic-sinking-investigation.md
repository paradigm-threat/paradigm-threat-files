## 2. Why Titanic is a black box

Most famous disasters leave a usable witness stack: survivors, photographs, competing newspapers, later memoirs that do not all rhyme. Titanic’s **load-bearing** facts sit on **water at night**. The people on the **wrong side of the box** — whoever decided speed, identity of the hull, which rockets meant what, which nearby ship would not come — are exactly the people who either **died** or **testified under company and state counsel**.

That is the definition of a **black-box conspiracy**: the public is given a **closed story** (iceberg, hubris, “unsinkable”) because the only people who could open the box are **dead or paid**. The US Senate and UK Board of Trade hearings are **not** a second set of eyes on the hull in Belfast. They are **aftermath theater** with surviving officers, wireless men, and Ismay.

**Author read:** Treat the official story as the **lid**. Books that argue switch, insurance, Jesuit/Fed kill lists, or mystery ships are attempts to **pry the lid**. They do not have to agree with each other. They have to show that **someone** has been trying to name the operators for thirty years in print.
