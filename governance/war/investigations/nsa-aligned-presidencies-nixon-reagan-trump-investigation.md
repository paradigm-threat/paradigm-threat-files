**Legal lineage (Tier A — do not collapse into one slogan):** Federal employee strikes were already unlawful before 1981. **Taft-Hartley (1947) §305** originally made federal strikes unlawful with discharge; that section was **repealed 1955** and replaced by what became **[5 U.S.C. §7311](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/7311)** (loyalty / striking) + criminal companion **18 U.S.C. §1918** ([DOJ OLC PATCO memo lineage](https://www.justice.gov/olc/media/1253441/dl?inline=); *United Federation of Postal Clerks v. Blount*, 325 F. Supp. 879 (D.D.C. 1971)). Popular shorthand “illegal under Taft-Hartley” names the **1947 root**; the **living** bar is **§7311**.

### 5.1.1 Author refinement — pro-federal deterrent, not “pro-American toughness”

**Session capture (author, 2026-07-26):**

> PATCO can be seen as pro-American, but I think it's more **pro-federal**, with lingering **good/bad effects** in the greater economy. It was a way to effectively **eliminate formal federal worker strikes**, acting as a massive **psychological and legal deterrent**. Prior to 1981, federal workers frequently used localized **“sick-outs,” slowdowns, and brief strikes** (such as the massive **1970 postal worker strike**) to force the government's hand. While technically illegal under the **1947 Taft-Hartley** lineage, a **“gentleman's agreement”** usually prevented mass terminations. The PATCO firings **obliterated that unwritten rule**. Since August 1981, there has not been a single major, **formally authorized** strike by federal government employees. The fear of **immediate termination**, a **lifetime federal employment ban**, and the **total decertification** of the union became a permanent roadblock for **public-sector labor militancy**.

**What the author means**

| Layer | Unpack |
|-------|--------|
| **Pro-federal ≠ pro-American** | The fist protected **federal operational monopoly** (continuous ATC / continuity of government services), not “the American worker” as such — PATCO members *were* Americans who had endorsed Reagan. |
| **Deterrent, not one-off** | The lasting product is **chilled federal militancy**: termination + lifetime ban + union death as a **permanent roadmap** others refuse to walk. |
| **Pre-1981 practice** | Law on books + **soft enforcement** (sick-outs, slowdowns, short strikes) let workers still **force the hand** without PATCO-scale annihilation. |
| **1970 postal strike** | Largest walkout vs U.S. government (~**210,000** in **33** cities); Nixon threatened / troops to move mail; outcome was **Postal Reorganization Act** bargaining rights — **not** Reagan-style mass permanent purge ([APWU history](https://historycenter.apwu.org/the-great-postal-strike/)). |
| **Gentleman’s agreement** | Unwritten norm: illegal strike ≠ mass firing. Labor histories treat **1970** as evidence the norm still held; **1981** as the break ([UTA Libraries — PATCO](https://libraries.uta.edu/news/1981-patco-strike)). |
| **Economy — mixed residue** | **Good** (continuity of ATC / federal ops; private-sector bargaining power shift toward employers — mainstream labor-econ claim). **Bad** (destroyed a skilled cadre overnight; years of training lag; permanent chill on federal worker leverage; private-sector strike rates also collapsed in the following decades — [NPR timeline](https://www.npr.org/2006/08/03/5599271/timeline-americas-air-traffic-controllers-strike)). |
