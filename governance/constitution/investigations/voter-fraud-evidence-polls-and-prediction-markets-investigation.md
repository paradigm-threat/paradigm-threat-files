## 2. Where the assumption meets the record

The public record runs on two lines at once.

**Specimen admission is real.** A court, a jury, or an election board has said, in a named case, that ballots were mishandled, an election was tainted, or a person was guilty. Bridgeport, North Carolina’s 9th District, Tower Hamlets, Malawi, and Kenya are in the tables below. Those are not rumors.

**Systemic denial is also real.** National spokespeople and many researchers still say fraud is rare and did not change a U.S. presidential result. See the volume fight in [§8](#8-volume-fight). Winning boards in contested national elections, Venezuela 2024 among them, deny the fraud allegation while withholding the precinct detail a reader would use to check.

**A miss and a made-up poll are different admissions.** YouGov has published its own reviews and called the misses sampling, turnout, and late swing ([§10.2](#102-yougov--documented-error-reviews)). That is an admission of a bad sample and a bad weight. It is not a guilty plea. Wedgewood Polls and Median Strategies did the other thing. Each firm said the interviews behind the published horse race had not happened ([§10.5](#105-pollsters-set-the-expected-matchup)). Those are the operators’ own words. They are not criminal convictions, and they are not a voided ballot count.

**Betting platforms have denied manipulation in the incident people actually fought about.** In October 2024 Polymarket said its review of the large Trump trader found no manipulation. Outside chain researchers, reported by Fortune, described wash trading and a volume mismatch on the same market. Denial and allegation were both public. Settlement of a bet pays the winning side. Payment is not a confession.

**Audit surfaces exist, and they are unequal.** A secret ballot blocks tying a marked ballot to a person. That is the design. Colorado’s risk-limiting audit checks a paper sample against the tabulated outcome ([C.R.S. § 1-7-515](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-1/general-primary-recall-and-congressional-vacancy-elections/article-7/part-5/section-1-7-515/); first statewide run, November 2017 coordinated election, [Colorado Secretary of State, 22 November 2017](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/newsRoom/pressReleases/2017/PR20171122ElectionAudit.html)). That audit speaks to whether the count matches the paper. It does not identify an ineligible voter, and it does not open vendor logs. YouGov publishes method notes. The panel’s respondent-level file stays private. Polymarket trades sit on a public chain, which is how outside firms inspected them. The trader’s legal identity and the funding path are not a public book. Kalshi is a regulated exchange. These are different objects. “Nobody can audit them” is too broad for all of them and still true for the closed parts.

**Faith, not fact, is the live claim where the books stay closed.** A voided election and a guilty plea are facts. A presidential result with sealed vendor logs, a proprietary poll weight, or an anonymous whale is where the author says the public is being asked for faith. That sentence is **Author**. The 2020 dossier already holds the midterm legitimacy version of it ([§9 there](/governance/constitution/investigations/2020-election-fraud-selective-battleground-audit-timing-investigation.md#9-all-or-nothing-legitimacy--author-sentiment)). This file points at that section. It does not repeat the essay.

**YouGov is not a community vote and not a bookmaker.** It is a commercial online panel. [§10](#10-polls-and-betting-markets--opened-not-closed) separates the classes before the compromise theory is laid on all of them.

## 3. What counts as evidence in this file

A row in the adjudicated tables needs one of these:

- a court order voiding the result, or declaring it invalid
- a guilty plea or a conviction
- an official election-board order refusing certification or ordering a new election

An allegation, a viral tally, or a partisan press release stays out of those tables. Contested national fights go in [§7](#7-international--contested-opacity) or back to the 2020 dossier.

Heritage’s own bar is wider than this file’s. Heritage counts a case when it ended in a finding of wrongdoing meant to affect an outcome, **or** when results were sufficiently in question to be overturned, and Heritage says the list is a sampling, not a complete census ([Election Fraud Map](https://electionfraud.heritage.org/), note dated **12 December 2025**). This file uses Heritage as an index. A row below is here because a primary or contemporary report was checked, not because Heritage listed it.

## 7. International — contested opacity

**Venezuela, presidential election 28 July 2024.** This row is not a neutral-court conviction.

The UN Panel of Experts, invited by the National Electoral Council (CNE), reported that the electronic system had been trusted by the contenders and that printed polling-station protocols, with QR codes, hashes, and signatures, were the transparency safeguard. Transmission “abruptly stopped” after the polls closed. The CNE president announced a terrorist cyber-attack and then postponed and cancelled post-election audits, including the audit of the communication system. In the early hours of **29 July** the CNE orally announced Nicolás Maduro with **5,150,092** votes (**51.2%**) and Edmundo González with **4,445,978** (**44.2%**), said to be **80%** of polling stations. On **2 August** it announced Maduro **6,408,844** (**51.95%**) and González **5,326,104** (**43.18%**), which the panel says the CNE described as **96.97%** of polling-station results. The announcements were oral. The CNE did not publish polling-station results. The panel reviewed a small sample of protocols the opposition had posted and found they carried the security features of the originals. The panel did not itself retotal the vote. Its conclusion:

> In the experience of the Panel, the announcement of an election outcome without the publication of its details or the release of tabulated results to candidates has no precedent in contemporary democratic elections.

([UN Panel interim report, 9 August 2024](https://news.un.org/en/sites/news.un.org.en/files/atoms/files/Interim_Report_PoE_Venezuela_090824.pdf), ¶¶8–13.)

On **23 August 2024** Venezuela’s Supreme Tribunal of Justice validated the CNE result. The Organization of American States rejected that ruling the same day and repeated the UN panel’s “no precedent” sentence ([OAS E-056/24](https://www.oas.org/en/media_center/press_release.asp?sCodigo=E-056/24)). ## 7.1 Author — UN speech influences the people on the ground

The author’s words are in [§1](#1-authors-originating-thesis). The power is not a cancel button. The power is influence. If the UN has “said so,” the people on that side have international backing, and an advantage the other side does not have.

**What the UN’s own policy says.** The policy directive approved by Rosemary A. DiCarlo, Focal Point for Electoral Assistance, effective **3 March 2021** and reissued **1 April 2022**, states two gates before electoral assistance: a Security Council or General Assembly mandate, or a formal request from a member state or territory, and a needs assessment by the Focal Point. Requests cannot come from a faction in the legislature or from civil society. General Assembly resolution **A/RES/74/158**, as cited in that directive, reaffirms that member states organize their own elections and may request help in the exercise of sovereignty ([DPPA policy PDF](https://dppa.un.org/sites/default/files/ead_pd_un_ea_principles_types_20210303_e.pdf), ¶¶9, 75).

|-----|----------------------|
| **Observation** | A mission reports to the Secretary-General on credibility. Missions “generally” end in a public statement on the quality of the election. “In some cases they can provide an internal report.” Observation is “extremely rare.” The policy names Burundi **2015** and Fiji **2001**. A mission is not sent without a government request and a General Assembly or Security Council resolution, “because of the possible political impact of a UN statement on the credibility of an election” (¶¶34–37). |

| **Certification** | The UN attests to credibility and “concludes with a pronouncement on the legitimacy of the electoral process.” The policy calls this sensitive, rare, and available only by a Security Council or General Assembly mandate, “usually only when it is deemed absolutely necessary,” and only where the government has agreed “to cede this sovereign activity to the control of the UN” (¶¶65–68). |
| **Ordinary public comment** | Statements “should be used strategically.” “Statements, if any, should not give a qualitative judgement on the validity of an electoral process or of the outcome” unless the UN is already in one of the mandated roles above (¶59). |

**The influence is what the policy already admits.** A credibility statement is withheld or sent because of “the possible political impact of a UN statement on the credibility of an election” (¶37). Ordinary comments are “diplomatic tools” and “should be used strategically” (¶59). An expert panel normally stays quiet, and the Focal Point decides what, if anything, is made public (¶39). That is a choice about who receives a sentence they can wave. It is not a choice about striking the ballots.

## 8.1 A negligible rate is not an honest audit

The author’s words are in [§1](#1-authors-originating-thesis). Two different objects get called an “anti-fraud measure.”

**A negligible admission.** Someone in charge publishes a tiny rate and the public is asked to take the system as clean enough. The Brennan Center’s noncitizen survey is one specimen: about **30** suspected incidents in **23.5 million** votes, written as **0.0001%**, and then used to say fraud is rare ([§8](#8-volume-fight)). The author’s example figure is **0.1%**. The job of the sentence is the same at either size. It tells the audience there is no significant fraud. The author calls that a public-relations polish. It names a crumb and asks the hearer to retire the concern. A company, an election office, or a forum can be wrong or lying about any one control, and the public still cannot open the file those controls sit in.

**An honest audit, by the author’s bar.** A government vote and a community-run vote are under the same rule. People other than the operator open the private files when the community decides it is necessary. The opening is secure and private. They see enough to know. When they walk away, the finding sits on their reputation. A press release that says “0.1%” does not do that. A court that voids one named election does not do that for the next election. Colorado’s risk-limiting audit samples paper ballots against the count ([§2](#2-where-the-assumption-meets-the-record)). It does not give the public a privilege to open police files, vendor logs, or a forum’s database on the public’s own calendar.

**Author read of the tiny rate (AU-075).** If the official line is that fraud is extremely negligible, treat that as a sign that serious fraud may be there. Some places may be clean. Mass mail, in the author’s view, makes a guarantee impossible. The larger claim is a species-wide pattern: voter fraud never mitigated, always open to strong influence. That pattern is **Author**. The catalog in [§4](#4-united-states-2022-2026--cases-checked-for-this-pass)–[§6](#6-international--court-or-board-acts) shows named voids and pleas. It does not, by itself, measure a species-wide rate.

**The lottery is the same disconnect, with one convicted rig on the record.** Eddie Tipton wrote the random-number program for Hot Lotto while he was the Multi-State Lottery Association’s security director. On **20 November 2010** he was in the draw room for a clock change. The security camera that day recorded about one second out of every minute. He later pleaded guilty to rigging computerized drawings in Iowa, Oklahoma, Wisconsin, Colorado, and Kansas, and was sentenced to up to **25** years ([Iowa Supreme Court opinion, via CourtListener](https://storage.courtlistener.com/pdf/2017/06/23/state_of_iowa_v._eddie_tipton.pdf); [Des Moines Register, 29 June 2017](https://www.desmoinesregister.com/story/news/crime-and-courts/2017/06/29/tipton-pleads-guilty-iowa-lottery-rigging-scandal/438039001/); [AP](https://apnews.com/general-news-1fc2ad3d824541e98d604464d23b0191)). The numbers the public received were not proof the generator was clean. The author extends the gap to televised ball draws, including Powerball: the number on the video can be a controlled number, and the viewer still has no sight of the original roll. “Every drawing was fake” is **Author**. Tipton is **Documented** proof that a published drawing number and a clean draw are not the same fact.

**The agreement the author wants.** The community has a right to audit whenever it deems necessary. That includes opening private files from police stations, governments, and community-run forums, in a secure and private way, knowing for sure, and putting the result on the auditor’s reputation. The author sees no other solution, and does not believe this privilege has been implemented anywhere. Programs checked for this pass — a candidate’s lawsuit, a state paper-ballot sample, a prosecuted lottery programmer — are narrower than that privilege. A jurisdiction that already grants it is [Q10](#12-questions-to-clarify-verify-or-debunk).

**The same matchup, without an admission.** In the days before **5 November 2024**, Nate Silver counted October swing-state polls in the Silver Bulletin database.
