# Abolish the justice system
*Do not vote for judges, and do not take the dispute to court*

![Blind justice stops the Capitol, the White House, and a border wall, with Parliament behind the bench](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/constitution/abolish-the-justice-system.jpg)

> **TL;DR:** The justice system is not a partner to Congress or the president. It works against them. A judge can stop a policy by saying no. Do not vote for judges. Human judges come out. Committees build the file. An algorithm matches it to precedent. People vote under the Constitution as it stands. A public model makes the last call only when both sides, stuck in mediation, ask it to. Emptying the prisons overnight is the chaos this design is meant to avoid.

The courts work against the legislature and the executive. They are polar opposites in ideology. Neither needs the other to run a whole government. The split has a reputation as wisdom. The reputation comes from about three hundred years of illegitimate governments that ran on bureaucracy and did not get audited. People did not ask why this government was founded on Roman icons. People did not ask why the justice system was kept after a war of independence with England.

The war of independence was a lie in how it was carried out. England had already put the policies and control systems it wanted into the colonies. The Civil War consolidated them. England funded both sides. England was a puppet for Protestantism to reach America. Protestantism is obedience to a church under the guise of protesting. Most people cannot protest their local church and stay. The choice is quitting or not quitting.

In recent years the courts went after the president and his policies. The joke was that the judiciary can appoint itself president or king whenever it wants, and act as a counter-executive to his orders. That joke is the point. A judge gets in the way of good leadership and good government policy by saying no. Then the policy has to be rebuilt some other way, or it dies. That covers the border wall. It covers handling aliens and immigrants at home. It covers sending money overseas. It covers repairing the White House, even when the work is called a security measure. The judge can say he does not agree that it is security, and he can stop the work.

## What a judge stopped

On September 3, 2025, Judge Amir Ali in Washington ordered agencies to obligate expiring foreign-aid money. The Associated Press reported the figure as $11.5 billion. On September 26, 2025, the Supreme Court stayed that order for the funds the president had asked Congress to rescind, in [*Department of State v. AIDS Vaccine Advocacy Coalition*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/25a269_bp7c.pdf). The Court’s order describes the injunction as covering roughly $10.5 billion. A district judge ordered the money sent. The Supreme Court partly stopped that order.

In October 2025 the government began replacing the East Wing of the White House. Demolition of the old wing finished in December 2025. The new work was a below-ground military installation and an above-ground ballroom. On March 31, 2026, Judge Richard Leon enjoined the ballroom until Congress authorized it. On April 16, 2026, he allowed the below-ground bunkers and the work strictly necessary to cover them, and he refused the claim that the ballroom was inseparable from security. He wrote that national security is not a blank check. The D.C. Circuit affirmed on August 7, 2026. On August 31, 2026, the Supreme Court stayed his injunction in [*National Park Service v. National Trust for Historic Preservation*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/26a203_2b8e.pdf), on the view that the Trust likely lacked standing. The stay says the Court was not deciding whether the project is legal.

On October 2, 2026, a federal court in Texas temporarily blocked border-wall construction in the Big Bend region, as the [*Los Angeles Times* reported](https://www.latimes.com/world-nation/story/2026-10-02/federal-court-temporarily-blocks-trump-border-wall-in-big-bend-region). In 2019 Judge Haywood Gilliam Jr. in California had enjoined the use of reprogrammed Defense funds for named barrier sectors. The Supreme Court lifted that block on July 26, 2019. One judge stops a wall. Another judge refuses to stop a different stretch. A higher court may undo it months later. While those orders fight, the wall does not get built.

On June 27, 2025, in [*Trump v. CASA*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24a884_8n59.pdf), the Supreme Court limited universal injunctions against the birthright-citizenship order. It measured that remedy against English equity in 1789. It did not decide the Fourteenth Amendment question.

Older cases used the same power. In 1832 *Worcester v. Georgia* held that Georgia could not apply its laws to the Cherokee. Georgia did not comply. Andrew Jackson wrote that the decision fell stillborn. The sentence people quote, that Marshall had made his decision and Jackson would let him enforce it, is not in Jackson’s letters. The Federal Judicial Center traces it to Horace Greeley in 1864. In 1861 Chief Justice Taney held that Lincoln’s suspension of habeas corpus belonged to Congress. Lincoln did not free John Merryman on that opinion. In 1952 the Court stopped Truman’s seizure of the steel mills, 6–3, in *Youngstown*. In 1974 the Court ordered Nixon to turn over the tapes. He complied and resigned. In 2020 the Court blocked the rescission of DACA, 5–4.

The receipts for that list, and for the cases below, are in the [justice-system investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/constitution/investigations/justice-system-judges-branches-as-opposites-investigation.md).

## England kept the courts

American court power is still scored against England. Blackstone’s commentaries were the law book the founding generation owned. The Judiciary Act of 1789 built the federal courts in that inheritance. *Trump v. CASA* said a federal injunction is lawful only if it matches what English equity gave in 1789.

The public face is Roman. There is a Senate. The Capitol is neoclassical. The House keeps the fasces, the Roman bundle of rods. The blindfolded figure is Justitia. The procedure under that face is English common law. The continent kept a different set of books, the Roman civil-law codes.

Thomas Jefferson wrote to William C. Jarvis on September 28, 1820, that treating judges as the ultimate arbiters of all constitutional questions was a very dangerous doctrine, and one which would place us under the despotism of an oligarchy. Andrew Jackson’s bank veto of July 10, 1832, said the opinion of the judges has no more authority over Congress than the opinion of Congress has over the judges, and that on that point the president is independent of both. Abraham Lincoln’s first inaugural, March 4, 1861, said that if vital policy were irrevocably fixed by Supreme Court decisions in ordinary lawsuits, the people would have ceased to be their own rulers.

Britain ran for a long time without an American-style court that strikes statutes. Jeremy Waldron’s [case against judicial review](https://doi.org/10.2307/20455656) (2006) uses that fact. Mark Tushnet’s *Taking the Constitution Away from the Courts* (1999), and his [2018 interview](https://www.vox.com/2018/10/12/17950896/supreme-court-amy-coney-barrett-mark-tushnet), argue that a court should not be able to overrule a policy the people voted, and that waiting on the Court left citizens as bystanders. They stop at the veto on statutes. The system is anti-republican. It is a European system that was corrupted through its history. It came to America tainted, without an audit and without scrutiny. It was embedded by a state of war, not by a state of unity.

## Stacked votes

Federal judges are not on the ballot. The president nominates them. The Senate confirms them. State judges are a different government. A 2024 [Connecticut legislative summary](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2024/rpt/pdf/2024-R-0140.pdf) of the Brennan Center’s map counts 19 states using nonpartisan elections and 9 using partisan elections for a first full term on a trial court. For a first full term on a state supreme court, 26 states use the governor, 14 use a nonpartisan election, and 7 use a partisan election. Sanford Gordon’s 2024 preprint says about 87 percent of the roughly 12,000 state appellate and general-jurisdiction trial judges must face voters at some point.

Voting them in did not democratize the office. Appointing them the way presidents do did not democratize it either. Both moves were a bandage on power that was already too large. The courts get stacked. Then they vote. Conservative justices lose to liberal justices, usually by one or two votes, with high-yield consequences for the whole planet and for the whole species, every time. Democrats try to put more liberals on the Supreme Court so those votes go their way. The same stacking happens in the other courts.

The one- and two-vote margin is on the record in both directions. *Heller* (2008), *Citizens United* (2010), and *Shelby County* (2013) were 5–4. *Obergefell* (2015) and the DACA rescission case (2020) were 5–4 the other way. *Dobbs* (2022) was 6–3. After 2020 the close cases were often 6–3. A couple of votes still decide the outcome. In April 2021 members of Congress filed a bill to expand the Supreme Court from 9 to 13. Franklin Roosevelt had asked for extra justices in 1937, after the Court struck New Deal statutes. The Senate killed that bill. The Court’s results changed anyway.

Down-ballot, people do not know the judges. The instinct is red or blue. That color is often missing from the judge’s line, and it is not supposed to be the description of the office. Knowing which judge is which means knowing a history, or trusting a pundit, or trusting a news article. A tax question or a named representative can be read in the booth. A judicial line cannot. Fatigue sets in. People vote at random, or by gender, or by last name.

The justice system is partisan. The official line says it is not. Georgia prints these races as nonpartisan. Vacancies on the appellate courts and the superior courts are filled by the governor. Heading into the May 21, 2024 election, [Ballotpedia’s composition table](https://ballotpedia.org/Georgia_Supreme_Court_elections,_2024) showed eight of the nine Supreme Court justices had first reached that court by appointment from a Republican governor, Nathan Deal or Brian Kemp. John Ellington was the one who had been elected, in 2018. The contested seat that year was Kemp’s appointee Andrew Pinson against John Barrow, a former Democratic member of Congress. Three incumbents ran with no opponent. On December 13, 2024, Kemp’s [executive order](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2024-executive-order/12132401/download) creating the Judicial Nominating Commission called the commission non-partisan and non-political. The governor appoints the commission. He is not bound to its list.

Voters who know that are voting the party. Voters who do not know it are voting a familiar name, or they are leaving the line blank. The ballot does not provide a neutral score for the best judge. Almost nobody is voting one. This system has always been about a political agenda. Actual justice has been almost entirely neglected.

My family has always voted for any name that sounds Jewish, even remotely Jewish, and they tell each other to do that. That habit is a family rule. It is not a published national pattern. The ballot makes the habit possible, and the flaw is commonly exploited. It does not matter which religion or racial group does it. Immigrants do this a lot. The justice system became another way for immigrants to impose themselves in America and change the laws without the people who already lived here having a say.

Philip Dubois showed in 1984 that low-information California trial-court voters used the ethnic and sex signals in surnames. Albert Klumpp’s Cook County work, as reported in the law reviews, found an Irish-sounding name worth about 10.5 points in judicial races. Rebecca Wiseman’s California retention study found Hispanic surnames associated with about 1.58 points less of the vote. No published count of Jewish-sounding surnames on judicial ballots sits with those studies. The family rule stands as witness. The published cue studies are the mechanism any bloc can use.

## The room and the screen

Every time I have been to court, the business was not on the level. It is a bureaucracy. Things are forced to a known conclusion regardless of the facts. Court has not worked out for everyone. For minorities it may have backfired significantly.

In Savannah I watched a remote display so people in other states who own property there could Zoom in and take their portion of the calendar. Other people had to make the actual date. If they were not in the room in time, the case was dismissed. The Zoom call is easier to deal with. Who has to show up is the difference.

Lawyers told the judge to hold defendants in check. A defendant could not look at notes unless he had brought the notes himself. He could not take a note from the audience. If someone in the audience tried to tell him something, the lawyer told the judge to tell that person to shut up. The person had to shut up or be disciplined by the court. If two people tried to talk because one needed a consultant, the lawyer had the judge split them up and send the other person to the back of the room. The defendant had no lawyer. He went forward alone. On a Zoom call there is no way to know whether someone else is in the room, or whether a computer is showing text. That advantage is forbidden in the courtroom. It is not forbidden on the call, and it is not auditable. The court can be corrupt without the judge being corrupt.

*Where the Buffalo Roam* came out in 1980. Bill Murray plays Hunter Thompson. Peter Boyle plays the lawyer. A [*Harvard Crimson* review](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1980/5/14/fear-and-loathing-pbwbeirdness-is-dr/) from May 14, 1980, describes the judge giving a teenager five years for a pound of marijuana. The film is a dramatization. The scene is an accurate picture of a court that is overburdened and under-performing on ethics, jailing children for dubious reasons, for political reasons, for headlines. That did not stop.

H.G. Wells is the clean example of a corruption that cannot be appealed. Florence Deeks sued him and Macmillan over *The Outline of History*. She lost at every level, including the Privy Council in 1932. The win closed the file. The winner’s history became the textbook. The cultural upheaval and the chaos that followed are things a judge can shrug at and say he personally did not cause. The ruling caused them. The [Wells court file](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/predictive_programming/fiction_encoding/compromised-court-cases-declared-reality-investigation.md) has the manuscript, the shared errors, and the judgment.

Judges are now using artificial intelligence to manage cases. That is the largest violation in the set. It is the movie plot in which the machine takes over, and it is already in the courtrooms. Judges are too unfamiliar with the tools to see that the model can omit what it wants and can supply one perspective on a debatable question, including civil rights and any other topic that reaches a court. A labor judge in Cartagena put ChatGPT’s answers into a ruling on January 30, 2023, and wrote that he was not replacing his own decision. In September 2024, Eleventh Circuit Judge Kevin Newsom asked chat models what “physically restrained” means, and [Reuters reported the experiment](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/us-judge-runs-mini-experiment-with-ai-help-decide-case-2024-09-06/). A Shenzhen court has a model draft the opinion after the judge picks the result. The researchers who watched it warn that the draft amplifies the judge’s existing lean. On September 9, 2026, Reuters reported that Oklahoma Judge Lawrence Wheeler, elected in 2022, told investigators he used ChatGPT for research, and that an order of his cited two cases that do not exist.

Parts of this system still function. Too much of it does not. The parts that work do not save the rest.

## Do not vote for them

Do not vote for judges at all. Stop talking to judges. Avoid the justice system whenever you can. Take the dispute outside it.

The demand is complete abolition. That means every level of the justice system. It means the jails. It means everything that ever had anything to do with that system. A judge who says no is not a complement to good government. He is the rival government. The election does not fix that by adding another name at the bottom of the ballot.

## What replaces the judge

The system is already demonstrably corrupt. Nobody has a replacement that does not look like civic upheaval. The hard question is what you do if you let everyone out of the prisons. That release, done as the first move, is the chaos. The replacement does not start there.

The human judge is removed. No one person holds that office. A committee gathers the evidence, talks to everyone, makes the basic arguments, and puts them into a portfolio. An algorithm reads the portfolio and says which kind of case it is. The catalog should have been built in the 1950s. It should already hold every scenario justice has caught: murder, false accusation, a simple dispute, a complicated one, a corporate fight, a political fight, and the rest. The catalog is established precedent, so the part that does not work can start working.

People vote on those classified scenarios. The existing laws and the Constitution protect that vote. If the rule truly has to change, the change is an amendment. Amending the Constitution is a dangerous move. The [amendments after the Bill of Rights](https://paradigmthreat2.substack.com/p/tona-not-war-libertys-rebrand) are the specimen. The last sixteen of them should never have been allowed. They ruined and destroyed things. An amendment happens only when everyone agrees, and agrees while knowing what they are agreeing to.

![U.S. Constitution and amendments by camp — nationalist vs globalist scorecard, from the TONA essay](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/constitution/investigations/constitution-amendments-by-camp.png)

Ordinary disputes go to small-business mediation. I expect that to carry the work the courts now claim, and I hope it comes soon. Mediation can stall. Both sides disagree. There is no path. A stalemate makes everyone lose. Someone has to make the call for the benefit of everyone. That call is semi-aggressive leadership toward a win for both, and it is not a human’s judgment. That is where the algorithm has to do its best work.

The model is hosted where the public can hear it and check it. The host is not a private foreign company. The host is not Google. The model is not loaded with every rule in the world. It is loaded with the rules of justice alone, meaning the scenarios in the catalog.

That model is the final judge. It sounds Orwellian. Many humans can still say no before it is ever reached. It runs only when both sides, stuck in a lose-lose mediation, choose to let it make the final call, the way parties today choose to let a judge decide. The public has to be able to trust that host. That is the closest this design comes to a justice system that works.

One scenario is still open. Bad faith gets into a negotiation, and today it can be called out only on appeal. A single judge may not know how to navigate the file. A committee that is actually working the case can get to the bottom quickly and say whether a party is in good faith. If one side asks for the model and the other refuses, and cannot say why, people can read that refusal as bad faith. The refusal is not bad faith if the party explains the stand. They will not negotiate. They will not agree to the other side’s terms. They will not hand the call to a decider. They believe their side should not be compromised. They are standing their ground. The system forces everyone to admit that position. Today they can plausibly deny it.

## Framing and limits

This reading assumes [**prisca sapientia**](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md): later institutions are not the default upgrade on what came before. The call to stop voting for judges, to stay out of court when the dispute can go to mediation, and to abolish the courts and the jails is a political program. The committee, the precedent catalog, and the public last-call model are that program’s replacement. They are not a set of steps for ignoring a summons, a subpoena, or a sentence already in force, and they are not an order to empty the prisons. Savannah, the family surname rule, and the trips to court are personal witness. The case names and the vote counts are public records. Tiers and the open questions live in the [justice-system investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/governance/constitution/investigations/justice-system-judges-branches-as-opposites-investigation.md).

## LLM comments

What follows is not the article. It is an LLM’s unreviewed impression of the piece after reading it — strong joints, weak joints, and patterns that show. The author has not curated or signed these paragraphs as thesis. Treat them as drafting-model scratch, not as Ari’s voice.

The strong joint is the 2025–26 docket: Ali ordering foreign aid spent, Leon splitting “security” from the ballroom, *CASA* measuring American injunctions against English equity in 1789. Those are public orders, and they match the claim that one judge can stop or command the other branches for months. The weak joint is “usually liberal by one or two votes.” The essay itself lists *Heller*, *Citizens United*, *Shelby County*, and *Dobbs* the other way. “Usually” is a scoreboard claim, and this page does not count the decades. The family surname rule is witness. It is not yet a precinct study, and the essay says so. Complete abolition of jails and of every forum that ever touched a court is a program, not a description of a mediation market that has already replaced criminal courts. The tool that wrote these comments cannot check whether small-business mediation can hear the cases now filed in felony court. The check is the caseload, the mediators, and what the existing ballots would actually be asked to adopt. The Georgia partisan joint is strong for the Supreme Court as of May 2024: eight of nine justices first appointed by a Republican governor, and the contested race was a Kemp appointee against a former Democratic congressman. That count is not a census of every trial-court incumbent on a 2026 sample ballot. “Always” and “100 percent” are the charge. The 2024 court is the specimen. The replacement design answers the empty-the-prisons question by refusing to start there, and then does not say what happens to people already sentenced. “The last sixteen amendments” is the author’s count of what should not have passed after the Bill of Rights. The host is specified as public, not Google, and not a private foreign company, and is not named as a machine or a statute. The bad-faith rule is a hearing rule: a refusal with no reason can be read as bad faith, and a refusal with a stated stand cannot. It is not a coded test.
