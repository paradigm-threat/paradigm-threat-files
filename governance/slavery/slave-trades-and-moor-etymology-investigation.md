## Etymology of "Moor": Mainstream vs. Hypothesis

### Mainstream Etymology

- **Latin:** *Maurus* — inhabitant of Mauretania (Roman NW Africa: Morocco, N Algeria)  
- **Greek:** *Mauros* — from *Maurus* or possibly Greek *mauros* "black" (though *mauros* may derive from the people’s name)  
- **Phoenician:** *Mahurin* — "Westerners" (reportedly)  
- **Related:** Mauretania, Maghreb ("the West"), Morocco  
- **Exonym:** A label applied by outsiders, not a strict ethnonym. Europeans used it loosely for Berbers, Arabs, Muslims, and black Africans.

**Source:** Etymonline, Wiktionary, Britannica, 1911 Encyclopædia Britannica, Wikipedia.

### User Hypothesis: "People of Death" (Latin *mor*)

**Claim:** The name "Moor" may have been perceived—or even derived—from Latin *mor* (death). To "everyone's ears," the Moorish were the "People of Death" because of their engagement in slavery.

**Linguistic assessment:**
- **Latin *mors* (death):** From PIE *mer-* "to die." Root *mor-* appears in *mori* (to die), *mors* (death), *mortalis*, *mortuus*, etc. The root was familiar in Latin and in medieval/early modern Europe.
- **Formal etymology:** *Maurus* and *mors* are treated as separate. *Maurus* is traced to Greek *Mauros* and Mauretania, not to *mors*.
- **Folk etymology / paronomasia:** Even if the official origin is Mauretania, speakers could have associated *Moor* with *mor*/*mors* because of sound similarity. Folk etymologies often arise from perceived meaning, especially when a group is associated with violence or death.
- **Cognate:** The landscape term *moor* (heath, "dead land") in Old English is sometimes linked to PIE *mer-* "to die" via "dead land" — etymonline notes this possibility. So a *mor* = death/infertility connection was present in Germanic contexts.
- **Conclusion:** The formal etymology does not support "Moor" = "People of Death" as a linguistic derivation. The hypothesis is better understood as **folk etymology** or **popular perception**: that the name could have been heard and interpreted as "death-related" by Latin- and Romance-speaking populations who knew *mors*/*mor* and who associated the Moors with slave-raiding and violence.

**Author's sentiment:** Whether or not "Moor" derives from *mor*, the idea that contemporaries heard "People of Death" in the name—because of slavery, raids, and conquest—is plausible as a layer of meaning. The Moors *were* heavily involved in slavery; Europeans captured by them would have had strong reason to associate the name with death and bondage. Folk etymology often encodes how a group is perceived, not how a word was first formed.

### Folk / vernacular notes (open-Q progress)

- **Landscape *moor*:** Etymonline allows OE *mor* ↔ PIE *mer-* “to die” / “dead land” — a death-root already in English ears beside the ethnonym.  
- **Morus / Maurus pun field:** Early modern Latin and vernacular playlinks *Maurus*–*Morus* (black / fool) — e.g. Erasmus/More code-name *Niger*; Parker on *mor-* networks (Moors, *amor*, *Moria*). Not a *mors*=death proof-text, but shows **mor-** syllables were a live pun field.  
- **Primary Latin “Maurus = mors” line:** Not yet located as a medieval gloss equating the ethnonym to death. Status remains **folk/paronomasia**, not formal derivation. Keep hunting Romance chronicles and glossaries.

## Social Security number — history and dubious implementation

**Standalone section** (feeds the covert-slavery pattern without requiring strawman metaphysics).

### Creation (1935–1937)

- **Social Security Act** signed **14 August 1935** (FDR). Payroll taxes begin **January 1937**; early law envisioned a large reserve invested only in U.S. government (or government-guaranteed) obligations ([SSA — development](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/ssb/v70n3/v70n3p1.html); [SSA — budget treatment](https://www.ssa.gov/history/BudgetTreatment.html)).  
- **Nine-digit SSN** invented to track covered workers for benefits — then spreads until it becomes **de facto national ID** for tax, banking, credit, and benefits ([Wikipedia — SSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_number); [AARP — SSN history](https://www.aarp.org/social-security/ssn-history/)).  
- **1939 amendments** create a formal **OASI Trust Fund** on Treasury books — politically labeled “trust,” operationally still government-to-itself securities ([SSA history](https://www.ssa.gov/history/BudgetTreatment.html); Hoover on reserve/trust labeling: [Congressional response to surpluses](https://www.hoover.org/research/congressional-response-social-security-surpluses-1935-1994)).

### Opposition and the “security” dress

- **Right / liberty opposition:** 1936 campaign rhetoric (e.g. Landon lane) stirred fears of **dog tags / fingerprint national tracking** ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/social-security/ssn-history/)). Early Social Security Board rules stressed the number was **for Social Security only** — a public reassurance that later practice abandoned as Congress and agencies expanded SSN uses; SSA itself warned (e.g. 1971 task force) against national-ID creep while Congress kept requiring the number anyway ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/social-security/ssn-history/)).  
- **Left / Communist Party:** CPUSA testimony **opposed** the Wagner administration bill as a **sham** that secured the rich and urged the broader **Lundeen / workers’ bill** instead ([SSA historical PDF — Hathaway](https://www.ssa.gov/history/pdf/hr35hathway.pdf)). So “Social Security = communist plot” is too crude: the Party attacked it as **insufficient** central insurance, while liberty critics attacked **enumeration and compulsion**. The **anti-American feel** in a republic of limited government is the **compulsory payroll ID + lifetime claim** — security branding over a tracking and extraction machine.  
- **Author note:** Centralized old-age insurance *dressed as security* while building a permanent federal claim on wages rhymes with empire fiscal tools more than with frontier independence — regardless of 1935 partisan labels.

### Trust fund mismanagement / IOU pattern (documented)

- By design, surplus payroll receipts buy **special-issue Treasury securities**. Cash goes to **general government use**; the “trust fund” holds **IOUs** from one government pocket to another ([NPR — FDR to IOU](https://www.npr.org/2011/02/09/133627103/from-fdr-to-iou-social-security-hits-tipping-point); [Cato — trust fund myth](https://www.cato.org/policy-analysis/social-security-trust-fund-myth); SSA’s own accounting history).  
- Critics from the **1935** Ways and Means minority onward warned Congress would **spend the reserve** and that government bonds as “savings” were dubious ([SSA — development](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/ssb/v70n3/v70n3p1.html)). Surpluses historically triggered **benefit expansions** rather than true lockboxing ([Hoover](https://www.hoover.org/research/congressional-response-social-security-surpluses-1935-1994)).  
- Program went **cash-flow negative** around **2010**; redeeming trust securities then requires **taxes, borrowing, or cutting other spending** — the surplus was already spent ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2011/02/09/133627103/from-fdr-to-iou-social-security-hits-tipping-point)).  
- **Pattern verdict:** Even if every bond is “backed by the full faith and credit” of the U.S., the worker’s surplus was **not** sequestered as private capital. Lifetime contributions fund present beneficiaries and past general spending — **pay-as-you-go + political IOU**, sold to the public as a personal nest egg. That **behaves** as a forced intergenerational claim on labor.
