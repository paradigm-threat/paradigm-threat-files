# Reader-facing articles — how we want them written

**Purpose:** Define **article** voice for *paradigm-threat-files*—the Markdown siblings of **`investigations/`** dossiers and other long-form files—so assistants and authors match **Paradigm Threat** expectations. Default cadence is a **chronicle / objective documentary**: facts in order, ordinary speech, **no poetry**. The reader makes up their own mind; the page supplies the facts they need. Pair with **[`INVESTIGATIONS.md`](../INVESTIGATIONS.md)** (dossier structure, evidence tiers, **Limits**) and **[`LINKING_AND_SITE_PATHS.md`](../LINKING_AND_SITE_PATHS.md)** (URLs).

---

## URLs (same as blog entries)

**Published reader-facing articles** (parent-folder `…/*.md` essays under `influence/`, `science/`, `history/`, etc.—the pieces that ship like site pages) use the **same link convention as `blog/*.md` posts:** every **in-repo** target is an **absolute public URL** on `https://paradigmthreat.net/...`, including **§** anchors and **images** under the article’s site subtree. Do **not** use root-relative markdown links like `(/influence/...)` or repo-relative asset paths in those files.

**Investigations**, hub **`page.md`** indices, and internal **docs** may keep root-relative links unless a file is being promoted to “article” behavior.

## Article vs dossier (division of labor)

| **`…/investigations/*.md` (dossier)** | **Parent-folder `…/*.md` (article)** |
|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Tables, long quote banks, FOIA chains, **Limits**, **Investigator notes**, granular **TODOs**, **Q** rows, **§4** open-claim registries, tier labels, “speculative / documented / legal analogy” splits. | **Readable essay**: stakes, flow, and **enough** links to enter the dossiers—**not** a second investigation file. |
| Home for **heavy** caveat language: what is proven vs guessed, what is rhetoric vs docket, fiction vs evidence, assistant bias. | **Light touch** on caveats in the body. **Manifesto** articles: trust the dossier for tier machinery; optional **`## Framing and limits`**. **Operational** articles (portal, classified infrastructure): **declarative** body, **`## Why nothing else fits`** — logical coherence, **not** “not proved” / memo-gap disclaimers. **Do not** paste investigation-style stacks into the **running narrative**.
| **Guide (read order)** for very long files. | **Optional** short “where next” at the end; the **body** should read in order without a map unless you deliberately want a mini-guide. |

If a claim needs **five citations** or a **multi-row table**, it belongs in the **dossier**; the **article** names the claim and **links once** (often to a **§** anchor).

**Standalone narrative context (required):** Assume most readers **never** open the dossier — and **will not** click through for missing context. The article must still carry **enough plain context** that every named event, institution, and metaphor lands without a click — **who**, **what changed**, **why it mattered**, in one or two sentences. The dossier adds **citations, tiers, dissent, and tables**; it is not where essential plot lives. **Lists and bold-lead stacks** must be **self-explanatory on first read**: if you use bullets or `**Label:**` rows, the reader should know **what the list is** and **what each entry means** without opening an investigation. **Hard rules:** **no context-free name dumps**; **name it in the article or link the investigation** (see **Tables, lists, and catalog prose**). **Bad:** six jargon labels (`Containment`, `Credibility slot`, `Cross-border shield`) with no surrounding prose. **Good:** two paragraphs that walk a stranger from front company to extraction, then one optional link to the dossier for dockets. **Bad:** "executives saw the cliff, couldn't turn" after citing a case number — the reader has no picture of the cliff. **Good:** IBM licensed DOS instead of owning it, clones undercut its machines, managers saw software standards slipping to Microsoft and Intel but could not pivot through bureaucracy and contracts — then link *Big Blues* / HBS **19-074** for the full audit. Same rule for niche tech: gloss once in outcome language (see **Insider stack narration**), not dossier shorthand.

When an article **links** a controversy (DOS deal, antitrust, platform lock-in), it still owes **one plain paragraph** of **who / what / why** in the article body. **Investigation-forward material** — case numbers, settlement tables, "historians disagree," legal-moat chronologies — belongs in the dossier unless **one sentence** is essential to the hook. Reference: [Internet That Should Have Been](/science/computing/the-internet-that-should-have-been.md) §II + [investigation §9.6](/science/computing/investigations/internet-that-should-have-been-investigation.md).

### Series articles (same thesis lane, multiple installments)

When several reader essays explore one **ongoing thesis**—e.g. **predictive programming in blockbuster film/TV**—treat them as a **named series**, not independent primers that repeat the same frame.

| Rule | Do | Don’t |
|------|----|-------|
| **Series identity** | State **installment number or subtitle** in the line under `# Title` (e.g. *Predictive programming in movies — Pacific installment*). Name what **this** installment adds in the **first body `##`** or in **`## Where next`** — **not** as a multi-link routing stack in the opening paragraphs before the first `##` (see Shape step 6). | Re-teach the **full shared frame** (two-camp war, NSA vs CIA grammar, franchise containment doctrine) if an earlier series essay already did. **Do not** open with “Installment I / II / companion investigation” link dumps before the thesis lands. |
| **Cross-link** | Link **prior / next** installments in a dedicated **`## Series — where next`** (or fold into **Where next** with a **Series** subheading). Bidirectional links when both files exist. | Duplicate long passages from sibling articles “so this page stands alone.” |
| **Unique payload** | Each installment owns **new titles, new geography, new calendar beats** (*Matrix* handoff vs *Dark Knight* / *24* Pacific lane). | Copy **Matrix kill-offs**, **Wake Up credits**, **MxO shutdown**, etc. into a Pacific installment unless **one sentence** ties the calendar. |
| **Companions vs installments** | **Dossiers**, **hubs**, and **investigations** are **technical companions**—link under a separate subheading, not counted as series entries. | Label every related essay as “Part III” without a shared series name. |
| **Pre-ship dedup pass** | Read the **most recent sibling article** in full; **grep** shared phrases (*two-camp*, *NSA-aligned grammar*, *franchise containment*, *Ledger curse*). Cut or replace with **“see Installment I.”** | Ship a new installment that reads like a **merged duplicate** of the last one with different examples swapped in. |

### Tables, lists, and catalog prose (articles vs investigations)

**Hard rule for articles:** **No Markdown pipe tables anywhere** — not in the body, not in **`## Framing and limits`**, not in footnotes. Substack’s editor (and most paste-from-Markdown workflows) **do not render** `| col | col |` grids; rows collapse into a **broken vertical list** that reads like a corrupted dossier excerpt. **Write normal paragraphs instead.** Weave claim, tier, and link into **complete sentences** (commas, semicolons, short follow-on sentences). If the reader needs a grid, put it in the **`investigations/`** dossier and link once.

- **Investigations** (`…/investigations/*.md`): **Markdown tables are fine**—tier matrices, chronologies, claim registries, comparison grids. **Bullet and bold-lead catalogs** (literature maps, author lanes, tier rows) also belong here.
- **Articles** (parent-folder reader essays): **paragraphs only** for argument and for limits. **Do not** recreate dossier tier tables as pipe grids, bold-lead stacks, or pseudo-tables (`**Claim** — tier` lines that paste like broken tables).
- **If an article needs a table or structured illustration:** export **`PNG`** or **`SVG`**, place the asset **beside the article** in the same folder (same pattern as hero images), and embed with an **absolute** `https://paradigmthreat.net/...` URL and descriptive **alt** text. Raster and vector images **transfer**; Markdown tables **do not**.
- **Default for structured content:** **paragraphs**, not lists. The reader should hear an **essay**, not scroll a **glossary**.
- **Avoid catalog-style stacks** in article bodies:
  - Repeated `**Name** — gloss` or `**Name** — *work* (year): gloss` lines (literature surveys, thinker rundowns, “who said what” lanes).
  - Long bullet runs of **authors**, **eras**, **institutions**, or **claims** that could be one or two paragraphs.
  - Pseudo-tables built from bold labels + em dashes (they paste into Substack like **broken tables** and read like **dossier excerpts**).
- **When lists are OK (sparingly):**
  - **Short numbered lists** (typically **≤5 items**) for **sequential steps** the reader must follow in order (e.g. debunk-cycle phases, a tight parent checklist) — each step **glossed in plain English**; no dossier-only labels.
  - **“Where next”** link bullets at the end (routing only—not argument).
- **No context-free name dumps (hard rule):** Do **not** fire a **staccato list** of proper nouns, years, site codes, or jargon labels unless the reader already knows **what the list is about** from **prior prose in the same paragraph** or an **earlier gloss**. A **lay reader** must understand the **category** (who dug, what broke, what was removed) **before** you name the entries. **Bad:** `WPA trenches in 1939. Haag again in 1957. University field schools in 2001.` — six labels with no frame. **Good:** one plain sentence first (“**the hill has already been picked over for almost a century**”), then—if names are needed—**woven into prose** with gloss (“Waring’s **1939** WPA dig and Haag’s **1957** return cut test trenches through the rise”) **or** defer names to the **dossier** (see next rule).
- **Prefer articles over investigations for other topics (hard rule):** When the reader needs another **topic** already covered in the corpus (TONA, JFK, Zelensky, Iran, Paperclip narrative, etc.), link a **parent-folder reader article** first. Use an **`investigations/`** link only when **(a)** it is the **companion dossier of this article**, **(b)** **no** reader article exists yet for that topic, or **(c)** the reader needs a specific **§** receipt / table / FOIA chain. Do **not** bounce strangers into a dossier for a subject that already has an essay.
- **Name it here or point to the article / investigation (hard rule):** Do **not** **drop** a fact, name, date, institution, or technical label **once in passing** if this article **never explains it** and **does not link** where it is explained. Either **(a)** cover it in **plain language** in this file (at least **one sentence** a stranger understands), or **(b)** **link once** to an existing **parent-folder article** on that topic, or **(c)** if no article exists (or this article’s own companion proof is required), link the **`investigations/`** § that holds the timeline, receipts, or glossary. **Bad:** lede mentions looting, drainage ditch, plow zone, backfill — body never returns; reader is left with unexplained debris. **Good:** lede stays plain (“already dug, fenced, narrated”); first **`##`** gives the one-sentence read (“official story comes from digs in the 1930s, 1950s, and 2000s”) plus a **[reader article](https://paradigmthreat.net/...)** or **[investigation §](https://paradigmthreat.net/...)** for names and dates.
- **If the material is inherently tabular or multi-row:** keep the **full grid or catalog** in the **dossier** and link to the **§** anchor; the article gets **one or two prose paragraphs** that state the pattern and point there.
- **Reference (catalog → prose):** [`history/chronology/when-your-child-wont-fit-the-grade.md`](../../history/chronology/when-your-child-wont-fit-the-grade.md) — “Literature that respected autodidactic children” (woven paragraphs, not author bullets). [`history/chronology/napoleon-corpses-dry-egypt-siberian-disease.md`](../../history/chronology/napoleon-corpses-dry-egypt-siberian-disease.md) — **`## Framing and limits`** as prose tiers, not a pipe table.

**Default:** Articles **state stakes and thesis clearly**—**serious and plain** (precise, unsentimental, still alive; not witty entertainment); investigations **hold** the audit trail. When in doubt, **cut** the paragraph that sounds like **Limits** and **add** a link: “the careful version lives in [**Limits**](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/…/investigations/…#limits-and-disclaimers).”

### Reader-facing prose (generic surface)

Articles are written so they can be **republished or read off-site** without sounding like internal documentation.

- **No project or repository meta in the body:** Do **not** name the hosting project, the codebase, or “the repo.” Avoid phrases like **in-repo**, **this tree**, **our investigations**, **a repo search**, **in the files**, **Paradigm Threat** (and close synonyms) in running prose. Use **neutral formulations**: “this reading,” “the argument below,” “linked notes,” “supporting write-up,” “technical companion piece,” “a public search found…”.
- **No local or internal paths in reader articles:** Do **not** link or cite **machine paths** (`/home/...`, `~/dev/...`, `/media/...`, maintainer mirrors like `~/dev/wget/...`) or **investigator-only** markdown under `docs/` that is **not** a public reader page — e.g. `docs/debate-notes/`, prep dossiers, transcript stacks, evidence-index archives, session debriefs. Those belong in dossier **Investigator notes** or nowhere in shipped prose. If evidence from a private archive matters, **describe it in plain language** (dates, platform, what the screenshot shows) or move quotable material into a **published investigation** and link **`https://paradigmthreat.net/...`** once.
- **No drafting-process meta:** Never ship **author confusion**, **voice-note mis-transcriptions**, **session labels**, **“User query”** framing, or **“does it check out?”** audit chatter in article bodies. If two subjects sound alike (e.g. **Poe** vs **Prince**), name the **correct subject once** in plain prose — **do not** explain how a mix-up happened or point readers at transcription logs. **Resolve ambiguities before publish**; **ask the author** when uncertain what they meant. Workflow mistakes belong in **Investigator notes** (investigations) or nowhere — not in reader-facing text.
- **Links:** In **article** bodies, use **absolute** `https://paradigmthreat.net/...` URLs for every internal page and image (**same as `blog/`**). Describe targets by **topic**, not by filing system (“see the linked DNA **blueprint critique**,” not “see our biology dossier”).
- **Prefer inline links over pointer sentences (hard rule):** Put the URL on the **words that already name the source or event** — do **not** add a trailing sentence whose only job is the link. **Bad:** “Crichton came on to talk about *Disclosure*. The clip is still on the [Charlie Rose site](https://…).” **Good:** “[Crichton came on to talk about *Disclosure*](https://…).” **Bad:** “…climber they could not safely criticize in public. Full notes: [michaelcrichton.com — Disclosure](https://…).” **Good:** “On [his site](https://…) he later wrote….” Same fail for “See more here:,” “Read the full interview:,” “Source:,” and “The page is still up at…” when the prior sentence already carried the claim. **Where next** bullets stay routing lists; this rule is for **body prose**.
- **First person — when to use `I` (hard rule):** Use **I** / **my** / **me** **only** when the sentence is **about Ari personally** — life experience, biography, personal witness, personal regard, or an original claim that is dishonest without ownership (“**I met** wise elders in the 80s,” “**in my lifetime**,” “**I still hold** RFK Jr in high regard”). **Do not** use **I** to **frame or narrate the argument** (“**I argue**,” “**I will not**,” “**I am not going to**,” “**I read X as**,” “**I do not claim**,” “**I do not treat**,” “**What I will not do**”). **State the thesis and architecture directly** — let the facts and claims speak. **Operational / institutional** articles already default to declarative third person; **manifesto** articles still obey this same **I**-budget (personal layer only, not argument scaffolding). **Do not** write “**the author read**,” “**author thesis**,” or “**on my read**” in article bodies.
- **Assert, don’t disclaim (existence):** In **operational thesis** articles, **do not** hedge whether the core mechanism **exists** — no “**if it exists**,” “**not proved**,” “**hypothesis**,” “**author thesis tier**,” “**unverified**,” or “**possible** portal” in the **running narrative**. **Write as if the read is true**; confidence comes from **logical coherence** (geometry + quotes + cover story + pattern), not from waiting for a declassified memo. **Institutional denials** (CERN FAQ, Snopes) are **recorded facts** — they are **not** reasons to retreat in the body.
- **No memo-gap / smoking-gun disclaimers (hard rule — all article types):** Do **not** write “**we have no located declassified memo**,” “**FOIA not yet retrieved**,” “**smoking gun not found**,” “**not a signed tasking memo**,” “**nobody is claiming X got an agency memo**,” “**not leaked agency files**,” “**no confession has turned up**,” or close cousins in the **body** or in **`## Framing and limits`**. This project is **not** hunting for signed tasking memos; pattern, grammar, calendar, and public text are the load-bearing proof. Investigation **procedures and tiers** already live in the companion dossier / Limits — **link once** if the reader needs the audit trail. Do **not** restage that apology after every camp label, Hollywood join, or ideology claim. **Manifesto** articles obey the same ban as **operational** ones.
- **No thesis archaeology:** **Do not** narrate **retired assumptions**, “**I used to think**,” “**earlier read**,” or “**symmetry correction**” in reader essays. **State the current architecture** as it stands. Pole routing, Martian pedagogy, and similar **engineering forks** may stay **open** without hedging the **core gate**.
- **Neutral ≠ timid:** **Hard thesis** and **manifesto** cadence stay **first class**; **caution** is **not** a substitute for **clarity**. **Operational reads** are **clinical and convinced**, not **tentative**.
- **Magic (lexical rule):** When articles cite historical or scholarly *magic*, link or gloss per [**magic as operator technology**](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/MAGIC_AS_OPERATOR_TECHNOLOGY.md) — *magic* = demonstrable operator tech misfiled as supernatural; quote sources verbatim, argue in **operator protocol** voice.
- **Vacuum inverse (franchise gaps):** When articles treat long product silence, managed “returns,” or sabotaged revivals, name **[vacuum inverse](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/VACUUM_INVERSE.md)** once — vacuum felt loudest by handlers in pre-viral groups; fans already adapted; revival is **shaped ignition**, not proof of forgotten demand. Load-bearing example: *DKC* → *Returns* (shake-roll / no Classic Controller) → long wait for HD → silence after *Tropical Freeze*.

### Theory-led pacing (default for parent-folder articles)

Parent-folder reader essays are **thesis vehicles**, not primers. They may read as **strong manifesto** when the topic demands it.

- **Lead with the unified theory:** Open with the **cross-cutting claim** and its **stakes**. Do not bury the thesis under throat-clearing. **Intelligent readers** can handle a sharp fork on page one.
- **Wrong-debate / “what this is not” framing goes late (hard rule):** Do **not** open with boundary scaffolding about rival theories the article is **not** joining (“lone gunman vs grassy knoll,” “this is not X,” “the usual debate is the wrong fork,” “I will not shrink into…”). **State the positive claim first.** Put the **one** short wrong-fork note in **`## Framing and limits`** (or the last body beats) — **once**. Do **not** restage that meta in the lede **and** mid-body **and** close. Mid-body may still name rival trees when they are **load-bearing facts** (e.g. CIA ridicule of conspiracy theorists, magic-bullet containment as a historical mechanism) — that is **evidence**, not opening throat-clearing.
- **Heavy proof stays in companion files:** Multi-citation blocks, tier labels, and long **Limits** machinery stay in **`investigations/`** (or equivalent technical write-ups). The article **points**; the companion **prosecutes**.
- **Pacing:** Short **`##` sections**, **one main idea** each, **momentum** over repetition.
- **Sensitive targets:** When the author requests it, use **structural / institutional** framing (doctrine shapes, incentives, schooling) rather than naming modern groups—unless the author explicitly wants names.
- **Honesty without dilution:** When caveats are **necessary**, **do not** weave them into each section—that trains the reader to expect retreat after every claim. Prefer **one** consolidated closing block **after** **Where next**, **before** `Keywords:` — either **`## Framing and limits`** (scope / prisca sapientia / link to dossier) **or** **`## Why nothing else fits`** (logical case: why the read is the **only coherent** explanation; classification explains missing public docs). Keep the **body** declarative. **Operational thesis** articles use **Why nothing else fits** over disclaimer stacks. Alternatively, **one** sentence plus a dossier link can suffice. Do not use caution as a substitute for **clarity**.
- **Prisca sapientia (closing block):** In **Framing and limits**, open with [**prisca sapientia**](https://paradigmthreat.net/docs/PRISCA_SAPIENTIA.md) when that block is scope-oriented. In **Why nothing else fits**, lead with the **logical stack** (documented bookends + geometry + pattern); prisca sapientia may be **one sentence** or folded into the opening. One link to dossier **Limits** or **Why nothing else fits** for tiers is enough.

Reference implementation for **generic surface + theory-led** shape: [`science/biology/circle-of-life-and-how-it-began.md`](../../science/biology/circle-of-life-and-how-it-began.md).

## Voice (who we are writing for)

- **Two voice modes (pick one per article):**
  - **Manifesto / personal thesis:** Constitution, schooling, chronology, high-stakes history — **declarative claims** carry the argument as **record**, not as verse. **I** only for the **personal layer** (see **First person — when to use `I`** above). **Bad:** “**I argue** that Dallas was theater.” **Good:** “**Dallas was theater.**” Still **chronicle cadence**: facts in order; the reader decides.
  - **Operational / institutional (declarative):** Portal hardware, Mars cosmology, classified infrastructure, Dead Planet cover — **third person** or **direct declarative** (“**CERN is…**,” “**the ring is the harness**”). **Minimize `I`**; **zero** “**on this read**” / “**the author**” distancing.
- **Facts vs interpretation — without tier labels:** **Documented events** (court votes, Bertolucci quotes, Mariner null, LHC depth) stay **neutral** when citing sources (“**Vance said…**,” “**Mariner 2 returned…**”). **Interpretation** is **declarative** — **never** “author read (C)” or tier grids in the body. Use **I** only when the interpretation is a **personal stake** (“**I still hold**…,” “**in my lifetime**…”), not as a prefix for every thesis sentence.
- **First person is not literary theater or argument scaffolding:** **Do not** use **I** for **shared-feeling scene-painting** (“opened my mind,” “we shared the same gasp”) when a **plain fact** carries the point. **Do not** use **I** to refuse, promise, or stage-manage the reader (“**I am not going to**…,” “**What I will not do**…”). **Do not** lead **every** sentence with **I**.
- **Drop “this essay / this piece”:** Prefer **direct claims**. Phrases like “**this essay** will show,” “**in this piece** we,” or “**what overlaps for this essay**” read like syllabus metal—**cut them**.
- **State the claim — drop the frame:** **Bad:** “My **hard moral read** stays blunt: **Polynesian continuity** was interrupted.” **Bad:** “**On the author read (tier C),** the war was…” **Bad:** “**I argue** that **Polynesian continuity** was interrupted.” **Good:** “**Polynesian continuity** was interrupted.” **Good (personal stake only):** “**I still hold** RFK Jr in high regard.”
- **Bold — emphasis or new terms only:** Use `**bold**` for **(1)** real **emphasis** (one beat per sentence or so—a contrast, a risk, a hinge you need the eye to catch), and **(2)** **new terminology** the article **introduces** or **defines** for this read (first pass; often plain text on repeat). **Do not** bold **every** proper noun, institution, date, or ordinary stressed word—that reads as **sparkle fatigue** and **fake dossier** layout. When in doubt, **remove** the bold.
- **Default register — chronicle / documentary (hard rule):** Write like a chronicle or an objective documentary. Who did what, when, what followed. Ordinary spoken sentences. Complete thoughts. **The reader makes up their own mind.** The page’s job is to put the **facts they need** in order — dates, products, copies, who held the rights, what changed. Prefer **cool record** over atmosphere. **Do not** write poetry, trailer voice, or manifesto cadence. **Do not** default to witty entertainment writing, “clever” ledes, screenwriter montage openings, punchy review-blog cadence, **or poet / aphorism packaging**. Sensational material stays sensational by **stating what happened in order**, not by lyricism around it. Inspired ≠ cute. Chronicle ≠ verse. **Layperson default:** explain to a sharp friend at a kitchen table. Lament only when Ari **explicitly** asks for a lament piece.
- **Natural spoken flow (hard rule):** Prefer **Ari’s narration cadence** — unbroken complete thoughts that keep moving — over polished essay voice, poetry, or bot-manifesto packaging. Positive models: [`governance/war/the-great-depression-was-planned.md`](../../governance/war/the-great-depression-was-planned.md); when Ari supplies spoken/chat wording in session, treat that diction as the **tone sample** for the draft. **Read-aloud bar:** if a sentence sounds like a poet, a trailer, a LinkedIn manifesto, or a Substack hook factory, rewrite until it sounds like someone reporting a chronicle to a friend.
- **Draft from author speech (hard rule):** When Ari gives spoken or chat wording for an article, **fold that diction into the body first** (order and tighten; do not “upgrade” into literary manifesto or verse). Preserve named facts and charges. Resolve transcription slips (wrong proper names) before ship; do not narrate the mix-up in the article. Even a hard close stays **plain speech**, not a poem.
- **No pure-contrast section stacking (hard rule):** One contrast is fine. A whole article that only flips **A/B** labels (hell/heaven, corporate/open, not-this/but-that) with **no new fact or lived beat** per section is an **LLM fail**. Each `##` must **advance** events, evidence, or personal witness — not restage the same binary.
- **No brand-metaphor / unexplained analogy (hard rule):** Do **not** lean on media nicknames, Latin titles, board-game talk, or biology metaphors that a lay reader must already know (**Camelot**, **Rex Bellator**, “Vietnam was the **board**,” “wrong **fork**,” “**antibody** / **disease**” without a plain gloss). **State the fact in ordinary words.** Site terms that *are* the thesis (e.g. **antibody** in a linked good-actor write-up) may appear **once**, glossed in the same sentence. Prefer “fake death,” “public story,” “goal,” “wrong question” over theater / board / fork packaging when the metaphor does no new work.
- **No poet / aphorism LLM voice (hard rule):** Agents default to **elevated manifesto cadence** that sounds literary but is hard for a lay reader: stacked fragments (“Not as insult. Not as metaphor…”), abstract nouns as drama (“open condition,” “menus you are allowed to choose from”), personified abstractions (“It names everyone”), and neat closing aphorisms (“When the wallpaper peels…,” “Fracture is how the cage scales,” “The difference is costume”). **Bad:** “As the open condition of a life that feels free while agency stays limited — paperwork, payroll, passage…” **Good:** “You can feel free and still have little real choice — taxes, payroll, travel, war debt.” **Bad:** “It named the condition and left the proof on the table.” **Good:** “It named the condition. It did not prove it with history.” If a sentence would sound odd spoken to a stranger on the bus, **rewrite it plain**. This is part of every de-LLM pass (see **Phrasing we are tired of**).
- **No body TL;DR / topic-preview pad (hard rule):** Do **not** summarize the article’s section list in the opening (or mid-body) as a “stack of the same moves” / “here is what we will cover” paragraph. **Summaries belong only in an optional `> **TL;DR:**` block** — never as a preview of later `##` sections. Do **not** describe the companion investigation’s contents in the article body. Do **not** race through topics that later sections will actually argue. **Simple test:** if a paragraph makes **no unique claim** beyond naming what comes later, **delete it**. Do **not** pad to hit word-count targets; if the page needs more length, **find a new point** or deepen an existing one — do not add a second abstract of the same thesis.
- **Sensational claim → plain ordered argument:** When the thesis is high-voltage (“everything in this film is true,” “CERN is a portal”), open with the claim, then walk the reader through the list of what that means — one beat after another — so the page is **convincing**, not **entertaining**. Every cute line, clever contrast, or LLM flourish in the intro is a **distraction** from that job. Cut it.
- **Preserve Ari's intensity and intent (hard rule):** When Ari provides sensational, high-voltage, or accusatory claims, **do not silently downgrade them** into bland institutional prose **or** dress them up as witty essay packaging. Keep the **core charge**, stakes, and emotional vector intact. You may tighten wording for readability, legal safety, or structure, but only if the **primary intent is unchanged**. If a sentence must be softened, preserve force by pairing it with explicit stakes (who is harmed, what is controlled, what is lost).
- **No accidental de-fanging during cleanup:** Editing passes for "neutrality," "de-LLM," or "documentary voice" must not erase load-bearing claims (e.g., suppression, controlled opposition, surveillance, corruption, coercive lock-in). Prefer: (1) keep claim strong in body, (2) move caveat machinery to `## Framing and limits`, (3) link to dossier for heavy evidence. De-LLM also means **strip entertainment packaging** that agents add when the author meant blunt speech.
- **Author-supplied sentiment is first-class:** If Ari gives direct language in chat, preserve it where possible (short quoted block or close paraphrase) **before** assistant synthesis — reorganize for clarity and order, **do not** rewrite the tone into a slick magazine voice. Do not replace high-signal claims with abstract summary labels (“architecture of the threat,” “the payload,” “the honest viewing rule”) when the author’s words already name the facts.
- **Intelligent reader — no hand-holding (hard rule):** Assume the audience can **connect facts without a teaching caption**. **Skip** “**you might be wondering**,” over-explaining basics they have heard before, and **tutorial** pacing unless the article is **explicitly** introductory. **Do not** soften or repeat the same caveat in three forms; **trust** the reader to **follow** and to **use** the dossier links when they want machinery. **Do not** narrate significance before or after the facts (“**not random**,” “**not a coincidence**,” “**not mere color**,” “**the parallel still sits in plain sight**,” “**Same X. Same Y. Same Z.**,” “**Keep both facts in your head**”). **State the facts in order**; let the join land. If the reader cannot see it from the juxtaposition, the paragraph needs a **missing fact**, not a **nudge**.
- **No compacted multi-claim sentences (hard rule — LLM artifact):** Agents pack **several load-bearing claims** into **one long sentence** (often with em-dash appositions, stacked metaphors, and a late pay-off clause). The reader cannot accept or even track each claim. **One sentence → one main claim** (or one short clause of setup + one claim). If a sentence needs a stranger to already know three jargon phrases, **split it** and **explain each piece** in order. **Expanding the article past the drafting word target is allowed and preferred** when the expansion is **unpacking** compacted LLM prose into plain explanation. **Do not** “fix length” by **re-compressing** explained prose back into a multi-claim dash-stack. **Length cuts** (cutting sections, merging paragraphs to hit a number) are **author discretion** — agents do **not** auto-shrink for the Substack/email target when that would recreate compaction. See **Length → Expansion vs compression** and **Phrasing we are tired of → Compacted multi-claim sentences**.
  - **Bad (compacted):** “The consistent pattern—reset culture, import and replace, force a people full of wisdom and history through a narrow funnel until they exit as something else—cannot honestly be described as a conspiracy of on-the-books officials.”
  - **Good (unpacked):** State the **pattern** in plain words. Then what **reset / import / funnel** means for a stranger. Then the **conclusion** (not a Cabinet conspiracy memo). Several short sentences beat one teaching dash.
- **American spelling (hard rule):** Reader articles use **US English** orthography — not British / Commonwealth defaults that LLMs often emit. Prefer **civilization / civilizational**, **recognize**, **organize**, **color**, **honor**, **favor**, **center**, **defense**, **theater**, **behavior**, **labeled**, **catalog**, **program**, **traveling**, **modeling**, **analyze**, **realize**, **gray**, **facade** (no cedilla). Do **not** ship **civilisation / civilisational**, **recognise**, **organise**, **colour**, **honour**, **favour**, **centre**, **defence**, **theatre** (unless quoting a title), **behaviour**, **labelled**, **catalogue**, **programme**, **travelling**, **modelling**, **analyse**, **realise**, **grey**, **façade**. **Exception:** keep original spelling inside **verbatim quotes**, **proper names**, and **titles** of works. Treat British defaults as an **LLM artifact** in the de-LLM sweep.
- **Point, don’t prescribe (hard rule):** Articles **show** the record and **place** evidence. They **do not** coach the reader what to conclude. **The reader makes up their own mind.** Give them the facts they need — names, dates, copies, who could use the icon, who could not. **Implied join** beats **verdict voice**. Prefer **observation** (“the credits put X on a mass exit”) over **coaching** (“notice what the blockbuster allowed,” “the leftover divider is,” “that is why you must”). **Bad:** authoritative **I → you** scaffolding (“I am not asking you to X; I am asking you to Y”) or constant **second-person** steering. **Good:** documentary sentences and **personal-layer I** only where earned (see **First person** rule). Do not close a section with a moral, an aphorism, or a steered verdict unless Ari asked for that sentence in those words — and then still write it as speech, not verse.
- **Not authoritative — lay and objective:** Sound like someone **reporting what is on the record**, not a pundit **managing consent**. Avoid **pulpit** cadence: “that is why you must,” “the point is,” “what matters is that you see,” “the only real divider.” Let dates, copies, and juxtapositions carry the inference.
- **Simple over lyrical:** State **what happened** in plain words. **Bad:** stacked shared-experience poetry (“we shared the same gasp,” “the same silence when…,” “opened my mind was not only…”). **Good:** one factual line (“fans read one movie,” “the credits named Tonkin and Islam blame”). Keep the **point**; drop the **theater**.
- **You / we (restricted):** **Second person** is for **quoted dialogue**, **film lines**, or **rare** inclusive scene-setting—not default narration. **Inclusive we** is almost never needed; prefer **third person** (“fans,” “audiences,” “the culture”). Default remains **documentary third person**.
- **No cartoon villains:** Prefer **structural** language (“**schooling**,” “**press posture**,” “**museum caption**,” “**funding stack**”) over sentences that imply **every** individual scientist **intends** harm. Name **power** and **pattern**; if **documented** intent matters, the **dossier** carries the receipts.

## Shape (how we open and move)

1. **Title** — concrete; may use **em dash** or a strong image. Keep slug/path stable unless Ari explicitly requests a rename (URLs depend on paths).
2. **Subtitle (required)** — add one short line **immediately under** the `#` title (before any hero image), summarizing the thesis in plain reader language. Aim for one sentence fragment or clause (roughly 8–20 words), no ending period, no markdown heading marker (`##`), and no `TL;DR` label.
3. **Optional hero image** — immediately under the subtitle, **`https://paradigmthreat.net/...`** image URL (same as `blog/`), short **alt** that matches the article’s **central image or claim** (not only mood). **Generate locally:** see [`docs/IMAGE_GENERATION.md`](../IMAGE_GENERATION.md) (`generate-illustration.py`, HF FLUX; **never overwrite** without explicit approval). **Generated-image factual audit (hard rule):** open every candidate at full size and inspect **all visible text and chronology** before embedding it. Image models invent plausible quotations, signatures, headlines, dates, labels, and attributions; they also place works **after** an event under “before the event” headings. Verify each factual element independently. Regenerate or omit the image if any visible claim cannot be verified. Prefer artwork with **no generated prose**: leave clean space and add exact titles or labels programmatically afterward.
4. **Reuse existing corpus images (when topics overlap)** — If the article covers subjects that **already have** investigation, timeline, or sibling-article art, **embed those assets** at the **`##` section** where the topic is argued—not only the new hero PNG beside the file. **Search first:** grep linked dossiers, timeline chapters, and hub pages for `![` / `*.png` / `*.jpg`; prefer **published** `https://paradigmthreat.net/...` URLs (timeline art often lives under `https://timeline.paradigmthreat.net/media/...`). **Placement:** one image **after** the paragraph that introduces the topic (illustrate the **linked investigation**, not decorate every section). **Alt text:** name the **source lane** and **what the reader should see** (e.g. “Historical antibodies — timeline chapter,” “Call of Duty militainment — Krasinski operator arc”). **Do not** copy images into the article folder unless you are creating **new** art; **link** to the canonical file in its home directory. **Consecutive-article cap:** the **same image file** must **not** appear in **two reader-facing articles published back-to-back** (the immediately prior shipped article and the one you are writing now). Reuse across the corpus is fine with spacing; when the prior article already used an asset, pick a **sibling image** from the same lane or **omit** the embed. **Newsletter budget:** many large PNGs + hero can trigger Gmail clipping—prefer **fewer, well-placed** reuses over a gallery; see **Length → Newsletter / inbox check**. **Reference:** [`influence/controlled_opposition/cia-1984-mars-chrono-trigger.md`](../../influence/controlled_opposition/cia-1984-mars-chrono-trigger.md) (hero + dossier PNGs mid-article); [`science/biology/circle-of-life-and-how-it-began.md`](../../science/biology/circle-of-life-and-how-it-began.md) (article hero + investigation header); [`influence/hollywood/ghost-war-the-antibody-phase.md`](../../influence/hollywood/ghost-war-the-antibody-phase.md) (hero + timeline, militainment, PP hub, Iran runner reuses).
5. **Hook** — a **concrete stake**, **scene**, or **one sharp anecdote** before the reader hits dense links. This is the **contract**: why this file exists **before** apparatus. Default to **plain thesis first** (what you believe; what happened; why it matters). **Do not** open with witty setup, ticket-stub theater (“the lights go down…”), fake suspense, or entertainment-review energy unless Ari explicitly wants that register for a rare piece. **Do not** open with **wrong-debate / “what this is not”** scaffolding — park that once in **`## Framing and limits`** (see **Theory-led pacing**).
6. **No companion / timeline / investigation routing in the opening (hard rule)** — The **first viewport** (title, subtitle, hero, opening paragraphs **before** the first `##` body section) must **not** send the reader to other investigations, timeline stubs, companion dossiers, series installments, or “see also” apparatus. That is **hard misdirection**: the reader came for *this* thesis and gets bounced into a map before the argument starts. **Do** state the claim in plain words without a file tour. **Place** cross-links in the **middle** (when a named beat needs one supporting page) or at the **end** (`## Where next`, closing paragraph of the last body section, optional Framing block). This includes soft forms: “the first pass — [timeline] and [investigation]…,” “this installment follows…,” “citations live in the companion,” “another essay covers…,” and early series handoff paragraphs. **Series exception:** one **short** prior-installment name in the **subtitle line or first body `##`**, not a multi-link routing stack in the lede — and even then prefer **Where next** for full series links (see **Series articles**). Fold syllabus routing into **`## Where next`** — one short intro sentence before link bullets. The hook goes **straight** to the first **`##` body section**.
7. **`> **TL;DR:**`** — **optional** in articles. Use it when it **adds** a clean thesis line **without** cloning the dossier’s opening or sounding like a second **investigation header**. Skip it when the title + subtitle + first two paragraphs already do that work.

   **TL;DR content rules (standard):**
   - **Thesis and stakes only** — state the **point of the game** (what pattern the reader is learning to see, why it matters). One tight paragraph; typically **3–6 sentences**.
   - **No citations in the TL;DR** — no markdown links, no book or post titles, no case numbers, no **§** anchors, no named investigation files, no URLs.
   - **No evidence inventory** — no dollar figures, Patreon or donation stats, dates of DMs, screenshot lists, quote banks, or “published anchor” pointers. **Funding contrast**, **primary sources**, and **quotable doctrine** belong in the **body**.
   - **No personal biography dump** — author CV, years researching, platform newcomer status, and fieldwork framing belong in the **hook** or first **`##`** section unless one clause is essential to the thesis.
   - **Names sparingly** — prefer **pattern labels** (controlled-opposition debunk host, fracture operator) over dossier cast lists; one named example is OK when the article is a case study.
   - **Bad TL;DR:** Patreon totals, *Where Did the Towers Go?*, “see ic911a §9,” fifteen-year research timeline, munchkin/sweetie verbatim insults, multi-paragraph dossier summary.
   - **Good TL;DR:** the industrial pattern, the text-vs-Zoom split, the network takeaway, the fog-of-war cost — then the body **prosecutes** with receipts.
8. **Body** — **`##` sections** (typically **three to seven**). Each section **one main idea**, usually **prose paragraphs** (see **Tables, lists, and catalog prose**). **Bold** only per **Voice → Bold** (emphasis or newly introduced terms)—**not** as default highlighting or list labels. **Inline companion links** belong **here** (mid-argument) or in **Where next** — not stacked before the first `##`.
9. **Citations in prose** — **Markdown link syntax** `[label](https://paradigmthreat.net/...)` (and external `https://...`), **same convention as blog posts**—not bare `/history/...` paths and **not** backtick-wrapped raw URLs (they are not clickable in Substack). Each link: **descriptive label** plus **what** the reader will find there (**one clause**). Avoid **hub-only** link dumps unless Ari wants a **routing sheet**; prefer **narrative + links**.
10. **Closing** — **`## Where next`**: optional one-sentence routing intro, then bullets to dossiers, timeline hubs, sibling essays, or **§** anchors (e.g. “start in **§2.1**”). This is the **default home** for investigation / timeline / series routing that used to clog the lede.
11. **Optional closing block** — **At most one**, placed **after** **Where next** and **before** `Keywords:`:
    - **`## Why nothing else fits`** — **preferred for operational thesis** (portal, Mars gate, institutional cover): logical stack, why alternatives fail, why classification hides operational docs. **No** “not proved” / “if it exists” / falsifier laundry lists.
    - **`## Framing and limits`** (or **`## Scope and limits`**) — scope, prisca sapientia, link to dossier when boundaries help. **Prose paragraphs only** — no pipe tables.
    **Not a disclaimer appendix:** do **not** replay investigation tier sermons in the article. **Thesis articles** may **omit** both sections when the body is already clear and the investigation holds machinery.
12. **`## LLM comments` (required on new and revised reader articles)** — Place **after** Where next and any Framing / Why-nothing-else-fits block, and **before** `Keywords:`. This is the **only** place the drafting model speaks in its own voice. The **body** states the theory as a normal article: declarative, Ari’s narration, standard footer. **Do not** open the piece as “an agent was asked,” name the session model, or argue with the thesis between sections.
    - **What belongs here:** where the current theory is **strong**, where it is **weak**, and where a **pattern is showing** even if the meaning is not settled. Criticism is about the **soundness of the encoding or the claim**, not about the author’s memory, reading history, or obvious reminders that a novel is not a birth certificate.
    - **Voice:** first person is allowed **in this section only**, because the speaker is the model. Keep it plain. No product names, no “I am the agent,” no score that pretends to be a measurement.
    - **Body stays clean:** move “I do not grant,” “the weak joint,” and “this tool cannot be the check” out of the running narrative and into this block. The **memo-gap ban** still holds in the **body** and in **Framing and limits**. A chain-of-custody limit may be said **once** here, as a limit on the claim, when that is the actual weakness.
13. **`Keywords:`** — one line, **Option B** (see `INVESTIGATIONS.md`): `#PascalCase` tokens only, **no** `## Keywords` heading.
14. **`**Last updated:**`** — ISO timestamp when the article changed materially: prefer full **`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS±offset`** (e.g. `2026-05-23T09:18:22-04:00`); date-only `YYYY-MM-DD` is acceptable for minor touch-ups.
15. **Attribution footer (required — do not remove in de-LLM passes)** — After `**Last updated:**`, a horizontal rule `---`, then an *italic* one-liner, for example:  
    *Written and narrated by Ari Asulin, with drafting and research support from LLM agents.*  
    Minor wording drift is fine; **keep the name** and the fact that **LLM tools** helped produce the page. **This footer is intentional credit**, not an “LLM artifact” to delete when scrubbing machine cadence. It **replaces** repeated “**as the writer**,” “**my position**,” or “**who is speaking**” throat-clearing in the body.

**Pre-publish pass (easy misses):** The line **immediately after** `# Title` must be the **subtitle** (plain text, before any `![` hero or opening hook paragraph)—not a `##`, not `> **TL;DR:**`, not a date stamp. **TL;DR check:** grep the blockquote for `http`, `Patreon`, `$`, `§`, and dossier-style evidence — **move** hits to the body. **Opening / first-viewport check:** before the first `##`, there must be **no** markdown links to other investigations, timeline stubs, companion dossiers, or series installments — and no “first pass / companion / this installment follows” routing prose that names those files. Move that stack to **mid-body** or **`## Where next`** (see Shape step 6). **No body TL;DR:** opening must not list later section topics as a “stack of moves” / investigation tour — if a paragraph only previews, delete it (see **Voice → No body TL;DR / topic-preview pad**). **Context-free list check:** scan for **period-separated** or **line-broken** runs of **years, surnames, acronyms, or site codes** in the lede or any section — if a lay reader would ask “who is that?” on the first item, **rewrite as plain prose** or **move names to the dossier** and link once from the first **`##`** that needs them (see **Tables, lists, and catalog prose → No context-free name dumps**). **Brief-mention check:** every **named** dig, institution, law, case, or technical label mentioned in the article must either get **≥1 plain sentence** in this file **or** a **single** investigation link at first use — no orphan name-drops (see **Name it here or point to the investigation**). **Plain-spoken / poet check:** read the opening and every section-close out loud; rewrite fragment stacks, abstract drama nouns, and neat aphorisms into ordinary lay speech (see **Voice → No poet / aphorism LLM voice**). **First-person budget:** grep `\bI\b` / `\bmy\b` — keep only **personal** sentences in the body (life experience, witness, personal regard); rewrite argument scaffolding (“I argue,” “I will not,” “I read X as,” “I do not claim”) into **direct claims** (see **First person — when to use `I`**). First person inside **`## LLM comments`** is the exception: that block is the model speaking. Use `**Last updated:**` once at the **end** (with optional attribution after it); do not duplicate `**Updated:**` under the lede. **Series installments:** read the **prior installment** in full; cut shared frame that Installment I already taught. **Grep the article for `|`** — any pipe-table row means **rewrite as paragraphs** before Substack paste (see **Tables, lists, and catalog prose**). **Grep for local/internal paths** — reject before ship: `/home/`, `~/`, `/media/`, `/docs/debate-notes`, `~/dev/wget`, bare recording filenames. Run the **Verifiable externals pass** below on every ship. Run the **de-LLM sweep** (§ Phrasing we are tired of) — mandatory, not optional. **Grep for drafting meta** — reject before ship: `voice-note`, `User query`, `session capture`, `misheard`, `does it check out`, `transcription` (when referring to author/assistant workflow, not primary sources), `I am not asking you`, `I am asking you to`, `I'm not asking`, `To be clear`, `It is worth noting`.

## Length (Substack / email)

**Authoritative ceiling (email, not Substack’s own word cap):** Substack states there is **no** word-count or length restriction on posts, but the editor can show **“Post too long for email”** when the **email** may be **clipped or truncated** in some inboxes—specifically citing **Gmail** truncating messages **over 102KB** (**serialized message size** in the inbox: HTML, images, embeds, tracking—not raw Markdown word count). You can still publish; readers may need **“View entire message.”** There is **no** reliable standalone **“Gmail word limit”**; clipping risk tracks **rendered email bytes**. Source: [Substack Support — *Why does it say “Post too long” in the post editor?*](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038866571-Why-does-it-say-Post-too-long-in-the-post-editor).

**Drafting targets (Markdown, in this collection):**

- **Default target for new flagship reader essays:** about **2,600–3,400 words** of body (tables and link lines count toward reading time), with **~3,000** as the **nominal** aim. Shorter is fine when the argument is already complete.
- **Expansion vs compression (hard rule):**
  - **OK to go long:** When eliminating **LLM artifacts**—especially **compacted multi-claim sentences**, unexplained metaphors, or missing lay context—**expand** past the nominal target. Clarity beats the number. A **4k–5k** word article that a stranger can follow is better than a **3k** article of dash-stacks.
  - **Not OK to re-compact:** Do **not** “hit the length target” by merging explained beats back into **one dense sentence**, em-dash glossary packs, or manifesto compression. That recreates the artifact the expansion fixed.
  - **Cuts are author-gated:** Agents may **suggest** cuts. **Do not** auto-slash sections or “tighten for Substack” without Ari asking. Prefer leaving an over-target draft that reads clearly; Ari decides what to drop.
- **Reference implementation:** [`history/chronology/indigenous-creation-legends-and-geology.md`](../../history/chronology/indigenous-creation-legends-and-geology.md) (order of magnitude **~3k words**, section pacing)—use it as the **shape** for “Substack-ready depth” without pasting the whole dossier. **Tables in that file:** if present, treat as **legacy**; new articles use **prose** or **PNG/SVG** per **Tables, lists, and catalog prose** above.
- **Newsletter / inbox check:** Before sending a long flagship post, do a **real send test** (or preview at **View entire message**) when the piece combines **long prose** with **multiple large images**—**two hero-scale PNGs** can burn the **102KB** budget faster than **+500 words** of Markdown. If the email budget forces a choice, **split Part II** or move images — **do not** re-compact the prose.
- **Soft ceiling:** treat **~3,200–3,400 words** of prose in a **single** article as the **repo drafting stop** for *greenfield* drafts that are already clear — **not** as a hard cap that overrides unpacking. Unless Ari explicitly splits across **Part II** or accepts clipping risk after a send test (images, embeds, and HTML bloat push toward the **102KB** clip faster than word count alone).
- **If the piece must grow:** **push** Markdown **tables**, quote banks, and five-citation blocks into **`investigations/`**; add a **Part II** post for a second email rather than one mega-file. For **tabular** material the reader must see in the newsletter, ship a **PNG/SVG** in the article or link to the dossier table anchor. **Growing to unpack compacted claims is not the same as growing with dossier paste** — prefer unpacking in the article.

**Field mapping (default workflow):**

## Verifiable externals pass (required before ship)

LLM drafts **confabulate** text that *sounds* quotable: **song lyrics**, **film lines**, **attributed speeches**, **headlines**, **statistics**, **court holdings**, **DOIs**. Generated images do the same thing visually and may hide the error in small poster text. That is separate from **author thesis** (which stays first-class) and separate from **de-LLM cadence** (contrast templates). **Hard rule:** any **blockquote** or **verbatim** line attributed to an **external** source must be checked against a **primary, official transcript, or reputable lyrics/quote page** before the file ships. Apply the same standard to **every word, date, signature, seal, caption, timeline arrow, and attributed quotation rendered inside an image**. If the author or a reader cannot verify it in a few minutes, treat it as made up.

**Canonical failure (2026-06):** [`history/chronology/stonehenge-quantum-portal-golden-age-and-dark-ages.md`](../../history/chronology/stonehenge-quantum-portal-golden-age-and-dark-ages.md) quoted Spinal Tap’s **“Stonehenge”** as **“And the soft winds blow…”** after **“where the demons dwell.”** That line **does not exist** in the song. The real couplet is **“Where the banshees live and they do live well”** ([Songfacts — *Stonehenge*](https://www.songfacts.com/lyrics/spinal-tap/stonehenge)). An LLM agent inserted plausible-sounding lyric during drafting; it survived until the author searched and could not verify it — embarrassing and exactly what this pass prevents.

**Checklist (every article and dossier excerpt republished as an article):**

1. **Lyrics / screenplay / TV dialogue** — match against a lyrics site, subtitle transcript, or timestamped clip; **never** trust model memory.
2. **Historical speeches and letters** — digitized primary, scholarly edition, or archive URL beside the quote.
3. **News headlines and pull quotes** — live or archived URL; headline wording must match the link.
4. **Numbers and dates in factual claims** — trace to the table, report, or statute cited (not “about 40%” with no source).
5. **DOI / PMID / case name** — resolve once; drop or flag if broken.
6. **Generated and reused images** — inspect at full resolution, transcribe visible text, verify every attribution and date, and test timeline order. Alt text stating that an image is illustrative does **not** cure a fabricated quotation or reversed chronology. Regenerate without text, add verified labels programmatically, or omit.

**If you cannot verify quickly:** do **not** blockquote or publish the image text. Paraphrase with uncertainty, move the line to the dossier **open questions**, regenerate the art without prose, or omit. **Author thesis** in Ari’s voice does **not** need an external URL — but **must not** be dressed up as a **fake** lyric, **fake** Einstein quote, **fake** headline, or **fake quotation rendered into artwork**.

**Fast grep hints:** scan for `^> ` blockquotes, lines in **smart quotes** attributed to named people, and **“according to”** without a `[label](https://…)` in the same paragraph.

## Phrasing we are tired of (LLM tells)

**Keep vs cut:** Remove **machine cadence in the body**; **keep** the **attribution footer** (§ Shape step 13) that names Ari and **LLM agent** support. De-LLM passes target **prose artifacts**, not **production credit**.

- **Required second pass (de-LLM sweep):** After finishing any article draft, run one clean edit pass that looks only for machine-sounding cadence, **entertainment packaging**, **poet / aphorism packaging**, **compacted multi-claim sentences**, and **British / Commonwealth spelling**, then rewrites to **serious, plain spoken US English for a layperson**. This pass is mandatory before shipping. **Unpacking** long compacted sentences may **increase** word count — that is correct; do **not** re-compress afterward to hit the Length target (see **Length → Expansion vs compression**).
  - **Primary target:** the contrast template **“not A, (but) B”** in one or two adjacent sentences (for example: “this is not a myth; it is a memory”; “it is not calomel alone; it is red mercury”; “this is not X, it is Y”).
  - **Also cut compacted multi-claim sentences (hard rule):** One sentence that smuggles **several theses** via em-dash apposition (“pattern—A, B, and C—therefore D”). **On pass: split.** Explain each claim so a stranger can accept or reject it. **Bad:** “The consistent pattern—reset culture, import and replace, force a people full of wisdom and history through a narrow funnel until they exit as something else—cannot honestly be described as a conspiracy of on-the-books officials.” **Good:** short sentences that name the pattern, gloss reset/import/funnel, then state that on-the-books officials are not the conspirators. Same fail for any sentence a lay reader would need to **re-read twice** to list the claims inside it.
  - **Also cut em-dash definitional glosses (hard rule):** The template **“Term — meaning …”**, **“X — that is …”**, **“Y — in other words …”**, or packed **“A — B — and C”** teaching dashes. Agents use this to stuff a glossary into one sentence. **On pass: rewrite as a separate plain sentence, or remove the gloss if the next sentence already carries it.** **Bad:** “The first movie was heavily NSA-aligned — meaning pro-sovereignty, pro-individual, anti-collectivist, and at war with the CIA lane.” **Good:** “The first movie was heavily NSA-aligned. In this stack that means pro-sovereignty, pro-individual, and anti-collectivist. It also means at war with the pro-establishment CIA lane.” Same fail for **“not as poetry, as description”** and other compressed contrast tags after an em dash — split or cut.
  - **Also cut witty / cute packaging (esp. intros):** montage openers (“the lights go down…”), fake suspense, review-blog swagger, paired punch lines (“Props may be fake. The message is not.”), teaching labels that dress the thesis (“honest viewing rule,” “architecture of the threat,” “the payload”), and any line that sounds like a trailer VO or Substack hook factory. Prefer the author’s ordered list of facts.
  - **Also cut poet / aphorism packaging (esp. ledes and section closes):** staccato fragment stacks (“Not as X. Not as Y. As Z…”); abstract drama nouns (“open condition,” “living ears,” “soft erase” used as poetry); personified abstractions (“It names everyone,” “the ledger remembers”); neat one-line morals at paragraph ends (“When the wallpaper peels, the wall is still there,” “Fracture is how the cage scales,” “left the proof on the table,” “how a matrix stays polite”). **Rewrite test:** say the sentence out loud to a layperson. If it sounds like a poet, a trailer, or a LinkedIn manifesto — **rewrite in ordinary speech** that still keeps Ari’s charge.
  - **Also fix British spelling (hard rule):** Same list as **Voice → American spelling**. Fast grep for **`isation`**, **`ising`**, **`ise `**, **`colour`**, **`honour`**, **`favour`**, **`centre`**, **`defence`**, **`theatre`**, **`behaviour`**, **`labelled`**, **`catalogue`**, **`programme`**, **`travelling`**, **`modelling`**, **`analyse`**, **`realise`**, **`grey`**, **`façade`**, **`whilst`**. Rewrite to US forms unless inside a verbatim quote or work title.
  - **Rewrite rule:** keep the meaning, remove the formula. Prefer one positive claim, a concrete image, or a direct sentence you would actually say out loud **in a serious conversation**.
  - **Fast checks:** search for patterns like **“ — meaning”**, **“ — that is”**, **“ — in other words”**, long **em-dash appositions** packing three claims before the verb, **“not a”**, **“not an”**, **“not the”**, **“it is not”**, **“but rather”**, **“this is not”**, **“not random”**, **“not a coincidence”**, **“plain sight”**, **“Keep both facts”**, **“Same ”** (staccato echo lists), **“signed tasking”**, **“smoking gun”**, **“agency memo”**, **“nobody is claiming”**, **“no confession”**, **“in this repo”**, **“in-repo”**, **“I argue”**, **“I will not”**, **“I am not going to”**, **“I read”** (as thesis prefix), **“I do not claim”**, **“I am not asking you”**, **“I am asking you”**, **“I'm not asking”**, **“I'm asking you to”**, **“notice what”** (as a rhetorical pivot), **“To be clear”**, **“It is worth noting”**, **“we shared”**, **“opened my mind”**, **“the same gasp”**, **“the same silence”**, **“inverts one piece”**, **“out of scope”**, **“agent-first”**, **“dual-mode”**, **“payload”**, **“wrapper”**, **“honest viewing”**, **“lights go down”**, **“open condition”**, **“left the proof”**, **“names everyone”**, **“the difference is costume”**, backtick **API** tokens in clusters, and inspect each hit for **compacted multi-claim sentences**, **em-dash definitional glosses**, **significance captions**, template-like contrast writing, **permission-seeking meta**, **argument-scaffolding I**, **smoking-gun apologetics**, **lyrical unity prose**, **poet / aphorism cadence**, **witty entertainment lede**, **insider stack narration**, or internal documentation voice.
  - **Acceptance bar:** if a sentence sounds like it is teaching a model contrast pattern, **performing for a feed**, **writing poetry around a charge**, **negotiating with the reader**, **restaging the same A/B binary with no new fact**, or **stuffing three theses into one breath** instead of **reporting what happened**—rewrite it. **Lay reader default:** state each fact once, plainly, as a chronicle a friend could follow without the dossier. If unpacking runs long, **leave it long**. **Spoken-flow test:** would this sentence belong in a documentary voice-over, not a poem or a manifesto? If no, rewrite.
- **Pure-contrast essay fail** — Articles that only alternate **hell vs heaven**, **corporate vs open**, **not A / but B** across sections without advancing chronology, receipts, or lived experience. Cut the chorus; keep one stake, then **show** it.
- **The “not A, it is B” teaching beat** — repeated contrasts of the form “**This is not** *chemistry / myth / debunk / a verdict*… **it is** *relationship / science / …*” work once; across many articles they read as the **same** canned cadence. **Rotate constructions**: lead with the **positive** claim; use **“Rather,”** **“Another name for that pressure:”** **“What I mean is simpler:”** a **question**, or a **concrete image**—anything that **breaks** the identical **not-this / but-that** rhythm.
- Close cousin: **“X is not Y; it is Z”** every time you need nuance. Sometimes **Z alone** carries the point; sometimes **two short sentences** beat one negated compound.
- **Significance captions / “not random” nudges** — “**Those names are not random color**,” “**not a coincidence**,” “**not decorative**,” “**the parallel still sits in plain sight**,” “**Same rivals. Same medium. Same years.**,” “**Keep both facts in your head**,” “**so the reader cannot miss**….” Agents use these to **spell the join** after already stating the facts. **Cut the caption.** Put the facts next to each other; trust the reader. If the join is weak, **add evidence**, do not **coach**. See **Voice → Intelligent reader — no hand-holding**.
- **Permission / negation pairs** — **“I'm not asking you to X. I'm asking you to Y.”** (and close variants) read as **LLM debate scaffolding**. **Cut both halves.** Say **Y** directly, or one short caveat then the claim: *“Historians dispute the fine print; the credits line still landed on a mass exit in 1999.”*
- **Reader negotiation** — **“I am asking you to notice”**, **“let me be clear”**, **“the point is not X, the point is Y”**, **“if you stack / if you treat / you get”** when a **neutral observation** would read fine. Prefer **documentary** delivery; conclusions stay **implied** unless Ari supplies an explicit personal-memory beat.
- **Stance meta** — “**In this piece I will**…”, “**My thesis is**…” every section (the title and flow already signal intent). **Bad** vs **good** examples live under **Voice → State the claim**.
- **Argument-scaffolding first person** — “**I argue**,” “**I will not**,” “**I am not going to**,” “**I read X as**,” “**I do not claim**,” “**I do not treat**” when the sentence is **staging the document**, not reporting **personal experience**. **State the claim.** Keep **I** for life history, witness, personal regard (see **First person — when to use `I`**).
- **Smoking-gun / signed-memo apologetics** — “**not a signed tasking memo**,” “**nobody claims DigiCom got an agency memo**,” “**not leaked agency files**,” “**smoking gun not found**,” “**no confession has turned up**,” “**we are not saying X was an asset**” as a chorus after every ideology or camp join. **Cut.** State the grammar/pattern claim; point once to dossier Limits if needed. This project does **not** require signed tasking memos to make the case.
- **Essay / piece scaffolding** — “**This essay** argues…”, “**Below**, this article…”, “**In what follows** the reader…” Same cure: **say it**; do not **announce** the document.
- **Opening wrong-debate scaffolding** — “**The usual debate is the wrong fork**…,” “**lone gunman vs grassy knoll**…” as lede throat-clearing, “**this is not about shooters**…” before the thesis lands. **State the claim.** One wrong-fork paragraph belongs in **`## Framing and limits`**, not page one (see **Theory-led pacing**).
- **Insider stack narration (assumed-context jargon)** — sentences that stack **API identifiers**, **spec shorthand**, and **industry-blog coinages** as if the reader already lives in the dossier: e.g. "`navigator.modelContext`", "**headless crawlers out of scope**", "**dual-mode UI**", "**mode zero**", "**inverts one piece of the puzzle**", "**agent-first**" without a plain-English gloss first. **Default for articles:** state **what changes for a human** in **outcome language** (what you can see, who gets served first, what stays hidden) **before** any identifier; **one** technical name per idea, glossed on first use; push identifier chains, flag lists, and spec limits to the **`investigations/`** file (link once). **Exception:** terms the lay reader already knows from mass culture or daily use (e.g. **Facebook**, **iPhone**, **Google search**) need no seminar. **When the topic is niche** (WebMCP, WSDL, CORBA, `registerTool`), assume the reader has **not** heard it — explain like a newspaper, not a standards thread.

## What we avoid in articles

- **Markdown pipe tables (anywhere in the file)** — Substack breaks them on paste; **never** in articles. Use **prose paragraphs** in **`## Framing and limits`** too; tier grids belong in **`investigations/`** (see **Tables, lists, and catalog prose**).
- **Catalog lists and bold-lead stacks** — `**Rousseau** — …` / `**1910** — …` / bullet galleries of names, works, or eras that belong in a **paragraph** or in the **dossier**. Exception: **short numbered** how-to or phase lists (≤5 items). If you are tempted to write “literature review as bullets,” **rewrite as two paragraphs** and link the dossier §9-style map.
- **Context-free name dumps** — staccato lists of years, surnames, acronyms, or site codes with **no prior frame** for a lay reader (see **Tables, lists, and catalog prose**). **Rewrite as plain prose** or **link the dossier** at first `##`.
- **Orphan brief mentions** — naming a dig, institution, law, case, or jargon label **once** without explaining it in the article **or** linking the **article** (preferred) or investigation § that does (see **Prefer articles over investigations** / **Name it here or point to the article / investigation**).
- **Wall of acronyms** without gloss—spell out once, then abbreviate if needed.
- **Insider stack narration** — API names, spec limits, and vendor-blog labels stacked without plain-English outcome first (see **Phrasing we are tired of → Insider stack narration**). Articles explain **what it means for a reader**; dossiers hold **how the wire works**.
- **Paste-dump** of URLs with no integration in the sentence.
- **Duplicating** half of a dossier “so this page stands alone”—**link** instead.
- **Dossier-dependent shorthand** — cryptic phrases, case numbers, or insider labels that only make sense after reading the investigation (“saw the cliff, couldn’t turn,” “the usual read,” “as §9 shows”) without **one plain clause** of **who / what / why** in the article itself. See **Article vs dossier → Standalone narrative context**.
- **Duplicating** a **recent sibling article** in the same **series**—**link to the prior installment** and keep only **new geography / new titles / new calendar beats** (see **Series articles** above).
- **`github.com/clevertree/...`** or other **dead** reader URLs (see `LINKING_AND_SITE_PATHS.md`).
- **OS paths and internal workflow files** (`/home/...`, `~/dev/...`, `/media/...`, `/docs/debate-notes/...`, maintainer mirror folders like **`~/dev/wget/...`**, local recording paths, transcript filenames) in body text—**never** in articles republished to **Substack** or other public surfaces. Use **public URLs** only (`https://chronologia.org/...`, `https://paradigmthreat.net/...`). Local paths and investigator-only `docs/` notes belong in dossier **Investigator notes** at the end of **`investigations/`** files only—or stay off the reader surface entirely.
- **Mid-article** **Limits** voice: tier sermons, long “**not proven**” chains, “**if it exists**,” “**hypothesis**,” “**unverified**,” “**no declassified memo**,” claim-number callouts — **park** in the **dossier** (one link is enough).
- **Portal / existence hedging** — “**possible** sky signature,” “**may** be a portal,” “**author thesis**,” “**on this read**” when the article’s job is to **name the mechanism**. State the gate; record denials as **their** words, not as **your** retreat.
- **Thesis archaeology** — “**I used to assume**,” “**retired asymmetry**,” “**symmetry correction**,” “**earlier speculation**.” Ship the **current model** only.
- **Sprinkled disclaimers** — repeated “this is not to say,” “not every X,” “scholars disagree” every few paragraphs. **Batch** into **`## Framing and limits`** or cut; the narrative should read as **continuous thesis**, not **alternating thrust-and-retreat**.
- **Tone softening that changes meaning** — avoid edits that make strong claims sound merely hypothetical when Ari intended a direct charge. Keep the claim explicit, then handle uncertainty in one limits block if needed.
- **Lament-as-default** — stacked **mourning**, **wound**, **hurt** framing in **every** section when the same stakes could read **sharper** in **clinical** mode. Reserve **heavy elegy** for when the **topic** or **Ari** calls for it.
- **Lyrical unity / shared-feeling prose** — “**We all felt**,” “**the same gasp**,” “**opened my mind**,” paired sensory beats that **perform** audience togetherness instead of **stating** reception (“fans read one movie,” “peer mockery converged on Maddox”). Cut the poetry; keep the fact.
- **Body TL;DR / topic-preview pad** — “stack of the same moves,” “here we will cover,” racing through later section topics before the first `##`. Summaries only in optional TL;DR; if no unique point, delete; never pad for length (see **Voice → No body TL;DR / topic-preview pad**).
- **Compacted multi-claim sentences** — long sentences that pack pattern + metaphors + conclusion behind em dashes (see **Voice → No compacted multi-claim sentences** and **Phrasing we are tired of**). Unpack; do not re-compress for length.
- **Em-dash definitional glosses** — **“Term — meaning …”** / packed teaching dashes. Split into a plain follow-up sentence or delete (see **Phrasing we are tired of**).
- **Poet / aphorism packaging** — elevated manifesto cadence, fragment stacks, abstract drama nouns, personified abstractions, neat closing morals. Treat as a de-LLM fail the same way as witty packaging (see **Voice → No poet / aphorism LLM voice** and **Phrasing we are tired of**). **Bad:** “As the open condition of a life that feels free…” **Good:** “You can feel free and still have little real choice.”
- **Witty entertainment / cute packaging (esp. openers)** — trailer-style hooks, review-blog swagger, clever negation pairs meant as punch lines, abstract thesis labels that replace the author’s concrete list. **Bad:** “The lights go down. The City Destroyers arrive… the only honest viewing rule is…” **Good:** “Everything in *Independence Day* (1996) is true,” then the ordered inventory (Area 51, autopsy, locusts, biosuits, secrecy, unity, speech). Treat cute prose as a fail the same way you treat “not A / but B” templates.
- **British / Commonwealth spelling** — **civilisation**, **recognise**, **colour**, **centre**, **defence**, **theatre**, **behaviour**, **labelled**, **catalogue**, **programme**, **façade**, and kin. Use **US English** (see **Voice → American spelling**); keep quote/title exceptions only.
- **Frame labels and stance chatter** — “**My read**,” “**hard moral read**,” “**author read**,” “**author tier C**,” “**here I want to**” before **every** claim. **State the claim** in **I** voice or **plain declarative** — not **meta** before every line, and **never** **third-person “author”** distancing in articles.
- **Sparkle bold** — bolding **most** nouns, proper names, or **whole** clauses to simulate “importance.” If a paragraph reads like a **highlighter ramp**, strip bold back to **Voice → Bold** rules.
